How did Ankylosaurus use its tail club?

A relative of Ankylosaurus was named "destroyer of shins", and broken spikes on its sides suggest its kind really did swing their clubs at each other.

The short answer

Ankylosaurus was a low, wide plant-eater covered in bony armour. At the end of its tail was a heavy club of fused bone. By swinging its tail from side to side, it could hit with a lot of force.

Scientists think the club could break a leg bone. Tests on fossil clubs, and healed injuries on a close relative, suggest it was used both against predators and in fights with other ankylosaurs. A blow aimed low, at the shins of a two-legged hunter, would have been the most dangerous.

Surprising but true

Some of Ankylosaurus's close relatives even had a plate of bone in each eyelid.

A walking fortress

Ankylosaurus lived at the very end of the age of dinosaurs, in the same places as T. rex. It was about as long as a minibus and probably weighed more than an elephant. It was low to the ground, with short legs and a body almost as wide as it was long.

Its back, neck and head were covered in osteoderms: plates and knobs of bone that grow inside the skin. Crocodiles have them too, which is why a crocodile's back feels knobbly. Some of its relatives even had a plate of bone in each eyelid. Its belly, though, had no armour.

How the club worked

The club itself was made of large osteoderms fused into a lump at the tip of the tail. The last part of the backbone before it was locked stiff by tendons that had turned to bone. That gave the club a rigid handle, a bit like the handle of a hammer or a cricket bat.

The front of the tail stayed flexible, with strong muscles near the hips. So the animal could swing the whole stiff end sideways in a wide arc. In 2009, a scientist used measurements of fossil clubs to estimate how hard they could hit. Large clubs, she found, could probably break bone. Smaller ones would only bruise.

The destroyer of shins

In 2017, scientists described an ankylosaur from Montana with an unusually complete skeleton, including its skin armour. They named it Zuul crurivastator, after a monster in the film Ghostbusters. The second name means "destroyer of shins".

In 2022, the same team looked closely at Zuul's sides. Several spikes along its flanks were broken and had healed. The damage was in places that another ankylosaur's swinging tail would reach. Big hunters would more likely bite the back or neck. So the clubs seem to have been used in fights between ankylosaurs, perhaps over mates or space, like rams butting heads.

Armour against teeth

Being covered in bone has a cost. It is heavy, and Ankylosaurus could not have run fast. Instead, it relied on being very hard to bite. A predator that grabbed its back would get a mouthful of bone.

This is a different plan from Stegosaurus, which lived tens of millions of years earlier and carried long spikes on a flexible tail. Ankylosaurs did not have their clubs from the start either. Early members of the family had armour but plain tails. The full club, with its stiff handle, appeared later, as if the family slowly improved one weapon.

The first Ankylosaurus bones were found in Montana in 1906 by Barnum Brown, the same fossil hunter who found the first T. rex skeleton. Even now, Ankylosaurus itself is known from only a few partial skeletons. Much of what we know about the club comes from its better-preserved cousins.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Ankylosaurus had a hard shell like a turtle.

Truth: It had no shell. Its back was covered in hundreds of separate bony plates set in the skin, a bit like a crocodile's back, so its body could still bend.
