What toys did children play with long ago?

Children in the Indus cities played with clay carts on wheels and bird-shaped whistles more than 4,000 years ago.

The short answer

Children long ago played with many toys that would look familiar today: dolls, balls, spinning tops, toy animals and carts on wheels. They were made from whatever was at hand, such as clay, wood, bone, cloth and stone.

Archaeologists have found toys in ancient cities across the world. In the Indus cities, children had clay whistles and carts. In Egypt they had wooden animals with moving jaws. In Greece they spun tops and played with yo-yos. Many traditional Indian toys, like wooden lattus and painted dolls, carry on these ancient ideas.

Surprising but true

An ancient Greek vase painted about 2,500 years ago shows a boy playing with what looks just like a yo-yo.

Toys of the Indus cities

At Harappa, Mohenjo-daro and other Indus Valley sites, archaeologists have found hundreds of small clay objects that were almost certainly toys. There are miniature carts with wheels, clay animals such as bulls, monkeys and birds, and little whistles shaped like birds.

Some animals have heads that nod when a string is pulled. These toys are about 4,500 years old. They also help historians, because they show what the real carts and animals of the time looked like, and remind us that the wheel was used in India very early.

Egypt, Greece and Rome

Ancient Egyptian children played with balls made of leather or papyrus, dolls of wood and cloth, and carved animals. One famous wooden toy in the British Museum is a cat, or possibly a lion, with a jaw that opens when you pull a string.

In ancient Greece, children played with spinning tops, hoops, rattles and knucklebones, small bones thrown like dice. A painted vase from about 440 BCE shows a boy with a yo-yo. When Greek children grew up, they sometimes dedicated their toys to the gods, as a sign that childhood was over.

Who invented the wheel?The wheel came surprisingly late. People were farming, making pots and building towns for thousands of years before anyone rolled a cart.

India's traditional toys

India has a long tradition of handmade toys, many made in particular towns. In Channapatna, near Bengaluru, craftspeople turn wood on a lathe and colour it with lacquer and natural dyes. Channapatna toys have a Geographical Indication tag, which protects their name.

Kondapalli in Andhra Pradesh is famous for light wooden figures, and Etikoppaka for lacquered toys coloured with plant dyes. The Thanjavur dancing doll, or thalaiyatti bommai, has a weighted base and a head that sways. Children across India have also played with lattus, gilli-danda, marbles and kites.

Toys from factories

In the 1800s, factories in Germany and elsewhere began making tin toys, train sets and porcelain dolls in large numbers. Toys became cheaper, but many families still made their own.

In 1902 a cartoon showed US President Theodore Roosevelt refusing to shoot a captured bear on a hunting trip. A shop owner in New York, Morris Michtom, made a stuffed bear and called it "Teddy's bear". A German company, Steiff, was making toy bears at the same time, and teddy bears soon spread around the world.

The plastic age

After the Second World War, cheap plastic changed toys again. Building bricks like LEGO, plastic dolls and action figures filled shops. Today children also play video games and use apps.

Yet many of the oldest toys never went away. A ball, a top or a doll is still among the first toys many children own. Archaeologists say this tells us something about being human. Children everywhere, in every age, have found ways to play.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The yo-yo is a modern invention.

Truth: The modern yo-yo became a craze in the USA in the 1920s and 1930s, but toys like it are much older. A Greek vase from about 440 BCE shows a boy playing with one.

Try this

Make a spinning top from a paper plate or a circle of card and a pencil pushed through the centre. Spin it on a table. Then try moving the pencil off-centre and see what happens. Children have played with tops like this for thousands of years.
