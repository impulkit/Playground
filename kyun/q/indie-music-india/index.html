What is India's indie music scene?

For more than fifty years, a singer in Shillong has held a free concert every May to celebrate Bob Dylan's birthday.

The short answer

"Indie" is short for independent. In India it usually means music made outside the film industry and the big record companies. That covers rock bands in Shillong and Bengaluru, rappers in Mumbai's lanes, singer-songwriters with one guitar, and folk musicians mixing old songs with electronic beats.

For decades, almost all popular music in India came from films, so indie musicians struggled to be heard. Cheap recording, YouTube and streaming apps changed that. Today an artist can record a song in a bedroom, upload it, and find listeners in Kolkata and California on the same day.

Surprising but true

In 2019, Barack Obama included "cold/mess", a song by the Indian singer-songwriter Prateek Kuhad, in his list of favourite songs of the year.

Life in the shadow of films

Bollywood songs and other film music have been India's most popular music since the 1930s. Radio, cassettes and TV mostly played film songs, so record companies had little reason to sign anyone else.

In the 1990s, music TV channels like MTV and Channel V arrived. A short "Indipop" boom followed, with singers such as Lucky Ali and groups such as Colonial Cousins and Euphoria. Their songs had videos, not films, behind them. The boom faded by the 2000s, but it showed that young listeners wanted something different.

Bands and college stages

Rock bands had been playing in India long before. Indian Ocean, formed in Delhi in 1990, mixed rock with folk and classical sounds. Parikrama in Delhi and Thermal and a Quarter in Bengaluru built fans by playing at college festivals, which were among the few places a band could perform to thousands.

In Kerala, the band Avial sang rock in Malayalam, borrowing tunes and rhythms from old folk songs. Many bands sang in English at first, but more and more now choose Hindi, Tamil, Bengali or other languages. Singing in your own language, many musicians say, lets them write about the streets and people they actually know.

Shillong, a city of guitars

In Meghalaya's capital, Shillong, church choirs, rock records and guitar lessons are part of everyday life. The singer Lou Majaw has held a free concert on Bob Dylan's birthday every May since the 1970s.

Why is Shillong called the Scotland of the East?Rolling green hills, pine forests, misty lakes and rain that drifts in on the wind reminded British visitors so much of home that they nicknamed Shillong "the Scotland of the East".

Bands from Shillong and the rest of Northeast India often win national rock contests. The Hornbill Festival in Nagaland hosts a big rock contest every December.

Festivals in fields and valleys

From 2010, the NH7 Weekender festival in Pune brought indie bands to big outdoor crowds. In 2012, the Ziro Festival of Music began in a valley of rice fields in Arunachal Pradesh. Musicians play on stages made of bamboo, and campers sleep in tents among the paddy.

These festivals gave indie artists something they had lacked: a place to meet fans face to face. They also helped bands earn money from tickets, T-shirts and sponsors, since few indie songs ever made much from record sales. A band that played well at one festival might be invited to a dozen more that year.

Rap and the internet

In the 2010s, young rappers in Mumbai began writing about life in their neighbourhoods, in Hindi and local slang. This style of hip-hop was called gully rap, from the word for a narrow lane. The 2019 film Gully Boy was inspired by real Mumbai rappers.

Singer-songwriters found audiences online too. Prateek Kuhad's love song "cold/mess" spread far enough that Barack Obama named it one of his favourite songs of 2019. It is a long way from the days when a song needed a film to be heard.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Indian music means film music.

Truth: Film songs are the best-selling music in India, but the country also has classical, folk, devotional, rock, hip-hop, jazz and electronic music made by artists who have nothing to do with films.

Try this

Make a playlist of five songs in five different Indian languages, none from films. Ask an adult to help you search. Listen for which instruments sound "Indian" and which sound "Western", and notice how many songs mix both.
