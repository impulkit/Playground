How do langurs and macaques live alongside us?

In Delhi, people were once hired to scare off monkeys by pretending to be langurs.

The short answer

India's towns and temples are full of monkeys, mostly of two kinds. Macaques, such as the rhesus macaque in the north and the bonnet macaque in the south, are stocky, brown and bold. Langurs are long-limbed and grey, with black faces and very long tails.

Both have learned to live close to people. Monkeys find easy food near homes, markets and temples, and in many places people feed them, partly because monkeys are linked to Hanuman, the monkey god of the Ramayana.

Living side by side isn't always easy. Monkeys can snatch food, bite and raid crops, while people cut down forests and build roads through their homes. Both sides are still working out how to share.

Surprising but true

The "Rh" in blood groups like O+ and B− stands for rhesus, after the monkey in which scientists first studied it.

Two very different monkeys

Langurs are leaf-eaters. Leaves are tough and not very nourishing, so langurs have a stomach with several chambers, a bit like a cow's. Helpful bacteria inside break down the leaves. Langur mothers often let other females in the group hold and carry their babies, which are born dark brown and slowly turn grey.

Macaques eat almost anything: fruit, seeds, insects, flowers and food from bins. They have stretchy pouches inside their cheeks, so they can grab food quickly and chew it later somewhere safe. This is one reason they do so well in cities. Clever and quick, they have been known to unzip bags and open fridges.

Forest partners

In the forest, langurs have a famous partnership with chital, the spotted deer. Langurs feed high in the trees and drop leaves and fruit. Chital wait below and eat them.

Langurs can see a long way from the treetops. When they spot a leopard or a tiger, they give loud barking alarm calls, and the deer bolt. Chital have keen noses and also give alarm calls when they smell danger. So each animal helps the other stay alive.

What is the Ramayana?A prince, a princess, a demon king and an army of monkeys. The Ramayana has been retold for more than 2,000 years, from Indian villages to Thai palaces.

Monkeys in the city

In Delhi, thousands of rhesus macaques live around government buildings, parks and homes. For years, officials hired handlers with trained langurs to scare them off, because macaques fear langurs. When keeping langurs this way was stopped under wildlife law, some men were hired to imitate langur calls instead.

In hill towns like Shimla, macaques snatch food and glasses from tourists. Experts say feeding monkeys makes things worse. Monkeys that rely on handouts lose their fear of people, form bigger groups and grow more aggressive when the food stops. In some places, scientists are trying birth control to slowly bring numbers down.

A monkey in your blood

The rhesus macaque has played a large part in science. In 1949, a rhesus monkey called Albert II flew to space on a US rocket, years before any human did.

In 1940, scientists studying the blood of rhesus monkeys discovered a substance that most people also carry. They named it the Rh factor, after the monkey. It is the plus or minus in blood groups like A+ or O−.

There are even rhesus macaques in the USA. In the 1930s, a tour boat operator released a few on an island in a Florida river to entertain visitors. Nobody told him monkeys can swim. Their descendants now number in the hundreds and roam the forests nearby.

Rarer cousins

Not all Indian monkeys are thriving. The lion-tailed macaque, with its silver mane, lives only in the rainforests of the Western Ghats and is endangered. The golden langur, with fur like polished brass, lives in a small area of western Assam and Bhutan.

These monkeys need forest, not city streets. Their future depends on keeping the trees standing.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Monkeys in the wild eat mostly bananas.

Truth: Wild monkeys eat leaves, fruit, flowers, seeds, insects and sometimes eggs. Langurs live mainly on leaves. Most bananas monkeys get come from people.
