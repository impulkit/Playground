Why do we get bored?

In a 2014 experiment, many people left alone with their thoughts for 15 minutes chose to give themselves a small electric shock rather than just sit there.

The short answer

Boredom is the uncomfortable feeling of wanting to do something satisfying and not being able to. Your mind is restless and ready, but nothing around you holds its attention. Time seems to drag. You might fidget, sigh or keep checking the clock.

Scientists think boredom works like a signal. Hunger tells you that your body needs food. Boredom tells you that what you're doing isn't giving your brain enough to work with, or doesn't seem to matter. It nudges you to change something. That can lead to good things, like a new hobby, or bad ones, like doing something silly just to break the dullness.

Surprising but true

The English word "boredom" only became common in the 1850s, helped along by Charles Dickens's novel Bleak House.

The button experiment

In 2014, psychologists at the University of Virginia asked volunteers to sit alone in a plain room for about 15 minutes with nothing to do but think. Most found it unpleasant. Their minds wandered and they struggled to stay with their own thoughts.

In one version, the room had a button that gave a mild electric shock. Earlier, everyone had tried the shock and many said they would pay money to avoid feeling it again. Yet about two-thirds of the men and a quarter of the women pressed the button at least once while they were alone. One man pressed it 190 times. To many people, it seems, doing something unpleasant can feel better than doing nothing.

What's happening in your head

Boredom is closely tied to focus. When a task is too easy, your attention slides off it. When a task is too hard, or makes no sense to you, attention gives up. Both can feel boring. Researchers also find that people get bored when they feel they have no choice, like waiting in a long queue or listening to a talk they didn't pick.

Boredom also bends time. While you're bored, every minute feels long, because you keep noticing the clock. Yet when you look back on a boring week, it seems to have passed quickly, because little happened that your memory could hold on to.

Who was Charles Dickens?At twelve, Charles Dickens was sticking labels on pots of boot polish in a rat-infested factory. He grew up to become the most famous writer of his age.

A surprisingly new word

People have always felt dull and restless, but the word "boredom" is young. English speakers used "bore" to describe a dull person or thing in the 1700s. "Boredom" only became common in the 1850s. Charles Dickens used it in his novel Bleak House, about a rich lady who is bored to death, and that helped spread it.

Other languages had their own words much earlier, such as the French ennui. Hindi speakers might say bore ho raha hoon, borrowing the English word.

Can boredom be useful?

Some studies suggest that a dull spell can lead to more creative thinking. In one British experiment, people who first spent time copying numbers from a phone book later came up with more ideas for uses of plastic cups than people who hadn't. The idea is that a bored mind starts to wander and make new connections. Scientists are still testing how strong this effect really is.

Boredom may also push people towards goals. Feeling stuck can be the spark that sends someone off to learn guitar, start a garden or write a story.

Phones fill every gap

Today, many people never have to sit with boredom. A phone fills every queue, car journey and quiet moment. That isn't always bad, but some researchers worry that we lose the chance to daydream, plan or simply notice the world. Thinking about your screen time is one way to give your mind a little empty space.

If boredom turns into a long-lasting feeling that nothing is worth doing, that can be a sign of low mood. It's worth talking to a parent, teacher or doctor about it. Most boredom, though, is just your brain asking for a better challenge.

Myth or fact?

Myth: You only get bored when there is nothing to do.

Truth: People can feel bored with a phone full of games and a room full of toys. Boredom comes from not finding anything that holds your attention or feels meaningful, not simply from a lack of things to do.

Try this

When you feel bored, set a timer for ten minutes and don't reach for a screen. Keep a notebook nearby and write down every idea, question or plan that pops into your head. Afterwards, look at the list. Is there one thing on it you'd like to try?
