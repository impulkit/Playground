Who was Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar?

As a boy walking to Calcutta with his father, Ishwar Chandra is said to have learned English numbers by reading the milestones by the road.

The short answer

Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar was a teacher, writer and social reformer in nineteenth-century Bengal. He wrote a simple Bengali first reader, Barnaparichay, that children still learn from. He campaigned for widows to be allowed to remarry, and in 1856 a law made it legal.

He also fought child marriage, opened dozens of schools for girls and gave away much of his money to people in need. In Bengal he's remembered for his learning and his kindness alike, sometimes called dayar sagar, an ocean of kindness.

Surprising but true

Vidyasagar wasn't his surname. It's a title, meaning "ocean of learning", given to him by his Sanskrit college in 1839 when he was still a teenager.

A poor boy who loved to read

Ishwar Chandra Bandyopadhyay was born in 1820 in Birsingha, a village in what is now West Bengal. His family was poor. When he was about eight, he walked with his father to Calcutta, now Kolkata, so he could study there.

A story often told says he learned English numerals on the way by reading milestones beside the road. Another says he studied at night under street lamps because his family couldn't afford oil. Whether every detail is exact, he became a brilliant student. In 1839 his Sanskrit college gave him the title Vidyasagar.

The alphabet book

In 1855 he published Barnaparichay, meaning "introduction to letters". It taught the Bengali alphabet in a clear order, with short words and simple sentences. He also tidied up the alphabet itself, settling on 12 vowels and 40 consonants for teaching.

The book was so clear that it is still used, more than 170 years later. Many Bengali speakers in India and Bangladesh learned to read from it.

He kept writing for young readers. Kathamala retold Aesop's fables in Bengali, and other books taught science, history and good habits in short, plain sentences. Before him, much written Bengali was stiff and full of long Sanskrit words. His clean style helped turn it into a language children could read for pleasure.

What makes Bengali special?In 1952, students in Dhaka died fighting for the right to use Bengali. The whole world now marks that day as Mother Language Day.

Opening the doors of learning

Vidyasagar became the head of Sanskrit College in Calcutta in 1851. At the time, only students from Brahmin families could study there. He changed the rules so students from other castes could join.

He believed girls deserved schooling too. He helped run the Bethune School, one of the first schools for girls in Calcutta, and in the late 1850s he helped set up about 35 girls' schools across Bengal. Around the same time, in Pune, Savitribai Phule was teaching girls against fierce opposition.

A fight for widows

In Bengal at that time, many girls were married very young. If their husbands died, even child widows were usually not allowed to remarry. They often lived hard, lonely lives.

Vidyasagar studied old Sanskrit texts and argued that they did not forbid widows from marrying again. He wrote books on the subject and gathered thousands of signatures on a petition. Many powerful people opposed him, and some threatened him. In July 1856 the Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act was passed. That December he helped arrange one of the first widow remarriages under the new law, and he later supported his own son's marriage to a widow.

Kind and stubborn

Vidyasagar was famous for refusing to bow to people he thought were wrong, whether British officials or rich landlords. He was equally famous for generosity. When the poet Michael Madhusudan Dutt was in debt in France, Vidyasagar sent him money.

He continued earlier work by reformers such as Raja Ram Mohan Roy, and he became a central figure of the Bengali Renaissance, a time of new ideas in literature and society. He died in 1891. Today a university, colleges, a bridge over the Hooghly and countless schools carry his name.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Vidyasagar was Ishwar Chandra's family name.

Truth: His family name was Bandyopadhyay. Vidyasagar was a title his college gave him for his learning, and it became the name everyone used.
