How did Bollywood dance become famous worldwide?

One of Hindi cinema's best-loved dances was filmed on the roof of a moving train in the Nilgiri hills.

The short answer

Bollywood dance grew out of Hindi films, where songs and dances have been part of the story since sound arrived in Indian cinema in 1931. Choreographers mixed steps from classical dance, village folk dances and Western styles like disco and hip-hop. The result was bright, energetic and easy to enjoy even if you didn't understand the words.

It spread through the millions of Indians living abroad, who carried films and music with them. Weddings, college clubs and fitness classes picked it up. By the 2000s you could take a Bollywood dance class in New York, London or Sydney, often taught by people who had never been to India.

Surprising but true

The song "Chaiyya Chaiyya" from the 1998 film Dil Se was filmed on top of a real train running on the Nilgiri Mountain Railway.

A song in every story

When Alam Ara came out in 1931 as India's first sound film, it had several songs. Audiences loved them, and songs soon became a normal part of Hindi films. Actors usually mimed to recordings by playback singers, so the stars were free to dance.

A film song is often a little break from the plot. A couple falls in love, and suddenly they are dancing in a Swiss meadow. A festival scene turns into a crowd of a hundred dancers. These numbers were filmed with care, and many people remember the songs long after they forget the story.

Borrowing from everywhere

Watch closely and you can spot many traditions at once. The quick spins and footwork often come from Kathak, the classical dance of northern India. The hand gestures may come from Bharatanatyam. The shoulder bounces and raised arms come from Punjab's bhangra.

Then come the Western moves. In the 1950s and 1960s, the dancer Helen brought cabaret and jazz styles to the screen. In the 1980s, Mithun Chakraborty made disco a craze, and in the 2000s Hrithik Roshan's fast hip-hop steps set a new standard.

How did Kathak spin its way through history?A Kathak dancer can spin dozens of times without stopping, then freeze, perfectly still, on the last beat.

The choreographers

Behind every famous dance is a choreographer, the person who designs the steps. Saroj Khan created many classic numbers, including "Ek Do Teen" in Tezaab (1988), and was known for expressive faces as much as footwork.

Farah Khan choreographed "Chaiyya Chaiyya" for Dil Se (1998). The dancers performed on the roof of a train on the Nilgiri Mountain Railway in Tamil Nadu, held by hidden safety ropes. Later she worked on stage shows abroad too, helping carry the style to new audiences.

How the world caught on

Indian families abroad kept watching Hindi films on video and satellite TV, and danced to the songs at weddings and festivals. Their children formed dance teams at American and British universities, where Bollywood fusion competitions became popular.

In 2009, the song "Jai Ho" from the British film Slumdog Millionaire, with music by A. R. Rahman, won an Oscar. Its dance finale in a Mumbai railway station was seen around the world. In 2023, "Naatu Naatu" from the Telugu film RRR won the Oscar for Best Original Song. RRR isn't a Bollywood film, but its dance showed how much the world now loved Indian film dance in general.

More than one Indian cinema

Hindi films aren't the only Indian films with famous dances. Telugu, Tamil and other South Indian cinemas have their own dance stars and styles. Prabhu Deva, from Tamil films, became famous for his rubbery, lightning-quick moves.

Today these styles borrow from each other all the time. A dance step from a Telugu hit might turn up in a Hindi film a year later, and then in a dance class in Chicago.

Myth or fact?

Myth: All Indian films are Bollywood films.

Truth: Bollywood is the Hindi-language film industry based in Mumbai. India also has big film industries in Telugu, Tamil, Malayalam, Kannada, Bengali, Marathi and other languages, and some of them release as many films a year as Bollywood.

Try this

Watch one Bollywood dance and one classical dance clip with an adult. Look for a hand gesture, a foot pattern or a spin that appears in both. Then try making up a short dance of your own that mixes a move from each.
