What is the Vande Bharat train, and what makes it different?

India's sleek white express has no engine at the front. Its motors are hidden under the coaches, spread along the whole train.

The short answer

Vande Bharat is a family of fast, modern express trains built in India. The first one ran from New Delhi to Varanasi in February 2019. More than a hundred Vande Bharat services were running by 2025, linking big cities across the country, usually on day trips of up to about eight to ten hours.

What makes them different is how they move. Most Indian trains are pulled by a locomotive, a separate engine at the front. A Vande Bharat has no locomotive. Instead, electric motors sit under many of the coaches. The whole train pulls itself along, which lets it speed up and slow down much faster.

Surprising but true

The first Vande Bharat train was designed and built at a factory in Chennai in about 18 months, much faster than such trains usually take.

Motors spread along the train

Engineers call this kind of train an electric multiple unit. Mumbai's local trains and most metro trains work the same way. Spreading the motors out has several advantages. The train grips the rails better, because many wheels are pushing, not just a few at the front. It can reach high speed in a short distance, which saves time on routes with lots of stops. And if one motor fails, the others can usually keep the train going to the next station.

When the train slows down, its motors work in reverse, like generators. They turn some of the train's speed back into electricity and feed it into the overhead wires. That's called regenerative braking, and it saves a good share of the energy.

Built in Chennai, fast

The first train was built at the Integral Coach Factory in Chennai, which has made railway coaches since 1955. It was called Train 18, because the plan was to finish it in 2018. A team of Indian engineers designed and built it in about 18 months, and it cost much less than buying a similar train from abroad. Factories in other cities have since joined in, so new trains can roll out faster.

The train was renamed Vande Bharat, meaning "salute to India", before its first trip. Newer versions have been improved again and again, with stronger noses, better seats and more safety features.

Inside the coach

Passengers board through automatic sliding doors, and the coaches are sealed and air-conditioned. Every seat has a charging point. Executive-class seats can rotate to face the direction the train is going. Screens show the next station, and the coaches have CCTV cameras.

Many of the trains are fitted for Kavach, an Indian-made system that can brake a train automatically if the driver misses a red signal. It only works where the track has Kavach equipment too, and that is being added route by route.

Fast trains on old tracks

On test runs, the first Vande Bharat reached 180 km/h. In service, though, the highest speed allowed is 160 km/h, and only on a few upgraded stretches near Delhi. On many routes it averages well under 100 km/h, because India's railways still have curves, crossings and slower trains on the same lines.

The trains have also faced practical troubles. In 2022, several had their front noses damaged after hitting cattle that wandered onto the tracks. Railways strengthened the design and added fencing on some routes.

A sleeper version, with beds for overnight journeys, began carrying passengers between Howrah and Guwahati in January 2026, so people can travel between distant cities while they sleep. True bullet trains, on special tracks, are a separate project. Vande Bharat is about getting faster on the railway India already has.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Vande Bharat trains zoom along at 180 km/h for their whole journey.

Truth: The trains can reach 180 km/h in tests, but in service the top permitted speed is 160 km/h, and only on a few upgraded stretches. Average speeds on most routes are much lower, because the tracks set the limit.
