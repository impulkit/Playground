Can sand dunes sing?

In some deserts, when sand slides down a dune, the whole hill starts to hum, loud enough to be heard kilometres away.

The short answer

Yes, some sand dunes really sing. When dry sand slides down the steep side of certain dunes, it makes a deep, steady hum, like a low note on a cello or the drone of a distant plane. The sound can last for minutes and carry for kilometres.

Scientists know of about 35 places in the world with "booming" or "singing" dunes, in deserts from China and Morocco to California, Chile and Oman. Each dune tends to hum at its own note. Some get as loud as a lawnmower beside you.

The sound comes from the sand itself. Exactly how millions of grains manage to sing one clear note is still argued over.

Surprising but true

Each singing dune tends to hum at its own note, and some can be as loud as a lawnmower right next to you.

A desert that hums

Not every dune sings. The sand has to be very dry, and the grains need to be rounded, polished and roughly the same size, mostly made of silica, the material in glass. Wet, dusty or jagged sand stays silent. Even singing dunes go quiet after rain until they dry out again.

The song begins when sand on the steep face of a dune, called the slip face, starts to avalanche. Sometimes wind sets it off. Often visitors do, by sliding down the slope. As the sand pours down, the dune begins to hum.

The note is low, usually between about 70 and 110 vibrations a second. That is similar to the lowest notes a man can sing. You feel it in your chest as much as hear it with your ears, as you can learn in how sound travels.

Travellers' tales

People have written about singing sands for more than a thousand years. Chinese writers described a hill near Dunhuang, on the old Silk Road, called Mingsha Shan, or "Echoing-Sand Mountain". It still hums today, and tourists ride camels to hear it.

In the 1200s, the Italian traveller Marco Polo described a desert where travellers heard music, drums and voices in the sand. He thought spirits were trying to lead people astray. Today, scientists think he may have heard booming dunes.

How does sand sing?

Here scientists disagree. One team in France, including Stéphane Douady and Bruno Andreotti, argues that the grains in an avalanche fall into step, bumping together in rhythm. Each bump pushes the air, and millions of synchronised bumps make one note, like a stadium crowd clapping together.

A team at Caltech in California, led by Melany Hunt, has a different idea. They found that singing dunes often have a dry upper layer sitting on a harder, damper layer below. They think the top layer traps sound waves and makes them ring, rather like the body of a guitar.

Both teams can make sand hum in experiments, but neither idea explains every dune yet.

Dunes that move

Dunes don't just sing. Many of them move. Wind blows sand up the gentle side of a dune and drops it over the top onto the slip face. Grain by grain, the whole dune creeps forward.

Small, crescent-shaped dunes called barchans can travel tens of metres in a year. In some deserts, moving dunes bury roads, railway lines and even villages. In the Thar Desert of Rajasthan, farmers plant grasses and trees to hold sand dunes in place.

Dunes move on other worlds too. Cameras orbiting Mars have watched Martian dunes shift over a few years, pushed by its thin winds.

And the strangest part? In one dry lakebed in California, it isn't sand that moves but heavy rocks. They slide across the ground leaving long trails behind them, a puzzle solved only in 2014, as you can find out in the sailing stones.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The humming sounds in deserts are made by spirits, drums buried in the sand or hidden caves.

Truth: Travellers once believed such stories, but the sound comes from sand grains sliding together in a huge avalanche. Scientists have recorded it and even made sand hum in laboratories.

Try this

Pour dry sugar or salt slowly from a cup onto a heap on a tray and listen closely with your ear near it. You may hear faint hissing. Now try with damp sugar. Singing dunes only work when sand is completely dry, for a similar reason.
