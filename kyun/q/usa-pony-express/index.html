What was the Pony Express?

In 1861, relays of teenage riders galloped Abraham Lincoln's first speech as president across the American West in about 7 days and 17 hours.

The short answer

The Pony Express was a mail service that used relays of riders on fast horses to carry letters across the western United States. It ran from April 1860 to October 1861, between St Joseph, Missouri, and Sacramento, California, about 3,100 km (1,900 miles).

Before it began, mail from the East Coast could take weeks or months to reach California by ship or stagecoach. The Pony Express cut that to about 10 days. It ended almost as soon as the first telegraph line crossed the continent, which could send a message in minutes.

Surprising but true

The Pony Express lasted only about 18 months and lost money the whole time, yet it became one of the most famous delivery services in history.

Why the rush?

By 1860, California was booming after the gold rush, and more than 300,000 people lived there. Yet it was cut off from the eastern states by deserts, mountains and plains with no railway. The country was also sliding towards civil war, and Californians wanted fast news.

Three businessmen, William Russell, Alexander Majors and William Waddell, decided to prove that a fast central route could work. They hoped it would win them a valuable government mail contract. They bought around 400 horses and built a chain of relay stations across the West in just a few months.

Riders and relays

Pony Express riders were usually young and light, often teenagers, because every kilogram slowed the horse. Each rode about 120 km or more on a shift, swapping horses roughly every 15 to 25 km at small stations. A swap took only a couple of minutes.

The mail travelled in a leather cover called a mochila, with locked pockets for letters, which fitted over the saddle. A rider could whip it off one horse and throw it onto the next in seconds. Riders galloped through blizzards, deserts and darkness, day and night. A full run from Missouri to California needed dozens of riders and hundreds of horse changes.

Lincoln's speech

The fastest Pony Express run ever carried President Abraham Lincoln's first inaugural address, from March 1861. Riders took it from the end of the telegraph line in Nebraska to the western end of the line in Nevada in about 7 days and 17 hours.

The speech mattered. Southern states were leaving the Union, and Californians were anxious to know how Lincoln would respond. Within weeks, the Civil War began.

Expensive and short-lived

Sending a letter by Pony Express was costly. At first, it cost $5 for half an ounce, about 14 g, which was a lot of money then, perhaps a week's wages for many workers. Prices later dropped, but the service never made a profit.

On 24 October 1861, the transcontinental telegraph was completed. Messages that took riders 10 days could now be tapped out in Morse code in minutes. Two days later, the Pony Express announced it would close. Its owners lost a fortune.

Legends and truth

Many stories about the Pony Express grew in the telling. Buffalo Bill Cody, the famous showman, claimed he rode for it as a boy, but historians aren't sure. Wild West shows and films later made the riders into heroes facing constant danger.

Real dangers existed, from weather, rivers and accidents. During a war with the Paiute people in Nevada in 1860, some stations were attacked and burned. But records suggest only one load of mail was ever lost.

Runners in India

India had its own relay mail long before the Pony Express. Runners called dak harkaras carried mail in stages, often at a jog, ringing bells to warn off wild animals and robbers. That system, described in the story of the Hindustani mail, lasted into the 1900s in remote areas, much longer than America's famous horse riders.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Pony Express ran for many years and was the main way Americans sent mail west.

Truth: It ran for only about 18 months. Most mail still went by slower stagecoach or ship. The Pony Express carried urgent letters and news for people who could afford its high prices.

Try this

Organise a relay with friends. Each person runs one stretch of a playground or park carrying a folded "letter". Time the whole delivery. Then try it with one person running the whole way and compare. Notice how much faster the relay is, even with handovers.
