When did the first plane fly in India?

India's first official airmail flight, in 1911, lasted about 13 minutes, crossed a river and carried around 6,500 letters during the Kumbh Mela.

The short answer

Aeroplanes first flew in India in December 1910, only seven years after the Wright brothers first flew in the USA. European pilots shipped flimsy wood-and-cloth planes to India and flew them at shows in Calcutta and Allahabad, while crowds stared up in amazement.

The most famous early flight came on 18 February 1911. A 23-year-old French pilot, Henri Pequet, flew from Allahabad across the river Yamuna to Naini, about 10 km away, carrying around 6,500 letters. It is widely recognised as the world's first official airmail.

Surprising but true

The 1911 Allahabad flight is widely recognised as the world's first official airmail, years before the USA or Britain started regular air post.

The winter of 1910

In November 1910, two Belgian pilots, Baron Pierre de Caters and Jules Tyck, arrived in Calcutta with their aircraft packed in crates. They flew several times from the grounds of the Tollygunge Club. On one flight in December, de Caters took up Mrinalini Sen, a Bengali writer, who became the first Indian woman to fly, as a passenger.

At about the same time, a British company, Humber, brought planes to an exhibition in Allahabad. Their pilots made flights there too, and they planned something new.

Planes then were very simple. They were made of wooden frames covered in cloth, held together with wires, and powered by engines weaker than a modern motorbike's. Pilots sat out in the open with no seatbelt. A gust of wind could flip them over, and many early pilots in Europe and America died in crashes. Flying in front of a crowd took real nerve.

Letters in the sky

The 1911 flight happened during the Kumbh Mela, when lakhs of pilgrims had gathered at Allahabad, now Prayagraj, where the Ganga and Yamuna meet. The organisers wanted to raise money for a students' hostel, so they asked people to send letters by air for a small extra fee.

Pequet took off in a Humber-Sommer biplane, a fragile machine with an open seat. The mailbags were strapped in beside him. He flew low over the river and landed at Naini about 13 minutes later. A postal official took the bags and sent the letters onward by ordinary post. Each envelope was stamped "First Aerial Post, U.P. Exhibition, Allahabad 1911".

The idea caught on fast. Later in 1911, Britain flew mail between London and Windsor to mark the crowning of King George V. In the USA, the first official airmail flight took place on Long Island in September 1911, and the US Post Office began regular airmail flights in 1918.

An airline begins

For the next 20 years, flying in India was mostly for adventurers, the army and rich hobbyists. That changed with a young man named JRD Tata. In 1929, he received the very first pilot's licence issued in India.

On 15 October 1932, he flew a small single-engine de Havilland Puss Moth from Karachi to Bombay, via Ahmedabad, carrying mail. It was the first flight of Tata Air Lines. In 1946, that company became Air India.

Women take to the air

Indian women followed fast. In 1936, Sarla Thakral earned her flying licence in Lahore, and she wore a sari in the cockpit. She was not the very first Indian woman to get one, but she became the most famous, and her story is told in India's first woman pilot.

From 13 minutes to 16 hours

In 1911, a 10 km trip was headline news. Today, India's airlines fly more than 4 lakh passengers on busy days (2025) within the country alone, and non-stop flights connect Bengaluru to San Francisco in about 16–17 hours. And the Indian Air Force, which now flies jets faster than sound, began in the very same year as JRD Tata's first mail flight, with just a handful of planes and pilots.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Wright brothers' first flight carried passengers and cargo straight away.

Truth: Their first flight, in 1903, lasted 12 seconds and carried only the pilot. It took years before planes could carry mail, and longer still before they carried paying passengers.
