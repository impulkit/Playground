What is the Shore Temple of Mahabalipuram?

For 1,300 years, the Shore Temple has stood right at the edge of the Bay of Bengal, with salty waves crashing almost at its walls.

The short answer

The Shore Temple is a stone temple standing right on the beach at Mahabalipuram, about 55 km south of Chennai. It was built in the early 700s CE by the Pallava king Narasimhavarman II, also known as Rajasimha. It has two shrines to Shiva and one to Vishnu, and its towers rise in neat stepped layers like a pyramid.

It is one of the oldest structural stone temples in South India. Instead of carving a temple out of a single rock, its builders stacked cut granite blocks. Sea winds and salt have worn the carvings soft, but the temple still stands, more than 1,300 years later.

Surprising but true

When the 2004 tsunami pulled the sea back from Mahabalipuram, people saw long-hidden rows of stones on the seabed, and shifting sand on the beach later uncovered a large stone lion.

A port of the Pallavas

In the 600s and 700s, the Pallavas ruled much of South India from their capital at Kanchipuram. Mahabalipuram, then called Mamallapuram, was their busy port. Ships sailed from here to Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia, carrying goods, ideas and even architects.

The town is named after King Narasimhavarman I, nicknamed Mamalla, "the great wrestler". Under him and his successors, sculptors turned the area's granite hills into a giant open-air workshop. Stone carving never left the town. Today hundreds of sculptors still work in Mahabalipuram's streets, and the tapping of chisels on granite can be heard from morning till evening. Their gods and elephants are shipped to temples across India and abroad.

From rock-cut to built

Earlier monuments at Mahabalipuram were carved straight out of boulders and cliffs. There are cave temples, the five Rathas cut from single rocks, and a huge carved rock face called Arjuna's Penance, or the Descent of the Ganga.

The Shore Temple was a step forward. Builders cut granite blocks, shaped them and stacked them into towers, with carvings added on top. That method later made giant temples possible, like the Chola temple at Thanjavur, built about 300 years later.

A temple facing the sunrise

The main shrine faces east, toward the sea, so the first light of morning falls on it. A smaller Shiva shrine faces west, toward the land. Between them lies a shrine to Vishnu, shown lying down in deep rest. This Vishnu is carved from the bedrock itself, and the temple was built around him.

Rows of stone bulls, Nandi, line the walls of the courtyard. Many have been worn almost smooth by centuries of salty wind.

The Seven Pagodas

European sailors once called Mahabalipuram the Seven Pagodas. The name led to a legend that seven temples once stood here, and the sea swallowed six. For centuries, people wondered if there was any truth in it.

On 26 December 2004, the Indian Ocean tsunami struck. Just before the wave hit, the sea pulled back hundreds of metres. People saw rows of stones and carvings that had been underwater for ages. Afterwards, sand washed away from the beach revealed a large stone lion and other carvings. Divers have since found remains of walls and structures offshore, though how old they are is still being studied.

Saving it from the sea

The sea has always been the temple's biggest danger. Waves and salt eat away at the stone. In the 1990s and 2000s, the Archaeological Survey of India built a stone wall, called a groyne, and planted trees to protect it. Experts also treat the stone to stop salt damage.

In 1984, UNESCO named the Shore Temple and the other Mahabalipuram monuments a World Heritage Site. Each winter, the Mamallapuram Dance Festival brings classical dancers to perform near Arjuna's Penance, with the sea breeze blowing.

The sea gives, and the sea takes. Along coasts around the world, waves slowly carve away cliffs, and the Shore Temple's builders seem to have known that only the hardest granite could last.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Shore Temple was carved out of a single rock, like Mahabalipuram's other monuments.

Truth: Many famous monuments at Mahabalipuram are cut from one rock. The Shore Temple is different. It was built from cut granite blocks stacked together, making it one of the earliest important stone temples built this way in South India.
