Why do cities have slums?

In Dharavi, in the heart of Mumbai, potters, tailors, leatherworkers and recyclers run thousands of small workshops between their homes.

The short answer

A slum is a crowded neighbourhood where homes are often small and built from whatever is cheap. Many families there lack proper water, toilets and drains, or a legal right to their land.

Slums grow when people move to a city faster than it can provide homes they can afford. Newcomers usually come for work. Rent near jobs is high, and travelling in from far away costs money and hours. So people build or rent small rooms on any spare land close to work: beside railway lines, along rivers or on empty plots.

India's 2011 Census counted about 6.5 crore people living in slums, roughly one in every six people in its towns and cities. Around the world, UN-Habitat estimates that about one billion people live in slums or similar settlements.

Surprising but true

Some of Dharavi's potters belong to families who came from Gujarat generations ago, and their part of Dharavi is still called Kumbharwada, the potters' quarter.

A city of workshops

Dharavi, in Mumbai, is one of Asia's best-known slums. It covers a little over 2 square kilometres, about as much land as 300 football fields, and estimates of its population range from a few lakh to around 10 lakh.

Dharavi is also a place of work. Potters fire pots in Kumbharwada. Leather goods, clothes, snacks and soap are made in small rooms. Thousands of people sort and clean plastic and metal for recycling, turning the city's waste into raw material again. Many homes are also workplaces, with a sewing machine by the bed or a kiln in the lane. Goods made here are sold across India and sometimes abroad.

Why slums keep growing

When a city booms, jobs appear faster than housing. Building homes takes land, money and years of paperwork. A family arriving from a village, often through migration within India, needs a roof the same week.

Land prices make things harder. In Mumbai, squeezed onto a narrow peninsula, land is among the most expensive in India. Cheap rooms are hard to find anywhere near the trains and offices.

Many people living in slums have been there for decades. Their children go to school nearby, and they pay rent, run shops and vote. But without papers for their land, they can be moved out, and the city may refuse to lay water pipes or sewers there.

New York's tenements

Slums are found far beyond India. In the late 1800s, New York's Lower East Side was packed with tenements: narrow buildings where immigrant families from Europe crowded into dark rooms. In 1890, a journalist named Jacob Riis published How the Other Half Lives, with photographs that shocked readers. It helped push the city to pass laws requiring windows, fresh air and toilets in new buildings.

Many of those same streets are now some of the most expensive in New York.

What helps

Cities have tried many things. Some knocked slums down and moved people to the edge of town, far from their jobs, and many families drifted back. Others have rebuilt homes on the same site, or given residents legal rights to their land so they could improve their own houses.

India's Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana, started in 2015, gives help to build or buy homes. Plans to redevelop Dharavi have been discussed for decades, and residents disagree about them. Some want new flats. Others worry about losing their workshops and neighbours. Most experts agree that the best plans are made with the people who live there, not just for them.

Myth or fact?

Myth: People who live in slums don't have jobs.

Truth: Most people in slums work, often very long hours. They drive autos, build houses, cook, clean, sew and recycle. Many live in slums because they cannot afford homes near their jobs.
