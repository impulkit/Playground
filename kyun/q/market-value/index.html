How can a company lose a trillion dollars in a week?

In 2026, the chipmaker Nvidia was worth more than every company on India's stock market put together. Within weeks, more than a trillion dollars of that value vanished, yet the company still had its factories, its chips and its customers.

The short answer

A company's market value is a simple sum: the number of its shares multiplied by the price of one share. Shares are bought and sold every second on a stock market, so the price, and the value, change all the time.

A company with billions of shares can lose a trillion dollars in a week if its share price falls by a fifth or so. Nothing has to break. People only have to change their minds about its future.

Surprising but true

When a company "loses a trillion dollars", almost none of it was ever cash in a bank. It is the price people are willing to pay for the company's shares, times the number of shares, and that price can change in seconds.

How it works

Imagine a lemonade stand split into 100 shares. If people pay 10 rupees for one share, the stand is worth 100 x 10 = 1,000 rupees. If a rumour spreads that the lemons will run out, buyers may only pay 8 rupees. Now the stand is worth 800 rupees. The lemonade is the same. The belief changed.

Big companies work the same way, with far bigger numbers. Nvidia, the American company that designs many of the world's AI chips, called GPUs, has about 24 billion shares. Each 1 dollar drop in its share price wipes out about 24 billion dollars of value.

Share prices move on expectations: what buyers think a company will earn in the years ahead. Good news about the future pushes the price up. Worry pushes it down. A falling price is like a percentage sale on the whole company. A 20% drop on a company worth 5 trillion dollars is a loss of 1 trillion.

Why markets bet on a famous founder

Investors call it key-person risk: the danger that a company depends too much on one person. Like many companies, Nvidia warns in its yearly report to investors that losing key leaders could harm its business. Jensen Huang co-founded the company in 1993 and ran it for 33 years. Reports said about 60 managers reported to him directly, and no successor had been named.

When such a leader leaves suddenly, buyers cannot be sure what comes next. Will the plans change? Will key people leave too? Uncertainty makes buyers offer lower prices, so the share price falls even before anything actually goes wrong. Investors are, in a way, doing probability sums about the future, and a big unknown makes those sums look worse.

Where it came from

Big, sudden falls are not new. In October 1929, prices on the New York stock market crashed, and the Great Depression followed. On 19 October 1987, a day called Black Monday, the Dow Jones, a main US share index, fell by about 22% in a single day.

Around 2000, prices of internet companies had shot up, and then they collapsed. From 2000 to 2002, many lost most of their value. In March 2020, as COVID-19 spread, markets everywhere fell steeply.

Huge single companies have big falls too. On 27 January 2025, Nvidia lost about 590 billion dollars in one day, after a Chinese company called DeepSeek released a cheaper AI model. News reports in late 2026 still called it the biggest one-day fall for any company in history.

India's story

India has seen leaders and markets collide as well. On 18 August 2017, Vishal Sikka quit as chief executive of Infosys, the Bengaluru software company. Its shares fell about 10% that day, and more than 22,000 crore rupees of value disappeared.

Nvidia matters to India in two ways. First, as money. Many Indian families own a little of Nvidia without knowing it, through mutual funds that invest in US companies. Others buy US shares directly on phone apps. To do that, rupees must be changed into dollars, another currency. Under RBI rules, a person can send up to 2.5 lakh dollars abroad each year to invest. One report from an investing platform found Indians' investments abroad grew about four times in five years, to more than 1.6 billion dollars in 2024-25, with Nvidia among the favourite picks.

Second, as chips. Reports say Nvidia chips make up more than 85% of the shared computers built under the government's IndiaAI Mission, which offers students and startups cheap time on powerful AI machines.

Indian shares do not always follow. The biggest companies in India's economy are mostly banks, oil and phone companies, carmakers and software firms, not chipmakers. On 6 November 2026, the day after US markets had their steepest fall since April 2025 and AI chip shares tumbled, India's Sensex and Nifty held steady. Indian IT firms such as TCS and Infosys sell software services, not AI chips, so some investors saw them as safer.

The numbers that matter

  • - About 5.8 trillion to about 3.9 trillion dollars: Nvidia's value at its record in early October 2026, and on 13 November 2026. That is a fall of about a third.
  • - About 1 trillion dollars: what news reports said Nvidia lost in roughly one week up to 10 November 2026, from nearly 5 trillion to about 4 trillion dollars.
  • - 4.24 against 4.37: Nvidia's value, in trillion dollars, against the whole Indian stock market on 31 March 2026. One company was worth almost as much as every listed company in India together.
  • - 96.2 billion dollars: Nvidia's sales in the three months to July 2026, its latest results before the fall, more than double a year earlier. The business was booming even as the share price fell.
  • - About 590 billion dollars: Nvidia's one-day loss on 27 January 2025, the record for any company.

What people argue about

Some investors say the fall went too far. They point out that Nvidia's sales were still doubling.

Others say the price had climbed too high, too fast, on hopes about AI, and that a company built so closely around one leader carries real risk. They also note that AI shares across the world were falling for other reasons, so not all of the drop was about one person. Both sides agree on one thing: nobody knows the future, which is why prices swing.

Myths and mix-ups

"The trillion dollars was stolen or spent." No cash moved. The value is a price tag that buyers and sellers agree on, minute by minute.

"A falling share price means the company is failing." Not always. Nvidia's sales were growing fast while its shares fell. Prices follow feelings about the future, not just today's results.

"A company cannot survive without its founder." Many do. Steve Jobs left Apple in 1985 and returned later. After he stepped down for good in 2011, Apple went on to become the first US company worth a trillion dollars, in 2018.

What it means for you

You do not need to own Nvidia for its news to touch your family. If your parents put money in a mutual fund, part of it may be in big US companies. When their prices swing, the fund swings too.

This is why many experts say: never put all your eggs in one basket. Spreading savings across many companies, many kinds of investment and many years makes any one fall hurt less. Money needed soon is usually kept somewhere safer, such as a bank deposit. Compound interest shows why patience matters, and what makes money worth anything explains why value is often about trust. Myth or fact?

Myth: When a company loses a trillion dollars of value, that money was taken out of its bank account.

Truth: Market value is just the share price times the number of shares. When the price falls, the people who own the shares are poorer on paper, but the company's cash, factories and workers are still there.

Try this

Make a pretend company with your family. Split it into 10 paper "shares" and agree a price, say 20 rupees each. What is the whole company worth? Now announce some news, good or bad, and let everyone shout out a new price. Work out the new value each time. Notice how the company itself never changed, only what people felt about it.
