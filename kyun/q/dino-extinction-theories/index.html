What other ideas were there about dinosaur extinction?

Before the asteroid idea won, scientists had blamed the dinosaurs' end on everything from egg-eating mammals to poisonous flowers.

The short answer

Today, most scientists agree that the non-bird dinosaurs died out about 66 million years ago, after an asteroid about 10 kilometres wide slammed into what is now Mexico. But that idea only became popular after 1980. Before then, people suggested dozens of other explanations, some sensible, some very strange.

Over the years, dinosaurs were blamed on disease, cold weather, egg-eating mammals, caterpillars, poisonous plants, exploding stars and even their own bodies wearing out as a group. A few of these ideas had real evidence behind them. Many did not. One old rival, giant volcanic eruptions in India, is still taken seriously as a partner to the asteroid.

Surprising but true

One of the leading suspects in the dinosaurs' end, apart from the asteroid, is a set of giant volcanic eruptions in India whose lava still forms much of the Deccan Plateau.

Ideas that didn't last

Early in the 1900s, some scientists thought dinosaurs simply grew too big, too spiky or too odd, as if the whole group had become old and tired. This "racial old age" idea has been dropped. There is no sign that dinosaurs were weakening as a group.

Others thought small mammals ate too many dinosaur eggs. But mammals and dinosaurs had lived side by side for over 100 million years, so why would that suddenly cause a disaster? Some suggested that new flowering plants poisoned plant-eaters, or that swarms of caterpillars ate all the leaves. Flowering plants, though, had already been around for tens of millions of years.

Space and climate ideas

Some researchers looked to the sky. A nearby star exploding as a supernova might have blasted Earth with harmful rays. Scientists looked for the chemical traces such a blast would leave and did not find them.

Others suggested a slow change in climate, with seas draining off the continents and temperatures swinging. Climate did change at the end of the age of dinosaurs. But a slow change struggles to explain why so many kinds of animals, on land and in the sea, vanished so quickly.

The asteroid takes the lead

In 1980, physicist Luis Alvarez and his son Walter, a geologist, published a bold idea. Walter had studied a thin layer of clay in Italy that marks the exact end of the dinosaur age. It was full of iridium, a metal rare on Earth but common in asteroids. The team argued that a huge space rock had hit Earth.

Many experts were doubtful. Then, in 1991, scientists confirmed a buried crater about 180 kilometres across at Chicxulub, on Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula. It was the right age. Tiny glass beads, shocked quartz and tsunami deposits around the Gulf of Mexico fitted the story. Most scientists were convinced.

India's volcanoes

At around the same time, enormous eruptions were pouring out lava in western India. Over hundreds of thousands of years, they built the Deccan Traps, layers of rock that can be more than 2 kilometres thick. You can see their steps in the hills around Mumbai, Pune and Mahabaleshwar, and across much of the Deccan Plateau.

These eruptions pumped gases into the air that could have changed the climate. Some scientists think they weakened life before the asteroid struck. Others think their effect was small. The debate continues, and new dating of the lava layers keeps adding evidence.

What the debate teaches

Out of all these ideas, only the ones backed by evidence in the rocks survived. That is how science is meant to work: you suggest an idea, then hunt for clues that could prove it wrong.

The asteroid did not kill every dinosaur. Small feathered ones lived on, and their descendants are the birds outside your window. Small mammals, turtles, crocodiles and frogs survived too, and the world they inherited became ours.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The dinosaurs died out because they were too big and slow to survive.

Truth: Dinosaurs ruled for more than 160 million years and came in every size, including small, fast ones. Many small species died too, and one group of small, feathered dinosaurs survived as birds. Size alone doesn't explain it.
