What are the Asian Games?

The first Asian Games were held in New Delhi in 1951. Today they are one of the largest multi-sport events in the world, sometimes with more athletes than the Summer Olympics.

The short answer

The Asian Games are a big multi-sport festival for countries in Asia, held every four years, usually two years after the Summer Olympics. Athletes compete in athletics, swimming, hockey, badminton, wrestling and dozens of other sports, and the winners take home gold, silver and bronze medals.

The Games are run by the Olympic Council of Asia, which has 45 member nations and territories, from Saudi Arabia to Japan. The first Asian Games were held in New Delhi in 1951. The latest were held in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan, in September and October 2026, where India won 85 medals, including 21 golds, and finished fourth.

Surprising but true

Colour television broadcasting in India began in 1982, partly so that people could watch the Asian Games being held in Delhi that year.

Born in Delhi

After the Second World War, many Asian countries had just become independent. Leaders wanted a sports event that would bring Asian nations together. Guru Dutt Sondhi, an Indian sports official, pushed hard for the idea. In March 1951, the first Asian Games opened in New Delhi, with around 11 countries taking part.

Delhi hosted again in 1982. The city built new stadiums and flyovers, and the Games' cheerful mascot was a baby elephant called Appu. India also began colour television broadcasting that year, so families could watch events in colour. For many households, the 1982 Asiad was the first time they saw a colour TV.

Sports you won't find at the Olympics

Part of the fun is the sports that are big in Asia but missing from the Olympics. Kabaddi, the Indian tag-and-tackle game played on one breath, joined in 1990. India's men won every kabaddi gold until 2018, when Iran beat them. India won both men's and women's gold again in 2023.

Sepak takraw, from Southeast Asia, is like volleyball played with the feet and a woven ball. Wushu is a Chinese martial art. Cricket has appeared several times, and esports became a medal event at the Games held in Hangzhou in 2023.

India's best days

India has a long Asian Games story. Athletes like Milkha Singh and P. T. Usha won famous golds on the track. Hockey has brought India many medals, including a gold in 2023 that also earned a place at the 2024 Paris Olympics.

India's biggest haul came at the Hangzhou Games, held in 2023 after a delay caused by COVID-19. Indian athletes won 107 medals, including 28 golds. The javelin thrower Neeraj Chopra won gold there, as he had in 2018. Indian fans had chanted a slogan for those Games about crossing a hundred medals, and the team managed it.

The 2026 Games in Japan

The 2026 Asian Games were spread across Aichi Prefecture and its main city, Nagoya, in central Japan. Japan had hosted twice before, in Tokyo in 1958 and Hiroshima in 1994.

China topped the medal table once again, as it has at every Games since 1982, with Japan second and South Korea third. India finished fourth with 21 golds, 27 silvers and 37 bronzes. The next Games are planned for Doha, Qatar, in 2030.

The torch and the ceremonies

Like the Olympics, the Asian Games have a flame that is carried through the host country before the opening ceremony. Each Games also has its own mascots and a theme song. The opening ceremony is a giant show of music, dance and lights, ending with the flame being lit in the main stadium.

Athletes from every nation march in behind their flags. For small countries, sending even a handful of athletes is a matter of pride, and a single bronze medal can make national news.

More than medals

Hosting the Asian Games is a huge job. Cities build athletes' villages, new stadiums and transport. Some of these become useful parks and sports centres afterwards, while others sit empty, so hosts now try to reuse existing venues.

For many young athletes, the Asian Games are a stepping stone. Winning in front of a crowd of tens of thousands, against Asia's best, can be the first big moment of a career that leads to world championships and the Olympics.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Asian Games are a small copy of the Olympics with the same sports.

Truth: Many Olympic sports appear, but the Asian Games also include sports popular in Asia that the Olympics leave out, such as kabaddi, sepak takraw and wushu. They have also included cricket and esports.

Try this

Try a game inspired by sepak takraw, an Asian Games sport. Scrunch a sheet of newspaper into a ball and wrap it with tape. See how many times you can keep it in the air using only your feet, knees, chest and head, with no hands. Count your best score.
