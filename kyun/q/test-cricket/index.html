Why can a Test match last five days?

A Test match can last five days and still end in a draw, and fans think that is part of the magic.

The short answer

A Test match can last up to five days because each team gets two innings. To win, you must get all 20 of the other side's batters out while scoring more runs. Good batters can bat for hours, so taking 20 wickets takes a long time.

Each day has about 90 overs, split into three sessions with breaks for lunch and tea. If one team wins quickly, the match ends early. If time runs out before a result, it is a draw, which makes Tests very different from shorter formats where someone always wins.

Surprising but true

The first Test, in 1877, was won by Australia by 45 runs. A hundred years later, the Centenary Test at the same ground had exactly the same result.

Why two innings?

Cricket has been played with two innings per side since its early days in England. Two innings give a team a chance to recover from a bad start, and they make the match a long test of skill, patience and fitness.

That is where the name comes from. Matches between national sides came to be called "Test" matches because they were seen as the toughest test of each country's strength. The first was played at the Melbourne Cricket Ground in Australia in March 1877. Australia beat England by 45 runs.

A pitch that changes

The pitch in the middle is made of rolled clay soil and very short grass. On day one it is usually firm and slightly damp, and fast bowlers can make the ball bounce and swing.

As the days pass, the sun dries the pitch, the surface cracks and bowlers' footmarks rough it up. By days four and five, the ball can grip the surface and turn sharply, helping spin bowlers. A team batting last often faces the hardest conditions. Captains must plan for all five days at once.

Draws, ties and timeless Tests

Draws can be exciting. A team far behind may bat for a whole day to "save" the match. Rain often plays a part too, which is why fans check the weather forecast as nervously as the scorecard.

A tie, where both sides finish with exactly the same score after all four innings, is much rarer. It has happened only twice in nearly 150 years, in Brisbane in 1960 and in Madras (now Chennai) in 1986, when India played Australia.

Some early Tests had no time limit at all. The most famous, in Durban in 1939, lasted 12 days and still ended in a draw.

India in Test cricket

India played its first Test at Lord's in London in 1932, captained by C. K. Nayudu. India lost, but its fast bowler Mohammad Nissar impressed the English crowd. India's first Test win came in 1952, against England in Madras.

India has since produced great Test players, from Sunil Gavaskar and Kapil Dev to Sachin Tendulkar, who played 200 Tests.

Old game, new ideas

Players in Tests traditionally wear white and use a red ball. Since 2015, some Tests have been played at night under floodlights, with a pink ball that is easier to see in the dark. The World Test Championship, started in 2019, gives Test series a league table and a final.

Twelve countries have full Test status. Many worry that shorter, richer formats like the IPL leave less room for Tests. Yet the five-day game survives because it is the one format where a team can be losing badly on day two and still win on day five.

Myth or fact?

Myth: If a Test match lasts all five days, somebody must win.

Truth: Many Tests end in a draw. To win, a team must bowl the other side out twice and score more runs. If time runs out before that, nobody wins, even if one team is far ahead.
