What was the Byzantine Empire?

The Roman Empire did not end in 476. In the east it carried on for almost another thousand years, ruled from a great city on the Bosporus.

The short answer

The Byzantine Empire was the eastern half of the Roman Empire, which kept going after the western half collapsed in 476 CE. Its capital was Constantinople, today's Istanbul, founded by the emperor Constantine in 330 CE on the site of an older Greek town called Byzantium.

For more than a thousand years, the empire ruled lands around the eastern Mediterranean, including Greece, Turkey, and at times Egypt, Syria and Italy. Its people spoke Greek, followed Orthodox Christianity and thought of themselves as Romans. The empire ended on 29 May 1453, when the Ottoman Sultan Mehmed II captured Constantinople.

Surprising but true

The people we call Byzantines never used that name. They called themselves Romans, and the word "Byzantine" was popularised by European scholars long after the empire fell.

A city between two seas

Constantine chose a superb spot. Constantinople sits on the Bosporus, a narrow strait that joins the Black Sea to the Mediterranean and separates Europe from Asia. Trade from Russia, Persia, India and China passed through.

The city was protected by water on three sides and by huge land walls on the fourth. These Theodosian Walls, built in the 400s, had a moat, an outer wall and an inner wall with nearly a hundred towers. For about a thousand years, they kept out almost every attacker. Long stretches still stand in Istanbul today.

Justinian and Hagia Sophia

The emperor Justinian, who ruled from 527 to 565, tried to win back the lost western lands, and his generals retook Italy and North Africa for a time. He also had Rome's laws collected and organised into one great code. Justinian's Code later shaped the legal systems of many European countries.

Justinian's most famous building is Hagia Sophia, "Holy Wisdom", completed in 537. Its dome, over 30 metres across, seems to float above a ring of windows. For centuries it was the largest church in the Christian world. It later became a mosque, then a museum, and since 2020 it has been a mosque again.

Silk, gold and Greek fire

The Byzantines were famous for their wealth. Their gold coin, the solidus, kept nearly the same weight and purity for about 700 years, and merchants as far away as India and China knew it. Around 552, monks are said to have smuggled silkworm eggs into the empire, and Constantinople began making its own silk.

At sea, the Byzantines had a secret weapon known as Greek fire, a burning liquid sprayed from tubes on their ships that kept burning on water. Its exact recipe was so well guarded that it is still unknown.

Plague and pressure

In 541 a terrible plague struck the empire, killing a large share of Constantinople's people. Scientists studying ancient DNA have shown it was caused by the same germ as the later Black Death.

Over the following centuries, the empire lost lands to Arab armies, Slavs, Bulgars, Turks and Normans. In 1204, soldiers of the Fourth Crusade, supposedly Christian allies, attacked and looted Constantinople. The Byzantines took it back in 1261, but the empire never fully recovered. By 1453 it was little more than the city itself.

What Byzantium passed on

Byzantine scholars copied and preserved ancient Greek books, including works by Plato, Aristotle and Euclid. When Greek scholars moved to Italy, especially as the Ottomans advanced, they helped spark interest in these texts during the Renaissance.

Byzantine missionaries also carried Christianity north to the Slavic peoples. Followers of two brothers, Cyril and Methodius, developed the Cyrillic alphabet, which is used to write Russian, Ukrainian, Bulgarian and Serbian today. Every time someone in Moscow writes a text message, they use letters with Byzantine roots.

Myth or fact?

Myth: When Rome fell in 476, the Roman Empire ended.

Truth: Only the western half of the empire collapsed in 476. The eastern half, ruled from Constantinople, survived until 1453, and its people kept calling themselves Romans.
