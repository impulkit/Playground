How do you look after a pet?

A goldfish can remember things for months, a dog can understand the mood in your voice, and every pet depends on you for almost everything.

The short answer

Looking after a pet means giving it everything it would find for itself in the wild, and a bit more. Every pet needs the right food, clean water, a safe and comfortable place to live, and a vet when it is sick. Most also need exercise, things to explore and company, whether from people or other animals.

Different pets need very different things. A dog needs daily walks and training. A cat needs places to climb and scratch. Fish need clean, filtered water at the right temperature. Before getting any pet, a family should find out how big it will grow, how long it will live and how much care it will need.

Surprising but true

In 2004, archaeologists reported a grave in Cyprus where a person was buried with a cat about 9,500 years ago, one of the oldest signs of people keeping cats.

The oldest friendship

People and animals have lived together for a very long time. Dogs were the first animals to be domesticated, descended from wolves at least 15,000 years ago, though estimates vary. They helped people hunt, guard camps and herd animals.

Cats probably moved in later, when farming began and grain stores attracted mice. In 2004, archaeologists reported a grave in Cyprus where a person was buried with a cat about 9,500 years ago. In ancient Egypt, cats were so loved that some were mummified. Today, roughly two-thirds of households in the United States have a pet, according to a 2023 industry survey, and pet-keeping is growing fast in Indian cities.

Everyday care

A pet's daily routine matters most. Dogs and cats should be fed proper pet food in the right amounts, because many human foods are harmful to them. Chocolate, grapes, onions and garlic can make dogs ill. Fresh water should be available all day, especially in hot Indian summers.

Dogs need walks and play every day. Cats need scratching posts, places to hide and a clean litter tray. Small pets like rabbits and guinea pigs need space to run, things to chew and company of their own kind. Fish tanks need a filter, regular partial water changes and a careful number of fish. Too many fish in a small tank quickly fouls the water, however often it is cleaned. A good rule is to set up the tank and let the filter run for a few weeks before adding fish.

Health and safety

Pets should visit a vet for check-ups and vaccines. In India, rabies is a serious danger. The World Health Organization says India has a large share of the world's rabies deaths, mostly from dog bites, and vaccinating pet dogs and cats helps protect both animals and people.

Children should also learn to read animals' moods. Never disturb a dog that is eating or sleeping, and always ask the owner before stroking an unfamiliar pet. If an animal ever bites or scratches you, tell a grown-up straight away, so the wound can be cleaned and checked by a doctor.

Pets that should stay wild

Some animals are not meant to be pets. In India, native wild birds such as rose-ringed parakeets and munias are protected under the Wildlife Protection Act, and keeping them in cages is against the law. Turtles, star tortoises and monkeys are often sold illegally too.

Indian street dogs, often called Indies, can make loyal, healthy pets and are well suited to the climate. Many families adopt dogs and cats from animal shelters instead of buying them. Whatever the pet, caring for it well is a long promise. Some cats and dogs live for 15 years or more, and some parrots and tortoises can outlive their owners.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Goldfish have a memory of only three seconds.

Truth: Experiments have shown that goldfish can learn tasks, such as pushing a lever for food, and remember them for weeks or months. A goldfish in a bare little bowl still needs a proper tank, clean water and space to swim.

Try this

Make a pet care chart for a real or imaginary pet. List what it needs every day, every week and every year, such as fresh water, walks, cleaning and vet visits. Decide who in your family would do each job. It's a good test before anyone brings a new animal home.
