Where did the alphabet come from?

The letter A began as a picture of an ox's head. Turn a capital A upside down and you can still see the horns.

The short answer

Almost every alphabet in use today, from English to Greek to Hebrew to Arabic, comes from one invention made about 3,800 years ago. People who spoke a Semitic language, related to today's Arabic and Hebrew, borrowed a few Egyptian hieroglyphs and used them in a new way. Instead of standing for whole words, each picture now stood for a single sound.

That idea was simple and powerful. Egyptian writing used hundreds of signs and took years to master. An alphabet needs only a few dozen, so far more people could learn to read and write.

India's main family of scripts has its own, debated story, which begins with a script called Brahmi.

Surprising but true

The first alphabet had no vowels at all. Readers had to work them out from the meaning of the sentence.

Pictures that became sounds

The early alphabet worked by a clever rule: a picture stood for the first sound of its name. The sign for an ox, aleph, stood for the sound at the start of that word. A sign for a house, bet, stood for "b". A wavy line for water, mem, stood for "m".

Examples of this early writing have been found at Serabit el-Khadim, an ancient turquoise mine in the Sinai desert, and at Wadi el-Hol in Egypt. They are short, scratched lines, probably made by workers, miners or soldiers rather than by royal scribes. It's a nice thought. The alphabet may have been invented by ordinary people who found Egyptian writing far too complicated.

The Phoenicians spread it

By about 1050 BCE, the Phoenicians, a seafaring people on the coast of what is now Lebanon, were using a neat alphabet of 22 letters. All of them were consonants. Readers filled in the vowels from the sense of the sentence, the way you might read "brd" as "bird" or "bread" depending on what came before.

Phoenician ships traded all around the Mediterranean, and their alphabet travelled with them. Through a related script called Aramaic, it became the parent of the Hebrew and Arabic scripts. Sailing west, it reached Greece.

The Greeks add vowels

Around 800 BCE, the Greeks borrowed the Phoenician letters. Greek words were hard to read without vowels, so the Greeks took some Phoenician signs for sounds they didn't use and turned them into vowels. Aleph became alpha, the letter A. For the first time, an alphabet wrote down every consonant and every vowel.

The Greek alphabet passed to the Etruscans in Italy and then to the Romans, whose Latin alphabet is the one you are reading now. Letters flipped, turned and changed shape along the way. The ox head lay on its side and finally stood on its point, which is why a capital A turned upside down still shows two horns. The Romans' letters went on to spread across Europe and, much later, to the Americas.

India's own path

In India, the oldest readable script is Brahmi, best known from the rock and pillar messages of Emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE. Brahmi is the ancestor of most modern Indian scripts, including Devanagari, Bengali, Tamil and Telugu, and of many scripts across Southeast Asia.

Where Brahmi came from is debated. Many scholars think its makers were inspired by a Semitic script such as Aramaic, which was used in the Persian Empire next door. Others argue it developed within India. Either way, Brahmi organised its sounds in a very Indian way, grouping them by where they are made in the mouth. You can follow that story in India's many scripts.

India's oldest writing of all, the Indus script, is older still. Nobody has managed to read it yet.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Egyptian hieroglyphs were only pictures of things.

Truth: Many hieroglyphs stood for sounds, not objects. That sound-based side of Egyptian writing is exactly what the inventors of the alphabet borrowed.

Try this

Invent your own alphabet the way the first letter-makers did. For each sound in your name, draw a tiny picture of something that starts with that sound, like a mango for "m" or a sun for "s". Write a short message in your picture-letters and give a friend the key to decode it.
