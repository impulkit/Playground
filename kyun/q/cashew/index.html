How did cashews come to India?

Every cashew nut grows on the outside of a fruit, hanging below a juicy "apple" like a strange little hook.

The short answer

Cashews came to India from Brazil. Portuguese traders found the cashew tree growing in northeastern Brazil in the 1500s and carried it on their ships to their colonies, including Goa. The tree grew well along India's warm, sandy coasts, and within a few centuries it had spread through Goa, Kerala, Karnataka and beyond.

Today India is one of the world's biggest processors and eaters of cashews. Kollam in Kerala became a famous cashew processing centre. India also imports raw cashew nuts, mainly from Africa, shells and roasts them, and sends them around the world.

Surprising but true

Cashews are cousins of mangoes and of poison ivy, and their shells contain an oil that can burn skin.

A nut on the outside

The cashew tree grows a fleshy, pear-shaped fruit called a cashew apple. It can be red, orange or yellow. The actual nut hangs from its bottom in a hard, kidney-shaped shell. It looks almost like a mistake.

The cashew apple is juicy but soft and spoils quickly, so it's rarely sold far from where it grows. In Goa, it's used to make a strong drink for adults called feni. The nut is what's traded around the world.

Cousins of the mango

Cashews belong to the same plant family as mangoes, pistachios and poison ivy. Many plants in this family contain irritating oils.

The cashew shell is filled with a dark, sticky oil that can burn the skin and cause painful rashes. That's why cashews are never sold in their shells. Every nut is roasted or steamed to make the oil safer before the shell is cracked open. Mango skin contains a related substance, which is why a few people get an itchy rash around the mouth from eating mangoes.

Why is the mango called the king of fruits?One mango tree near Lucknow bears about 300 different kinds of mango, all grafted onto the same trunk.

The Portuguese voyage

In the 1500s, Portuguese ships sailed between Brazil, Africa and India along their sea trade routes. They carried many American plants, including chillies, pineapples, papayas and cashews. Cashew trees were probably growing in Goa by the late 1500s.

A common story says the Portuguese planted cashews on India's coast to hold sandy soil in place and stop erosion. The trees certainly grew well in poor, sandy soils where few other crops thrived. Over time, local farmers began to value the nuts.

Cracking the shell

Processing cashews takes a lot of work. The nuts are first roasted or steamed. Then the shells are cracked open one by one, often by hand or with a simple foot-operated cutter. The thin brown skin around each nut is peeled off, and the nuts are sorted by size and colour.

In Kerala, much of this work has long been done by women. The shell oil can damage their hands, so many wear gloves or rub their hands with oil. Workers' health and fair pay have been important issues in the industry. Today some factories use machines for parts of the job.

From Goa to the world

India started exporting cashews in the early 1900s, and they became an important export for Kerala. In the USA, cashews became a popular snack. Today the biggest grower of raw cashews is Côte d'Ivoire in West Africa, but India remains a major processor.

Cashews go into Indian sweets like kaju katli, rich gravies in Mughlai dishes, and Goan cooking. Some people are allergic to them, as with other tree nuts, which can be serious. If someone has a nut allergy, even a tiny amount can cause trouble.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Raw cashews sold in shops are completely raw, straight from the tree.

Truth: All cashews are heated by roasting or steaming during processing, because the shell contains an oil that can burn skin. "Raw" on a packet just means they haven't been roasted again for flavour.
