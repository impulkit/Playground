Who invented basketball?

In December 1891, a teacher nailed two peach baskets to a balcony to stop a class of bored students from fighting indoors.

The short answer

Basketball was invented in December 1891 by James Naismith, a Canadian physical education teacher at a YMCA training school in Springfield, Massachusetts, in the United States. His boss asked him to create an indoor game that would keep young men active through the long, snowy New England winter.

Naismith wrote 13 rules, hung two peach baskets from a balcony and gave his class a football to throw. Within a few years the game had spread across America. Today it is one of the most played sports on Earth.

Surprising but true

The first basketball game ended 1–0. With 18 players on the floor and no dribbling allowed, scoring was very hard.

A problem in the gym

Winter in Massachusetts is cold and dark, and Naismith's students were tired of marching drills and gymnastics. They were rough, restless and bored. Naismith had about two weeks to come up with something better.

He wanted a game that was safe indoors, so no tackling. He wanted skill to matter more than strength. So he decided the goal should be high above the players' heads, where nobody could guard it by standing in front of it. A ball would have to be thrown in an arc, which takes aim, not force.

Peach baskets and a balcony

The building's caretaker could not find boxes, so he brought two old peach baskets. Naismith nailed them to the railing of the gym's running track. The railing happened to be 10 feet above the floor, about 3 metres.

That accident became the rule. The hoop is still exactly 10 feet high in schools, colleges and the NBA, more than 130 years later.

The first game

Naismith had 18 students, so the first game was nine against nine. Players could not run with the ball, and there was no dribbling yet. They could only pass. After a long struggle, a student named William Chase landed the only basket. The game ended 1–0.

Someone had to climb up to retrieve the ball whenever a shot went in. Metal hoops with open nets replaced the baskets over the following years. Dribbling, the backboard and the five-a-side team all came later.

Spreading fast

Naismith's students went home for Christmas and took the game with them. YMCAs across the United States and Canada, and soon in other countries, began to play it.

Women started almost at once. In 1892 Senda Berenson, a teacher at Smith College in Massachusetts, adapted the rules for her students. Basketball joined the Olympics in Berlin in 1936, and Naismith, by then in his seventies, was there to see it. The professional league that became the NBA began in 1946.

Basketball in India

Basketball reached India in the early 20th century, carried partly by the YMCA, the same organisation that ran Naismith's school. Today it is played in schools and colleges across the country, on cement courts as often as wooden ones.

In 2015 Satnam Singh Bhamara, from a village in Punjab, became the first Indian-born player chosen in the NBA draft.

Why players keep getting better

Modern players train with sports scientists who measure jump height, shooting angles and sleep. Coaches have learned that a high, arcing shot gives the ball a bigger target to fall into, which is exactly what Naismith's high basket was meant to reward.

Naismith himself never made money from his game. He spent most of his career teaching at the University of Kansas, where he coached the basketball team. He is one of the very few coaches in the school's history with more losses than wins.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Basketball was invented by an American.

Truth: James Naismith was born in Canada, in Ontario. He invented the game while working in the United States and became a US citizen only in 1925.
