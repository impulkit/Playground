Why are there dragons in stories all over the world?

For centuries, Chinese medicine shops sold "dragon bones". Scientists who looked closely found they were fossils, and one trail led to an early human.

The short answer

Dragons turn up in stories from China, India, Europe, the Middle East and the Americas, among peoples who never met. Nobody knows for certain why. Scholars point to a few likely reasons that may work together.

One is fossils: ancient people dug up giant bones and skulls and tried to explain them. Another is fear. Humans have always feared snakes, big cats and birds of prey, and a dragon combines all three. A third is travel. Stories moved along trade routes, changing as they went, like a long game of telephone.

Surprising but true

A trail of fossil teeth bought from Chinese medicine shops helped lead scientists to the cave near Beijing where "Peking Man", an early human, was found in the 1920s.

Two very different dragons

A Chinese dragon, called long, is long and snaky, with whiskers, horns and four legs, and usually no wings. It lives in rivers, seas and clouds and brings rain for crops. It was the symbol of the emperor, and 2024 was a Year of the Dragon, considered a lucky year to be born. You can find many more in Chinese myths.

A European dragon is heavier, with bat-like wings and fiery breath. It hoards treasure and fights heroes like St George, or the old warrior Beowulf in an English poem more than 1,000 years old. In the Christian art of Europe, it often stood for evil.

So the same word covers creatures that are almost opposites. When early European travellers wrote about Chinese dragons, they translated long as "dragon", and the mix-up has stuck ever since.

Serpents of India and the Americas

In the Rigveda, the god Indra defeats Vritra, a giant serpent who has trapped the world's waters, and rivers flow again. Indian stories are also full of nagas, serpent beings who guard treasure and water, and of Garuda, the eagle who is their enemy.

In ancient Mexico, people worshipped Quetzalcoatl, the feathered serpent, a snake covered in the green feathers of the quetzal bird. Across the Pacific, Japan has its own water dragons. Over and over, dragons are linked with water, rain and rivers.

Bones in the ground

The historian Adrienne Mayor has argued that fossils shaped many monster stories. In China, "dragon bones" were ground up as medicine for centuries. When scientists examined them in the early 1900s, they turned out to be fossil teeth and bones. A trail of such teeth helped lead researchers to a cave near Beijing, where they found Peking Man, an early human about 750,000 years old (estimates vary).

Dinosaur bones lie exposed in deserts from Mongolia to the American West. Seeing a skull as long as a bicycle, who would not think of dragons?

A brain wired for fear

The anthropologist David E. Jones suggested another idea. Our early ancestors were hunted by three main killers: snakes, leopards and eagles. Our brains learned to react to them quickly. A dragon has a snake's body, a big cat's claws and a bird's wings, almost a mash-up of everything our ancestors feared.

Real animals may have helped too. Crocodiles are scaly, have huge jaws and lurk in rivers, just like many water dragons.

Dragons on flags and maps

Dragons are still proud national symbols. The flag of Wales has a red dragon. The kingdom of Bhutan calls itself Druk Yul, the Land of the Thunder Dragon, and a white dragon stretches across its flag.

And there is one real dragon. On a few Indonesian islands lives the Komodo dragon, a lizard up to about 3 metres long, close to the length of a small car. It does not breathe fire, but its bite carries venom, and it can eat a deer. The strangest twist is that it is a cousin, distant but real, of the little lizards that chase insects on your wall at home.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Dragons in every culture breathe fire and are evil monsters.

Truth: Fire-breathing villains belong mostly to European stories. Chinese dragons are usually wise and lucky, live in water and bring rain, and many have no wings at all.
