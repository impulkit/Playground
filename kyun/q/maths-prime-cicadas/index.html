Why do cicadas come out every 13 or 17 years?

Some American cicadas spend 17 years underground in the dark, then crawl out by the million within a few warm nights.

The short answer

In May 2024, people in Illinois found tree trunks crusted with empty brown shells and heard a roar as loud as a lawnmower. Periodical cicadas had come out. These insects live 13 or 17 years underground as young nymphs, sipping watery sap from tree roots. Then, in a single spring, almost all of them dig out within a few weeks.

Thirteen and 17 are both prime numbers. They can only be divided evenly by 1 and themselves. Many scientists think this is no accident, because a prime cycle makes it hard for predators or rival broods to fall into step with the cicadas. Nobody has proved it, though, and other ideas are still in play.

Surprising but true

In 2024, a 13-year brood and a 17-year brood came out side by side in the US for the first time since 1803, and they will not meet again until 2245.

A clock buried in the soil

A nymph has no calendar, so how does it count to 17? The best guess is that it counts years through its tree. Every spring the sap inside the roots changes as new leaves open. In a well-known experiment published in 2000, scientists tricked peach trees into leafing out twice in one year. Some of the cicada nymphs feeding on them came out a year early.

When the soil about 20 cm down warms to roughly 18°C (64°F), the grown nymphs tunnel up, mostly after dark. They climb a trunk, split their skins down the back and pull out pale, soft bodies with crumpled wings. By morning they have hardened and turned black, with bright red eyes.

The prime number idea

Here is where maths comes in. Suppose a predator has a boom year every 4 years. Cicadas on a 12-year cycle would arrive in that boom year every time. Cicadas on a 13-year cycle would hit it only once in 52 years.

Primes also keep broods apart. A 13-year brood and a 17-year brood sharing the same woods only come out together once every 221 years, because 13 × 17 = 221. That stops them from mixing and blurring each other's timing. Some researchers think the long cycles began during the ice ages, when cold summers slowed growth. Others say dodging predators is enough to explain them.

Safety in numbers

One cicada is easy food. It's slow, noisy and about as long as a paper clip. So the cicadas flood the woods. More than a million can crawl out of ground smaller than a football field. Birds, squirrels, snakes and pet dogs gorge until they can't eat another bite, and still the cicadas keep coming.

Biologists call this predator satiation: so many prey appear at once that predators simply cannot finish them. A wild bamboo in Mizoram does something similar. It flowers all together roughly every 48 years, dropping so much seed that rat numbers explode.

Brood names and a rare double

Scientists give each brood a Roman numeral. Brood X, nicknamed the Great Eastern Brood, came out in 2021 across states from Indiana to Maryland. In 2024, Brood XIII and Brood XIX emerged at the same time. The last time those two met, Thomas Jefferson was president. They will meet again in 2245.

India has plenty of cicadas too, and their shrill buzz fills forests in the hot months. Their life cycles are not lined up, so some adults appear every year. Only the eastern United States has these huge, clockwork broods.

Loud, harmless and gone in weeks

A chorus of males can reach around 90 to 100 decibels, close to a motorbike roaring past. Cicadas do not bite or sting, and the adults barely feed. After four to six weeks they are gone. Their eggs hatch inside tree twigs, and the tiny nymphs drop to the ground and burrow down to start counting again.

Brood X will next see daylight in 2038. Every nymph down there is already keeping time, a little like the hidden number spirals that sunflower seeds follow as they grow.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Cicadas are a kind of locust.

Truth: Many Americans call them locusts, but locusts are grasshoppers that can strip fields bare. Cicadas are related to aphids, and the adults barely eat at all.
