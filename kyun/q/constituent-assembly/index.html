Who wrote India's Constitution?

India's Constitution was argued over line by line for nearly three years. Then a single calligrapher wrote out the whole English text by hand.

The short answer

India's Constitution was written by the Constituent Assembly, a body of Indian leaders that met from December 1946 to January 1950. Its Drafting Committee was led by Dr B.R. Ambedkar, a lawyer and social reformer often called the chief architect of the Constitution.

The members debated every article in public for almost three years. They studied constitutions from around the world, including those of the United States, Britain, Ireland and Canada, and argued over rights, languages, religion, the powers of states and much more. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950, the day India became a republic.

Surprising but true

The original copies of India's Constitution are kept in special cases filled with helium gas in the library of Parliament, to stop the paper from decaying.

Who was in the room

The Assembly first met on 9 December 1946 in the Constitution Hall in Delhi, now the Central Hall of Samvidhan Sadan. Most members were elected by the provincial assemblies, and others represented the princely states. After Partition in 1947, the number fell from 389 to 299.

Dr Rajendra Prasad, later India's first President, was chosen to chair it. Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution, setting out its goals. Sardar Patel led work on fundamental rights and on minorities. Fifteen members were women, including Hansa Mehta, Sucheta Kripalani, Ammu Swaminathan and Dakshayani Velayudhan, the only Dalit woman in the Assembly.

Ambedkar and the draft

On 29 August 1947, the Assembly set up a Drafting Committee of seven members, with Ambedkar as chair. The constitutional adviser, B.N. Rau, had prepared an early draft, which the committee rewrote and expanded.

Ambedkar explained and defended each part of the draft in the Assembly, answering thousands of questions and proposed changes. Members put forward more than 7,000 amendments, and about 2,500 were discussed. Ambedkar argued strongly that the Constitution must protect people from discrimination, including untouchability, which it abolished.

Arguing it out

Some debates went on for days. Members argued about whether India should have one national language, how much power the states should have, and how to protect religious freedom while keeping the state neutral.

In his closing speech on 25 November 1949, Ambedkar warned that India was entering a life of contradictions. In politics, everyone would have one vote of equal value. In social and economic life, great inequality would remain. Unless that gap closed, he said, democracy itself would be in danger.

Written by hand

Instead of printing the final text, the Assembly had it handwritten. The calligrapher Prem Behari Narain Raizada wrote out the English version in flowing italic script, using hundreds of pen nibs. Artists from Santiniketan, led by Nandalal Bose, decorated the pages with paintings, from Indus seals to scenes of the Buddha and Mahatma Gandhi.

A Hindi version was hand-written separately by another calligrapher, Vasant Krishan Vaidya. Raizada asked for no fee, only that he be allowed to sign each page, and his name appears on every page of the English copy. On 24 January 1950, the members signed both copies. These originals are now kept in helium-filled cases in the Parliament library.

A living document

When it began, the Constitution had 395 articles and 8 schedules. It is one of the longest written constitutions of any country. It has been amended more than a hundred times since.

One Assembly member, Hansa Mehta, also served on the United Nations committee that wrote the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. She helped change its opening from "All men are born free and equal" to "All human beings are born free and equal". Every 26 November, India marks Constitution Day.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Dr Ambedkar wrote the whole Constitution alone.

Truth: Ambedkar led the Drafting Committee and guided the draft through the Assembly, which is why he is called the chief architect of the Constitution. But it was debated, changed and approved by nearly 300 members, with help from advisers such as B.N. Rau.
