What is Seattle known for?

Seattle is famous for being rainy, yet it gets less rain each year than New York or Miami. It just drizzles on more days.

The short answer

Seattle is a city in Washington state, in the far north-west of the US, built on hills between a long inlet of the Pacific called Puget Sound and a large freshwater lake. On clear days, snowy volcanoes and mountains appear on the horizon. It's famous for its Space Needle tower, its coffee shops, its seafood market, its music and its technology companies.

Seattle is also known for its grey, drizzly weather. In fact, it gets less rain than many US cities, about 950 mm a year, but the rain falls gently, on many days, mostly from autumn to spring. The damp, mild climate keeps everything green, which is why Washington's nickname is the Evergreen State.

Surprising but true

Seattle is named after a real person, Si'ahl, a leader of the Duwamish and Suquamish peoples who lived around the bay before the settlers came.

Named after a chief

Long before settlers arrived, the land around Seattle was home to Coast Salish peoples, including the Duwamish and Suquamish. They fished for salmon and built cedar longhouses. In the 1850s, American settlers founded a town and named it after a local leader, Si'ahl, whose name became "Seattle" in English.

The Duwamish and other native nations lost most of their land as the city grew. Today, their descendants still live in the area, and Coast Salish art and canoe journeys are an important part of the region's life.

A city of booms

In 1897, ships arrived in Seattle carrying gold from the Klondike, in northern Canada. News spread fast, and tens of thousands of people passed through Seattle on their way north to join the gold rush. Shops selling supplies made the city rich.

In 1916, William Boeing founded an aircraft company in Seattle. It became one of the world's biggest makers of planes, and helped the city grow during the 20th century. Later, technology companies, including Microsoft and Amazon, made the Seattle area a major tech centre. Starbucks opened its first coffee shop beside Pike Place Market in 1971, and Seattle became famous for its coffee culture.

Needle, market and floating bridges

The Space Needle was built for the 1962 World's Fair, when the city wanted to show off a futuristic image. It is about 184 m tall, roughly the height of a 50-storey building, with a viewing deck near the top.

At Pike Place Market, open since 1907, fishmongers famously throw whole salmon to each other across the counter when customers buy them. Seattle also has some of the longest floating bridges in the world. Because Lake Washington is too deep and its bed too soft for normal bridge supports, the road rests on huge hollow concrete pontoons that float on the water.

Volcanoes, orcas and music

On a clear day, Mount Rainier rises about 4,392 m to the south-east, covered in glaciers. It's an active volcano and is watched closely by scientists, because mudflows from an eruption could reach towns in the valleys. Orcas swim in Puget Sound, and visitors take boat trips to see them. Scientists have studied the sound "dialects" of these orca families, which differ from pod to pod.

Seattle also has a big place in music history. Jimi Hendrix, one of the most famous guitarists ever, grew up in Seattle. In the early 1990s, local bands such as Nirvana and Pearl Jam made a raw, loud style of rock called grunge, and the city's name became known to teenagers around the world.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Seattle is the rainiest city in the United States.

Truth: Many US cities, including New York, Miami, Houston and New Orleans, get more rain in a year. Seattle has many cloudy, drizzly days, which gives it its reputation, but its summers are usually dry and sunny.

Make a rain gauge from a clear plastic bottle with straight sides. With an adult, cut off the top, turn it upside down into the base like a funnel, and mark centimetres on the side with tape. Put it outside in an open space and record the rain every day for a month.
