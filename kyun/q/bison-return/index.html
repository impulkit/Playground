How did the bison come back from almost zero?

Tens of millions once roamed North America. By 1889, only about a thousand were left.

The short answer

American bison once filled the grasslands of North America in herds so big they took days to pass. In the 1800s they were shot in huge numbers, for skins, for sport, and to push out the Native American nations who depended on them. By 1889, only about a thousand were left.

A few people, ranchers and Native American tribes saved small groups. Yellowstone National Park protected the last wild herd. Today there are again hundreds of thousands of bison, though most live on ranches, not in the wild.

Surprising but true

Yellowstone's bison are the only herd in the USA that has lived wild in the same place since prehistoric times.

Life on the plains

For thousands of years, bison were the centre of life for nations of the Great Plains such as the Lakota, Cheyenne, Blackfeet and Comanche. Hunters used nearly every part of the animal. Meat was eaten fresh or dried. Hides became tipis, robes and shields. Bones became tools, and sinews became bowstrings and thread. Bison appear in their songs, ceremonies and stories, and many people still see them as relatives.

Nobody counted the herds properly. Estimates for how many bison lived around 1800 range from about 30 to 60 million.

The great slaughter

In the 1870s, new railways crossed the plains, and factories in the eastern USA and Europe found they could turn bison hides into tough leather for machine belts. Professional hunters shot thousands of animals a week, took the skins and left the bodies to rot. Passengers sometimes fired at herds from train windows for fun.

Some army officers and politicians openly welcomed the killing, because without bison, Native nations would be forced onto reservations. By 1889, the naturalist William Hornaday, working for the Smithsonian, counted only about a thousand bison left in North America. Piles of bleached bones were collected from the prairie and shipped east to be ground into fertiliser.

Saving the last few

In Yellowstone, poachers kept shooting the last wild herd. By 1902 only a couple of dozen remained there. The US Army, which then guarded the park, caught poachers, and a law in 1894 made killing wildlife in the park a crime. The park also brought in bison from private ranches to build up the herd.

Meanwhile, a handful of ranchers and Native American families had kept small groups alive. In 1905, Hornaday and others founded the American Bison Society. Two years later, the Bronx Zoo in New York sent 15 bison by train to a new wildlife refuge in Oklahoma. It was one of the first times a zoo helped return an animal to the wild.

Shaping the land

Bison graze, roll in dust and trample the soil. This helps many plants, birds and insects. Their rolling leaves shallow hollows called wallows, which fill with rainwater and become tiny ponds for frogs and insects. Their grazing keeps grasses short in some patches and lets wildflowers grow.

Some tribes are bringing bison back to their lands to restore the prairie. The InterTribal Buffalo Council, formed in the early 1990s, now links dozens of tribes that keep herds of their own. Yellowstone sends some of its bison to tribal lands each year.

Back from the brink in India

In 2016, the bison became the national mammal of the USA. It joined the bald eagle, another animal saved from near extinction.

India has its own comeback stories, such as tigers after Project Tiger and the return of the cheetah. Its own giant wild cattle, the gaur, still roam forests in the Western Ghats and central India. Like the bison, they show that big animals can recover if they are given room and protection.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Bison are slow, gentle cows that are safe to walk up to.

Truth: Bison are wild and unpredictable. They can sprint at about 55 km/h and turn quickly. Every year a few visitors in Yellowstone are hurt after getting too close, so park rules say to stay at least 23 metres away.

Measure a bison. An adult male can stand about 1.8 m tall at the shoulder and be about 3 m long. Use a measuring tape and chalk to mark those sizes on a wall or playground.
