Is a strawberry really a berry?

To a botanist, a strawberry is not a berry. A banana, a tomato and a brinjal are.

The short answer

In the kitchen, a berry is any small, soft, juicy fruit. By that rule a strawberry is a berry.

But scientists who study plants, called botanists, use the word in a stricter way. To them, a true berry is a fleshy fruit that grows from a single flower with one ovary, the part that holds the seeds. It usually has several seeds inside the flesh, and the whole wall of the fruit is soft.

By that rule a strawberry is not a berry. Its seeds are on the outside, and the juicy red part grows from a different bit of the flower. Meanwhile bananas, tomatoes, grapes, guavas, brinjals and chillies all pass the test.

Surprising but true

The "seeds" on the outside of a strawberry are each a tiny fruit of their own, with a real seed inside. An average strawberry carries about 200 of them.

Two meanings of one word

Long before botany, people called small soft fruits "berries". The English word goes back over a thousand years. When scientists began sorting fruits by how they grow, they borrowed the word and gave it a tighter meaning.

So both are right, in their own place. A cook calling a strawberry a berry isn't wrong. A botanist saying it isn't one isn't wrong either. They are just using the word in different ways, as people often do with borrowed words.

Why a strawberry isn't a berry

Look at a strawberry flower. In the middle is a little raised cushion with lots of tiny ovaries on it. After the flower is pollinated, each ovary turns into a small, hard, dry fruit, with one seed inside. Those are the "seeds" you see on the outside of a strawberry. Botanists call them achenes.

The red, juicy part we eat isn't really fruit at all. It is the swollen cushion of the flower that held them up. So a strawberry is a cluster of about 200 tiny fruits sitting on a sweet, fleshy base.

Strawberries are also in the rose family, along with apples, pears and almonds. Raspberries and mulberries are different again. Each one is a bundle of many tiny juicy fruits stuck together, which is why they look bumpy.

How do seeds fly?Plants can't walk, so many of them build their seeds parachutes, propellers and gliders, and let the wind do the rest.

The surprising true berries

A banana grows from one flower ovary, with soft flesh all through and tiny seeds in the middle. Those black specks are the seeds of the wild banana, shrunk almost to nothing in the kinds we eat. So a banana is a true berry.

Tomatoes, chillies and brinjals belong to the same plant family, and they are berries too. So are grapes, blueberries, kiwi fruit, guavas and jamun.

Some fruits are close relatives of berries with a tougher skin. Pumpkins, cucumbers and watermelons have a hard rind, and botanists give them their own name, pepo. Lemons and oranges are another special kind of berry, called a hesperidium, with a leathery peel and juicy segments.

Strawberries in India and America

The large garden strawberry we eat today is quite young. It was first bred in France in the 1700s by crossing two wild species, one from North America and one from Chile.

In India, the town of Mahabaleshwar, high in the hills of Maharashtra, is famous for strawberries. Cool winters suit them, and families visit to pick them fresh, make jam and drink strawberry milkshakes. Farmers in parts of Himachal Pradesh and the northeast grow them too. India also has its own berry stars, like the purple jamun that stains your tongue in early summer.

In the USA, California grows most of the country's strawberries. Blueberries and cranberries are native to North America. Cranberries are often harvested by flooding their fields, so the ripe red berries float to the surface, where farmers scoop them up.

Ask a friend to hand you a berry, and the botanist's answer might be a banana.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Anything small, round and juicy that we call a berry is a berry.

Truth: In everyday language, yes. But botanists use the word for a particular kind of fruit, and by their rules strawberries, raspberries and mulberries are not berries, while bananas, tomatoes and even pumpkins are close relatives of berries.

Try this

Ask an adult to cut a banana, a tomato and a grape across the middle. Look for the seeds in each and draw where they sit. Then look closely at a strawberry and count the "seeds" on one side. Is it built the same way?
