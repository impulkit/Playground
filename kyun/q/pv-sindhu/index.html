Who is PV Sindhu?

As a girl she travelled about 56 kilometres a day to reach badminton practice. She became the first Indian to be crowned world badminton champion.

The short answer

PV Sindhu is one of India's greatest badminton players. She won a silver medal at the 2016 Rio Olympics and a bronze at the 2020 Tokyo Games, held in 2021. That made her the first Indian woman to win two individual Olympic medals. In 2019 she became world champion, the first Indian to win the title.

She is tall, with a long reach and a powerful smash. On court she shouts loudly after winning points, and fans across India have learnt to roar with her.

Surprising but true

Both of Sindhu's parents were national-level volleyball players, and her father won the Arjuna Award for it, yet she chose a sport with a feathered shuttle instead.

A sporting family

Sindhu was born in Hyderabad in 1995. Her parents, P. V. Ramana and P. Vijaya, both played volleyball at national level, and her father was part of the Indian team that won a bronze at the 1986 Asian Games.

Sindhu chose badminton after watching Pullela Gopichand win the All England Championship in 2001. Gopichand later opened an academy in Hyderabad, and Sindhu joined it. As a child she travelled about 56 kilometres a day between home and training, and practice often began before dawn.

Rio 2016

At the Rio Olympics, Sindhu was ranked tenth in the world. She beat higher-ranked players one after another to reach the final, where she met Spain's Carolina Marín. Sindhu won the first game, but Marín came back to win.

Sindhu's silver was still a huge moment. She was 21, and millions of Indians had stayed up late to watch. Her rivalry with Marín became one of the best in women's badminton.

Before the Games, Gopichand had famously taken away her phone and cut out sweets so she could focus completely. After the Games she got both back, along with a lot of new followers. Back home in Hyderabad, crowds lined the roads for an open-bus parade, and several state governments gave her cash prizes and land.

World champion

Sindhu won silver at the World Championships in 2017 and 2018. In 2017 she lost a final to Japan's Nozomi Okuhara after a 110-minute match, one of the longest women's singles finals ever.

In 2019, in Basel, Switzerland, she met Okuhara again in the final. This time Sindhu won 21–7, 21–7 in under 40 minutes. She was the first Indian to become world champion in badminton. She dedicated the title to her mother, whose birthday it was.

Tokyo and after

At the Tokyo Games she lost the semi-final to Tai Tzu-ying of Taiwan, then beat China's He Bingjiao to win bronze. Her coach by then was Park Tae-sang of South Korea.

She has also won gold at the Commonwealth Games, in 2022 in Birmingham. India's badminton is now strong across the board. Saina Nehwal won India's first Olympic badminton medal, a bronze in 2012. Players like Kidambi Srikanth and Lakshya Sen have won big titles too, and many trained in Hyderabad.

The science of the shuttle

A shuttlecock has 16 feathers stuck into a cork base. When hit hard, it leaves the racket faster than a cricket ball off a bat, then slows sharply because the feathers catch the air. In 2023 India's Satwiksairaj Rankireddy set a Guinness World Record for the fastest badminton hit, at 565 km/h.

That means players must judge speed and angle in a split second. Sindhu trains her reflexes, her legs and her footwork, and works on reading where the shuttle will go before it is hit.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Badminton is a gentle garden game, not a serious sport.

Truth: Top players can smash the shuttle at more than 400 km/h, and a long rally can leave them as out of breath as a sprinter. Badminton has been an Olympic sport since 1992.

Try this

Make a mini shuttle by pushing a few paper strips into the end of a wine cork or a ball of clay. Drop it from the same height as a small ball and watch which lands first. Then throw both as hard as you can and see which slows down faster.
