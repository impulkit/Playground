Why is a marathon 42.195 km?

The marathon's odd distance was fixed in 1908, so that a race in London could finish in front of the royal family's seats.

The short answer

A marathon is 42.195 km because of the 1908 Olympics in London. The organisers planned a course from Windsor Castle to the new Olympic stadium at White City. To finish in front of the royal box, the race measured 26 miles and 385 yards, which is 42.195 km.

At first, marathons were all slightly different lengths, usually about 40 km. The 1908 distance became popular, and in 1921 the world athletics body made it the official length.

Surprising but true

The first Olympic marathon, in 1896, was won by Spyridon Louis, a Greek water carrier. He was given a horse-drawn cart as a prize from the Greek king.

The legend of Marathon

The race is named after Marathon, a plain in ancient Greece about 40 km from Athens. In 490 BCE, the Athenians defeated a Persian army there.

According to a famous story, a messenger ran all the way to Athens with the news, cried "We have won", then collapsed and died. But this version was written down by authors such as Plutarch and Lucian, who lived more than 500 years later. Herodotus, who wrote much closer to the time, tells of a runner called Pheidippides who ran about 240 km from Athens to Sparta to ask for help before the battle. Historians are not sure which story, if any, is true.

Reborn in 1896

When the modern Olympics began in Athens in 1896, a French scholar, Michel Bréal, suggested a race from Marathon to Athens to honour the legend. It was about 40 km long.

A Greek water carrier named Spyridon Louis won it, to the joy of the home crowd. No Greek had yet won a running or field event at those Games, so his victory made him a national hero.

The 1908 finish line

The marathon for the 1908 Olympics in London started at Windsor Castle, so the royal children could watch. It ended in the stadium. Adding the extra yards to finish under the royal box gave 26 miles and 385 yards.

That race had a dramatic ending. Italian runner Dorando Pietri entered the stadium first but was so exhausted he fell several times. Officials helped him across the line, and he was disqualified. Queen Alexandra gave him a special gilded cup for his courage, and he became more famous than the winner.

Hitting the wall

Running 42 km is hard because of how the body stores energy. Muscles burn a fuel called glycogen, stored in muscles and the liver. Many runners run low on it after about 30 km. Their legs feel heavy and every step is a struggle, a feeling runners call "hitting the wall".

Marathon runners train for months and eat plenty of carbohydrate-rich food, such as rice or pasta, before a race. During it, they drink and take energy gels to keep their muscles going.

Records and runners

The Boston Marathon in the United States has been run every year since 1897. Women were not officially allowed to enter until 1972. In 1967 Kathrine Switzer entered as "K. V. Switzer" and finished, even after an official tried to pull her off the course. The first women's Olympic marathon was held in Los Angeles in 1984.

In 2023 Kelvin Kiptum of Kenya set the men's world record in Chicago, 2 hours 0 minutes 35 seconds. In April 2026 his countryman Sabastian Sawe ran 1 hour 59 minutes 30 seconds in London, the first time anyone had finished a marathon race in under two hours. In India, the Mumbai Marathon, first run in 2004, draws tens of thousands of runners each January, many in fancy dress, raising money for charity. Measuring a kilometre exactly, through city streets, is a job for specialists who ride bikes with calibrated counters.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The marathon is exactly the distance Pheidippides ran from Marathon to Athens.

Truth: The ancient run may never have happened, and early Olympic marathons were all different lengths. The exact 42.195 km comes from the course used at the 1908 London Olympics.
