What was the First World War?

A shooting in one European city in 1914 led to a war that pulled in soldiers from India, America, Africa and Australia.

The short answer

The First World War was fought from 1914 to 1918. It began in Europe and spread across the world, which is why it was called the Great War at the time.

On one side were the Allies, led by Britain, France and Russia, and later joined by Italy and the USA. On the other were the Central Powers: Germany, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire (centred on today's Turkey) and Bulgaria. About 90 lakh soldiers were killed, along with millions of civilians. When it ended, empires had collapsed and maps had been redrawn. It also planted seeds of the next world war.

Surprising but true

More than a million Indian soldiers served in the First World War, and India Gate in Delhi was built to remember those who died.

How it started

In 1914, Europe's big powers had built up armies and tied themselves together in alliances, promising to defend each other. Many leaders expected that any war would be short.

On 28 June 1914, Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the throne of Austria-Hungary, was shot in Sarajevo by a young man who wanted his region free of Austrian rule. Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia and declared war. Within about a week, Russia, Germany, France and Britain were all at war, each pulled in by its promises.

Life in the trenches

On the Western Front, through Belgium and France, both sides dug long lines of trenches facing each other. Between them lay "no man's land", full of barbed wire and craters. For years the lines hardly moved.

Soldiers lived in mud, cold and rats. Attacks across no man's land often failed with huge losses. New weapons made the war deadlier: machine guns, poison gas and, from 1916, the first tanks.

Aircraft were new too. Only eleven years after the Wright brothers first flew, planes were scouting enemy lines and then fighting each other in the sky.

How did the Wright brothers learn to fly?The first powered aeroplane flight lasted 12 seconds and covered less distance than the length of a big jet's wings today.

India's war

India was ruled by Britain, so it was drawn in. More than 10 lakh Indian soldiers and labourers served overseas, all of them volunteers. Indian troops reached France in the autumn of 1914, when Britain badly needed men, and fought through a freezing winter in cotton uniforms meant for warmer places.

In October 1914, Khudadad Khan, a machine-gunner from Punjab, became the first Indian soldier to win the Victoria Cross, Britain's highest award for bravery. Indian soldiers also fought in Iraq, Egypt, Palestine and East Africa. About 74,000 died.

Many Indian leaders supported the war, hoping Britain would grant India more self-rule in return. When that did not happen, disappointment fed the freedom movement.

The USA joins

The USA stayed out at first. German submarines sinking ships, including some carrying Americans, and a secret German plan to encourage Mexico to attack the USA, changed its mind. In April 1917 the USA declared war. Fresh American troops helped tip the balance.

The end

The fighting stopped at 11 a.m. on 11 November 1918, the "eleventh hour of the eleventh day of the eleventh month". The peace treaty, signed at Versailles in France in 1919, blamed Germany and made it pay heavily. Many Germans were angry, and that anger was later used by Adolf Hitler.

Leaders also created the League of Nations to prevent future wars. It failed, but the idea returned as the United Nations.

In Britain and Canada, people wear red poppies in November, after a poem written by a Canadian doctor about poppies growing in Flanders. In Delhi, India Gate carries the names of more than 13,000 soldiers who died, and an eternal flame burns nearby at the National War Memorial.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The First World War was fought only in Europe.

Truth: Fighting also took place in the Middle East, Africa, Asia and on the oceans. Indian soldiers fought in France, Belgium, Iraq, Egypt, Palestine and East Africa.
