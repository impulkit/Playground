How does a refinery turn crude oil into petrol?

Crude oil comes out of the ground as a smelly black goo that no car could use. A refinery turns that goo into petrol, diesel, cooking gas, jet fuel and even the tar on our roads.

The short answer

A refinery is a giant factory that turns crude oil into fuels we can use. It heats the crude until most of it turns to vapour, then lets the vapour cool in a tall tower. Different parts turn back into liquid at different heights, giving petrol, diesel, jet fuel and more.

Then the refinery cleans these fuels, much as a water filter cleans water, removes harmful sulphur, and breaks big heavy pieces into smaller, more useful ones. One barrel of crude ends up as many different products.

Surprising but true

When people first refined oil in the 1800s, they wanted kerosene for lamps. Petrol was a leftover that was often thrown away, until cars came along and made it precious.

How it works

Crude oil is a mixture of thousands of molecules made of carbon and hydrogen. Some are tiny and light, some long and heavy. Each kind boils at a different temperature. A refinery uses this to sort them.

Here is what happens, step by step:

  • 1. Heating. The crude is heated in a furnace to about 400°C. Most of it turns into hot vapour.
  • 2. The tower. The vapour goes into a tall steel tower, as high as a 15-storey building. It is hottest at the bottom and cooler towards the top.
  • 3. Sorting. As the vapour rises, it cools. Heavy parts turn liquid low down, and lighter parts rise higher before they turn liquid. Trays at different heights catch each part. This is called distillation.
  • 4. Cracking. Heavy leftovers are broken into smaller molecules using heat, pressure and special helper substances called catalysts. This makes more petrol and diesel from each barrel.
  • 5. Cleaning. Sulphur and other unwanted things are removed, so engines burn the fuel more cleanly.

It is a bit like what you see when you boil water with a lid on. The steam rises, touches the cooler lid and turns back into drops. A refinery does this with many liquids at once, each one turning back at its own temperature.

From top to bottom, a tower gives cooking gas, then petrol, then kerosene and jet fuel, then diesel, then lubricating oil and wax, and finally a thick black tar called bitumen, which is used to build roads.

Where it came from

People have used oil that seeped out of the ground for thousands of years, to seal boats and light lamps. Modern refining began in the 1850s, when inventors learned to make kerosene from crude oil. Kerosene lamps lit homes around the world.

Petrol was a nuisance back then, because it caught fire too easily. Then, around 1900, cars with petrol engines arrived. Suddenly, petrol became the star product. Later came jet fuel for planes and gas for cooking.

India's first refinery opened at Digboi in Assam in 1901. It is the oldest refinery in Asia that still works.

India's story

After independence, India built refineries all over the country, at places like Mumbai, Koyali in Gujarat, Barauni in Bihar, Mathura, Panipat, Kochi and Paradip. Today India has about two dozen refineries and is one of the four biggest refining countries in the world.

The most famous is at Jamnagar in Gujarat, run by Reliance Industries. Its first refinery opened in 1999 and a second in 2008. Together they can handle about 1.4 million barrels of crude a day, making Jamnagar the biggest refining complex on Earth.

India buys most of its crude oil from abroad, as the story of where India gets its oil explains. But it refines more fuel than it needs, so it sells diesel, petrol and jet fuel to other countries, including many in Europe.

Indian refineries are good at handling heavy, sulphur-rich crude that simpler refineries cannot use. Such crude is often cheaper. That skill is why a top US official called India "a refining superpower" in 2026.

India has also made its fuel much cleaner. Since April 2020, all petrol and diesel sold in India must meet Bharat Stage VI rules, which allow only a tiny trace of sulphur. Less sulphur means less smoke and dirty air from vehicles.

The numbers that matter

  • - About 400°C: the heat at the bottom of a distillation tower, more than four times hotter than boiling water.
  • - 1.4 million barrels a day: what Jamnagar can process. One barrel is about 159 litres, so that is over 22 crore litres of crude every day.
  • - 1901: the year Digboi refinery opened. It has been working for more than 120 years.
  • - 10 parts per million: the most sulphur allowed in Bharat Stage VI fuel. That is like 10 drops in a million drops.

What people argue about

Refineries bring jobs, money from exports and fuel security. Many people see big refineries as a sign of India's strength.

Others worry about pollution near refineries and about climate change, since burning the fuels releases carbon dioxide. Some also argue about where refineries get their crude. For example, after 2022 India refined a lot of cheap Russian oil and sold some fuel to Europe, and some Western countries complained. India said it was buying oil legally to keep prices low for its people.

Myths and mix-ups

"A refinery makes only petrol." It makes dozens of products, including cooking gas, wax for candles, oil for machines, road tar, and raw materials for plastics.

"All crude oil is the same." Some is light and runny, some thick and heavy. Some has lots of sulphur. Each refinery is built to handle certain kinds.

"Jet fuel is just strong petrol." Jet fuel is closer to kerosene. Jet engines need a fuel that does not freeze high in the cold sky.

What it means for you

The cooking gas in your kitchen, the diesel in your school bus, the road under your feet and even parts of your plastic water bottle may all have come out of the same tower. A well-run refinery helps keep these things available and affordable.

Cleaner fuels from refineries also mean cleaner air in cities. And when India refines extra fuel and sells it abroad, the money it earns helps pay for the crude oil it must buy.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Petrol is pumped out of the ground ready to use.

Truth: Petrol is made. Crude oil from the ground is a mixture of thousands of substances, and a refinery must split, clean and reshape it before any engine can use it.

Try this

With an adult, hold a cold steel plate above a pan of boiling water for a few seconds. Watch drops form on the plate. The steam cools and turns back into water. A refinery tower does the same trick, but with many liquids that turn back at different temperatures.
