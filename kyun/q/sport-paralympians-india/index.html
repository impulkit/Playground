Who are India's Paralympic heroes?

At the 2024 Paris Paralympics, a 17-year-old from Jammu and Kashmir, born without arms, won a medal in archery holding the bow with her foot.

The short answer

The Paralympics are the top sporting event for athletes with disabilities. They are held soon after the Olympics, in the same city. India has sent athletes since 1968, and in recent years Indian para-athletes have become some of the country's biggest sporting stars.

At the Tokyo 2020 Games, held in 2021, India won 19 medals. At Paris 2024, it won 29, including 7 golds, its best ever. Javelin throwers, shooters, archers, high jumpers and table tennis players have stood on the podium. Several of them faced injury, illness or poverty before they ever reached a stadium.

Surprising but true

India's first Paralympic gold, in 1972, was won by a soldier who had been badly wounded in war. He swam 50 metres in a world-record time.

A soldier who swam into history

Murlikant Petkar was a soldier and boxer in the Indian Army. During the 1965 war he was shot several times and badly injured. Doctors suggested swimming to help him recover.

In 1972, at the Heidelberg Paralympics in Germany, he swam the 50-metre freestyle in a world-record 37.33 seconds. It was India's first Paralympic gold. For years, few people in India knew. He was given the Padma Shri in 2018, and in 2024 a Hindi film told his story.

The javelin kings

Devendra Jhajharia lost his left arm after an electric shock when he was a boy. He took up the javelin and won Paralympic gold in Athens in 2004. Twelve years later, in Rio in 2016, he won gold again with a world record.

Sumit Antil lost part of his left leg in a road accident as a teenager. Throwing with a prosthetic leg, he won gold at Tokyo and again at Paris, where he set a Paralympic record of 70.59 metres. That is longer than a cricket boundary on many grounds.

Firsts for India's women

In 2016, Deepa Malik won silver in the shot put at Rio. She became the first Indian woman to win a Paralympic medal. She competed from a wheelchair after an illness affected her spine.

At Tokyo, the shooter Avani Lekhara won gold in the 10-metre air rifle. She was 19, and had been in a wheelchair since a car accident at age 11. In Paris in 2024, she won gold again. She became the first Indian woman to win two Paralympic golds.

An archer who shoots with her feet

Sheetal Devi was born without arms in a village in Jammu and Kashmir. She learned archery by sitting on a chair, holding the bow with her right foot and pulling the string with her shoulder and jaw. Coaches built her a special technique from scratch.

At Paris 2024, aged 17, she and Rakesh Kumar won bronze in the mixed team compound event. Videos of her shooting were watched by millions around the world.

High jump and more

In Rio, Mariyappan Thangavelu, from a village in Tamil Nadu, won high jump gold. As a child, a bus accident had left his right leg badly damaged. He went on to win more medals at Tokyo and Paris.

Paralympic athletes compete in classes, so people with similar impairments face each other. That is why event names include codes like F64 or SH1. Each class keeps the contest fair. Other Indians have made history too. At Tokyo, Bhavina Patel won silver, India's first Paralympic medal in table tennis. At Paris, Harvinder Singh won India's first Paralympic gold in archery.

How the Paralympics began

In 1948, a doctor named Ludwig Guttmann organised an archery contest for injured soldiers at Stoke Mandeville hospital in England. It grew into the first Paralympic Games, in Rome in 1960. And the strangest part? Many of the tools that help Paralympians, from racing wheelchairs to bionic hands, are now changing everyday life too, as the story of accessibility technology shows.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Paralympic sport is gentler and less competitive than the Olympics.

Truth: Paralympians train full-time and break world records often. Athletes are grouped into classes by type of impairment so that races are fair, and the competition for medals is fierce.
