What is the Gateway of India?

The Gateway of India was built to welcome a British king, but the king never walked through it. It wasn't finished until 13 years after his visit.

The short answer

The Gateway of India is a huge stone arch on the waterfront in south Mumbai. It faces the harbour, at a spot once called Apollo Bunder. It is about 26 m tall, about as high as an eight-storey building.

It was built to remember the visit of the British king George V and Queen Mary in December 1911. Work began in 1913 and finished in 1924.

Today it is one of Mumbai's best-known sights. Crowds gather around it at all hours, pigeons fill the square, and ferries leave from the steps nearby for Elephanta Island and Mandwa.

Surprising but true

When King George V and Queen Mary landed in Bombay in December 1911, the Gateway didn't exist yet. They were welcomed under a temporary structure made largely of cardboard and plaster.

Built for a king

In 1911 King George V became the first reigning British monarch to visit India. He and Queen Mary arrived by sea at Bombay, as the city was then called. A grand welcome had to be laid on fast. So the royal couple stepped ashore under a temporary arch made largely of cardboard and plaster.

The city decided to build a permanent gateway on the spot. A Scottish architect, George Wittet, drew the design. The foundation stone was laid in March 1913.

First the ground had to be made. The sea along the shore was filled in and a new sea wall built. The arch itself took years more. It cost about 21 lakh rupees, a vast sum at the time. It was finally opened in December 1924, thirteen years after the visit it was meant to remember.

A mix of styles

The Gateway is built of yellow basalt, a volcanic rock common around Mumbai, with reinforced concrete inside. Its style is called Indo-Saracenic, a mix that British architects in India loved.

Look closely and you can find pointed arches from Islamic buildings, carved stone screens called jalis and decorations borrowed from Hindu and Jain temples. Many people say the arch looks a bit like Gujarati buildings from the 1500s. The central arch is wide and tall, with four towers on top.

Wittet also helped design other Mumbai buildings in the same style, including the Prince of Wales Museum, now called the Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya.

A gateway out

The Gateway was built to show British power. It ended up marking the end of it.

India became independent in August 1947. Over the next months, British troops sailed home. On 28 February 1948, the last soldiers, the First Battalion of the Somerset Light Infantry, marched through the Gateway in a ceremony and boarded a ship. Indian troops gave them a farewell salute.

So the arch built to welcome a British king became the door through which the last British soldiers left. Many people see it as a symbol of India's freedom struggle reaching its end.

The Gateway today

Opposite the Gateway stands the Taj Mahal Palace hotel, which opened in 1903, even before the arch. The two buildings together are one of the most photographed views in India. Security around both was tightened after the 2008 attacks on Mumbai, when the hotel was one of the places targeted.

The Gateway is lit up for festivals and national days, and its square fills with crowds on Diwali, New Year and Independence Day. Boats bob in the harbour, and hawkers sell roasted peanuts and balloons. Mumbai's sea breeze carries salt, which slowly eats at the stone, so the arch is cleaned and repaired from time to time. Its basalt has also darkened in places from traffic smoke and sea spray.

In the evening, the arch glows golden in its lights against the dark water. Many visitors from other parts of India and abroad begin their trip to Mumbai right here, as it was always meant to be, a gateway.

Myth or fact?

Myth: King George V and Queen Mary landed through the Gateway of India.

Truth: The Gateway was only an idea when they arrived in 1911. Its foundation stone was laid in 1913 and it opened in 1924, long after they had gone home.
