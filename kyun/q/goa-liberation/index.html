How did Goa become part of India?

India became free in 1947, but Goa stayed a Portuguese colony for another fourteen years, until Indian troops moved in one December night in 1961.

The short answer

Goa was ruled by Portugal for about 450 years, from 1510 to 1961. When the rest of India became independent from Britain in 1947, Portugal refused to leave. It claimed Goa was a part of Portugal itself, not a colony.

For fourteen years, Goans and other Indians protested, and India tried to negotiate. Portugal would not move. Finally, on 18 December 1961, the Indian Army, Navy and Air Force entered Goa, Daman and Diu in Operation Vijay. The Portuguese Governor-General surrendered the next day. Goa became part of India, and in 1987 it became a full state.

Surprising but true

In 1967, Goans voted in a special opinion poll on whether to merge with Maharashtra. They chose to stay separate, and Goa became a full state in 1987.

How the Portuguese came

In 1498 the Portuguese explorer Vasco da Gama reached Calicut, in Kerala, opening a sea route from Europe to India. Portugal wanted control of the trade in pepper and other spices, which sold for huge profits in Europe.

In 1510, the commander Afonso de Albuquerque captured Goa from the Sultan of Bijapur. It became the capital of Portugal's empire in Asia. Old Goa grew into a grand city of churches, including the Basilica of Bom Jesus, which holds the remains of the missionary Francis Xavier. Portuguese rule left a deep mark on Goa's languages, music, architecture and food.

Freedom movements in Goa

Many Goans wanted freedom too. Tristão de Bragança Cunha, an engineer and writer often called the father of Goan nationalism, founded the Goa Congress Committee in 1928.

On 18 June 1946, the socialist leader Ram Manohar Lohia spoke at a public meeting in Margao in defiance of a ban, and was arrested. That date is still marked as Goa Revolution Day. The Portuguese jailed many freedom fighters, and some, such as Tristão de Bragança Cunha, were sent to prison in Portugal itself.

What makes Goan food special?The name of Goa's famous vindaloo has nothing to do with potatoes. It comes from a Portuguese dish of meat in wine and garlic.

Fourteen years of waiting

After 1947, Prime Minister Nehru hoped Portugal would leave peacefully, as France was beginning to do with its Indian territories. Portugal's dictator, António de Oliveira Salazar, refused to even discuss it.

In 1954, volunteers took control of the tiny Portuguese enclaves of Dadra and Nagar Haveli. In 1955, thousands of unarmed satyagrahis, inspired by Gandhi's methods, marched into Goa from different points. Portuguese police fired on them, and some were killed while many more were injured or arrested. People across India were outraged. India cut diplomatic ties with Portugal soon after.

Operation Vijay

By 1961, India's patience had run out. Around the world, colonies in Africa and Asia were winning freedom, part of the wave of decolonisation, and Indian leaders felt Goa could wait no longer.

On the night of 17–18 December, Indian forces crossed into Goa from several directions, while the navy moved against Diu and Anjediva island. The Portuguese had only a few thousand troops. On 19 December, Governor-General Manuel António Vassalo e Silva surrendered, ignoring orders from Lisbon to fight to the last. Goans celebrate 19 December as Liberation Day.

Goa chooses its future

Some countries, including the United States and Britain, criticised India at the United Nations for using force. A resolution against India was blocked by the Soviet Union. Portugal finally recognised Indian rule over Goa in 1974, after its own revolution ended the dictatorship.

In January 1967, Goans voted in an opinion poll on whether to join Maharashtra. A majority chose to remain separate. In 1987 Goa became India's 25th state, and Konkani was made its official language.

Myth or fact?

Myth: All of India became free on 15 August 1947.

Truth: Several areas stayed under foreign rule. Goa, Daman and Diu remained Portuguese until 1961, and French territories such as Puducherry were handed over in stages between 1950 and 1962.
