What is calligraphy?

On the gateway of the Taj Mahal, the letters get bigger as they go higher, so that from the ground they all look the same size.

The short answer

Calligraphy is the art of beautiful writing. The word comes from two Greek words: kallos, meaning beauty, and graphein, meaning to write. A calligrapher shapes each letter with care, using a brush or a specially cut pen, so the writing is something to admire as well as to read.

Almost every culture with writing has a calligraphy tradition. Chinese and Japanese artists use soft brushes. Arabic and Persian writers use reed pens. Medieval European monks used feather quills, and Indian scribes wrote on palm leaves and paper. Calligraphy decorates holy books, mosques, temples, wedding cards and certificates.

Surprising but true

Steve Jobs said a calligraphy class he dropped in on at college inspired the beautiful range of fonts on the first Apple Macintosh computer.

Chinese brushes

In China, calligraphy has been one of the most respected arts for more than 2,000 years. A calligrapher grinds a solid ink stick with a little water on a stone to make fresh ink, then writes on paper or silk with a soft, pointed brush.

Pressing harder makes a thicker stroke, and lifting makes it thinner. Every character has a set order of strokes, which children learn at school. Great calligraphers, like Wang Xizhi, who lived about 1,700 years ago, were as famous as painters.

Letters on a tomb

In the Islamic world, calligraphy became one of the highest arts, especially for copying the Quran. Many religious buildings use beautifully written verses instead of pictures of people, so letters themselves became decoration. Persian writers developed a flowing style called nastaliq, which is also used to write Urdu.

The Taj Mahal in Agra is wrapped in verses inlaid in black marble on white. They were designed by the calligrapher Amanat Khan, who signed his work there. On the great gateway arches, the letters grow larger towards the top. From the ground, your eye sees them as the same size all the way up.

Writing in India

India has many scripts, from Devanagari and Bengali to Tamil and Gurmukhi. Each has its own calligraphy tradition, as you can explore in How many scripts does India use?. For centuries, scribes in south India scratched letters onto dried palm leaves with a metal stylus, then rubbed soot or lampblack over them so the letters showed dark.

Rounded scripts like Odia and Malayalam may have grown curly partly because straight lines along the grain would split a palm leaf. Today Indian calligraphers create artworks, logos and film titles in many scripts.

Monks with quills

In medieval Europe, before printing, books were copied by hand, often by monks. They used quills cut from goose feathers and wrote on parchment made from animal skin.

The Book of Kells, made in Ireland or Scotland around 800 CE, is one of the most famous. It holds the four Gospels in Latin, decorated with swirling patterns, animals and huge painted capital letters. It is now kept at Trinity College Dublin. After the printing press arrived in the 1400s, handwritten books slowly became rare.

From calligraphy to computer fonts

In the 1970s, a young American called Steve Jobs dropped out of college but kept attending a calligraphy class there. Years later, in a famous speech, he said that class shaped the many typefaces on the first Apple Macintosh in 1984, at a time when most computers had only one blocky font.

Every font you see on a phone or laptop was designed by someone who thought about letter shapes, and many type designers start by learning calligraphy. The letters on your screen are a kind of everyday design with roots thousands of years deep.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Calligraphy is just neat handwriting.

Truth: Neat handwriting is about being easy to read. Calligraphy is an art, where the shape, rhythm and spacing of the letters are meant to be beautiful in themselves, often following centuries-old rules.

Try this

Make a simple calligraphy pen by asking an adult to cut the tip of a thick ice-lolly stick flat and wide. Dip it in poster paint or ink and write your name slowly, holding the stick at the same angle all the time. Notice how lines are thick in one direction and thin in another.
