Where in the world do Indians live?

About 3.5 crore people of Indian origin live outside India, from Dubai and New Jersey to Fiji and the Caribbean.

The short answer

People of Indian origin live in almost every country on Earth. Together they are called the Indian diaspora, a word for a people spread far from their homeland. India's Ministry of External Affairs counted about 3.5 crore in 2024. That includes Indian citizens working abroad and people whose families left India generations ago.

The biggest communities are in the Gulf countries, especially the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia, and in the USA, the UK and Canada. There are also old, deep-rooted Indian communities in Mauritius, Fiji, South Africa, Malaysia, Trinidad and Tobago, and Guyana.

According to the United Nations, more people born in India live outside their home country than from any other country: about 1.8 crore in 2020.

Surprising but true

In Mauritius, an island nation in the Indian Ocean, roughly two out of every three people have ancestors who came from India.

The long voyages

In 1834, slavery had just been abolished in the British Empire, and sugar planters needed new workers. The British began shipping labourers from India under contracts called indentures. Workers agreed to labour for about five years, often in harsh conditions, in return for passage and small wages.

Over the next eighty years or so, more than a million Indians made these voyages, to Mauritius, Fiji, Trinidad, Guyana, Suriname, South Africa and elsewhere. They called themselves girmitiyas, from their mispronunciation of "agreement". The system ended around 1917. Many never returned. In Mauritius, the stone steps where they landed, Aapravasi Ghat, is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Their descendants kept festivals, foods and songs alive. Diwali is a public holiday in Mauritius, Fiji, Trinidad and Tobago, and Guyana.

Gandhi, the first famous returnee

In 1893, a young lawyer named Mohandas Gandhi sailed to South Africa for a job. He saw how Indians there were treated and stayed for over twenty years, leading peaceful protests for their rights. He returned to India on 9 January 1915. That date is now marked as Pravasi Bharatiya Divas, a day to honour overseas Indians.

His time abroad shaped the rest of his life. The ideas he tested in South Africa, such as refusing to obey unfair laws without using violence, became the tools of India's freedom struggle. In a sense, one of India's most famous leaders was formed by the diaspora.

Gulf jobs and Silicon Valley

From the 1970s, oil money brought huge building projects to the Gulf, and millions of Indian workers followed, especially from Kerala. Cities like Dubai were partly built by them. Most go on work visas and return home after some years, and they send much of their pay back to their families. In some Kerala villages, so many people have worked in the Gulf that new houses, schools and shops were paid for largely with Gulf wages.

In the USA, many Indian engineers, doctors and students arrived after American immigration laws changed in 1965. Indian Americans now number about 5 million. Several have led giant tech firms in Silicon Valley, and the astronaut Kalpana Chawla became the first woman born in India to go to space.

Money and memory

People abroad send home more money than any other diaspora. According to the World Bank, Indians abroad have sent more than US$100 billion a year since 2022, paying for homes, school fees and village businesses.

Ties run the other way too. Families in Toronto watch Indian cricket at odd hours. Children in New Jersey learn Gujarati on weekends. A cook in Fiji makes curry with a recipe her great-great-grandmother carried across the ocean, from a village in India she never got to see again.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Most Indians living abroad are in the USA or the UK.

Truth: Large numbers live in both, but the Gulf countries together hold more. The UAE alone has the largest Indian community of any foreign country, and Saudi Arabia is also near the top.
