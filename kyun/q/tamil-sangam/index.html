What is Sangam poetry?

About 2,000 years ago, Tamil poets wrote a line still quoted today: "Every town is our town, everyone our kin."

The short answer

Sangam poetry is the oldest known body of Tamil literature. It is a collection of about 2,400 poems, written by more than 470 named poets, and many unnamed ones, roughly 2,000 years ago. The poets included kings, farmers, potters, merchants and women.

The poems are short and sharp, full of real places, plants and animals. Some describe falling in love, missing someone or quarrelling. Others praise brave kings, mourn heroes or scold rulers who were mean with money. The name comes from sangam, meaning "gathering", after a legend that poets met in academies at Madurai under the Pandya kings.

Surprising but true

Many of these poems survived only on crumbling palm leaves until a scholar spent decades in the late 1800s tracking them down and printing them.

Love and war

Sangam poems fall into two big groups. Akam, meaning "inside", poems are about love and private feelings. They rarely name the lovers. Instead, they speak in voices like "the girl", "her friend" or "the mother", so any reader can step into the scene.

Puram, meaning "outside", poems are about public life: battles, kings, generosity, death and fame. One famous puram poem, by Kaniyan Pungundranar, opens with the line usually translated as "Every town is our town, everyone our kin". It goes on to say that good and bad do not come from others, and is still quoted in speeches today.

A code of landscapes

Akam poets used a clever code called tinai. Five landscapes each matched a mood of love. Mountains meant lovers meeting. Forests and pastures meant patient waiting. Farmland meant quarrels. The seashore meant anxious longing. Dry wasteland meant painful separation.

Each landscape came with its own flowers, birds, animals and gods. The mountain landscape is named after the kurinji flower, which grows in the Western Ghats and blooms in great blue waves only every 12 years. So when a poem mentioned kurinji or a peacock, readers knew where they were and how the speaker felt. Poets could pack a lot of feeling into very few words.

Why are the Western Ghats so important?A wall of old hills along India's west coast catches the monsoon, feeds rivers used by crores of people, and hides animals found nowhere else.

Red earth and pouring rain

One of the best known Sangam love poems, from an anthology called Kuruntokai, compares two lovers to rain falling on red earth. The poet says their parents were strangers, and they themselves had never met, yet their hearts mixed together like the red soil and the water. The translator A. K. Ramanujan made it famous in English under the title "Red Earth and Pouring Rain".

The poem is only five lines long. Like a haiku, it uses one clear picture to say something big. Many Sangam poems work this way, and that is one reason modern readers find them so fresh.

Lost and found

For centuries, Sangam poems survived on palm-leaf manuscripts, copied by hand in monasteries and family homes. Palm leaves rot, crack and get eaten by insects. By the 1800s, some poems were almost forgotten.

The scholar U. V. Swaminatha Iyer spent decades travelling from village to village in Tamil Nadu, searching for these manuscripts. He compared copies line by line, fixed copying mistakes and printed many of the anthologies for the first time. Tamil readers still remember him with great respect for it.

Why the poems matter

Sangam poetry tells historians about ancient Tamil life: trade with Rome, the Chola, Chera and Pandya kingdoms, farming, food and festivals. It also shaped later Tamil literature, including the Thirukkural.

In 2004, Tamil became the first language to be declared a classical language by the Government of India, and the Sangam poems were a big part of the reason. They are still taught in schools, set to music and quoted at weddings. A poem written two thousand years ago can still say exactly what a person feels today.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Sangam poems are all about gods and religion.

Truth: Most Sangam poems are about ordinary human life. They describe love, loneliness, friendship, farming, war, generosity and grief, with very few prayers. Religious Tamil poetry became much more common in later centuries.

Try this

Write a four-line poem about a feeling without naming the feeling. Instead, describe one place, one plant or animal, and one time of day. Read it to someone and ask them to guess the mood. Sangam poets used this trick all the time.
