Who was Pablo Picasso?

Picasso could paint like the old masters as a teenager, then spent his life breaking every rule they had taught him.

The short answer

Pablo Picasso (1881–1973) was a Spanish artist who spent most of his life in France. He is one of the most influential artists of the 1900s. Together with the French painter Georges Braque, he invented Cubism, a way of painting that breaks objects into flat, angular pieces and shows them from several sides at once.

Picasso never stuck with one style for long. Over about eighty years of work he painted, sculpted, made prints and pottery, and designed theatre costumes. His most famous painting, Guernica, is a giant black, white and grey protest against the bombing of a town during the Spanish Civil War.

Surprising but true

Picasso made tens of thousands of artworks in his long life, including paintings, sculptures, prints and thousands of pieces of pottery.

A boy who could already draw

Picasso was born in Málaga, in the south of Spain. His father was an art teacher who gave him lessons from an early age. By his teens, Pablo could paint careful, realistic portraits and passed entrance exams for art schools in Barcelona and Madrid.

He soon grew bored with copying what the academies wanted. In 1904 he moved to Paris, then the centre of the art world, where young artists from many countries were trying new ideas.

Blue, then rose

His first famous phase is called the Blue Period, from about 1901 to 1904. After a close friend died, Picasso painted poor and lonely people almost entirely in shades of blue. The pictures feel cold and sad.

Then came the Rose Period, with warmer pinks and oranges and pictures of circus performers, acrobats and clowns. Both periods still look fairly realistic. What came next did not.

Breaking the picture apart

In 1907 Picasso painted Les Demoiselles d'Avignon, five women made of sharp, flat shapes and angles, some with faces like masks. Even his friends were shocked. He had been looking at ancient Iberian sculpture and at African masks he saw in Paris museums.

Over the next few years he and Braque developed Cubism. Their paintings of guitars, bottles and people look as if they have been smashed and rebuilt. They also stuck real things onto their pictures, such as newspaper and wallpaper, inventing collage as a serious art form. These experiments opened the door to much of modern art.

Guernica

In April 1937, during the Spanish Civil War, German and Italian warplanes supporting General Franco bombed the Basque town of Guernica. Many civilians were killed. Picasso, living in Paris, was asked to paint a large mural for the Spanish pavilion at the World's Fair that year.

He made Guernica: nearly 8 metres wide, painted only in black, white and grey, like a newspaper photograph. It shows a screaming horse, a bull, a mother holding her child and a broken soldier. There is no blood, yet the picture feels full of pain. It toured the world and became a symbol against war, especially as the Second World War began two years later. Today it hangs in a museum in Madrid.

The man and his reputation

Picasso was hugely famous in his lifetime and kept working until he died at 91. He tried ceramics in his sixties, making thousands of plates, jugs and vases at a pottery workshop in the south of France.

His personal life has been criticised. Several of the women close to him, some of whom were also artists, later described him as controlling and unkind. Today many museums mention this alongside his art. His influence is still everywhere, from Indian painters like M.F. Husain to cartoonists and designers who borrow his bold, broken shapes.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Picasso painted strange pictures because he couldn't draw realistically.

Truth: As a teenager he could already draw and paint in a careful, lifelike way, and some of his early academic works still survive in the Museu Picasso in Barcelona. He chose to break the rules once he had mastered them.

Try this

Choose a mug or a cup. Draw it from the front, from the top and from the side, on three small pieces of paper. Cut them out and glue the pieces together into one picture, overlapping them. You have just made a Cubist drawing.
