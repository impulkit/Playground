Who were the ten Sikh Gurus?

Every day, the kitchen at the Golden Temple in Amritsar serves free meals to tens of thousands of people, all sitting together on the floor. The idea goes back to Guru Nanak.

The short answer

Sikhism began in Punjab about 500 years ago with Guru Nanak. He taught that there is one God, that all people are equal and that ordinary family life can be holy. Over the next two centuries, nine more Gurus, or spiritual teachers, led the Sikh community in turn.

Each Guru added something. Some built towns and temples, one compiled the holy book, and two were killed by Mughal authorities for refusing to give up their beliefs. The tenth, Guru Gobind Singh, founded the Khalsa, the community of initiated Sikhs, and declared that after him the holy book itself, the Guru Granth Sahib, would be the eternal Guru.

Surprising but true

Guru Har Krishan became the eighth Sikh Guru when he was about five years old.

Guru Nanak and the open kitchen

Guru Nanak was born in 1469 at Talwandi, now Nankana Sahib in Pakistan. He travelled widely, perhaps as far as Mecca and Sri Lanka, singing hymns with his Muslim companion Mardana, who played the rabab, a stringed instrument.

He taught that God cannot be divided by religion or caste. In his last years he settled at Kartarpur, where his followers farmed, prayed and ate together. The free community kitchen, called langar, became a lasting Sikh tradition. Rich and poor, of any faith or caste, sit side by side and eat the same food.

Building a community

The second Guru, Guru Angad, helped shape the Gurmukhi script, used to write the Gurus' hymns and Punjabi today. The third, Guru Amar Das, expanded langar and asked that everyone who came to see him eat there first. Sikh tradition says even the emperor Akbar sat on the floor to eat.

The fourth Guru, Guru Ram Das, founded the town that became Amritsar. The fifth, Guru Arjan, built the Harmandir Sahib there, with four doors to welcome people from every direction. In 1604 he compiled the Adi Granth, the holy book, which includes hymns by the Gurus and by saints such as Kabir, Namdev and Sheikh Farid. In 1606 he was executed under the emperor Jahangir, and Sikhs remember him as their first martyr Guru.

What is the Gurmukhi script?The same Punjabi words can be written in two completely different scripts, depending on which side of the India-Pakistan border you live on.

Two swords and a child Guru

The sixth Guru, Guru Hargobind, began wearing two swords: one for spiritual authority and one for worldly authority. He built the Akal Takht, a seat of Sikh authority, facing the Harmandir Sahib.

The seventh, Guru Har Rai, is remembered for his gentleness and for keeping a garden of healing herbs. The eighth, Guru Har Krishan, became Guru as a small child. During a smallpox outbreak in Delhi in 1664, he is said to have cared for the sick before falling ill and dying himself, aged just seven. Gurdwara Bangla Sahib in Delhi stands on the site.

Standing up for others

The ninth Guru, Guru Tegh Bahadur, was executed in Delhi in 1675 on the orders of the emperor Aurangzeb. Sikh tradition says he gave his life defending the right of Kashmiri Hindus to practise their faith. Gurdwara Sis Ganj in Chandni Chowk marks the spot.

His son, Guru Gobind Singh, was a poet and warrior. On Vaisakhi in 1699, at Anandpur Sahib, he founded the Khalsa. Its members were asked to keep five articles of faith, including uncut hair and a steel bracelet. Men took the name Singh and women the name Kaur, so that no surname could show caste. He lost all four of his sons in the struggles with Mughal forces.

The living scripture

Before his death in 1708, Guru Gobind Singh ended the line of human Gurus. The Guru Granth Sahib became the Guru. Sikhs read from it, sing its hymns and treat it with great respect, carrying it to rest each night. Today there are around 25–30 million Sikhs, in Punjab and in communities around the world, from Canada and the UK to California.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Sikhism is a mix of Hinduism and Islam.

Truth: Sikhism is a separate religion with its own scripture, Gurus, beliefs and practices. Its holy book does include poems by Hindu and Muslim saints whose ideas the Gurus admired, but Sikhs see their faith as its own revelation.

Try this

Hold a langar-style meal at home or in class. Everyone sits on the floor in a row, whoever they are, and the food is the same for all. Take turns serving each other, and wash up together afterwards.
