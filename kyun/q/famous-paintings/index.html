Which are the world's most famous paintings?

The Mona Lisa was a fairly quiet museum treasure until 1911, when a handyman walked out of the Louvre with it under his clothes.

The short answer

A handful of paintings are known almost everywhere. Leonardo da Vinci's Mona Lisa, Vincent van Gogh's The Starry Night, Johannes Vermeer's Girl with a Pearl Earring and Pablo Picasso's Guernica are among them. So is The Great Wave off Kanagawa, a Japanese print by Katsushika Hokusai.

None of them became famous only because it was beautiful. A painting usually needs a good story too: a theft, a tragic artist, a war, or an image so simple it can be printed on anything, from posters to coffee mugs. In the last 150 years, photography and printing have spread these images far beyond the museums that own them.

Surprising but true

The Mona Lisa is only about 77 cm tall, roughly the height of a school desk, and today it hangs behind bulletproof glass.

The smile that was stolen

The Mona Lisa was painted in the early 1500s and probably shows Lisa Gherardini, the wife of a merchant in Florence. For centuries it was admired by artists, but it was not world-famous.

Then, in August 1911, an Italian handyman named Vincenzo Peruggia, who had worked at the Louvre, hid in the museum and walked out with it. The theft made headlines around the world. Crowds queued to stare at the empty space on the wall. When the painting was found in Italy two years later, it came home a celebrity. Today millions of people visit the Louvre each year, and many of them head straight for it.

A night sky that swirls

Vincent van Gogh painted The Starry Night in 1889, while staying at a hospital in Saint-Rémy, in the south of France. It shows the view from his window, with an added village and a dark cypress tree, under a sky of glowing stars and rolling swirls.

Van Gogh sold very few paintings during his life and died poor the following year. His sad story, and his letters to his brother Theo, made people look again at his work after his death. The Starry Night now hangs in the Museum of Modern Art in New York.

How was photography invented?The oldest surviving photograph from a camera needed hours, perhaps days, of sunlight to appear. It shows a blurry view of rooftops from a window in France.

A painting against war

In April 1937, during the Spanish Civil War, the town of Guernica in northern Spain was bombed from the air. Many civilians died. Picasso, living in Paris, painted Guernica in response.

It is huge, almost 8 m wide, about as long as three autorickshaws parked end to end, and painted only in black, white and grey. It shows a screaming horse, a bull, a mother holding her child, and broken figures. There is no blood and no planes, yet people find it hard to look away. It has become one of the world's best-known pictures about the suffering of war.

A girl, a wave, and many more

Vermeer's Girl with a Pearl Earring, painted around 1665, shows a girl turning to look over her shoulder. Nobody knows who she was, and that mystery is part of its pull. It hangs in the Mauritshuis museum in The Hague, in the Netherlands.

Hokusai's Great Wave, from about 1831, shows a giant wave curling over boats, with Mount Fuji small in the distance. It was printed from wooden blocks, a bit like the printing press printed books, so thousands of copies were made.

India has its own famous paintings, too. Raja Ravi Varma's goddesses were printed in huge numbers and hung in homes across the country. Amrita Sher-Gil's Three Girls (1935) is one of the best-known works of modern Indian art. Which painting counts as "most famous" depends a lot on where you grew up.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Mona Lisa's eyes follow you around the room because Leonardo used a secret trick.

Truth: Almost any portrait where the subject looks straight out at the painter seems to follow you as you move. It is an effect of how our brains read a flat picture, not a hidden technique.

Try this

Pick a painting you like from a library book or a museum website. Spend two full minutes looking at it without talking, then list ten things you noticed. Ask a friend to do the same with the same picture and compare your lists. Most people are surprised how different they are.
