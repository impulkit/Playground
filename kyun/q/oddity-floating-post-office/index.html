Where is the floating post office?

On Dal Lake in Srinagar, you can climb off a wooden boat, post a letter from a houseboat, and get a stamp with a picture of a boat on it.

The short answer

The best-known floating post office sits on Dal Lake in Srinagar, Kashmir. It is a wooden houseboat moored near Nehru Park, painted and carved like the other houseboats on the lake. Inside, staff sell stamps, accept parcels, send speed post and help people with savings forms, like any Indian post office.

Customers often arrive by shikara, the slim, canopied boats of Dal Lake. Letters posted there can get a special postmark showing a shikara and boatman, which makes it a favourite with tourists and stamp collectors. A second floating post office, on a boat in Detroit, USA, delivers letters to ships as they sail past. Both are working post offices. They are not museums or tourist props, and they handle real letters every day.

Surprising but true

The USA has a post office on a boat in Detroit that delivers mail to giant ships while they are still moving, using a rope and a bucket.

A post office on the water

Dal Lake is a neighbourhood as well as a view. Thousands of people live on and around it, on houseboats and on small islands with floating vegetable gardens. For them, getting to the city post office meant a boat trip.

News reports say a postal boat began serving lake residents in 1953. The houseboat post office grew from that service. In 2011, the head of the postal circle gave it its current name, the Floating Post Office, and it became a tourist attraction too.

Floods and a museum

In 2014, huge floods hit Kashmir. The houseboat was damaged and drifted from its place, and much of a small stamp museum inside was lost. It was repaired and opened again. Today it has a little display of old stamps and the history of mail in Kashmir.

The post office has only a few staff. Mail collected during the day goes to Srinagar's main post office, then on across India.

The houseboat looks much like its neighbours on Dal Lake, with carved wooden panels and a long, flat hull. The post office counter is at one end, and the little display of stamps is at the other. People who live on the lake still come by shikara to send money orders, pay bills and pick up parcels, just as they would at any post office on land. Visitors take off their shoes before stepping in, as they would in a home.

Mail by bucket in Detroit

Halfway round the world, the Detroit River links two of the Great Lakes and is one of the busiest shipping lanes in North America. Huge cargo ships, some longer than two football fields, pass day and night.

Since 1895, a company has carried mail out to these ships by boat. Today its small mail boat, the J. W. Westcott II, is a real US post office with its own ZIP code, 48222. It pulls up beside a moving freighter, crew on deck lower a bucket on a rope, and the mail goes up. Sailors who spend months on the lakes can get letters, parcels and supplies this way without their ships stopping.

Stamps and postmarks

A postmark is the ink mark printed over a stamp to show it has been used. Most just give the town and date. Special postmarks with pictures, called pictorial cancellations, are made for unusual places and events. India Post uses them at several famous sites, and the Dal Lake shikara design is among the most popular.

Collectors love these little ink pictures, and many travel to Srinagar partly to get one. For an Indian collector, the biggest prize is a copy of the very first stamp issued in the subcontinent, in 1852, which is far rarer than any floating postmark.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Dal Lake post office is the only floating post office in the world.

Truth: It is a famous and unusual one, and probably the best known. But Detroit, in the USA, has run a floating post office on a boat since the 1890s, with its own ZIP code.
