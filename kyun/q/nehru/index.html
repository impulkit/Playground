Who was Jawaharlal Nehru?

He spent about nine years in British prisons, wrote history books in his cell, then led a newly free India for 17 years.

The short answer

Jawaharlal Nehru was one of the main leaders of India's freedom movement and the country's first Prime Minister. He led India from independence in 1947 until he died in 1964.

Nehru grew up rich, studied in England, and could have lived an easy life. Instead he followed Gandhi into the struggle against British rule and went to jail again and again. As Prime Minister, he tried to build a modern, democratic India: one with factories, dams, science labs and a vote for every adult. Children know him as "Chacha Nehru", and his birthday, 14 November, is Children's Day.

Surprising but true

Nehru wrote one of his best-known books, The Discovery of India, in about five months while locked in Ahmednagar Fort.

From Allahabad to Cambridge

Nehru was born in 1889 in Allahabad, the son of Motilal Nehru, a successful lawyer. The family home, Anand Bhavan, was large enough to have its own swimming pool. At 15 he was sent to Harrow, a famous school in England, and then to Trinity College, Cambridge, where he studied science. He trained as a lawyer in London and came home in 1912.

He first met Gandhi in 1916, and over the next few years that friendship changed everything. Nehru joined the freedom struggle, and the whole family followed. His father gave up a rich lawyer's lifestyle, and the family burned their foreign cloth. Anand Bhavan was later given to the Congress party as an office, and a new family home was built next door.

Freedom at midnight

In December 1929, as Congress president, Nehru led the Lahore session that demanded Purna Swaraj, complete independence. On the banks of the Ravi river he raised the flag of freedom.

He spent about nine years in prison over his life. He used the time to read and write. From jail he wrote letters to his daughter Indira about world history, and later The Discovery of India.

On the night of 14 August 1947, he spoke to the new nation: "At the stroke of the midnight hour, when the world sleeps, India will awake to life and freedom."

Building a new country

India in 1947 was poor, and Partition had just torn millions of lives apart. Nehru believed science and planning could help. His government started Five-Year Plans, the first Indian Institutes of Technology and an atomic energy programme led by Homi Bhabha.

He called big dams such as Bhakra Nangal "temples of modern India", because they stored water for farms and made electricity.

How do dams make electricity?Behind a big dam, a whole river waits. Let it fall through a pipe, and it can light up millions of homes.

Nehru also made sure India stayed a democracy. In 1951–52 India held its first general election, and every adult could vote, rich or poor, man or woman. Around 17 crore people were eligible, and most of them had never voted before.

Choosing no side

After the Second World War, the USA and the Soviet Union competed for friends across the globe. Nehru refused to join either camp. With leaders from Egypt, Yugoslavia, Indonesia and Ghana, he helped start the Non-Aligned Movement in 1961, for countries that wanted to stay out of the Cold War.

Praise and criticism

Nehru is admired for building democratic institutions that still work today. He is also criticised. In 1962, China defeated India in a border war, which shocked the country and hurt Nehru deeply. Some economists argue his government controlled business too tightly and slowed growth. How he handled Kashmir is still argued over.

He died on 27 May 1964. His house in Delhi, Teen Murti Bhavan, is now a museum and library, and its shelves still hold the books he read.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Nehru was India's first head of state.

Truth: Nehru was India's first Prime Minister, the head of government. The head of state was first the Governor-General and, from 1950, the President, starting with Dr Rajendra Prasad.
