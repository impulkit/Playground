Who were Pelé and Maradona?

One won three World Cups. The other scored the most famous cheating goal and the most beautiful goal in the same match, four minutes apart.

The short answer

Pelé and Diego Maradona were two of the greatest footballers ever. Pelé, from Brazil, won three World Cups, in 1958, 1962 and 1970, the only player to do so. Maradona, from Argentina, led his country to the 1986 World Cup almost by himself, with goals that are still replayed today.

They were very different. Pelé was graceful and became a smiling worldwide ambassador for the game. Maradona was a small, fiery genius whose life off the pitch was often troubled. Fans still argue about which one was better.

Surprising but true

Both Pelé and Maradona played in Kolkata. Pelé came with the New York Cosmos in 1977, and Maradona visited in 2008 and 2017.

Pelé: the boy with a sock ball

Edson Arantes do Nascimento was born in 1940 in Três Corações, Brazil, into a poor family. As a boy he played with a sock stuffed with newspaper or rags because his family could not afford a ball. Nobody is sure where his nickname "Pelé" came from.

He joined the club Santos at 15. At 17 he played in the 1958 World Cup in Sweden and scored twice in the final. He stayed at Santos for nearly 20 years. Late in his career he joined the New York Cosmos, helping to make football popular in the USA from a base in New York.

Three World Cups

Pelé was injured early in the 1962 World Cup, but Brazil won it without him. In 1970, in Mexico, he played in one of the best teams ever. Brazil won the final 4–1 against Italy, and Pelé scored with a header.

The 1970 World Cup was one of the first shown widely on colour television, so millions saw Pelé's skills in bright yellow shirts. He scored more than 1,000 goals in his career, though many came in friendly matches.

Maradona: the little genius

Diego Armando Maradona was born in 1960 in Lanús, near Buenos Aires, and grew up in a poor neighbourhood called Villa Fiorito. He was about 1.65 metres tall, but his balance and close control were astonishing. He seemed able to keep the ball no matter how many defenders surrounded him.

He played for Barcelona and then Napoli in Italy, where he led a struggling southern club to its first league titles in 1987 and 1990. In Naples he is still treated almost like a saint. Murals of his face cover walls in the city, and Napoli's stadium was renamed after him when he died.

Mexico 1986

In the 1986 World Cup quarter-final, Argentina played England in Mexico City. Early in the second half, Maradona punched the ball into the net with his hand. The referee allowed it. Maradona later called it "the hand of God".

Four minutes later, he picked up the ball in his own half, dribbled past several England players and the goalkeeper, and scored. In 2002, a FIFA online poll voted it the Goal of the Century. Argentina went on to win the final against West Germany.

Fame and trouble

Maradona struggled with drug addiction. He was banned in 1991 and sent home from the 1994 World Cup after failing drug tests. He later coached Argentina, and died in 2020 at 60.

Pelé was named an athlete of the century by the International Olympic Committee and became Brazil's sports minister. In 2000 FIFA could not choose between them. An internet poll picked Maradona, a panel of experts picked Pelé, and they shared the award. Pelé died in 2022, aged 82. Both visited Kolkata, where fans packed stadiums just to see them.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Maradona's "Hand of God" goal was allowed because nobody saw it.

Truth: Many players and fans saw it, and the English team protested at once. The referee and his assistants did not see the handball clearly, and in 1986 there were no video replays to check.
