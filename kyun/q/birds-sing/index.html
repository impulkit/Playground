Why do birds sing so early in the morning?

5 a.m. Everyone's asleep. Except the birds.

The short answer

Birds sing mostly to say two things: "this patch is mine" and "choose me". In the breeding season, a male's song warns other males away from his territory and attracts a mate. The dawn chorus is the burst of song that starts just before sunrise.

Why dawn? Scientists have several ideas. The air is often still and cool at daybreak, so sound carries farther. It is too dark to hunt insects well, so singing wastes little feeding time. And a loud song at first light shows neighbours that the singer has survived the night and is still in charge. Each idea has some evidence, and the answer may differ from species to species.

Surprising but true

The koel you hear singing in Indian summers was probably raised by crows. Koels lay their eggs in crows' nests and let the crows feed their chicks.

A voice box in the chest

You make sounds with vocal cords in your throat. Birds have something quite different: a syrinx (say "SIR-inks"), deep in the chest where the windpipe splits in two. Air rushing out of the lungs makes thin walls inside it vibrate.

Because the two sides of the syrinx can work separately, some birds sing two notes at once and seem to harmonise with themselves. Birds' songs are fast. Some notes come too quickly for our ears to separate, though slowing a recording reveals them, a bit like slowing down written music to see each note.

Learning to sing

Many songbirds are not born knowing their song. Young birds listen to adults, then practise. Their first attempts are rough, a little like a baby's babbling, and improve over weeks. Birds raised alone often sing a strange, simple version. Zebra finches, small birds studied in many labs, must hear an adult sing while they are young, or they never get their song right. Scientists study them to learn how children pick up speech.

Some birds borrow songs from others. The greater racket-tailed drongo of Indian forests copies the calls of other birds. In the USA, the northern mockingbird can learn dozens of other species' songs and is the state bird of Texas and four other states. Parrots take copying even further, as you can read in how parrots talk.

Listening for science

Birdwatchers often find birds by ear long before they see them. Phone apps can now listen to a recording and suggest which birds are singing. Each February, people around the world, including thousands in India, join the Great Backyard Bird Count, which began in North America in 1998. They count birds for at least 15 minutes and share their lists, helping scientists see how bird numbers change.

Recordings show that the dawn chorus itself is changing. In 2021, scientists rebuilt the sound of thousands of spring mornings in Europe and North America over 25 years. They found the chorus had become quieter and less varied in many places, as many common birds grew rarer. A loud, busy morning chorus is a sign of a place where plenty of birds still live.

The koel and the crow

In much of India, summer has a soundtrack: the koel's rising "ku-oo, ku-oo", often before dawn. That loud singer is a male. The female, speckled brown, has a sharper call.

Koels do not build nests. A female lays her eggs in the nest of a crow, often while her mate distracts the crow owners. The crows then raise the koel chick as their own. This kind of trick is called brood parasitism. It is risky, because crows are clever and sometimes spot the intruder. In noisy cities, some birds have changed too. Studies in Britain found robins singing at night where daytime traffic noise was loudest.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Only male birds sing.

Truth: Females sing in many species. A 2014 study found female song in about seven out of ten songbird species that scientists had information on, and it is especially common in the tropics.

Try this

Make a dawn chorus diary. On three mornings, sit quietly by a window or balcony at sunrise for ten minutes and count how many different songs you hear. Do it again at lunchtime and compare. Which time of day is loudest?
