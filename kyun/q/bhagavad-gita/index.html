What is the Bhagavad Gita?

The Bhagavad Gita begins with a warrior so upset before a battle that he drops his bow and refuses to fight.

The short answer

The Bhagavad Gita, meaning "Song of the Lord", is a Hindu scripture of 700 verses in Sanskrit. It is part of the great epic the Mahabharata, but it is so widely read that it is often printed as a book on its own.

It is a conversation on a battlefield between Prince Arjuna and Krishna, who is acting as his charioteer. Many Hindus worship Krishna as a form of the god Vishnu. Arjuna doesn't want to fight a war against his own cousins, teachers and elders. Krishna answers his doubts with teachings about duty, action, the soul and devotion to God. For many Hindus, the Gita is one of the most sacred and loved texts.

Surprising but true

The Gita was translated into English in 1785, and American writers such as Henry David Thoreau later read it closely.

A warrior's crisis

The Mahabharata tells of a long feud between two branches of a royal family, the Pandavas and the Kauravas. Talks fail, and both sides gather huge armies at Kurukshetra, in what is now Haryana.

Just before the battle, Arjuna, the finest archer of the Pandavas, asks Krishna to drive his chariot into the middle. He sees grandfathers, teachers and cousins on the other side. His hands shake and his bow slips. He says he would rather lose than kill his own family. The rest of the Gita is Krishna's answer over 18 chapters.

What Krishna teaches

Krishna tells Arjuna that the true self, the soul, is never born and never dies. Bodies change, like clothes, but the self goes on. He urges Arjuna to do his duty, or dharma, as a warrior.

One of the best-known ideas is about action. Krishna says people should do their work well without being attached to the results, whether success or failure. He describes several paths to God: selfless action (karma yoga), knowledge (jnana yoga) and loving devotion (bhakti yoga). Near the end, Krishna shows Arjuna a vast cosmic form, filling all of space and time.

What is the Mahabharata?It has about 100,000 verses, making it one of the longest poems ever composed. Hidden inside it is the Bhagavad Gita.

Many ways of reading it

For more than a thousand years, teachers have written commentaries explaining the Gita, and they don't all agree. Adi Shankara, in about the 8th century, stressed knowledge. Later teachers like Ramanuja stressed devotion. In the 13th century, the saint Dnyaneshwar explained it in Marathi, so ordinary people could understand it.

In modern times, Bal Gangadhar Tilak wrote a long book on the Gita while in prison in Mandalay, reading it as a call to action. Gandhi, who called it his guide, read the battle as a picture of the struggle between good and evil inside every person, and found in it support for non-violence. Others read the battle more literally. These different readings are part of the Gita's long life.

Travelling the world

In 1785, Charles Wilkins, who worked for the East India Company, published the first English translation. Soon it was read in Europe and America. The American writers Ralph Waldo Emerson and Henry David Thoreau admired it. Swami Vivekananda spoke about its ideas during his visit to America in the 1890s.

Today the Gita has been translated into many languages. When the scientist Robert Oppenheimer watched the first atomic bomb test in 1945, he later said a line from the Gita came into his mind. Its questions about duty and fear speak to people far beyond India.

The Gita in daily life

Many Hindu families keep a copy at home, and some recite a few verses every day. Gita Jayanti, celebrated in the month of Margashirsha, marks the day the teachings are believed to have been given. At Kurukshetra, pilgrims visit Jyotisar, where tradition says Krishna spoke to Arjuna.

People who aren't Hindu read the Gita too, as poetry, philosophy or history. Some study it in school, others in a quiet moment before an exam. The opening question, what should I do when doing the right thing feels hard, is one almost everyone faces.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Bhagavad Gita is a separate book written apart from the Mahabharata.

Truth: The Gita is a section inside the Mahabharata, in its sixth book. It is often printed and read on its own, which makes many people think it is separate.

Try this

Think of a time you had to do something hard, like a test, a performance or saying sorry. Write down what you were worried about and what helped you go ahead. The Gita is a conversation about this kind of moment. Talk with a family member about how they decide what the right thing to do is.
