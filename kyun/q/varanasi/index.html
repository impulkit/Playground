Why is Varanasi one of the oldest cities?

People have lived beside the Ganga at Varanasi for well over 2,500 years. The American writer Mark Twain said it was "older than history".

The short answer

Varanasi, also called Kashi and Banaras, sits on the western bank of the Ganga in Uttar Pradesh. Archaeologists digging at Rajghat, at the city's northern edge, have found signs that people lived there more than 2,500 years ago. Hindu texts describe Kashi as a holy city from very early times.

What makes Varanasi special is that it has never really stopped being lived in. Cities in other places rose and were abandoned, but here people kept praying, trading, studying and weaving beside the same river for thousands of years. In 1897 the American writer Mark Twain visited and wrote that it was "older than history, older than tradition, older even than legend".

Surprising but true

The Buddha gave his first teaching at Sarnath, only about 10 km from Varanasi's ghats.

A city of steps

Along the river, Varanasi is lined with ghats, wide stone steps leading down to the water. There are more than 80 of them, stretching for several kilometres. At dawn, people come to bathe, pray, wash clothes, do yoga and sell flowers. Boats row slowly past.

Every evening at Dashashwamedh Ghat, priests perform the Ganga Aarti. They wave large brass lamps in circles, while bells ring and conch shells sound. For many Hindus, the Ganga is a goddess, and bathing in her water at Kashi is very holy.

Why so many come here

Hindus believe that Varanasi is the city of Shiva. The Kashi Vishwanath temple is one of the most important Shiva temples in India. The present building was put up in 1780 by Ahilyabai Holkar, a queen of Indore, and its spire was later covered in gold given by Maharaja Ranjit Singh.

Many Hindus believe that dying in Kashi, or being cremated on its ghats, frees the soul from the cycle of rebirth. That is why some elderly people come to spend their last years in the city. At Manikarnika Ghat, cremation fires have burned day and night for centuries.

Sacred to many faiths

Just outside the city lies Sarnath, where the Buddha gave his first teaching after his enlightenment, about 2,500 years ago. The emperor Ashoka later built a pillar there. Its lion top is now the national emblem of India.

Jains honour Varanasi as the birthplace of Parshvanatha, one of their tirthankaras, or great teachers. The poet-saint Kabir lived here in the 1400s. He was raised by a family of Muslim weavers and wrote poems that both Hindus and Muslims still sing. Tulsidas, who retold the Ramayana in Awadhi, also lived and wrote in the city.

Silk, music and learning

Varanasi's weavers, many of them Muslim families, make Banarasi saris with patterns in gold and silver thread. A single fine sari can take weeks to weave on a handloom. They are often worn at weddings across India.

The city is also a home of Hindustani classical music. The great shehnai player Ustad Bismillah Khan lived here and played every day by the river. In 1916, Madan Mohan Malaviya founded Banaras Hindu University, now one of the largest residential universities in Asia.

A river under strain

The Ganga at Varanasi is under pressure from sewage and waste from towns upstream. The government and local groups have spent years building treatment plants and cleaning the ghats, with mixed results. People who live here see the river as a mother, and they also want her to be clean.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Varanasi is the oldest city in the world.

Truth: It is one of the oldest cities still lived in, but no one can prove it is the very oldest. Places such as Damascus, Jericho and Byblos in West Asia also claim many thousands of years of settlement.
