Why is Amitabh Bachchan called the Shahenshah?

The actor with one of the most famous voices in India was, by his own account, turned down for a job as a radio announcer when he was young.

The short answer

Amitabh Bachchan is one of the most famous actors in the history of Indian cinema. Fans call him the Shahenshah, the "emperor". That is partly because of a 1988 film of that name, in which he played a masked avenger, and partly because he ruled Hindi cinema like one for decades.

He became a star in the 1970s playing the "angry young man", a tough outsider fighting injustice. Later he became the friendly host of the quiz show Kaun Banega Crorepati, and he is still acting in his eighties. His other nicknames include Big B and Sadi ka Mahanayak, the "superstar of the century".

Surprising but true

In 1999, a BBC News Online poll asked readers to name the greatest star of stage or screen of the millennium. Amitabh Bachchan came first, ahead of Charlie Chaplin.

A poet's son

Amitabh was born in 1942 in Allahabad, now Prayagraj. His father, Harivansh Rai Bachchan, was a well-known Hindi poet, best remembered for the long poem Madhushala. His mother, Teji Bachchan, was active in theatre. Amitabh sometimes recites his father's verses in public, in the same deep voice audiences know from his films.

After college in Delhi, he worked for a company in Calcutta. According to a story he has often told, he applied to be an announcer at All India Radio and was turned down. That voice would later narrate films and sell everything from paint to chocolate.

Flops, then fire

His first film, Saat Hindustani, came out in 1969. For a few years, success was slow. Then came Zanjeer in 1973, in which he played a stern, honest police officer. Audiences who were frustrated with poverty, unemployment and corruption in 1970s India loved this new kind of hero, who did not sing love songs in gardens but fought back.

Two years later came Deewaar and Sholay. Deewaar, about two brothers on opposite sides of the law, gave him some of Hindi cinema's most quoted lines. Sholay ran in one Bombay cinema for about five years. By the late 1970s, cinema halls across India were full of his films, often several at once.

The accident that stopped a country

In 1982, while filming a fight scene for Coolie in Bengaluru, Amitabh was hit in the stomach and badly hurt. For weeks his life was in danger. Across India, people prayed in temples, mosques, churches and gurdwaras, and crowds waited outside the hospital in Bombay. When he recovered, the film's makers changed the ending so that his character survived, and froze the frame of the fateful punch on screen.

Shahenshah and hard times

In 1984 he entered politics and won a seat in Parliament, but he resigned within three years. His film Shahenshah came out in 1988 and was a hit. Its dialogue "Rishte mein toh hum tumhare baap lagte hain, naam hai Shahenshah" is still imitated in school playgrounds.

The 1990s were harder. A company he founded ran into heavy debts, and several films failed. Many people thought his career was over.

The quiz show comeback

In 2000, he agreed to host Kaun Banega Crorepati, the Indian version of the British show Who Wants to Be a Millionaire?. Film stars rarely did television then. The show was a huge hit. His polite, warm manner with contestants from small towns, calling them "devi ji" and "sir", made him a new kind of hero for Indian television.

He went back to films with roles suited to an older actor: fathers, teachers and wise old warriors. In Kalki 2898 AD (2024) he played Ashwatthama from the Mahabharata. He received the Dadasaheb Phalke Award for 2018, India's highest film honour. More than 50 years after Zanjeer, the man once rejected by radio is still one of the busiest voices in Indian entertainment. His films are dubbed into many languages for new audiences.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Amitabh Bachchan was an instant success from his first film.

Truth: His early films mostly flopped, and some producers thought he was too tall and too thin for a hero. His first big hit, Zanjeer, came in 1973, four years after his debut.
