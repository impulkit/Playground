How did India become a hockey superpower?

India's men won eight Olympic gold medals in hockey, six of them in a row, more than any other country.

The short answer

India became a hockey superpower in the late 1920s. A team of skilled players, led by the legendary Dhyan Chand, won the Olympic gold in Amsterdam in 1928. India then won every Olympic hockey gold until 1956, six in a row, and two more in 1964 and 1980.

Indian teams of that era played on grass with dazzling close control. Opponents often said the ball seemed glued to their sticks. When the sport moved to artificial turf in the 1970s, India struggled for decades. In recent years it has climbed back, winning Olympic bronze medals in Tokyo and Paris.

Surprising but true

At the 1932 Los Angeles Olympics, India beat the United States 24–1, still one of the biggest wins in Olympic hockey history.

How hockey came to India

Modern field hockey was organised in England in the 1800s, and British soldiers brought it to India. The first Indian clubs formed in Calcutta in the 1880s. Indian players took to it fast, especially in Punjab, the Bombay region and among army regiments.

The Indian Hockey Federation was formed in 1925. Three years later, India sent its first Olympic hockey team to Amsterdam. It won all five matches without conceding a goal and took gold.

The Wizard

Dhyan Chand, a soldier in the British Indian Army, is India's greatest hockey player. He scored 14 goals at the 1928 Olympics. His dribbling was so tight that, as the story goes, officials in the Netherlands once broke his stick to check it for a magnet.

In the 1936 Berlin Olympics final, India beat the home team, Germany, 8–1. Dhyan Chand's birthday, 29 August, is India's National Sports Day. In 2021 the country's highest sports award was renamed the Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna.

Gold after freedom

The 1940 and 1944 Olympics were cancelled because of the Second World War. When the Games returned in London in 1948, India played as an independent country for the first time. In the final, it beat Great Britain 4–0 at Wembley. For players who had grown up under British rule, it was a deeply emotional win.

India won again in 1952 and 1956. Pakistan, whose team grew from the same hockey traditions, beat India in the 1960 final, and the two neighbours became fierce rivals. India also won the men's World Cup in 1975, beating Pakistan 2–1 in the final in Kuala Lumpur.

The turf problem

In 1976 the Montreal Olympics used artificial turf for hockey for the first time. On grass, bumps and long blades slow the ball. On the smooth carpet of plastic, with much less friction, the ball zips along at speed. The game became faster and more physical, and fitness mattered as much as fine stick work.

Turf pitches were expensive, and India had only a handful. European and Australian teams trained on them every day. India's last Olympic gold for many years came in 1980, at Games where many top teams stayed away.

The comeback

Change came slowly. Better coaching, more turf pitches and sponsorship helped. In 2018 the state of Odisha began sponsoring India's national hockey teams, and later built a large new stadium in Rourkela for the 2023 World Cup.

At the Tokyo Olympics, held in 2021, India's men beat Germany 5–4 to win bronze, their first Olympic medal in 41 years. The women's team finished fourth, their best ever result. In Paris in 2024 the men won bronze again, and Indian fans had a reason to cheer Olympic hockey once more.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Hockey is India's official national game.

Truth: Many textbooks and quiz books say so, but in 2012 the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports said in reply to a question that India has not declared any sport its national game.

Try this

Roll a marble or small ball across a carpet, then across a smooth tiled floor, pushing it with the same force each time. Measure how far it goes. Hockey teams faced exactly this change when they moved from grass to turf.
