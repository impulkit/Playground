Who was Frank Lloyd Wright?

America's most famous architect built a house that hangs right over a waterfall, and he said his love of buildings began with a box of wooden blocks.

The short answer

Frank Lloyd Wright was an American architect who lived from 1867 to 1959. Over about 70 years he designed more than 1,000 buildings, and roughly 530 were actually built. They include houses, churches, offices and one of the world's most unusual museums.

Wright believed buildings should fit their surroundings, as if they had grown there. He called this "organic architecture". His homes are often long and low, hugging the ground, with big overhanging roofs, natural wood and stone, and rooms that flow into each other. His most famous house, Fallingwater, sits right over a waterfall in the woods of Pennsylvania.

Surprising but true

Lincoln Logs, the classic American toy building set, was invented by Frank Lloyd Wright's son John.

Blocks on the nursery floor

Wright was born in Richland Center, a small town in Wisconsin. When he was a boy, his mother gave him a set of Froebel blocks. These were smooth maple cubes, cylinders and triangles invented by a German teacher, Friedrich Froebel, to help small children learn about shapes.

Wright later said the blocks shaped how he thought about buildings for the rest of his life. The love of building toys ran in the family. His son John invented Lincoln Logs, the notched wooden logs that he created around 1916, long before plastic LEGO bricks existed.

Houses of the prairie

Wright trained in Chicago, working for the architect Louis Sullivan, who designed some of America's early skyscrapers. Then he set up his own office and began designing homes for families in Chicago's suburbs.

These became known as Prairie houses. They are low and wide, like the flat grasslands of the American Midwest. Rows of windows run in long bands, roofs reach far out past the walls, and a big fireplace sits at the centre of the home. Inside, Wright often designed everything: the furniture, lamps, rugs and even the stained-glass windows.

A house over a waterfall

In the 1930s, the Kaufmann family of Pittsburgh asked Wright to design a weekend house near a waterfall on a stream called Bear Run. They expected a house with a view of the falls. Wright put the house right on top of them.

Fallingwater, built between 1936 and 1939, has wide concrete terraces that stretch out over the stream, held up only at one end. This is called a cantilever, like a diving board. The stone floor of the living room is built around boulders from the site, and steps lead down to the water. Over the years the terraces sagged and needed major repairs. Today the house is a museum, and visitors come from all over the world.

The spiral museum

Wright's last great building was the Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum in New York. It is shaped like a giant white spiral, wider at the top than at the bottom. Visitors take a lift to the top and walk slowly down a long ramp that curves around an open centre, looking at art as they go.

Wright worked on the design for about 16 years. The museum opened in October 1959, six months after he died at the age of 91.

Genius, with flaws

Wright could be difficult. He was famously proud, often went over budget and sometimes ignored what his clients asked for. Several of his buildings had leaking roofs, and he was known for brushing off the complaints. His private life was often in the newspapers.

Still, his influence is everywhere. The open-plan living room, where kitchen, dining and sitting areas flow together, owes a lot to him. In 2019, UNESCO listed eight of his buildings, including Fallingwater and the Guggenheim, as a World Heritage Site.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Imperial Hotel in Tokyo was the only building left standing after the great 1923 earthquake.

Truth: Wright's hotel did survive the Great Kantō earthquake fairly well, and he made the most of the story. But many other buildings survived too, and his hotel was damaged. It was later demolished in 1968.

Try this

Build a tower from books or blocks, then try making a shelf that sticks out from the side without anything holding it up underneath. How far can it stick out before it tips? Fallingwater's terraces use the same idea, called a cantilever, anchored into the heavy stone core of the house.
