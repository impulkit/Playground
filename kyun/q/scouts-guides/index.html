What do Scouts and Guides do?

It all started with 20 boys camping on a small English island in 1907. Today there are tens of millions of Scouts and Guides.

The short answer

Scouts and Guides are clubs for children and young people that teach outdoor skills, teamwork and helping others. Members meet in small groups called patrols, go camping, learn to tie knots, read maps, cook outdoors and give first aid. They earn cloth badges for skills such as swimming, birdwatching or first aid, and wear them on a uniform.

They also do service: planting trees, cleaning up parks, helping at fairs or during floods. Each member makes a promise to do their best and to help other people. The idea is that you learn by doing, with older members teaching younger ones.

Surprising but true

The Scout motto, "Be Prepared", shares its initials with the founder, Baden-Powell, and he is said to have enjoyed the match.

A camp on an island

Robert Baden-Powell was a British army officer who had written a book on scouting for soldiers. To his surprise, boys began reading it too. In 1907 he took about 20 boys from different backgrounds to camp on Brownsea Island, off the south coast of England. They learned tracking, cooking and first aid, and played games in patrols.

In 1908 he published Scouting for Boys. Troops sprang up all over Britain within months, then across the world. Girls turned up at a big Scout rally in 1909 asking to join. The answer was the Girl Guides, founded in 1910 with help from Baden-Powell's sister Agnes.

Scouting in India

Scouting reached India in 1909, but at first only British and Anglo-Indian boys could join. Indians set up their own Scout groups in response. Leaders such as Annie Besant and Madan Mohan Malaviya helped start some of them.

After independence, the different groups joined together. The Bharat Scouts and Guides was formed in 1950 and is now one of the largest Scouting bodies in the world. Its members help at events such as the Kumbh Mela, where they guide lost pilgrims and give first aid.

Badges, knots and the North Star

Skills are the fun part. A Scout learns that a reef knot joins two ropes, while a bowline makes a loop that will not slip. At night, Scouts learn to find the North Star and use it to tell direction. With a map and compass, they practise finding their way across a park or forest. Reading a map's scale tells them how far the next checkpoint really is, and counting their own steps tells them how far they have walked.

A big gathering of Scouts from many countries is called a jamboree. The first World Jamboree was held in London in 1920. Some jamborees now bring together more than 40,000 young people, a crowd that could fill a cricket stadium.

Scouting in the USA

The Boy Scouts of America was founded in 1910. Two years later, Juliette Gordon Low started the first Girl Scout troop in Savannah, Georgia. Girl Scouts are known across the USA for selling cookies to raise money, a tradition that began in the 1910s.

The Boy Scouts began admitting girls into its programmes in 2018 and 2019. In 2025 it changed its name to Scouting America, to show that the programme is for everyone.

Learning by helping

Scouting has had critics. Some point to its military beginnings, and some groups were slow to welcome everyone. Supporters say it gives children something school often cannot: the chance to lead a small team, solve real problems outdoors and do useful volunteer work. Many astronauts, including several who walked on the Moon, were Scouts as children.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Scouting is only for boys.

Truth: Girl Guides began in 1910, only three years after the first camp. Many countries now have mixed groups, and the Bharat Scouts and Guides has both. In the USA, girls have been able to join the main Scouts programme since 2019.

Try this

Learn the reef knot (called the square knot in the USA) with two shoelaces. Cross left over right and tuck under, then right over left and tuck under. Pull both ends and it should lie flat. If it twists, you have tied a "granny knot" instead, so try again.
