Why did America fight for independence?

The fight that created the United States began with a tax on tea, and that tea was shipped by the same company that ruled much of India.

The short answer

In the 1700s, thirteen British colonies stretched along the east coast of North America. The people living there were ruled from London, more than 5,000 km away across the Atlantic.

After an expensive war, Britain began taxing the colonists to pay its bills. The colonists had no members in Britain's Parliament, so they had no say in those taxes. "No taxation without representation" became their cry. Protests turned into fighting in 1775. In 1776 the colonies declared themselves independent, and after eight years of war they won. The new country was the United States of America.

Surprising but true

Lord Cornwallis, the British general who surrendered to George Washington in 1781, was sent to govern British India only a few years later.

Paying for a war

From 1754 to 1763, Britain fought France for control of North America in what the colonists called the French and Indian War. Britain won, but the war cost a fortune, and keeping soldiers in America cost more.

London decided the colonies should help pay. The Stamp Act of 1765 taxed newspapers, playing cards and legal papers. Later laws taxed glass, paint, paper and tea. Each new tax led to boycotts and protests, and some were dropped. In 1770, British soldiers in Boston fired into a crowd that was taunting them and throwing snowballs, killing five people. Colonists called it the Boston Massacre. The anger stayed.

Tea in the harbour

In 1773 Britain passed the Tea Act. It let the East India Company, which was struggling with debts, sell its tea in America cheaply and directly. The tea itself was grown in China at the time.

Many colonists saw this as a trick to make them accept Britain's right to tax them. On the night of 16 December 1773, a group of men in Boston, some loosely dressed as Mohawk Indians, boarded three ships and threw 342 chests of tea into the sea. It became known as the Boston Tea Party.

How did tea become India's favourite drink?Most Indians drink chai every day, but a hundred years ago many had never tasted it, and advertisers worked hard to change that.

From protest to war

Britain punished Boston by closing its harbour. In April 1775, British soldiers marched out to seize weapons stored at Concord, near Boston. Colonial fighters met them at Lexington and Concord, and the war began.

The colonies' Congress chose George Washington of Virginia to lead their army. His soldiers were often short of food, boots and pay. Some were teenagers, and some were Black soldiers, free and enslaved, who fought on both sides. During the winter of 1777–78 at Valley Forge, thousands fell ill in the cold camp.

The Declaration

On 4 July 1776, Congress approved the Declaration of Independence, written mostly by Thomas Jefferson. Its most famous line says that "all men are created equal" and have rights to "Life, Liberty and the pursuit of Happiness".

Those words did not apply to everyone yet. About one in five people in the colonies was enslaved, and women and Native Americans were left out. Later generations used the same words to demand their own rights.

Winning

The turning point came in 1777, when Americans defeated a British army at Saratoga in New York. France then joined the war on America's side, bringing ships, soldiers and money.

In October 1781, French and American forces trapped General Cornwallis at Yorktown, Virginia, and he surrendered. The Treaty of Paris in 1783 recognised the United States as independent. A few years later the new nation wrote its Constitution.

Cornwallis, meanwhile, became Governor-General of India in 1786. Britain had lost thirteen colonies in America, but it was steadily gaining power in India. Every 4 July, Americans celebrate with parades and fireworks.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Declaration of Independence was signed by everyone on 4 July 1776.

Truth: Congress approved the wording on 4 July. Most delegates signed the handwritten copy on 2 August 1776, and a few signed even later.
