What is Earth Day?

On 22 April 1970, about 2 crore Americans, roughly one in ten people in the country, went out to protest pollution on the very first Earth Day.

The short answer

Earth Day is held every year on 22 April. On that day, people around the world plant trees, clean beaches and parks, hold rallies and learn about problems like pollution and climate change. Schools often run special lessons, poster contests and clean-up drives. Some families switch off lights for an hour or cycle instead of driving.

It began in the USA in 1970, at a time when rivers were so dirty they sometimes caught fire and smog hung over big cities. Today, organisers say it is marked in more than 190 countries, which makes it one of the most widely observed days in the world.

Surprising but true

The date 22 April was chosen partly because it fell between spring holidays and final exams at American colleges, so students would be free to join in.

Rivers on fire

By the late 1960s, many Americans were worried about pollution. Factories poured waste into rivers, cars burned leaded petrol and pesticides were killing birds. In 1962, a scientist named Rachel Carson had written Silent Spring, a bestselling book warning about the harm caused by pesticides.

Then, in early 1969, an oil well off the coast of Santa Barbara, California, blew out. Millions of litres of oil spread across the sea and onto beaches, coating seabirds and seals. A few months later, the Cuyahoga River in Ohio, thick with oily waste, caught fire. It had burned before, but this time the story spread across the country. People who had never thought much about nature began to ask what was happening to their own rivers and air.

A senator's idea

Gaylord Nelson, a US senator from Wisconsin, saw the oil spill and had an idea. Students were already holding "teach-ins" against the Vietnam War. Why not a nationwide teach-in about the environment? He hired a young organiser named Denis Hayes to make it happen.

On 22 April 1970, about 20 million Americans took part. Schools and colleges held talks, people marched in city streets and volunteers cleaned up rubbish. By the end of 1970, the USA had created the Environmental Protection Agency. Within a few years came new laws to clean the air and water and protect endangered species.

Going global

In 1990, on its 20th anniversary, Earth Day went worldwide, with around 200 million people in about 140 countries taking part. It helped push governments towards the big Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro in 1992.

On Earth Day 2016, leaders from around 175 countries gathered at the United Nations in New York to sign the Paris Agreement on climate change. The UN now calls 22 April International Mother Earth Day. Each year the organisers pick a theme, such as planting trees, cutting plastic or switching to clean energy, so that groups everywhere can work on the same problem at once.

Earth Day in India

In India, schools and cities mark Earth Day with tree planting, cleanliness drives and talks. India also has its own long history of protecting nature. In the 1970s, in what is now Uttarakhand, village women in the Chipko movement hugged trees to stop them being cut down.

Many Indian schools put even more energy into World Environment Day on 5 June, which the UN started. The two days have similar goals, and some schools celebrate both.

Does one day help?

Critics say a single day of tree planting can't fix pollution or climate change, and that some companies use Earth Day for advertising while carrying on as usual. Supporters reply that the first Earth Day led directly to laws that cleaned up American air and rivers. They also say the day gives young people a reason to start caring, and that habits built on one day, like recycling or switching off lights, can last the whole year.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Earth Day and World Environment Day are the same thing.

Truth: Earth Day is on 22 April and began as a US protest in 1970. World Environment Day is on 5 June and was started by the United Nations, first held in 1974. Both now happen around the world.
