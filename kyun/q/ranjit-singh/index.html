Who was Maharaja Ranjit Singh?

A boy who lost an eye to smallpox captured Lahore at 19 and built an empire stretching from Punjab to the Khyber Pass.

The short answer

Maharaja Ranjit Singh was the founder of the Sikh Empire, which ruled Punjab and the lands around it in the early 1800s. Born in 1780, he took control of the city of Lahore in 1799, when he was just 19, and was proclaimed Maharaja of Punjab in 1801.

Over the next four decades he united the quarrelling Sikh groups, called misls, and conquered Multan, Kashmir and Peshawar. He built one of the strongest armies in Asia, kept the British at a distance through a treaty and ran a court where people of many faiths served. Within ten years of his death in 1839, his empire had fallen to the British.

Surprising but true

Ranjit Singh hired veteran officers who had fought for Napoleon to train his army in the European style.

The one-eyed boy

Ranjit Singh was born in Gujranwala, in today's Pakistan, the son of the leader of one of the Sikh misls. As a small child he caught smallpox, which left his face scarred and blinded his left eye. He never learned to read or write.

His father died when he was about ten. Ranjit Singh soon proved a sharp leader. In 1799 he rode into Lahore, which had been squabbled over by several chiefs, and its people welcomed him. Lahore became his capital.

A Sikh empire

Through battles, alliances and marriages, Ranjit Singh brought the misls together. He took Multan in 1818 and Kashmir in 1819. In 1834 his forces captured Peshawar, at the foot of the Khyber Pass, an entry point that invaders had used for centuries.

He called his government the Sarkar-i-Khalsa, the government of the Khalsa, and issued coins in the name of the Sikh Gurus rather than his own. Visitors noted that he dressed simply and often sat cross-legged among his courtiers. His kingdom stretched from the Sutlej river to the mountains of Afghanistan.

What is Punjab known for?Punjab's name means "five waters", and those rivers turned its plains into one of the great grain baskets of the world.

An army trained by Napoleon's men

Ranjit Singh wanted an army that could match the British. He hired European officers, including Jean-Baptiste Ventura from Italy and Jean-François Allard from France, who had fought in the armies of Napoleon.

They trained his infantry to march and fight in disciplined lines, with modern cannon and uniforms. The army, called the Fauj-i-Khas for its best units, became one of the most powerful forces in Asia. In 1809 he signed the Treaty of Amritsar with the British East India Company, agreeing that the Sutlej river would be the border between them. Both sides kept that promise while he lived.

Gold, a diamond and many faiths

Ranjit Singh paid to cover the upper floors of the Harmandir Sahib in Amritsar with gilded copper, giving it the name the Golden Temple. He also gave gold for the Kashi Vishwanath temple in Varanasi.

In 1813 he obtained the Koh-i-Noor diamond from Shah Shuja, an exiled ruler of Afghanistan who had taken shelter in Lahore. Ranjit Singh wore it in an armband.

His court mixed faiths and backgrounds. Fakir Azizuddin, a Muslim, was his foreign minister. Dogra Hindu brothers held top posts, and Europeans commanded troops. In 2020, readers of a BBC history magazine voted him the greatest leader of all time, ahead of figures like Abraham Lincoln.

After the lion

Ranjit Singh, often called Sher-e-Punjab, the Lion of Punjab, died in Lahore in 1839. Without him, his heirs and generals fought each other, and several rulers came and went in a few years.

The British fought two wars with the Sikh army in 1845–46 and 1848–49, and in 1849 they took over Punjab. The last Maharaja, Duleep Singh, was a boy of ten. He was sent to Britain, and the Koh-i-Noor was given to Queen Victoria. It is now part of the British Crown Jewels in London. Ranjit Singh's memorial, his samadhi, still stands in Lahore beside the great Badshahi Mosque.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Ranjit Singh's court was only for Sikhs.

Truth: His ministers and generals included Hindus, Muslims, Sikhs and even Europeans. His foreign minister, Fakir Azizuddin, was a Muslim, and he gave gifts to mosques and temples as well as gurdwaras.
