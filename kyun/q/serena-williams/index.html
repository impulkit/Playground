Who is Serena Williams?

She won the Australian Open in 2017 without dropping a set. Only later did the world learn she had been about eight weeks pregnant.

The short answer

Serena Williams is an American tennis player, and many people consider her the greatest woman ever to play the game. She won 23 Grand Slam singles titles, the most by any player since professionals were allowed into the big tournaments in 1968. She also won four Olympic gold medals and 14 Grand Slam doubles titles with her sister Venus.

She played professionally for about 27 years. Her powerful serve and fierce style changed women's tennis, and she became one of the most famous athletes on the planet.

Surprising but true

Serena and her sister Venus played 14 Grand Slam women's doubles finals together and won all 14.

Two sisters and a plan

Serena was born in Saginaw, Michigan, in 1981, the youngest of five sisters. The family moved to Compton, in Los Angeles county. Her father, Richard Williams, had decided to make two of his daughters tennis champions, and wrote a long plan for their careers before they were even born, according to his own account.

He taught Venus and Serena on public courts, often worn and cracked. The girls practised for hours. When they were about ten and nine, the family moved to Florida for better coaching.

The first Slam

Serena won her first Grand Slam singles title at the 1999 US Open, aged 17. She was the first Black woman to win a Grand Slam singles title since Althea Gibson in 1958.

The sisters often met each other in finals, which was hard for both. Between 2002 and 2003 Serena won four Grand Slams in a row, beating Venus in all four finals. Fans called it the "Serena Slam". She did it again in 2014–15.

How she played

Serena's serve was her most famous weapon. It was fast, accurate and hard to read, because she used almost the same toss for different serves. She hit hard from the back of the court and moved well for a powerful player.

She also fought back from losing positions again and again. In tight moments she seemed to raise her level, something players call being clutch. Her muscles were strong, but her planning and nerve were just as important.

She was ranked world number one for 319 weeks in total, including 186 weeks in a row. She won Wimbledon seven times, the Australian Open seven times and the US Open six times. The French Open, on slow clay courts, was her hardest Slam, yet she won it three times.

Pregnant champion

In January 2017 Serena won the Australian Open, her 23rd Grand Slam singles title, without losing a set. A few months later she announced she had been about eight weeks pregnant during the tournament. Her daughter, Olympia, was born in September 2017.

The birth was difficult and she had serious health problems afterwards. She later spoke out about how Black women in the USA face higher risks during childbirth, and pushed for better care.

Courts and causes

Serena often spoke up about equal pay and fair treatment for women athletes. She faced racist abuse at times during her career, and she talked about it openly. In 2001, after a crowd booed her and her family at Indian Wells, she stayed away from that tournament for 14 years.

She also had controversies of her own, such as an angry argument with an umpire in the 2018 US Open final, for which she was fined. She stopped playing after the 2022 US Open and now runs an investment company. Her last match was watched by millions.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Serena won mostly because she was stronger than everyone else.

Truth: Her serve was one of the best ever, but she also won with clever shot choices, fast movement and great returns. Many experts call her the best competitor tennis has seen when a match was close.
