What is the longest word in English?

The longest word in major English dictionaries has 45 letters, and it was invented in 1935 by a puzzle fan as a joke.

The short answer

The longest word in major English dictionaries is pneumonoultramicroscopicsilicovolcanoconiosis. It has 45 letters and is meant to name a lung disease caused by breathing in fine volcanic dust. The funny part is that nobody really uses it. A puzzle-maker invented it in 1935 to be the longest word, and dictionaries recorded it because people kept talking about it.

The answer changes depending on your rules. Chemists can build names thousands of letters long. Place names can be longer than most dictionary words. And English, unlike Sanskrit or German, doesn't usually glue many words together into one.

Surprising but true

The full chemical name of the protein titin is about 189,000 letters long. Reading it aloud takes hours.

A word built as a joke

In 1935, Everett M. Smith, president of the National Puzzlers' League in the US, made up the 45-letter word for a meeting. Newspapers picked it up. It was so long and so odd that people loved it, and dictionaries eventually added it.

It is built like a stack of blocks from Greek and Latin roots. The pieces are pneumono (lungs), ultra (beyond), microscopic (very small), silico (silica, a mineral in sand), volcano and coniosis (a disease caused by dust). Doctors just say silicosis, which is a real illness among miners and stone cutters.

Long words people actually use

Some long words are real and everyday. Antidisestablishmentarianism, at 28 letters, comes from a 19th-century argument in Britain about whether the Church of England should stay the official church. Floccinaucinihilipilification, at 29 letters, means "deciding something is worthless". It began as a joke among students at Eton.

Shakespeare used one long word just once, in the play Love's Labour's Lost: honorificabilitudinitatibus, 27 letters, meaning roughly "the state of being able to receive honours". It's still the longest word in his works, and it alternates consonants and vowels almost all the way through.

Others are shorter but still hard: incomprehensibilities has 21 letters. Uncopyrightable, at 15, is one of the longest English words with no letter used twice.

The word that takes hours to say

Scientists name chemicals by listing their parts. The largest known protein in the human body, titin, helps your muscles stretch. Its full chemical name, listing every building block, runs to about 189,000 letters.

People have posted videos of themselves reading it aloud, and one took about three and a half hours. No dictionary includes it, because it's more of a formula than a word. Titin itself is built by instructions in your DNA, the longest "word" of all, written in just four letters.

Places with very long names

A hill in New Zealand has an 85-letter name in the Māori language: Taumatawhakatangihangakoauauotamateaturipukakapikimaungahoronukupokaiwhenuakitanatahu. Roughly, it means "the summit where Tamatea, the man with the big knees, played his flute to his loved one". Locals simply call it Taumata.

In Wales, a village has a 58-letter name, Llanfairpwllgwyngyllgogerychwyrndrobwllllantysiliogogogoch. Its railway station sign is a popular spot for photos. The name means roughly "St Mary's church in the hollow of the white hazel, near a fierce whirlpool, and the church of St Tysilio by the red cave". It was stretched out in the 1860s, partly to attract tourists, and it worked.

Long words in other languages

Some languages make long words easily. Sanskrit can join many words into a single compound. One famous compound in an old Sanskrit poem runs for well over a hundred letters in Roman script. German glues nouns together too. A legal word about checking beef labels, 63 letters long, was removed from law in 2013.

Long words are fun, but good writers usually prefer short ones. They're easier to read and remember. And English still keeps inventing new ones, short and long, as people need them, which is how made-up words sometimes end up in dictionaries.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Supercalifragilisticexpialidocious is the longest word in English.

Truth: It's long and famous, thanks to Mary Poppins, but at 34 letters it's beaten by pneumonoultramicroscopicsilicovolcanoconiosis, which has 45. Many dictionaries list supercalifragilistic as a made-up word.
