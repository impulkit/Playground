How do knitting and weaving turn thread into cloth?

Your jeans are woven and your T-shirt is knitted. Pull one loose thread and you'll see the difference at once.

The short answer

Thread is weak on its own. To make cloth, you have to lock lots of it together. There are two main ways.

Weaving uses two sets of threads at right angles. The long threads, called the warp, are stretched tight on a frame called a loom. A second thread, the weft, goes over and under them from side to side, row after row. Knitting uses just one long yarn. It is pulled through itself in rows of loops, each row hanging from the one before.

Woven cloth is firm and holds its shape. Knitted cloth stretches, because the loops can open and close. That is why socks and sweaters are knitted, and shirts and jeans are woven.

Surprising but true

Punched cards that told looms which threads to lift, made famous by the Jacquard loom of 1804, later inspired the first designs for computers.

Over, under, over

The simplest weave goes over one warp thread and under the next, then does the opposite on the next row. That gives plain weave, used in cotton shirts and the khadi that Indians spun and wove by hand during the freedom movement.

Change the pattern and you change the cloth. In a twill weave, each row shifts one step sideways, making diagonal lines. Look closely at your jeans and you will see them. Denim is a twill with blue warp threads and white weft threads, which is why the inside of your jeans is paler than the outside. In satin weave, threads float over several others at once, which makes the surface shiny and smooth.

A loom makes the over-and-under quicker. Pedals lift every other warp thread at once, leaving a gap called a shed. A shuttle carrying the weft shoots through, the pedals swap, and the next row goes back the other way.

Cards with holes

Weaving complex patterns used to mean a helper sitting on top of the loom, lifting threads by hand. In 1804, Joseph-Marie Jacquard in France fitted looms with chains of punched cards. A hole meant "lift this thread". No hole meant "leave it down". The loom could weave a detailed picture by reading the cards.

The English inventor Charles Babbage planned to use punched cards to give instructions to his calculating engine. A hole or no hole is a yes-or-no choice, the same idea as the 0s and 1s inside computers today.

Who designed a computer 200 years ago?In the 1830s, an English mathematician designed a steam-age computer of brass gears. It took until 1991 for anyone to build one of his machines.

Loops on loops

Knitting needs no loom, just two needles, or later a machine. Each stitch pulls a loop of yarn through a loop below it. Because the whole fabric can be one thread, a dropped stitch can run like a ladder down a sock.

In 1589, an English clergyman named William Lee built a frame that could knit stockings by machine. Queen Elizabeth I is said to have refused him a patent, worried it would put hand knitters out of work. Today, huge circular knitting machines make tubes of T-shirt fabric at high speed. Hundreds of needles sit in a ring, each one hooking a fresh loop as the ring spins, so the fabric grows downwards like a long sock.

Hands and machines in India

India has woven cloth for at least 4,500 years. A scrap of cotton cloth was found at Mohenjo-daro, one of the cities of the Indus Valley. Today most cloth is made on power looms, but India still has about 35 lakh handloom workers, according to the 2019–20 government census. They weave Banarasi silk, Kanchipuram sarees and Assam's muga silk on looms in their homes.

Knitting has its own hubs. Ludhiana in Punjab is known for woollen sweaters, and Tiruppur in Tamil Nadu for cotton T-shirts. Weaving still rules in Kashmir, though. There, soft wool from mountain goats is spun and woven by hand into pashmina shawls, and some are embroidered for months afterwards.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Knitting is as old as weaving.

Truth: Weaving goes back many thousands of years. The oldest true knitting found so far is about a thousand years old, from Egypt. Some older pieces that look knitted were made by a different looping method using a single needle.

Try this

Cut a paper plate or square of card with slits along two opposite edges and wind wool back and forth between them. That is your warp. Then weave strips of paper or another colour of wool over one thread and under the next, and push each row tight. You have made a small piece of woven cloth.
