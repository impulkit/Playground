Why does Diwali jump to a different date every year?

Last year Diwali came in October. This year it waited until November, and the Moon is the reason.

The short answer

Diwali follows the Moon, not the 12 months on your school calendar. It falls on amavasya, the new moon night, when the sky is darkest, in the month of Kartik.

A Moon month is a little shorter than an ordinary month, so the new moon arrives on a different date every year. That is why Diwali can come anywhere from mid-October to mid-November.

Surprising but true

Diwali came so late this year because 2026 had a whole extra month in the Hindu calendar, a second Jyeshtha, which pushed every festival after it about three weeks later.

Why it's in the news

Diwali was celebrated across India on Sunday, 8 November 2026. The evening before, on 7 November, Ayodhya lit more than 36 lakh earthen diyas along 56 ghats of the Saryu river for its 10th Deepotsav, with help from over 35,000 volunteers. That was about 10 lakh more lamps than last year.

Many families noticed that Diwali felt late. In 2025 it fell on 20 October. This year it came almost three weeks later.

Two clocks in the sky

The Moon takes about 29.5 days to go from one new moon to the next as it travels in its orbit around Earth. Twelve of these Moon months add up to about 354 days. But Earth takes about 365 days to go round the Sun, and the Sun decides the seasons.

So the Moon calendar falls 11 days behind the Sun calendar every year. If nothing were done, Diwali would slowly wander from autumn into the monsoon, and then into summer.

Indian calendars fix this cleverly. About once every two and a half to three years, they add an extra month, called adhik maas. In 2026 the extra month was a second Jyeshtha, from mid-May to mid-June. Everything after it, from Raksha Bandhan to Diwali, shifted later. Next year there is no extra month, so Diwali will come earlier again.

Why the darkest night?

Diwali is the festival of lights, and lights shine brightest when there is no Moon. In the Ramayana story, people of Ayodhya lit lamps to welcome Rama home. A clay diya works by combustion: the cotton wick soaks up oil, and the flame burns the oil vapour, slowly and steadily, for hours.

What happens next

The Moon is already growing again. About a week after Diwali comes Chhath in Bihar and eastern Uttar Pradesh, and about two weeks after Diwali comes Kartik Purnima, the full moon. Watch the sky and you can see the calendar ticking.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Diwali is on a different date every year because priests pick a new day.

Truth: The date follows a rule. Diwali falls on a particular new moon night, and the new moon arrives on a different day of our ordinary calendar each year. Astronomers and calendar makers can work it out years ahead.

Try this

Look at the Moon every clear evening for the next two weeks and draw its shape in a notebook. Diwali night had no Moon at all. Count how many days until it is full again, and you will have measured half a lunar month yourself.
