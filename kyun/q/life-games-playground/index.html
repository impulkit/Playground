What playground games do kids play around the world?

A painting from 1560 shows about 80 children's games, and you probably still know how to play many of them.

The short answer

Wherever children have a bit of open ground, they invent games. Some need nothing at all: just running, chasing and hiding. Others need a chalk drawing, a few stones or a stick.

Many games turn up across the world with different names. Tag is pakdam pakdai in Hindi. Hopscotch is stapu or kith-kith in India, rayuela in Latin America and marelle in France. Hide-and-seek is chhupan chhupai. Five stones is gutte in north India and jacks in the US.

Some games belong to one place, like kho-kho in India or Red Rover in the US and UK. Kids learn most of them from other kids, not from adults or books.

Surprising but true

Children in ancient Greece played a game like five stones using the small ankle bones of sheep and goats.

A painting full of games

In 1560, the Flemish artist Pieter Bruegel the Elder painted a town square crowded with children. Look closely and you can find about 80 different games. Children play leapfrog, spin tops, walk on stilts, roll hoops, play blind man's buff and ride on each other's shoulders.

The painting hangs in a museum in Vienna. It is one of the most detailed records of children's play from that time, and a favourite puzzle among famous paintings. Many of its games are still played today, nearly 500 years later.

Stones, bones and sticks

Some games are very old. In ancient Greece, children played knucklebones, tossing and catching the small ankle bones of sheep and goats. The rules look a lot like five stones, which children across India play with pebbles.

Gilli-danda uses a long stick and a short one, tapered at both ends. You flick the small one up and whack it as far as you can. Some people think gilli-danda may be an ancestor of cricket and baseball, though the link is debated.

In lagori, also called pittu or seven stones, one team knocks down a pile of flat stones with a ball. Then they must rebuild it while the other team tries to hit them with the ball. Marbles, called kanche in Hindi, have been found in ancient sites from Egypt to the Indus Valley, as told in marbles and tops.

What traditional games do Indian children play?Ludo, snakes and ladders and chess all began as Indian games, long before they came back to India in cardboard boxes.

Choosing who's "it"

Before a game starts, kids need to pick who goes first. Around the world, they use counting rhymes. In English, there is "eeny, meeny, miny, moe". Many Indian languages have their own rhymes, sung while pointing round a circle.

Rock-paper-scissors is played almost everywhere. In Japan it is called janken, and versions of it are believed to have come from China centuries ago. In many Indian neighbourhoods, kids also do a quick hand-flip: everyone shows a palm up or down at the same moment, and the odd one out is chosen. Rules vary from street to street, and every group swears its version is the right one.

Collectors of games

In the 1950s and 1960s, two British researchers, Iona and Peter Opie, collected games from thousands of children across Britain. They found that children keep games alive for generations, passing them down from older kids to younger ones with little help from adults.

They also found that games change as they travel. A rhyme might lose a line, or a rule might get added in one school and not the next. Folklore experts in India and the US have found the same thing.

Kho-kho is a great example of a playground game becoming a sport. It grew from an old chasing game in Maharashtra, got official rules, and is now played in leagues and tournaments. In 2025, India hosted the first Kho Kho World Cup.

And the strangest part? Games help children make friends faster than almost anything else, which may be why playgrounds everywhere sound the same, whatever language the shouting is in.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Hopscotch was invented by Roman soldiers as training for battle.

Truth: This story is often repeated, but there is no good evidence for it. Hopping games are old and found in many places, and nobody knows exactly where hopscotch began.

Try this

Ask an older relative what games they played at your age, and write down the rules. Then teach one to your friends. You may find the game is almost the same as one you already know, with a different name.
