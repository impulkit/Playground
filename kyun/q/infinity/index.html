What is infinity?

A hotel with endless rooms can be completely full and still find space for a new guest, and mathematicians can prove it.

The short answer

Infinity means without end. However high you count, you can always add one more. The counting numbers 1, 2, 3 and so on never stop, so we say there are infinitely many of them. The symbol for infinity, ∞, looks like an 8 lying on its side.

Infinity is not an ordinary number like 7 or a crore. You cannot reach it, and adding one to it does not make it bigger. Instead it is an idea that mathematicians handle with special rules. Some of those rules are very surprising, such as the discovery that some infinities are larger than others.

Surprising but true

Some infinities are bigger than others. There are more points on a line than there are counting numbers, even though both go on forever.

Endless counting

No matter how big a number you name, a trillion, a googol or a googolplex, there is always a bigger one. Just add 1, or double it. That is the simplest kind of infinity. It is also why pi's digits can go on forever without repeating.

People have puzzled over infinity for thousands of years. Around 450 BCE, the Greek thinker Zeno argued that a fast runner could never catch a slow tortoise with a head start. Each time the runner reaches where the tortoise was, it has moved on a little. Mathematicians later explained that an endless list of ever-smaller steps can add up to a finite distance, so the runner wins.

Ancient Indian thinkers wrote about infinity too. Jain mathematicians sorted numbers into countable, uncountable and infinite, and even described different kinds of infinity. A famous verse in the Isha Upanishad says that when the whole is taken from the whole, the whole remains.

The hotel that is never full

In the 1920s, the German mathematician David Hilbert described a strange hotel with infinitely many rooms, all full. A new guest arrives. The manager asks every guest to move from room n to room n + 1. Everyone still has a room, and room 1 is now free.

Even an infinite busload of new guests can be fitted in. Each current guest moves to the room with double their number, filling all the even rooms. The odd rooms are now empty, and there are infinitely many of them. With infinity, "full" does not mean what it usually does.

Bigger infinities

In the 1870s and 1890s, the German mathematician Georg Cantor proved something that shocked many people. He showed you cannot match every point on a line, including all the decimals, one-to-one with the counting numbers. There are always points left over. So the infinity of points on a line is bigger than the infinity of counting numbers.

Some leading mathematicians of his day rejected his ideas. Today they are a basic part of maths. Cantor went on to show there is no largest infinity at all. For every infinity, there is a bigger one.

Infinity around us

You can glimpse infinity in two mirrors facing each other, which make an endless tunnel of reflections. Fractals, shapes like snowflakes and coastlines, show more detail the closer you zoom in, in theory forever.

Whether space itself goes on forever is still an open question. Astronomers can see only a part of the universe, containing hundreds of billions of galaxies, because light from further away has not had time to reach us. Beyond that edge, nobody knows if the universe ends or carries on without limit.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Infinity is the biggest number there is.

Truth: Infinity is not a number you can reach by counting. It is the idea of something without end. Mathematicians have also shown that there are different sizes of infinity, so there is no single "biggest" one.

Try this

Hold two hand mirrors facing each other, a little apart, with a small toy between them. Look over the top of one mirror. You will see a tunnel of toys getting smaller and smaller. Try counting them. The reflections seem endless, though in real life each one gets dimmer, because mirrors absorb a little light.
