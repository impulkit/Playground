What if Earth stopped spinning?

Right now, if you live in Bengaluru, you are whizzing east at about 1,600 km/h. If Earth stopped suddenly, the air wouldn't.

The short answer

If Earth stopped spinning in an instant, it would be a disaster. The ground would stop, but the air, the oceans and everything not tied down would keep moving east at the old speed. At the equator, that's about 1,670 km/h, faster than a passenger jet. Winds that strong would scrape the land bare.

If Earth slowed down gently over thousands of years, things would be stranger but survivable in places. A single "day" would last a whole year, the oceans would slide towards the poles, and a giant continent would appear around the equator. Neither is going to happen. Earth is slowing, but only by about 2 thousandths of a second each century.

Surprising but true

Earth bulges at the middle because it spins. If it stopped, the oceans would slide towards the poles and a giant new continent would rise around the equator.

You are moving faster than a jet

Earth turns once every 24 hours or so. The equator is about 40,000 km around, so the ground there moves at about 1,670 km/h.

The further from the equator you are, the shorter your circle, and the slower you move. Bengaluru rushes east at about 1,600 km/h, Delhi at about 1,470 km/h and New York at about 1,270 km/h. At the North Pole you simply turn on the spot.

We don't feel it because everything moves together: the ground, the air and you. It's like sitting on a smooth train where a cup of chai stays still on the table.

A sudden stop

If the solid Earth stopped instantly, your body would keep moving at that speed. So would buildings, trees and the atmosphere. Winds near the equator would be several times faster than the strongest tornado. Oceans would surge over coastlines as giant waves.

Scientists use this example to show why it can't happen. To stop Earth that fast would take an enormous force, more than anything in the solar system could deliver.

A slow stop: the new map

Because Earth spins, it bulges at the middle. The equator is about 21 km further from Earth's centre than the poles are. The oceans bulge too, held in place by the spin.

In 2010, a scientist at the mapping company Esri, Witold Fraczek, modelled what would happen without the spin. Gravity would pull the water towards the poles, forming two great polar oceans. Around the middle, the sea floor would rise out of the water to form one giant continent stretching right around the planet.

India would sit on that new land, joined to Africa and Southeast Asia by dry ground. The Himalaya would look even taller from the new low plains.

A year-long day

If Earth stopped turning but kept circling the Sun, each place would face the Sun for about six months, then face away for six months. One day and one night would together last a whole year.

The day side would bake for months and the night side would freeze. The air between them would rush from hot to cold, creating new wind patterns. Plants and animals adapted to 24-hour days would struggle. Time zones would stop making sense, since noon would last for weeks.

No more spinning storms

Earth's spin makes moving air curve, an effect called the Coriolis effect. It is why hurricanes and cyclones spin anticlockwise north of the equator and clockwise south of it.

Without spin, storms wouldn't twist into those great spirals. Ocean currents, which carry warmth around the world, would change too. No one knows exactly what the new weather would be like.

The magnet question

Earth's magnetic field comes from molten iron swirling in the outer core. Most scientists think the planet's rotation helps organise that swirling. Venus spins extremely slowly and has no strong magnetic field of its own.

Without a magnetic shield, more particles from the Sun would reach the upper air, which could slowly strip some of it away. That part is still debated. Earth will keep spinning for billions of years, gradually slowed by the Moon, a story that begins with the question of what if there were no Moon.

Myth or fact?

Myth: If Earth stopped spinning, we would all float off into space.

Truth: Gravity holds us down, not spin. In fact, spinning slightly reduces your weight at the equator, so without it you would weigh about 0.3 per cent more there.

Try this

Fill a bowl halfway with water and spin it gently on a table, then stop it suddenly. The water keeps sloshing around for a while after the bowl stops. Earth's air and oceans would do the same, on a far bigger scale.
