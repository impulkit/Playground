How big is China?

China is almost as wide as the USA, about 5,000 km from east to west, yet the whole country runs on one clock.

The short answer

China is huge. It covers about 9.6 million km², about three times the size of India and close to the size of the USA. Only Russia and Canada are clearly bigger, and China and the USA are so close in size that the ranking depends on how you count water and disputed land.

China also has an enormous population, about 1.4 billion people. For a long time it had more people than any other country. In 2023, the United Nations estimated that India had overtaken it.

Surprising but true

China's high-speed rail network is longer than those of all other countries put together.

Mountains, deserts and rivers

China's land steps down from west to east, like a giant staircase. In the south-west is the Tibetan Plateau, so high that it is called the roof of the world. Mount Everest sits on the border with Nepal. In the north-west lie the Taklamakan and Gobi deserts.

Two great rivers flow east across the country. The Yangtze, about 6,300 km long, is the longest river in Asia. The Yellow River gets its name from the yellow silt it carries. Most people live in the flatter, wetter east, where cities such as Shanghai and Beijing hold more than 2 crore people each.

One clock for everyone

China stretches across about 5,000 km, which would naturally cover five time zones. But since 1949, the whole country has used Beijing time. In the east, the Sun rises at a normal hour. In the far west, in Xinjiang, the Sun can rise after 9 am in winter, and people adjust their working hours.

India, which is smaller, also uses one time zone. The USA, by contrast, uses several time zones across the mainland.

Inventions and old roads

Chinese civilisation goes back thousands of years. Ancient China gave the world paper, printing, the compass and gunpowder. Silk was made there for centuries before anyone else learned the secret.

Who invented paper?The first people to make paper weren't humans. Wasps have been chewing wood into paper nests for millions of years.

Traders carried silk and other goods along the Silk Road, a network of routes linking China with Central Asia, India and Europe. Ideas travelled too. Buddhism came to China from India, and in the 600s the Chinese monk Xuanzang walked to India and studied at Nalanda. He took hundreds of texts home and spent years translating them.

The Great Wall

The Great Wall is not one wall but many walls built over about 2,000 years by different dynasties to protect against raids from the north. A survey published in 2012 found all its sections together run about 21,000 km. Much of what visitors see today was built during the Ming dynasty, 1368 to 1644.

Fast trains and big moves

Since 2008, China has built more high-speed railway than all other countries put together, over 40,000 km of track. Bullet trains link most large cities, running at up to about 350 km/h.

Every year around the Chinese New Year, also called the Spring Festival, hundreds of millions of people travel home to their families. Officials count several billion journeys over about 40 days, making it the largest yearly movement of people on Earth.

Pandas and languages

China is home to the giant panda, which lives in mountain bamboo forests in the south-west. Thanks to protection, its numbers have risen, and it is no longer listed as endangered.

Mandarin Chinese is spoken by more people as a first language than any other language. China also has many other languages and dialects, such as Cantonese, as well as 56 officially recognised ethnic groups.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Great Wall of China is the only human-made object you can see from space, even from the Moon.

Truth: From the Moon no human-made objects can be seen with the naked eye. From low orbit, the wall is very hard to spot because it is narrow and similar in colour to the land. China's first astronaut, Yang Liwei, said he could not see it.
