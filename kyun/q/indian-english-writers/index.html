Who are some famous Indian writers in English?

English arrived in India with traders and rulers. Indian writers took it, bent it, filled it with chai and monsoon, and made it their own.

The short answer

India has produced many famous writers in English. Some of the best known are R. K. Narayan, who wrote the Malgudi stories, and Ruskin Bond, who writes about the Himalayan hills. Salman Rushdie, Arundhati Roy, Vikram Seth and Amitav Ghosh are read across the world.

English came with the British, who ruled much of India for nearly two hundred years. At first it was the language of offices and courts. Then Indians began to write poems and novels in it. They brought in Indian rhythms, food, festivals and jokes. Today Indian English is one of the liveliest forms of the language anywhere.

Surprising but true

In 2018 Amitav Ghosh became the first writer in English to receive the Jnanpith Award, one of India's highest honours for literature.

The first voices

Toru Dutt, from a Bengali family in Kolkata, wrote poems in English and French in the 1870s, and died at only 21. Sarojini Naidu published poems in the early 1900s before becoming a freedom fighter. Rabindranath Tagore wrote mostly in Bengali, but his own English version of Gitanjali helped win him the Nobel Prize in 1913.

In the 1930s three novelists set the pattern. Mulk Raj Anand wrote Untouchable about a young sweeper. Raja Rao wrote Kanthapura about a village joining Gandhi's movement. And R. K. Narayan invented Malgudi. The English writer Graham Greene liked Narayan's first manuscript and helped him find a publisher in London in 1935.

Stories of everyday life

Narayan wrote in simple, gentle English about schoolboys, shopkeepers and astrologers. Swami and Friends (1935) follows a ten-year-old who hates arithmetic. Its stories later became the TV series Malgudi Days.

Ruskin Bond was born in Kasauli in 1934 and has lived in the hills near Mussoorie since the 1960s. He has written hundreds of stories, many for children, about trains, tigers and rain. His first novel, The Room on the Roof, was written when he was a teenager.

How did English spread around the world?About 1,500 years ago, English was spoken by a few groups of farmers in what is now England. Today it is the shared language of pilots, scientists and the internet.

The big novels

In 1981 Salman Rushdie's Midnight's Children won the Booker Prize. Its hero is born at the exact moment of India's independence, and his life is tangled with the country's. Vikram Seth's A Suitable Boy (1993) runs to about 1,350 pages and is one of the longest single novels in English. Arundhati Roy's The God of Small Things won the Booker in 1997.

Amitav Ghosh writes about the Sundarbans, the opium trade and climate change. In 2018 he received the Jnanpith Award, the first writer in English to do so.

Indian English on the page

What makes these books feel Indian is the voice. Characters say "cousin-brother", eat "tiffin" and "prepone" a meeting. Rushdie mixed Hindi and Urdu words straight into his sentences without italics or explanation. Arundhati Roy played with capital letters and made up words, the way a child might.

Some readers find this hard at first. Others say it is simply how English sounds on the streets of Mumbai, Chennai or Kolkata. The novelist Raja Rao wrote in 1938 that "one has to convey in a language that is not one's own the spirit that is one's own", and generations of writers have tried.

Writers across the oceans

Some writers grew up outside India. Jhumpa Lahiri, born in London to Bengali parents and raised in the USA, won the Pulitzer Prize in 2000 for Interpreter of Maladies. Her stories are about families who move between two countries, a feeling many in the Indian diaspora know well.

There are also bestselling storytellers. Sudha Murty's simple tales are loved by children and grandparents alike. Chetan Bhagat's novels about students sold in huge numbers, and several became Hindi films. Critics argue about which books are "literary", but readers decide for themselves.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Writing in English is not "real" Indian literature.

Truth: India has great literature in many languages, and English is now one of them. The Sahitya Akademi, India's national academy of letters, gives its awards in English as well as in 23 other languages.

Try this

Write one paragraph in English about your street, but use at least three words from another language you know, like dabba, jugaad or yaar, without explaining them. Ask a friend if they could still follow it. Many Indian writers do exactly this.
