How did India win independence?

India's Republic Day is 26 January because on that date in 1930, Indian leaders publicly promised to win complete freedom.

The short answer

India won independence on 15 August 1947 after a long struggle against British rule that lasted around 90 years, from the revolt of 1857 onwards.

It took many forms. Early leaders wrote petitions and gave speeches. Later, Mahatma Gandhi led huge campaigns of non-violent protest, in which millions refused to obey unfair laws. Revolutionaries such as Bhagat Singh took up arms. Subhas Chandra Bose raised an army abroad. Workers struck, students left colleges and women joined protests in great numbers. After the Second World War, a weakened Britain agreed to leave. But independence came with the painful Partition of British India into India and Pakistan.

Surprising but true

The Indian National Congress, which led much of the freedom movement, was founded in 1885 with help from a retired British official, Allan Octavian Hume.

A congress of educated Indians

In 1885, a group of lawyers, teachers and journalists met in Bombay and formed the Indian National Congress. At first, they asked politely for more Indians in government and fairer taxes.

Some leaders wanted to push harder. Bal Gangadhar Tilak is remembered for declaring that swaraj, self-rule, was his birthright. When the British divided Bengal in 1905, people answered with the Swadeshi movement, boycotting British goods. Rabindranath Tagore wrote songs for the protests.

Gandhi and the mass movements

Gandhi returned from South Africa in 1915. In 1919, British troops fired on an unarmed crowd at Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar, killing hundreds of people. The massacre shocked India. Rabindranath Tagore gave up the knighthood the British had given him in protest, and many Indians who had trusted British fairness now turned against British rule.

Gandhi then led the Non-Cooperation Movement of 1920–22, asking Indians to leave British schools, courts and jobs, and to spin their own khadi cloth. On 26 January 1930, the Congress declared that its goal was purna swaraj, complete independence. That same year, Gandhi's Salt March drew the eyes of the world.

Many roads to freedom

Not everyone followed Gandhi's methods. Young revolutionaries believed only force would drive out the British. Bhagat Singh and his comrades were hanged in 1931 and became heroes to millions.

B.R. Ambedkar fought for the rights of Dalits, people treated as "untouchable", and argued that freedom from Britain meant little without freedom from caste. Muslim leaders in the Muslim League, led by Muhammad Ali Jinnah, increasingly demanded a separate country. Women such as Sarojini Naidu and Aruna Asaf Ali led protests and went to jail.

Quit India and the war years

During the Second World War, Britain brought India into the war without asking Indian leaders. On 8 August 1942, the Congress launched the Quit India Movement. Its leaders were arrested within hours, but protests spread across the country.

Meanwhile, Subhas Chandra Bose led the Indian National Army, fighting alongside Japan against the British. When INA officers were put on trial in Delhi in 1945, there were huge public protests. In 1946, sailors of the Royal Indian Navy mutinied in Bombay.

Freedom and Partition

By 1946, Britain was exhausted by war and could no longer hold India. Talks between the Congress, the Muslim League and the British failed to agree on a united country.

On 15 August 1947, India became independent, and Pakistan was created a day earlier. Partition forced around 1.5 crore people to flee their homes, and hundreds of thousands were killed in riots. On 26 January 1950, India adopted its Constitution and became a republic, exactly twenty years after the promise of complete freedom.

Myth or fact?

Myth: India won independence through the work of only one or two leaders.

Truth: Millions of people took part, including farmers, workers, students and women who marched, went to jail or gave up their jobs. Leaders with very different ideas, from Gandhi and Nehru to Bose, Ambedkar and Patel, all shaped the result.
