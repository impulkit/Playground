What is the difference between turtles and tortoises?

Every tortoise is a turtle, but not every turtle is a tortoise. One tortoise, Jonathan, has been plodding around an island garden since the 1830s.

The short answer

"Turtle" is the name for the whole group of reptiles with a bony shell. Tortoises are one branch of that family: the ones that live only on land.

You can usually tell them apart by their feet. Tortoises have thick, stumpy legs, like a tiny elephant's, made for walking on dry ground. Their shells are often high and domed. Sea turtles have long flippers for swimming. Pond and river turtles have webbed feet with claws, good for both paddling and climbing onto logs.

There are about 360 kinds of turtle in all. They live in deserts, rivers, forests and every warm ocean.

Surprising but true

Jonathan, a giant tortoise on the island of St Helena, is thought to have hatched around 1832, making him the oldest known living land animal (2026).

The words can be confusing

The names change depending on where you live. In the USA, people often call almost any shelled reptile a turtle. In Britain and India, "turtle" usually means a water species, and "tortoise" a land one. "Terrapin" is used for some small turtles that live in fresh or slightly salty water.

Scientists avoid the muddle by using one group name, Testudines, for them all. Whatever you call them, they share the same basic body plan, and it has hardly changed in more than 200 million years. That makes turtles a kind of living fossil.

A house made of bone

A turtle's shell isn't a separate case. The top part, called the carapace, grows from the turtle's ribs and backbone, which are fused to it. The bottom part is called the plastron. Over the bone lie plates of keratin, the material in your nails.

So a turtle can't climb out of its shell. Many can pull their head and legs inside when danger comes. Some side-necked turtles fold their necks sideways instead. Sea turtles can't hide their heads at all. Box turtles can shut their shells almost completely, thanks to a hinge on the underside.

Slow and steady

Tortoises are mostly plant-eaters, munching grass, leaves, flowers and fruit. They move slowly, burn little energy and can live a very long time. Most lay their eggs in holes dug in the ground, and the babies must dig their own way out.

Giant tortoises on the Galápagos Islands and on Aldabra, in the Indian Ocean, can weigh more than 200 kg. Jonathan, a giant tortoise on the island of St Helena in the South Atlantic, is thought to have hatched around 1832. In 2026 he was about 194 years old, the oldest known living land animal.

India's best-known land species is the Indian star tortoise, with yellow star patterns on its shell. Sadly, it is often caught and sold illegally as a pet.

How do baby turtles find the sea?They hatch at night, alone, on a beach. Nobody shows them the way.

Ocean travellers

Sea turtles spend almost their whole lives at sea. Females come ashore only to dig a nest and lay eggs, often on the same beach where they hatched.

In Odisha, Olive Ridley turtles gather in huge numbers to nest at beaches such as Gahirmatha and Rushikulya. This mass nesting is called an arribada, Spanish for "arrival". In a good year, lakhs of turtles come ashore over a few nights.

The biggest of all is the leatherback, which has a rubbery shell instead of hard plates and can weigh more than 500 kg.

In stories and in danger

Turtles turn up in stories across the world. There's Aesop's fable of the tortoise and the hare, and in Hindu tradition, Vishnu's Kurma avatar is a tortoise who holds up a mountain on his back.

Real turtles face real threats. Many species are endangered because of hunting, the pet trade, fishing nets, plastic and the loss of nesting beaches. Turtles grow slowly and may take decades to start breeding, so populations take a long time to recover once they fall.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A turtle can crawl out of its shell, like a snail or a hermit crab.

Truth: A turtle's shell is part of its skeleton, fused to its ribs and spine. It can never leave it, and it grows bigger as the turtle grows.
