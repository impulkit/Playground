How old are Kannada and Telugu?

Carved on stones more than 1,400 years ago, Kannada and Telugu still look like cousins today, with round, looping letters that seem to be smiling.

The short answer

Kannada and Telugu are both well over a thousand years old as written languages, and the spoken languages are older still. The oldest known Kannada inscription, the Halmidi stone in Karnataka, is usually dated to about 450 CE, though some experts think it is a little later. The earliest Telugu inscriptions come from around 575 CE.

Both belong to the Dravidian family of languages, alongside Tamil and Malayalam. Today about 8 crore people speak Telugu and over 4 crore speak Kannada as their first language, according to the 2011 Census. In 2008, the Government of India declared both to be classical languages, a title for languages with a long, rich and independent history.

Surprising but true

Kannada and Telugu scripts were once a single script, and they only split into two separate alphabets around 700 years ago.

Cousins on a family tree

Linguists think the Dravidian languages grew from one shared ancestor, often called Proto-Dravidian, spoken thousands of years ago. Over many centuries, groups of speakers drifted apart and their speech changed in different ways, until they became separate languages.

Kannada and Telugu still share many words. "Water" is neeru in Kannada and neellu in Telugu. Both also borrowed heavily from Sanskrit, especially for religious, scientific and literary words, which is one reason some people wrongly think they come from Sanskrit.

The two languages have their own dialects too. Kannada spoken in Mangaluru sounds different from Kannada spoken in Dharwad, and Telugu in Hyderabad differs from Telugu along the coast.

One script that split in two

If you put Kannada and Telugu writing side by side, they look strikingly alike. That is because they grew from the same script, sometimes called the Telugu-Kannada script, which itself came from the ancient Brahmi script used in Emperor Ashoka's time.

For centuries, kingdoms such as the Chalukyas wrote both languages in this shared script. Around the 13th and 14th centuries, the two began to drift apart into separate forms. Each is an abugida: every consonant has a built-in vowel, changed by adding small marks. The rounded shapes probably developed because early writers scratched letters onto palm leaves, where straight lines could split the leaf along its grain.

Poets, kings and songs

The oldest surviving book in Kannada is the Kavirajamarga, a guide to poetry written around 850 CE at the court of the Rashtrakuta king Amoghavarsha I. In the 12th century, Basavanna and other saints wrote vachanas, short sayings in everyday Kannada that people still quote.

In Telugu, the poet Nannaya began translating the Mahabharata in the 11th century, and he is often called the Adi Kavi, the first poet. Krishnadevaraya, the great emperor of Vijayanagara in the early 1500s, wrote poetry in Telugu himself.

Telugu also became a language of music. Tyagaraja, one of the most famous composers of Carnatic music, wrote most of his songs in Telugu in the 18th and 19th centuries. Because so many Telugu words end in vowels, they flow easily when sung. Some people call Telugu "the Italian of the East" for this reason, a phrase usually credited to a European traveller of the 1400s.

Still growing

Kannada and Telugu are not museum pieces. They have huge film industries, newspapers, websites and songs. Kannada writers have won the Jnanpith, India's highest literary award, many times. The poet Kuvempu wrote Karnataka's state song, and the Telugu writer Viswanatha Satyanarayana was the first Telugu writer to receive the Jnanpith. Telugu is also one of the fastest-growing languages in the USA, where many families from Andhra Pradesh and Telangana have settled.

Myth or fact?

Myth: All the languages of South India come from Sanskrit.

Truth: Kannada, Telugu, Tamil and Malayalam belong to the Dravidian family, which is separate from Sanskrit's family. They borrowed many Sanskrit words, but their roots and grammar are their own.
