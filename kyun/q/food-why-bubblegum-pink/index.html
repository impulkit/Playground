Why is bubblegum pink?

In 1928, an accountant in Philadelphia made the first good bubblegum, and coloured it pink because pink was the only food colouring he had.

The short answer

Bubblegum is pink mostly because of an accident of history. In 1928, a young accountant named Walter Diemer was working at the Fleer chewing gum company in Philadelphia. In his spare time, he experimented with gum recipes, trying to make one that would stretch into bubbles without sticking to everything.

When he finally got a batch that worked, he needed to colour it. According to Diemer, the only food colouring in the factory was pink. So pink it was.

His gum, called Dubble Bubble, sold out fast. Other companies copied the recipe and the colour. Within a few years, "bubblegum pink" was a colour of its own.

Surprising but true

Singapore banned the sale of chewing gum in 1992, partly because people were sticking it on train doors.

The quest for the perfect bubble

Gum that blows bubbles needs to be stretchy but not too sticky. Frank Fleer, who started the company, had tried before, in 1906. His gum, Blibber-Blubber, stretched but stuck to faces and was hard to wash off.

Diemer tried batch after batch. He later said he wasn't a chemist and had no idea what he was doing. Then one day in 1928, a batch came out right: stretchy, strong and much less sticky.

The company sold Dubble Bubble for a cent a piece. Diemer is said to have taught shop staff how to blow bubbles, so they could show children. He never got rich from it, but he was proud of the gum for the rest of his life. Pink stayed the classic colour even after other brands tried green, blue and purple.

What gum is made of

Early chewing gum was made from chicle, a milky sap from the sapodilla tree of Mexico and Central America. The Maya and Aztecs chewed chicle more than a thousand years ago.

Today most gum base is made from synthetic polymers. These are long, chain-like molecules, similar to those in some plastics and rubbers. One of them, polyisobutylene, is also used in the inner tubes of tyres. It sounds alarming, but food-grade versions are tested for safety.

The base is mixed with sugar or sweeteners, softeners and flavours, a recipe unpacked in what chewing gum is made of. Gum makers test how long the flavour lasts and how the gum feels after an hour of chewing.

What flavour is "bubblegum"?

Bubblegum flavour is not one fruit. It is a mix, and each company keeps its recipe secret. It often includes fruity notes of banana, strawberry, cherry or pineapple, sometimes with a hint of wintergreen or cinnamon.

The colour may help our brains taste it. Many people link pink with sweetness and fruit, a connection explored in what colours mean.

The pink in modern gum comes from food colourings. In January 2025, the US Food and Drug Administration banned one of them, called Red No. 3, from food, giving companies until 2027 to remove it. Many gum makers use other colours instead.

The country that banned gum

People have chewed things for a long time. Archaeologists in Denmark found a lump of chewed birch tar about 5,700 years old, with tooth marks and DNA from the person who chewed it.

But gum can be a nuisance. Old gum sticks to pavements and is costly to scrape off. In 1992, Singapore banned the sale of chewing gum, partly after people stuck it on the doors of its new metro trains, stopping them from closing. Since 2004, gum that helps people quit smoking or protects teeth can be sold there by pharmacists.

And the strangest part? The rubbery stretch that lets gum blow bubbles comes from the same family of materials as car tyres, which got their strength from a lucky kitchen accident in the 1800s.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Swallowed chewing gum stays in your stomach for seven years.

Truth: Your body can't digest gum base, but it doesn't stay inside you. It passes through your gut and leaves in a few days, like other things you can't digest. Swallowing a lot at once is still a bad idea.

Try this

Chew a piece of sugar-free gum until it is soft, then try blowing a bubble. Notice how thin it has to stretch before it pops. Then ask an adult to compare it with an ordinary rubber band. Both are stretchy polymers.
