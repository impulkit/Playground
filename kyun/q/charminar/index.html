What is the Charminar?

In 1591 a young sultan built a giant gateway of four towers, and then built the city of Hyderabad around it.

The short answer

The Charminar is a huge stone monument in the old city of Hyderabad, in Telangana. Its name means "four minarets", after the tall towers that rise from its four corners. Sultan Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah built it in 1591.

The building is a square, about 20 m on each side, with a giant arch opening onto each of four streets. It stands about 56 m tall, roughly as high as an 18-storey block of flats. On the top floor is a small mosque. Four hundred years later, it is the picture most people think of when they hear the word Hyderabad.

Surprising but true

There is a small mosque on the top floor of the Charminar, facing west towards Mecca.

A new city for a new capital

The Qutb Shahi kings first ruled from Golconda, a fort on a granite hill. By the late 1500s Golconda was crowded and short of water. Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, the fifth sultan, decided to build a new city across the river Musi.

The Charminar was its centrepiece. Four wide roads ran out from its arches towards the four directions, with markets, palaces and gardens laid out along them. In a sense, the city grew outwards from this one building. Muhammad Quli was also a poet who wrote in Dakhni, Telugu and Persian. He ruled for more than 30 years, from 1580 to 1612, and many buildings in old Hyderabad date from his reign. He is often said to have prayed for his new city to be filled with people like a river is filled with fish.

Why was it built?

That question has more than one answer. A popular story says a deadly plague swept the region, and the sultan promised to build a mosque where he prayed for it to end. The Charminar, the story goes, marks that spot.

Some historians think the plague story came later. They point out that a grand gateway at the heart of a planned city makes sense on its own. It may have marked the city's centre and the start of a new millennium in the Islamic calendar, which began around this time. Nobody has found a document from 1591 that settles it.

What is Hyderabad famous for?Some of the world's most famous diamonds passed through the markets of Golconda, near Hyderabad. Today the city is just as well known for biryani and software.

How it was made

The Charminar is built from granite and limestone, held together with mortar that contained crushed marble. Its walls were covered in lime plaster and decorated with flowers, arches and balconies. Inside each minaret, a narrow staircase of about 149 steps winds up to the top.

The four arches are each about 11 m wide. Above them, two floors of smaller arches run around all four sides. The design mixes Persian ideas with the craftsmanship of the Deccan. Like the Qutub Minar in Delhi, the tall towers were meant to be seen from far away.

In 1889, clocks were added on all four sides. They still keep the time for the streets below.

The streets around it

The Charminar has never been a quiet museum. Lanes packed with shops crowd up to its base. Laad Bazaar, just to the west, has sold glittering lac bangles for centuries, the kind of jewellery brides still buy there. Pearl sellers line the nearby roads, a reminder that Hyderabad was once a great trading centre for pearls.

Next door stands the Mecca Masjid, one of the largest mosques in India, begun by a later Qutb Shahi king. In the evenings, especially during Ramzan, the area fills with stalls selling haleem and biryani.

The Charminar has been worn down by traffic smoke, rain and time. The Archaeological Survey of India looks after it, and restorers have repaired its plaster and minarets several times. Cars are now kept further from its base than they once were, to protect the old stone.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A secret tunnel runs from the Charminar to Golconda Fort, about 8 km away.

Truth: People have told this story for centuries, but no such tunnel has ever been found. Golconda does have clever acoustics and water channels, which may have helped the legend grow.
