Why is Chennai called the gateway to South India?

Chennai began as a small fort on an empty beach in 1640. Today it is one of the busiest ports in India and the start of most journeys south.

The short answer

Chennai, once called Madras, is the capital of Tamil Nadu and the largest city on India's south-east coast. It got its nickname because for centuries it was where people and goods entered South India. Ships docked at its harbour, the main railway lines south began there, and later its airport linked the region to the world.

About 1 crore people live in the wider city today. It is famous for its long beach, its ancient temples, its car factories, its music and its films.

Surprising but true

Chennai gets most of its rain from October to December, after the monsoon has left most of India.

A fort on the beach

In 1639, two officers of the East India Company, Francis Day and Andrew Cogan, got permission from a local ruler to build on a strip of sand. In 1640 they began building Fort St George, the first major English fort in India, and finished it a few years later. A town called George Town grew up beside it, near older Tamil villages such as Mylapore and Triplicane.

Mylapore was already ancient. Its Kapaleeshwarar Temple, dedicated to Shiva, has a tall gopuram, a gateway tower covered in hundreds of brightly painted figures. Today Fort St George houses Tamil Nadu's government and a museum.

A gateway by sea, road and rail

Chennai's port became one of the busiest on the Bay of Bengal. Railways spread out from the city in the 1800s, and the routes to Bengaluru, Madurai and Kerala all start here. Today big car makers have factories nearby, and so many vehicles are made in the region that it is often called the Detroit of India, after the American car city.

Further down the coast, about 55 km away, lies Mahabalipuram, with rock-cut temples carved by the Pallava kings around 1,300 years ago. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Music in December

Every December the city hosts the Music Season, one of the biggest festivals of classical music anywhere. Hundreds of concerts of Carnatic music and dance take place in halls called sabhas, many at low prices. The Madras Music Academy has organised a December season since 1927. Fans hurry between concerts, often stopping for filter coffee and a hot dosa in the canteens.

Chennai is also home to the Tamil film industry, sometimes called Kollywood after the neighbourhood of Kodambakkam. South Indian cinema now has fans across the world.

Why is Tamil called one of the world's oldest living languages?A Tamil schoolchild today can read lines of poetry composed about 2,000 years ago. Few languages on Earth can say that.

Coffee, kolams and Tamil pride

Walk through an old Chennai street at dawn and you will see kolams, patterns drawn in rice flour on doorsteps. Many families draw a fresh one every morning. Filter coffee, made by dripping hot water through ground coffee and mixing it with frothy milk, is poured between two steel cups to cool it. Tamil, the language of the city, is one of the oldest languages still spoken, with poems written more than 2,000 years ago. Signboards, films and politics in Chennai all show deep pride in it.

Living with the sea

Marina Beach stretches for several kilometres along the city's edge and fills with families every evening. On 26 December 2004, a tsunami caused by an earthquake near Indonesia struck the coast. Waves swept across the beach and many people lost their lives along the Tamil Nadu coast. Warning systems have since been built across the Indian Ocean.

Too much water, then too little

Chennai has no big river feeding it all year, so it depends on lakes and rain. In 2015 heavy rain flooded large parts of the city. In 2019 the main reservoirs nearly dried up and water had to be brought in by train. The city now recycles more water and takes salt out of seawater in desalination plants, trying to be ready for both extremes.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The monsoon brings rain to every part of India in the same months.

Truth: Most of India gets its main rain from the southwest monsoon between June and September. Tamil Nadu's coast, including Chennai, gets most of its rain from the northeast monsoon between October and December.
