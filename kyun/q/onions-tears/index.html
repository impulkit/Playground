Why do onions make you cry?

When you slice an onion, you set off a chemical alarm that the plant evolved to protect itself, and your eyes are caught in the blast.

The short answer

An onion's cells hold sulphur-containing chemicals and, in separate compartments, enzymes that can change them. When a knife breaks the cells, the two mix. In a few seconds, a chain of reactions makes a gas called syn-propanethial-S-oxide.

The gas drifts up from the chopping board into the air. When it reaches your eyes, it stimulates nerves on their surface, which sense it as an irritant. Your brain responds the way it does to dust or smoke: it tells your tear glands to flush the eyes clean. So you cry, even though you aren't sad at all.

Surprising but true

The enzyme that makes the tear gas in onions was only discovered in 2002, by scientists in Japan.

An onion's defence

Onions didn't evolve to make cooks cry. The tear gas is part of a defence system. An animal or insect that bites into a raw onion gets a sharp, stinging mouthful, and many leave the rest alone.

The chemicals stay harmless as long as the cells are whole. That's why a whole onion on a shelf doesn't make your eyes water. Only damage starts the reaction, in the same way that cutting an apple starts its browning, as you can read in why apples turn brown.

The tear enzyme

For a long time, scientists thought one enzyme made all the onion's sharp chemicals. In 2002, a team of Japanese researchers at a food company found a second enzyme that makes the tear gas specifically. They named it lachrymatory factor synthase, from the Latin word for tears.

The discovery opened the way to tearless onions. Plant breeders have since developed onions that make much less of the tear gas, and some are now sold in shops in the USA. Others prefer the traditional sharp kind for its strong flavour. Garlic, leeks and chives belong to the same plant family and use similar chemistry, but garlic makes far less of the tear gas, which is why peeling it doesn't make you cry.

Tricks that work

Since the gas travels through the air, anything that keeps it away from your eyes helps. Cutting near an open window or under a kitchen fan blows it away. Goggles block it completely, though you may look odd in the kitchen.

Chilling an onion for half an hour slows the reactions. A sharp knife slices through cells cleanly instead of crushing them. Cutting the root end last helps too, because chemicals are more concentrated there. Your blinking and tears are doing their job, protecting the delicate surface of your eyes.

From sharp to sweet

When onions are cooked slowly in oil, the sharp chemicals break down or escape as vapour, and the onions turn soft, golden and sweet. Heat also breaks long chains of sugar in the onion into simpler, sweeter ones, which then brown. This is why the first step in so many Indian dishes is frying onions until they are golden, as you can read in what cooking does.

Onions in history

People have grown onions for at least 5,000 years. Ancient Egyptians placed onions in tombs, and the mummy of the pharaoh Ramesses IV, who died about 3,100 years ago, was found with small onions placed in his eye sockets.

How were mummies made?Egyptian embalmers pulled out the brain through the nose with a hook, but left the heart exactly where it was.

India and China grow far more onions than any other country, and in 2024 India grew the most. Lasalgaon, in Nashik district of Maharashtra, has the largest onion market in Asia. Onions are eaten so widely in India that when bad weather ruins the crop and prices jump, it becomes front-page news.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Holding bread or a matchstick in your mouth stops onion tears.

Truth: There's no scientific evidence for this. The gas reaches your eyes through the air. Chilling the onion, using a sharp knife and cutting under a fan or near an open window do help.

Try this

The next time an adult is cutting onions, ask them to cut one onion straight from the fridge and another at room temperature, each with the same sharp knife. Ask which one made their eyes sting more, and time how long before the tears start.
