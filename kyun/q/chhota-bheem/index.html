How did Chhota Bheem become popular?

A small Hyderabad studio put a laddoo-loving boy in an Indian village and created one of India's most popular cartoons.

The short answer

Chhota Bheem became popular because it felt Indian in a way most cartoons on TV did not. Its hero is a brave, strong boy in a village kingdom called Dholakpur. He eats laddoos, celebrates Indian festivals, and protects his friends from bullies, bandits and monsters.

The show started on the children's channel Pogo in 2008. It was made by Green Gold Animation in Hyderabad, a studio founded by Rajiv Chilaka. Simple stories, catchy songs, free TV and cheap toys and school bags helped it spread to millions of homes, from big cities to small towns.

Surprising but true

Before Chhota Bheem, most Indian animation studios earned their money drawing cartoons for foreign companies, not making their own characters.

A hero borrowed from an epic

The name comes from Bhima, one of the five Pandava brothers in the Mahabharata. In the epic, Bhima is famously strong and loves to eat. "Chhota" means small, so the name means "Little Bheem".

What is the Mahabharata?It has about 100,000 verses, making it one of the longest poems ever composed. Hidden inside it is the Bhagavad Gita.

Beyond the name and the strength, the cartoon tells its own stories. Bheem lives with ordinary village children, not princes. His adventures are about helping people, outsmarting villains and winning contests, not about the great war of the epic.

Meet the gang

Bheem's best friend is Chutki, a clever girl whose mother makes the laddoos. There's Raju, a small boy who wants to be brave like Bheem, and Jaggu, a talking monkey. Kalia is the bully, always trying to beat Bheem, with his two sidekicks, the twins Dholu and Bholu.

The kingdom is ruled by Raja Indravarma, and his daughter, Princess Indumati, is one of Bheem's friends. The characters are simple and easy to tell apart, which helps very young children follow along. Each has one clear trait: Bheem is brave, Chutki is smart, Raju is eager, and Kalia is jealous. Episodes are short, usually around ten minutes, with a problem, a chase and a happy ending.

Why Indian children loved it

Before Chhota Bheem, most cartoons on Indian TV came from the USA or Japan. They were fun, but set in faraway cities with unfamiliar food and festivals. Chhota Bheem showed village fairs, Diwali, Holi, and plates of Indian sweets.

The show was dubbed into several Indian languages, so children could watch in Tamil, Telugu and others as well as Hindi. Pogo was part of the basic cable package in many homes, so families didn't need to pay extra to watch.

From a small studio to a big brand

Rajiv Chilaka founded Green Gold Animation in Hyderabad in 2001. For years the company struggled. Like many Indian studios, it did animation work for others while trying to sell its own ideas.

Once Chhota Bheem took off, the characters appeared everywhere: on lunchboxes, T-shirts, toys and books. Several feature films came out in cinemas. Studios across India took note that a home-grown character could earn money, and more Indian cartoons followed. Green Gold went on to create other series, and Bheem himself starred in longer TV specials and adventures set far beyond Dholakpur.

Mighty Little Bheem goes global

In 2019, Netflix released Mighty Little Bheem, a spin-off about Bheem as a toddler. It has almost no dialogue, so children anywhere can follow the jokes. The baby Bheem crawls around causing chaos with his superhuman strength.

Because it needs no translation, the show found fans in many countries. A cartoon born in Hyderabad, about a boy from an imaginary Indian village, was being watched by toddlers in many countries outside India. Few Indian cartoons had travelled so far.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Chhota Bheem's stories come straight from the Mahabharata.

Truth: Only the name and the great strength are borrowed from Bhima, the strong Pandava brother. The cartoon's characters, village and adventures are new stories invented for the show.
