What does the Red Cross do?

In 1859, a Swiss businessman saw thousands of wounded soldiers left to die after a battle in Italy. What he did next became the Red Cross.

The short answer

The Red Cross is a worldwide movement that helps people in wars, disasters and other emergencies. It gives first aid, food, water, shelter and medical care, and it helps separated families find each other. It also visits prisoners of war and reminds armies of the rules of war.

It has three parts. The International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC), based in Geneva, works mainly in wars. There are 191 national societies (2024), such as the Indian Red Cross Society and the American Red Cross, which help people in their own countries. And the International Federation links those societies together.

Surprising but true

The International Committee of the Red Cross has won the Nobel Peace Prize three times, in 1917, 1944 and 1963, and its founder Henry Dunant shared the very first one in 1901.

A battle and a book

On 24 June 1859, armies of France and Austria fought near the village of Solferino in northern Italy. Within a day, around 40,000 soldiers were dead or wounded. A Swiss businessman named Henry Dunant happened to arrive. He found wounded men lying in fields and churches with almost no one to care for them.

Dunant organised local villagers, mostly women, to bring water, food and bandages. They helped soldiers from both sides, saying "tutti fratelli", "all are brothers". Back in Geneva, he wrote a book, A Memory of Solferino, published in 1862. It asked two questions. Could every country train volunteers to help the wounded in war? And could countries agree to protect those helpers?

The rules of war

In 1863, Dunant and four other men in Geneva set up a committee to answer those questions. It became the ICRC. The next year, 12 countries signed the first Geneva Convention. It said wounded soldiers must be cared for, whatever side they were on, and that medical workers wearing the red cross must not be attacked.

More conventions followed, the latest in 1949, protecting prisoners of war and civilians. Today every country in the world has signed the 1949 Geneva Conventions. They are a big part of international human rights law.

Three symbols, one meaning

The red cross was chosen in honour of Switzerland. But in the 1870s, during a war between Russia and the Ottoman Empire, Ottoman forces used a red crescent instead, saying the cross offended Muslim soldiers. The red crescent was officially recognised in 1929. In 2005, a third emblem, the red crystal, was added for countries that did not want to use either.

All three mean the same thing: do not attack, these people are here to help.

In India and the US

The Indian Red Cross Society was set up by an act of Parliament in 1920. Its national headquarters is in New Delhi, with branches across the states. It runs blood banks, trains people in first aid and helps after floods, cyclones and earthquakes. Many Indian schools have Junior Red Cross groups.

The American Red Cross was founded in 1881 by Clara Barton, a nurse who had cared for soldiers in the US Civil War. Today it collects a large share of the donated blood used in American hospitals, which is why many people know it best for blood donation.

Neutral, even when it's hard

The ICRC follows strict principles. It is neutral, meaning it doesn't take sides in a war. It is impartial, helping people based only on need. And it is independent of governments.

That neutrality lets its workers cross front lines and visit prisons that others can't enter. It also brings criticism. During the Second World War, the ICRC knew about the Nazi camps but did not speak out publicly, a decision it has since called a failure. Henry Dunant, meanwhile, went bankrupt and was forgotten for years. A journalist found him living in a Swiss care home, and in 1901 he shared the first Nobel Peace Prize.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The red cross is a Christian symbol.

Truth: It was chosen as the reverse of the Swiss flag, not for religious reasons. Even so, some countries saw it as Christian, which is why the red crescent and later the red crystal were added as equal emblems.
