Why do AI chatbots find some languages harder than others?

Ask a chatbot something in English and it answers smoothly. Ask the same thing in Santali or Konkani, and it may stumble. The reason is how much it has read.

The short answer

An AI chatbot learns a language by reading enormous amounts of text in it. The more it reads, the better it gets.

Some languages, like English, have billions of pages online. Others, including many of India's languages, have far less writing on the internet. With less to read, the AI learns less, so it makes more mistakes.

Surprising but true

Some AI models are small enough to run on a mobile phone with no internet connection at all.

Learning by reading

Chatbots are built from large language models, which are made of neural networks, layers of maths loosely inspired by brain cells.

The text is chopped into small pieces called tokens, which can be whole words or bits of words. During training, an algorithm keeps adjusting tiny settings called parameters, to make better guesses about the next token. Big models have billions of parameters and read trillions of tokens.

How do AI chatbots learn language?An AI chatbot learns language by playing one game billions of times, guessing the next word in a sentence.

Why some languages get left behind

Roughly half of all web pages are in English. A language spoken by crores of people, like Bhojpuri or Maithili, may have only a tiny share. Some languages are mostly spoken, not written, so there is even less text.

Scripts add another puzzle. Indian languages use many different scripts, from Devanagari to Tamil to Ol Chiki. A model trained mostly on English may chop these words into many tiny tokens, which makes it slower and less accurate.

Teaching AI more languages

To help, researchers collect and record speech and writing in more languages, from books, radio, government documents and volunteers. They also train models built specially for these languages.

In 2026, India released EKA-1, a model that works in all 22 languages in its Constitution plus English, along with two smaller models that can run without the internet. Models like these could help a farmer ask questions in Marathi, or improve translation apps for millions. Experts also say every AI must be tested carefully, because AI safety matters as these tools spread.

Myth or fact?

Myth: An AI understands words the way a person does.

Truth: A language AI learns patterns. It predicts which word is most likely to come next, based on everything it has read. It can be very useful, but it can also make confident mistakes.

Try this

Ask someone at home to say the same simple sentence, like "Where is the water?", in every language they know. Notice which words look or sound alike. An AI learns patterns like these, but from billions of sentences.
