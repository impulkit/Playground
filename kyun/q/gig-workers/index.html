What safety net do gig workers like delivery riders get?

The rider who brings your food in 20 minutes may have no sick pay, no pension and no boss in an office. Countries are now working out how to protect people who work through apps.

The short answer

A gig worker is someone paid for each task, like each delivery, each cab ride or each repair, instead of getting a fixed monthly salary. When they find that work through an app, they are also called platform workers.

Most gig workers have no paid leave, no pension and no health cover from a company. India's law now recognises them, and in 2026 the government set up a national board to plan schemes like insurance and pensions for them.

Surprising but true

The word "gig" comes from jazz musicians in the 1920s, who used it for a single paid show. Today it means any one-off job, like one delivery or one cab ride.

How it works

When you order food, an app matches your order with a nearby rider. A computer program, called an algorithm, decides who gets which job, how much it pays and sometimes how fast it must be done. The rider is paid for each delivery, plus tips or bonuses.

Ratings matter a lot. After each job, customers give stars. A rider with low ratings, or who turns down too many orders, may get fewer jobs or even be blocked from the app. Because there is no manager to talk to, it can be hard to find out why, or to ask for a second chance.

Apps usually call riders "partners", not employees. That means the app does not have to give them the things a regular employer gives, such as a provident fund, sick days or a fixed minimum wage.

Think of a cricket club that pays a player only for each run scored. If the player is injured, or it rains all season, they earn nothing. A safety net is like a fund that still helps them in bad times.

India's Code on Social Security asks app companies to pay 1% to 2% of their yearly turnover into a special fund. The money is meant for things like accident insurance, health cover, maternity help and old-age support. Workers register on a government website called e-Shram to be counted.

What is a minimum wage?When the USA first set a national minimum wage in 1938, it was 25 cents an hour. Economists still argue about what such laws really do.

Where it came from

The idea of social security is about 140 years old. In the 1880s, Germany started the first national insurance for workers who fell sick, got hurt or grew old. Many countries copied it. India started insurance for factory workers in 1948 and a provident fund in 1952.

Those systems were built for people with one employer and a monthly payslip. Then came smartphones. From the 2010s, apps for rides, food, groceries and home services grew fast, and millions of people began working for them.

Courts and governments around the world had to catch up. In 2021, the UK's top court ruled that Uber drivers should be treated as "workers", with rights to a minimum wage and paid holidays. In 2024, the European Union agreed new rules for platform work.

India's story

In 2020, India's Code on Social Security became the first national law to name gig and platform workers. It came into force in November 2025. In 2023, Rajasthan passed India's first state law just for gig workers, and other states followed.

In 2025, the government also said gig workers could get free hospital treatment cover under Ayushman Bharat, the national health scheme. In October 2026, it set up a National Social Security Board, chaired by the Labour Minister, with members from governments, app companies and workers' groups.

India's gig workers are often young men who have moved to cities for work, though more women are joining, especially in beauty and home services. Many moved from villages hoping for steady income. Delivery riders often spend part of their earnings on fuel, phone data and bike repairs, so what they take home can be much less than what the app pays.

The numbers that matter

  • - About 1 crore: gig workers in India in 2024-25. That is more than the population of many countries.
  • - 2.35 crore: the number expected by 2029-30, according to the government's planning body, NITI Aayog.
  • - 1% to 2%: the share of yearly turnover app companies must put into the workers' fund, capped at 5% of what they pay workers.
  • - About 30 crore: workers who have signed up on e-Shram, the government's list of informal workers, which gig workers also join.
  • - Nearly 90%: the share of India's workers who are informal, without written contracts or regular benefits. Gig workers are a fast-growing part of this group.

What people argue about

Workers' groups and trade unions say gig workers should get fair minimum pay, safe working hours, accident cover and a clear way to complain if an app blocks them. They say a few percent of turnover is too little.

Many app companies say gig work gives people freedom to choose when and how much to work. They warn that treating everyone as full-time staff would raise costs, push up prices for customers and mean fewer jobs.

Governments are trying to find a middle path, protecting workers without ending flexible work. Different countries are choosing different balances.

Myths and mix-ups

"Gig workers are employees of the app." Under most current rules, they are not. That is exactly why special laws are needed to protect them.

"Gig work is only delivery." It also includes cab drivers, electricians, beauticians, tutors, designers and coders who find work through apps or websites.

"A safety net means the rider earns more per order." Not directly. It means help when things go wrong, like an accident or illness, and support when they grow old.

What it means for you

Gig workers bring groceries, medicines and meals to millions of Indian homes every day. Their safety and fair pay are part of the cost of that convenience.

Small things help. Being patient when an order is late, giving clear directions and offering a glass of water on a hot day make a real difference to someone's long shift.

The way India protects gig workers will also shape your own future. As apps and artificial intelligence change work, many jobs may become more like gigs. The rules being written now could one day protect you too, in an economy where a growing percentage of people work this way.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Gig workers are just students earning pocket money in their spare time.

Truth: Some are, but for many people gig work is their main job and the only income for their family. Many work long hours, six or seven days a week.

Try this

Next time a delivery arrives at home, ask an adult to find out, politely, how many hours the rider works in a day and how many orders they finish. Then work out roughly how much they earn per hour. Discuss what would happen if they fell sick for a week.
