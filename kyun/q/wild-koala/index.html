Why do koalas sleep so much?

A koala can sleep or rest for up to 20 hours a day, and its fingerprints are so like ours that they could confuse a detective.

The short answer

Koalas sleep so much because of what they eat. They live almost entirely on eucalyptus leaves, which are tough, full of fibre, low in nourishment and laced with chemicals that are poisonous to most animals.

Digesting them takes a lot of time and energy, and the leaves give back very little. So a koala saves energy the simplest way possible: by barely moving. It sleeps or sits still for about 18 to 22 hours a day, wedged into the fork of a tree.

That's not laziness. It is a clever way to live on a diet that would starve most other mammals.

Surprising but true

Male koalas make a deep, rumbling bellow that sounds like it should come from an animal ten times their size. Scientists found in 2013 that they have an extra pair of vocal folds to make it.

A diet nobody else wants

Eucalyptus trees grow all over Australia, and there are hundreds of kinds. Their leaves are full of oils and bitter chemicals that help protect them from being eaten. Few animals can live on them.

Koalas can. Their livers break down the poisons. Their teeth grind the leaves into a fine paste. And in their gut is a long pouch called a caecum, about 2 metres long, which is enormous for an animal about the size of a small dog. Inside it, trillions of microbes slowly break the leaves down.

Even so, a koala is fussy. It eats only certain kinds of eucalyptus and picks the youngest, most nutritious leaves. It can eat up to about 500 g of leaves a day.

Saving every bit of energy

Koalas have a slow metabolism, the rate at which the body burns fuel. Their brains are small for their body size, which also saves energy, since brains are greedy organs.

Koalas hardly need to drink. They get most of their water from leaves, though they do drink in hot weather and droughts. On hot days, they hug the cool trunks of trees to stay comfortable.

So their day goes like this: sleep, eat a little, sleep, move to a new branch, eat some more, sleep. Most of their activity happens at night and around dusk.

Fingerprints and a big voice

Koalas have fingerprints with ridges, loops and swirls very like ours. Under a microscope, they are hard to tell apart from human prints. Very few animals have them, mostly primates. Koalas evolved them separately, probably to help grip branches and handle leaves.

They also have a surprising voice. Male koalas give a deep bellow, a bit like a snoring roar, to show off to females and warn rivals. In 2013, scientists discovered why it is so deep: koalas have an extra pair of vocal folds, outside the voice box, that no other land mammal is known to have.

Babies in pouches

Koalas are marsupials, like kangaroos and wombats. A newborn koala is about the size of a jellybean. It crawls into its mother's pouch, which opens towards her back legs, and stays there for about six months, drinking milk.

Then comes a strange step. To digest eucalyptus, the joey needs the right microbes in its gut. So its mother makes a special soft poop called pap, and the joey eats it. After that, the joey can start on leaves. It rides on its mother's back until it is about a year old.

Koalas in trouble

Koalas are not bears, despite the common name "koala bear". They are found only in Australia. Their numbers have fallen as forests are cleared for farms, roads and houses. Dogs and cars kill many, and a disease called chlamydia makes others ill.

The 2019 and 2020 bushfires burned huge areas of koala forest. In 2022, the Australian government listed koalas as endangered in Queensland, New South Wales and the Australian Capital Territory.

Koalas are one of the slowest-living mammals around. Their only real rival for doing so little lives in the rainforests of South and Central America. The sloth hangs upside down so still that algae grow on its fur.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Koalas are sleepy because eucalyptus leaves make them drunk or drugged.

Truth: Eucalyptus does not make koalas drowsy. They sleep so much because the leaves give very little energy and take a long time to digest, so resting is the best way to save fuel.
