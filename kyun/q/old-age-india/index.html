How do Indian families care for grandparents?

In 2021, about one Indian in ten was 60 or older. By 2050, it is expected to be about one in five, around 35 crore people.

The short answer

In much of India, grandparents have traditionally lived with their grown-up children, usually a son, in a joint family. Children grew up with grandparents in the next room. The young cared for the old, and the old helped raise the young.

That pattern is still common. Most Indians over 60 live with family. But it is changing. Many young people move to cities or abroad for work, families are smaller, and people are living longer. More grandparents now live alone or as a couple, and families rely more on pensions, video calls, neighbours, paid carers and, sometimes, old-age homes.

Surprising but true

India has a law, passed in 2007, that makes adult children legally responsible for looking after their elderly parents. Parents can go to a special tribunal to claim a monthly allowance.

The joint family

A joint family brings several generations under one roof: grandparents, their sons, their sons' wives and children, and sometimes unmarried daughters and other relatives. Elders were often respected as heads of the household. Many Indian traditions, across religions, stress caring for parents as a duty.

In this system, grandparents are much more than people to be cared for. They cook, tell stories, teach prayers and recipes, watch the children while parents work and pass on family history. A lot of Indian storytelling has been handed down at grandparents' knees.

A country getting older

India is still a young country. In 2021, only about one person in ten was 60 or older. But that share is growing fast, because people live longer and families have fewer children. A report by the UN Population Fund in 2023 projected that by 2050, about one in five Indians will be elderly. That's around 35 crore people, roughly the whole population of the US today.

Some states are ageing faster than others. Kerala and Tamil Nadu already have a much higher share of older people than Bihar or Uttar Pradesh, where families are larger.

When children move away

In villages across India, many young people move to cities for work. Some go abroad, to the Gulf countries, the US or Europe. Their parents often stay behind, especially in places like Kerala, where so many people work overseas that some villages are mostly older people.

Phones help. Grandparents video-call grandchildren they see only once a year. Children send money home. But a phone can't take someone to the doctor at night. Neighbours, local doctors and community groups often fill the gap.

Pensions, laws and care

Many older Indians, especially women and farm workers, never had a job with a pension. The government pays a small monthly old-age pension to poor elders through the Indira Gandhi National Old Age Pension Scheme, with states adding their own money.

In 2007, India passed a law making children responsible for supporting their elderly parents. A parent who is neglected can ask a tribunal to order the children to pay a monthly allowance. There is also a free national helpline for senior citizens, Elderline, on the number 14567.

Old-age homes exist in most cities, run by charities, religious groups and companies. For some families they bring a sense of shame. Others see them as a way for elders to get daily care and company.

How it works in the US

In the United States, most older people live on their own or with a husband or wife, not with grown-up children. Many receive Social Security, a government pension started in 1935. Some move to retirement communities or nursing homes.

But multigenerational living is growing there too, partly because of high housing costs and partly because of immigrant families, including many Indian-American ones, who bring the habit of living together. In both countries, families are working out new ways to look after people who once looked after them.

Myth or fact?

Myth: In India, grandparents are always looked after by their families, so nobody needs other help.

Truth: Many families do care for elders at home, but not all can. Some older people live alone or with only a spouse, especially in villages where young people have moved away. Pensions, health care and community help matter more as India ages.

Try this

Interview a grandparent or an older neighbour. Ask what games they played as children, what their school was like and what has changed most in their lifetime. Write down or record their answers, and make a small booklet to give them.
