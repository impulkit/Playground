What is the Nobel Peace Prize?

The man who invented dynamite left his fortune to reward people who make the world more peaceful.

The short answer

The Nobel Peace Prize is a yearly award for the person or group that has done the most to bring nations together, reduce wars or protect people's rights.

It was set up by Alfred Nobel, a Swedish chemist who invented dynamite and grew very rich. When he died in 1896, his will left most of his money for five prizes. Four are awarded in Sweden. The Peace Prize is chosen by a committee in Norway and handed out in Oslo every 10 December, the day Nobel died.

Winners get a gold medal, a certificate and prize money of around a million US dollars. More than 100 people and about 30 organisations have won it.

Surprising but true

Mahatma Gandhi was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize five times but never won. The Nobel Committee later called it a great omission.

The man behind the prize

Alfred Nobel was born in Stockholm in 1833. He worked with explosives and, in 1867, patented dynamite, which made blasting rock for tunnels, mines and railways much safer and easier. He built factories in many countries and held more than 350 patents.

Dynamite was also used in war, and Nobel's company made weapons. Many people have wondered whether that weighed on him. What we know for sure is what he wrote in his will in 1895. Most of his fortune would pay for prizes in physics, chemistry, medicine, literature and peace.

His family was shocked, and some tried to challenge the will. It took about five years to sort out, which is why the first prizes were only handed out in 1901.

How the winner is chosen

Thousands of people are allowed to nominate, including members of national parliaments, university professors and past winners. Each year the committee receives a few hundred nominations. Its five members, chosen by Norway's parliament, study the list for months and announce the winner in October.

Who has won

The first Peace Prize, in 1901, went to Henry Dunant, who founded the Red Cross, and Frédéric Passy, a French campaigner for peace. Since then, winners have included presidents, doctors, journalists and teachers.

Two Indian citizens have won it: Mother Teresa in 1979, for her work with the poor in Kolkata, and Kailash Satyarthi in 2014, for freeing children from forced work. He shared his prize with Malala Yousafzai of Pakistan, the youngest winner ever. American winners include Martin Luther King Jr, who won in 1964 at the age of 35.

Who was Martin Luther King Jr.?He started college at 15, led a city-wide boycott at 26, and won the Nobel Peace Prize at 35.

Groups can win too

The prize doesn't have to go to a single person. The International Committee of the Red Cross has won it three times, more than anyone else. The UN refugee agency, UNHCR, has won twice.

Some years, the committee gives no prize at all. That happened often during the two world wars, when nobody seemed to deserve it.

The prize Gandhi never got

Mahatma Gandhi was nominated five times, but the committee never chose him. In 1948, the year he was killed, no prize was given, and the committee said there was "no suitable living candidate". Decades later, its own historians wrote that leaving Gandhi out was its greatest omission.

Choices that cause arguments

Some winners have been controversial. In 1973, two committee members resigned over the prize given to the American official Henry Kissinger, who was involved in the Vietnam War. People argue about other choices too, because peace is hard to measure.

The names of people nominated are kept secret for 50 years. So, today, nobody outside the committee can know who almost won.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Mahatma Gandhi won the Nobel Peace Prize.

Truth: Gandhi was nominated five times, the last in 1948, but never won. After his death, no prize was given that year. The Nobel Committee has since said that leaving him out was one of its biggest mistakes.
