What is Times Square?

Times Square in New York is named after a newspaper. In 1904 The New York Times moved into a tower there, and the city renamed the square after it.

The short answer

Times Square is a busy junction in the middle of Manhattan, in New York City, where two big streets, Broadway and Seventh Avenue, cross. It's famous for giant glowing screens that cover the buildings from street to rooftop, for its theatres and for the ball that drops on New Year's Eve.

About 220,000 people walked into Times Square on an average day in 2024, and on the busiest days far more. Many are tourists, some are office workers rushing to the subway, and some are theatre fans heading to a show. It's often called the Crossroads of the World.

Surprising but true

City rules don't just allow the giant glowing signs in Times Square. They require buildings there to put up big, bright signs.

A newspaper's name

The area used to be called Longacre Square, and it was known for horse stables and carriage makers. In 1904 The New York Times moved into a tall new tower at the south end of the square. The city renamed the square after the newspaper, and a new subway station opened beneath it that same year.

To celebrate its new home, the Times held a New Year's Eve party with fireworks at the end of 1904. When fireworks were banned, it came up with a different idea.

The ball drop

On the last night of 1907, a ball made of iron and wood, covered in 100 light bulbs, was lowered down a pole on the tower. It reached the bottom exactly at midnight. The tradition has continued almost every year since.

The exceptions were 1942 and 1943. During the Second World War, New York dimmed its lights at night in case of enemy attack, so the ball stayed dark. Crowds still gathered at midnight. They stood for a minute of silence, and chimes rang out from a sound truck parked below the tower. The ball returned, glowing, at the end of 1944.

Today's ball is far bigger and covered in thousands of crystal triangles and LED lights. Around a million people often pack the streets to watch, and many more see it on television. It's one of the most famous ways to see in the New Year anywhere.

Signs, by law

Times Square glows all night. That's partly because New York's planning rules require buildings in the area to carry large, lit-up signs. Companies pay huge sums to put their adverts on these screens, because so many people look up at them.

Some screens wrap around corners or stretch many storeys high. A few are among the largest in the world. On a cloudy night, the glow can light up the clouds above. Each screen is carefully timed, and adverts change every few seconds so the whole square seems to flicker and move.

The theatre district

Times Square sits at the heart of New York's Theatre District. Dozens of theatres nearby put on Broadway shows, the long-running musicals and plays that draw audiences from around the world. Some shows have run for decades.

The neighbourhood hasn't always been glamorous. By the 1970s parts of it were run-down and many people felt unsafe there. In the 1990s the city and businesses cleaned it up, and big shops, restaurants and hotels moved in.

Walking, not driving

For decades Times Square was jammed with cars, and people squeezed along crowded pavements. In 2009 the city closed parts of Broadway to traffic and turned them into pedestrian plazas with chairs and benches. Some people predicted traffic chaos, but road injuries fell and the plazas filled up.

Times Square is still noisy and packed, with street performers, people dressed as cartoon characters and crowds taking photos. Some New Yorkers try to avoid it, while visitors from Mumbai to Melbourne put it at the top of their list.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Times Square ball has dropped every New Year's Eve since 1907.

Truth: It didn't drop in 1942 or 1943. During the Second World War, the city's lights were dimmed, and crowds marked midnight with a minute of silence instead.
