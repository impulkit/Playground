What is country music?

In the summer of 1927, a record producer set up a microphone in an empty building in Bristol, Tennessee, and accidentally launched a whole kind of music.

The short answer

Country music is a style of American popular music that grew out of the folk songs of the rural South, especially the Appalachian mountains. It is known for story-telling lyrics about everyday life, love, family, hard work, faith and small towns. Its sound comes from the fiddle, banjo, acoustic guitar and the sliding, crying steel guitar.

Its roots lie in the ballads brought by settlers from Scotland, Ireland and England, mixed with the banjo and musical styles of African Americans. In the 1920s, radio and records turned these local sounds into a national music. Today country is one of the best-selling kinds of music in the United States, centred on Nashville, Tennessee.

Surprising but true

The banjo, the twangy sound of so much country music, comes from instruments brought to America by enslaved West Africans.

Mountain songs

In the 1700s and 1800s, many settlers from Scotland, Ireland and England made homes in the Appalachian mountains. They brought old ballads, long songs that told stories of love, murder and adventure, and dance tunes played on the fiddle.

In the American South, they met the music of enslaved Africans and their descendants. The banjo came from African instruments made of a gourd and a skin, with a long neck. Black musicians also brought rhythms, the bluesy sliding notes and new ways of singing. Mixed together over generations, these sounds became "old-time" or "hillbilly" music, played at dances, churches and on porches.

The big bang in Bristol

In 1927, a record producer named Ralph Peer set up a recording studio in an empty building in Bristol, a town on the border between Tennessee and Virginia. He put an advert in the local paper inviting musicians to come and record.

Among those who turned up were the Carter Family, who sang mountain songs in close harmony, and Jimmie Rodgers, a former railway worker who yodelled. Both became stars. Historians often call the Bristol Sessions "the big bang of country music", and Bristol now has a museum about them.

What are the Appalachian Mountains?These gentle, tree-covered mountains in the eastern USA are hundreds of millions of years old, and their long-lost cousins are in Scotland and Morocco.

The Grand Ole Opry

In 1925, a radio station in Nashville started a Saturday night show of fiddle and string-band music. It became the Grand Ole Opry. Families across the South gathered around their radios to listen, and singers who appeared on it became famous.

The Opry is still broadcast every week, making it one of the longest-running radio shows in the world. Nashville grew into the home of country music recording, and is now nicknamed Music City.

Many kinds of country

Country music has many branches. Bluegrass, created by Bill Monroe in the 1940s, is fast and acoustic, with brilliant banjo and fiddle playing. Honky-tonk, made famous by Hank Williams, is the sound of bars and broken hearts. Johnny Cash sang about prisoners and the poor, and Dolly Parton wrote hits including "Jolene" and "9 to 5".

Modern country often blends with pop and rock. Taylor Swift began as a teenage country singer before moving to pop. In 2019, Lil Nas X's country-rap song "Old Town Road" spent 19 weeks at number one in the US. In 2025, Beyoncé's country album Cowboy Carter won the Grammy for Album of the Year.

Country in India

Country music has a loyal following in parts of north-east India, especially in Meghalaya and Nagaland, where Western music has been loved for generations. In Shillong, local bands play country and rock songs at cafés and festivals. Its songs about land, family and home have a lot in common with India's own folk songs.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Country music has only ever been made by white Americans.

Truth: Black musicians helped shape country music from the start. The banjo has African roots, the harmonica player DeFord Bailey was one of the first stars of the Grand Ole Opry, and singers such as Charley Pride and, more recently, Beyoncé have topped the country charts.

Try this

Pick any country song and listen for the instruments. Raise a hand when you hear a fiddle, tap your knee for a banjo, and wave for the slide of a steel guitar. Then try writing a short verse that tells a true story from your own life, which is what many country songs do.
