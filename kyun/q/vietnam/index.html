What is Vietnam like?

Vietnam is shaped like a long letter S, and one of its caves is big enough to hold its own jungle.

The short answer

Vietnam is a long, narrow country on the eastern edge of mainland Southeast Asia. It curves like a letter S for about 1,650 kilometres, from the mountains on the Chinese border down to the flat, watery Mekong Delta. At its narrowest point it is only about 50 kilometres wide.

About 10 crore people live there. The capital, Hanoi, is in the north, and the biggest city, Ho Chi Minh City, is in the south. Vietnam is known for green rice paddies, limestone islands rising from the sea, noodle soup for breakfast, strong coffee and streets full of motorbikes. To many older Americans, Vietnam also means a long and painful war that ended in 1975.

Surprising but true

Son Doong, in central Vietnam, is the largest known cave passage on Earth, and a local man found its entrance in the jungle around 1990, decades before explorers went inside.

Two rice baskets on a pole

People often describe Vietnam as two rice baskets hanging from a carrying pole. The baskets are the two great river deltas: the Red River Delta in the north and the Mekong Delta in the south. Both are flat and fertile, crisscrossed by canals, and covered in rice paddies.

The pole is the narrow middle, squeezed between mountains and the sea. Vietnam grows so much rice that it is one of the world's biggest exporters. In the Mekong Delta, families sell fruit and vegetables from boats at floating markets, and children may go to school by canoe.

Islands and a giant cave

Ha Long Bay in the north holds around 1,600 limestone islands and islets that rise straight out of green water. A local legend says a dragon came down from the mountains, and its thrashing tail carved the valleys that the sea later filled.

Further south, in Phong Nha-Ke Bang National Park, a local man named Ho Khanh found a cave entrance in the jungle around 1990. In 2009, British and Vietnamese cavers explored it and found Son Doong, the largest known cave passage on Earth. Some chambers could hold a 40-storey building. Parts of the roof have collapsed, letting in sunlight, so a jungle grows on the cave floor. The cave even has its own clouds. It was carved by an underground river dissolving limestone over millions of years, the way caves form in rock all over the world.

Puppets on water and an alphabet from abroad

Water puppetry began in the Red River Delta about a thousand years ago, when farmers performed in flooded rice paddies after the harvest. Today it is staged in theatres with a pool of water as the stage. The puppeteers hide behind a bamboo screen and work the puppets with long poles under the water.

For centuries, Vietnamese was written in Chinese characters. In the 1600s, European missionaries worked with Vietnamese helpers to write the language in Latin letters, with accent marks to show its six tones. In the early 1900s this script, called chữ Quốc ngữ, became the main way of writing Vietnamese, part of a long story in the history of the alphabet.

War and coffee

In the 20th century Vietnam fought France for independence, then was split into north and south. The Vietnam War, between the communist north and the US-backed south, became one of the most violent conflicts of the Cold War. It cost millions of lives and ended in 1975 when the country was reunited under the north.

Since the 1980s, Vietnam has opened up to world trade and grown fast. It is now the second-biggest coffee producer in the world, after Brazil. Vietnamese iced coffee is made with sweetened condensed milk, strong and sweet enough to keep you awake through a hot afternoon.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Vietnamese is written with Chinese characters.

Truth: For centuries Vietnamese scholars did write in Chinese characters. Today Vietnamese uses the Latin alphabet, with extra marks above and below letters to show its six tones.

Make a mini water puppet. Tape a small cork or plastic toy onto the end of a long stick or ruler. In a bath or a big basin, hide your hand behind a towel "screen" and move the toy along the surface so it looks as if it is moving on its own.
