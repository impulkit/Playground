Why are some lakes pink?

In June 2020, Lonar Lake in Maharashtra turned pink almost overnight, and people came from miles around to stare at it.

The short answer

Most pink lakes are pink because they are full of tiny living things that love salt. A green alga called Dunaliella salina makes a red-orange pigment called beta-carotene to protect itself from strong sunlight. Microbes called halophiles, which means "salt lovers", carry red pigments too. When billions of them crowd into very salty water, the whole lake turns pink.

These lakes are usually much saltier than the sea, and hot, sunny weather helps. That's why some lakes are pink only in the dry season and fade to blue or brown after rain.

Surprising but true

Lake Hillier in Western Australia stays bubblegum pink even when you scoop its water into a bottle.

The pink lake of Maharashtra

Lonar Lake sits inside a crater made when a meteorite slammed into the Deccan plateau. Scientists still argue over when: estimates range from about 50,000 to more than 500,000 years ago. Its water is salty and alkaline, like a very weak soap. In June 2020, people noticed the lake had turned a striking pink.

Scientists from the Agharkar Research Institute in Pune tested the water. A hot, dry spell had lowered the water level and made the lake saltier. That suited a group of microbes called Haloarchaea, which carry a red pigment. They multiplied fast and coloured the water. When monsoon rain topped up the lake, the pink faded away.

Bubblegum in Australia

Lake Hillier lies on Middle Island, off the southern coast of Western Australia. It is about 600 metres long, roughly six football fields, and it is separated from the dark blue ocean by a thin strip of forest and sand dunes. From a plane, it looks like someone has spilled strawberry milkshake.

Unlike many pink lakes, Lake Hillier stays pink all year, and its water is still pink in a glass. Scientists who studied its DNA in 2015 found it full of salt-loving algae and bacteria, with Dunaliella salina among the most common. At another pink lake in Western Australia, Hutt Lagoon, companies farm that same alga in ponds. They extract its beta-carotene to colour foods and make vitamin pills.

Salt and colour in Senegal

Lake Retba, near Dakar in Senegal, is also called Lac Rose, the Pink Lake. In parts of the year it is saltier than the Dead Sea, and people float easily. It is pinkest in the dry season, from about November to June.

Local workers wade into the lake up to their chests and scrape salt from the bottom into boats. They rub their skin with shea butter to protect it from the stinging brine. The salt is sold across West Africa.

Flamingos join in

Pink water and pink birds are connected. Flamingos aren't born pink. They get their colour from eating tiny shrimp and algae full of carotenoid pigments, the same family as the orange in carrots. A flamingo that eats no pink food slowly turns white.

So salty pink lakes are often good flamingo lakes. In India, huge flocks gather on the salty flats of the Rann of Kutch in Gujarat, and in Mumbai they now crowd the mudflats near Sewri and Thane creek in winter.

Pink by the sea

Some pink waters are made by people. When seawater is left in shallow ponds to dry and make salt, the water gets saltier and saltier. At one stage, the microbes bloom and the ponds turn pink or even red. Seen from above, salt works in Gujarat, Tamil Nadu and San Francisco Bay look like patchwork quilts of pink, orange and green.

India has another famous salt lake too. Sambhar Lake in Rajasthan is the country's largest inland salt lake, and parts of it can turn pink-red in the dry months. Every grain of salt on your table may have passed through a pink pond on its way to you.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Pink lakes are pink because of chemicals or pollution.

Truth: Most pink lakes get their colour from natural living things. Tiny algae and microbes that love salt make red and orange pigments, and huge numbers of them tint the water.

Try this

Dissolve as much salt as you can in a glass of warm water, stirring until no more will dissolve. Leave it on a sunny windowsill for a week or two. Watch salt crystals form on the sides as the water evaporates. This is how salt lakes get saltier in the dry season.
