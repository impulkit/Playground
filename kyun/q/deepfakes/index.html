What are deepfakes?

In 2024, police in Hong Kong said an office worker sent about US$25 million to fraudsters after a video call in which every other face on screen was fake.

The short answer

A deepfake is a video, picture or sound recording that has been made or changed by artificial intelligence to show someone doing or saying something they never did. It might swap one person's face onto another's body, make a politician appear to give a speech, or copy a relative's voice on the phone.

The name mixes "deep learning", a powerful kind of AI, with "fake". Deepfakes can be fun or useful, in films and games for example. They can also be used to trick, bully or spread lies, which is why it helps to know how they work.

Surprising but true

The word "deepfake" comes from the username of a person on the website Reddit who began posting face-swapped videos in 2017.

How they are made

To make a face-swap video, a computer studies hundreds or thousands of pictures of two people. It learns to describe each face as a set of features, such as the shape of the mouth, the angle of the head, and the expression. Then it redraws person A's features on person B, frame by frame, so the new face moves and talks in time.

One clever design, invented in 2014, uses two programs that compete. One, the generator, makes fakes. The other, the discriminator, tries to spot them. Each round, the generator learns from its mistakes, and the fakes get better and better. These are called generative adversarial networks.

Fake voices

Voices can be copied too. A program listens to recordings of a person and learns how their voice rises and falls, how they pronounce sounds, and how they pause. Then it can read out any text in that voice.

Criminals have used this to phone people pretending to be a boss, or a family member in trouble, asking for money urgently. The FBI in the USA and police in India have both warned about such scams.

Good uses

The same tools power some exciting work. Filmmakers use them to make actors look younger, or to bring back a character after an actor has died, with permission from the family. Dubbing studios can match an actor's lip movements to a new language, so a film dubbed into Hindi looks as if it was shot in Hindi.

Voice technology can also help people who have lost their voice to illness, by recreating it from old recordings so they can still "speak" like themselves.

Harm and rules

Deepfakes have been used to embarrass people, especially women and girls, and to spread false stories about politicians before elections. In 2023, after deepfake videos of Indian film stars spread online, India's government told social media companies to remove such content quickly.

Many countries are writing new laws. Some require AI-made content to be labelled. Tech companies are also testing invisible watermarks that mark a picture or video as AI-made. None of these fixes is perfect yet.

How to protect yourself

Look for odd details, like blurry edges around the face, teeth that look strange, or lighting that doesn't match. But don't rely on that alone. Ask where the video came from. Is a trusted news organisation reporting it? Does it seem designed to make you angry or scared, fast?

If a message asks for money or secrets, check with a real person you trust, using a phone number you already know. The same questions that help you catch fake news work on deepfakes too. And never share a fake video of someone, even as a joke, because it can hurt them and may be against the law.

Myth or fact?

Myth: You can always spot a deepfake if you look closely, for example by checking whether the person blinks.

Truth: Early deepfakes had giveaways like strange blinking, but newer ones fix many of those flaws, and even experts can be fooled. Checking where a video came from, and whether trusted news sources report it, is more reliable than staring at pixels.

Try this

Agree on a family "safe word" that only you and your parents know. If anyone ever calls or messages claiming to be a family member in trouble, asking for the safe word is a quick way to check. Practise it once so everyone remembers.
