Why is it called a googol?

The word "googol" was made up by a nine-year-old boy. Decades later, a famous company named itself after a misspelling of it.

The short answer

A googol is the number 1 followed by 100 zeros. Mathematicians write it as 10¹⁰⁰.

The name was invented by a child. Around 1920, the American mathematician Edward Kasner asked his nine-year-old nephew, Milton Sirotta, to think up a name for a very large number. Milton suggested "googol". Kasner liked it and used it in his lectures, and in 1940 he put it in a popular book, Mathematics and the Imagination.

Milton also suggested an even bigger number, a "googolplex". He said it should be one followed by as many zeros as you could write before getting tired. Kasner made it more exact: 1 followed by a googol zeros.

Surprising but true

Indian number names keep going far past a crore. Old texts list names for numbers with dozens of zeros, and a Buddhist story has the Buddha naming numbers up to 10 with 53 zeros.

Bigger than the universe

A googol is far bigger than anything you could count. Scientists estimate that the observable universe contains about 10⁸⁰ atoms. A googol is 10²⁰, or a hundred billion billion, times bigger than that.

A googolplex is unimaginably bigger again. Even writing it out is impossible. There aren't enough atoms in the universe to make the zeros, even if each atom were a single zero.

Kasner wanted children to see that a number can be huge without being infinite. A googol is enormous, but you could still add one to it.

A company with a misspelt name

In the 1990s, two students at Stanford University, Larry Page and Sergey Brin, built a new search engine. They wanted a name that suggested the huge amount of information on the internet.

According to the story told by people who worked with them, a fellow student suggested a name based on "googol". When they checked whether the web address was free, someone typed "google" by mistake. They liked it and kept it. Their company is now one of the biggest in the world, and its name is a misspelling of a child's word.

Lakhs, crores and beyond

In India, big numbers have their own names. A lakh is 1,00,000 and a crore is 1,00,00,000. Indian commas are placed differently too, after every two digits once you pass a thousand, so that lakhs and crores are easy to read.

The names keep going. A hundred crore is an arab, and a hundred arab is a kharab. Ancient texts in Sanskrit list number names much further. The Yajurveda names powers of ten up to parardha, a million million. A Buddhist text called the Lalitavistara tells how the young Siddhartha, later the Buddha, named numbers in a contest, climbing to one with 53 zeros.

Million, billion and confusion

The word "million" comes from Italian and means roughly "a big thousand". "Billion" was created later. But for centuries, countries disagreed about what a billion meant.

In the United States, a billion has always meant a thousand million, 1,000,000,000. In Britain, it used to mean a million million. In 1974, the British government began using the American meaning, and today most English-speaking countries do the same. Some European languages still use the older meaning, which can cause mix-ups in translation.

Where number words come from

Number words often hide old ideas. "Eleven" and "twelve" come from Old English words meaning "one left over" and "two left over", counting past ten on your fingers. In Hindi, many numbers between 20 and 100 have names that are quite irregular, which is one reason children take time to learn them.

Different languages count in different ways. In French, 80 is quatre-vingts, "four twenties", a leftover from counting in twenties. Every one of these words is a small piece of history.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The word "googol" came from the company Google.

Truth: It's the other way round. "Googol" was invented around 1920 and printed in a maths book in 1940. Google's founders picked their name in the 1990s, and the usual story is that it came from a misspelling of googol.

Try this

Try writing a googol on paper. Write a 1, then 100 zeros, grouping them in threes with commas so you don't lose count. Time how long it takes. Then think about a googolplex, which has a googol zeros. There's no room in the universe to write it out.
