How did breakfast cereal begin?

The first cold breakfast cereal was so hard that you had to soak it overnight, or risk breaking a tooth.

The short answer

Cold breakfast cereal began in the United States in the 1800s as a health food. Doctors and health reformers thought the heavy breakfasts of the time, with lots of meat, eggs and fried food, were bad for digestion. They wanted something simple made from grain.

The most famous names were the Kellogg brothers of Battle Creek, Michigan. In the 1890s they invented toasted flakes of wheat and then corn. A rival, C.W. Post, started his own cereal company nearby. Within a few decades, a bowl of cereal with cold milk had become a typical American breakfast, and cereal companies were among the biggest advertisers in the country.

Surprising but true

Corn flakes were invented as a plain, unsweetened health food. One of the Kellogg brothers who made them fought against adding sugar.

Granula and the overnight soak

The first ready-made cold cereal was called Granula. It was made in 1863 by James Caleb Jackson, who ran a health resort in Dansville, New York. He baked whole-wheat flour into sheets, broke them up, and baked the pieces again.

The result was rock hard. Diners had to soak the nuggets in milk overnight before they could be chewed. It was not exactly a treat, but it showed people that breakfast could come out of a box.

The Kelloggs of Battle Creek

John Harvey Kellogg was a doctor who ran the Battle Creek Sanitarium, a large health resort. He had many strict ideas about diet, some sensible and some very odd. His younger brother, Will Keith Kellogg, managed the business side.

In 1894, according to the family story, a pot of cooked wheat was left standing for many hours. When the brothers rolled it, each grain came out as a separate flake, which could be toasted crisp. Patients loved them. Corn flakes followed a few years later, and in 1906 Will started his own company to sell them to the public. Against his brother's wishes, he added sugar.

Grape-Nuts and the cereal rush

One of the sanitarium's patients, C.W. Post, started a rival company in Battle Creek. In 1897 he launched Grape-Nuts, which contains neither grapes nor nuts. He said the name came from grape sugar, a name for glucose, and the nutty taste.

Soon dozens of cereal makers rushed into the town. Many failed quickly. The ones that survived spent heavily on advertising, toy prizes in boxes and, later, cartoon characters on the front. By the 1950s, sugar-coated cereals aimed at children filled supermarket shelves.

Shot from guns

Puffed cereals came from a different idea. In 1901, a scientist named Alexander Anderson found that heating grains in a sealed container and then suddenly releasing the pressure made them burst open. Puffed rice was advertised in the US as cereal "shot from guns".

India had been eating puffed rice for centuries already. Muri in Bengal, kurmura in Maharashtra and pori in Tamil Nadu are made by roasting rice in hot sand until it pops. They go into bhel puri and jhal muri, or are eaten with jaggery. Poha, rice pressed into flat flakes, is also an ancient ready-to-cook grain.

Cereal in India today

Kellogg's started selling corn flakes in India in 1994. At first many families poured hot milk over them, the way they would with porridge, and the flakes went soggy fast. Today breakfast cereal shares the table with poha, upma, idli and paratha.

The word cereal comes from Ceres, the Roman goddess of grain and farming. Whether it is wheat flakes or a plate of poha, nearly every breakfast still begins with her gift. Many packaged cereals contain more sugar than people expect, so reading the label is worth the effort.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Breakfast cereal was always a sweet food for children.

Truth: The first cereals were plain, bland health foods made for adults at health resorts. Sugar-coated cereals and cartoon mascots came decades later, mostly after the 1940s.
