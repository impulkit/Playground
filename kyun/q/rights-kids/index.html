Do children have rights too?

Yes! Some rights belong to you just because you're a child.

The short answer

Yes. In 1989, countries agreed on the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, a treaty listing the rights every person under 18 should have. Almost every country in the world has joined it.

It says children have the right to be safe, to go to school, to health care, to play and rest, and to have a say in decisions that affect them. Grown-ups and governments must put children's best interests first.

India's Right to Education Act (2009) says every child aged 6–14 should get free schooling. Rights come with respect: your rights matter, and so do everyone else's.

Surprising but true

The children's rights agreement is the most widely agreed human rights treaty in history.

Where the idea came from

A hundred years ago, most countries had few laws protecting children. Many worked long hours in mines, factories and fields. After the First World War, a British teacher named Eglantyne Jebb founded Save the Children to help hungry children in Europe.

In 1923 she wrote a short declaration of children's rights. The League of Nations, the body before the United Nations, adopted it in 1924. It was only a few lines long, but it planted an idea that grew.

The big treaty of 1989

On 20 November 1989, the United Nations adopted the Convention on the Rights of the Child. It has 54 articles. That day is now celebrated as World Children's Day.

The treaty rests on four main ideas. No child should be treated unfairly. Adults should do what is best for children. Every child has the right to life and to grow up well. And children should be listened to. India joined in 1992. Today 196 countries have joined, more than for any other human rights treaty. The United States signed in 1995, but has not ratified it.

What is the mid-day meal scheme?Every school day, India serves a hot lunch to more than 10 crore children, the largest school meal programme in the world.

The right to learn

Indian law says every child from 6 to 14 has the right to free and compulsory education. This was added to the Constitution in 2002, and the Right to Education Act followed in 2009. Private schools must keep a quarter of their entry-class seats for children from poorer families.

Free school meals, like India's mid-day meal scheme, help too. Hungry children find it hard to learn, and many families send children to school partly because of the lunch.

The right to be safe

No child should be hurt or made to do dangerous work. Campaigners like Kailash Satyarthi have spent decades freeing children from factories and quarries. Indian law bans employing children under 14 in almost all jobs.

In the USA, a 1938 law limited child labour across the country, after years of campaigning against children working in mills and mines.

The right to be heard

Children have the right to share their views on things that affect them, and adults should take those views seriously. That might mean a student council at school, or a judge asking a child where they want to live after their parents separate.

Young people around the world have used this voice. Malala Yousafzai spoke out for girls' schooling in Pakistan when she was just 11.

Getting help

In India, any child in trouble can call 1098 for free, day or night, for help. Trusted adults, such as teachers, parents and doctors, are there to help too. Knowing your rights is the first step towards making sure they are respected, for you and for every child around you.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Children's rights mean kids can do whatever they want.

Truth: Children's rights are about being safe, healthy, educated and heard. They don't remove rules or parents' guidance. The treaty says parents have the main responsibility for raising children, and children should respect others' rights too.

Try this

Write ten children's rights on separate slips of paper, such as the right to play, to learn, to be safe and to be heard. In a small group, arrange them from most to least important. Compare your order with another group and talk about why you disagreed. Was it hard to put any right last?
