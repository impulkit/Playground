How does mail reach remote villages?

India runs the world's highest post office, more than 4,400 metres up in the Himalaya, and a post office that floats on a lake in Kashmir.

The short answer

In remote villages, mail is carried by a mix of roads, trains, buses, boats, jeeps and, very often, people on foot. In India, the last stretch is usually handled by a Gramin Dak Sevak. This village postal worker runs a small branch post office and carries letters, parcels, pensions and money orders door to door.

India has about 1.65 lakh post offices, the largest postal network in the world, and nearly 90 per cent of them are in villages (March 2025). Some sit in places so high or so far away that a letter takes days to get there.

Surprising but true

In the USA, letters to one village at the bottom of the Grand Canyon still travel the last stretch on the backs of mules.

The world's highest post office

The post office at Hikkim, in the cold desert of Spiti in Himachal Pradesh, stands more than 4,400 metres above sea level. Guinness World Records lists it as the highest in the world, at 4,724 metres, though maps usually put the village a little lower. That is more than half as high as Mount Everest.

Mail from Hikkim goes on foot or by vehicle down to Kaza, the nearest town, then onward by bus. In winter, heavy snow can cut the roads for weeks, and the post office may close. Travellers now come all the way up just to send a postcard with the Hikkim stamp on it.

A post office on a boat

On Dal Lake in Srinagar, a post office floats on a houseboat. It sells stamps, collects letters from people living around the lake, and has a small museum of stamps. Many visitors post a card from it just to say they did.

In the high Himalaya, the far Northeast and the islands of the Andamans, the post travels by everything from ferries to helicopters. Before modern roads, India relied on relay runners, called dak runners. Each ran a fixed stretch, then handed the bag on, like a relay race across the country.

Where is the floating post office?On Dal Lake in Srinagar, you can climb off a wooden boat, post a letter from a houseboat, and get a stamp with a picture of a boat on it.

A worker for everything

A village postal worker does much more than deliver letters. In many places, they also handle savings accounts, pay out old-age pensions, deliver government documents and collect electricity bills. Since 2018, the India Post Payments Bank has let village postal workers carry a small device that works like a bank counter in their bag.

Many walk or cycle long distances. In hilly areas, a single round can mean crossing streams and forest paths. People in the village often know their postal worker by name, and the post office may be the only government office for kilometres around.

How the PIN code helps

Before 1972, Indian letters were sorted by reading the name of the town, which caused confusion, since many towns share names. On 15 August 1972, India introduced the six-digit PIN code, short for Postal Index Number.

The first digit picks one of nine zones (eight regions plus one for the army). The second picks a sub-region, the third a sorting district, and the last three a single post office. A clerk who cannot read a messy address can still send it the right way using the numbers alone.

Mules in the Grand Canyon

The United States has its own remote routes. The village of Supai, home of the Havasupai people, sits deep inside a side canyon of the Grand Canyon in Arizona. No road reaches it. The US Postal Service sends mail, groceries and supplies down a trail of about 13 km by mule train, one of the last mule mail routes in the country.

Mail has reached India's Antarctic research bases too. Back in the 1980s, India opened a post office at its first base there, Dakshin Gangotri. The future may be stranger still. India Post has tested drones to fly parcels to hard-to-reach places, cutting journeys that take hours by road to a few minutes in the air.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Nobody uses the post office any more, because of phones and email.

Truth: Letters have fallen, but post offices now carry huge numbers of parcels from online shopping. In villages they are also banks, pension offices and places to send money, often the only ones for many kilometres.

Try this

Write a short letter or postcard to a grandparent or relative who lives far away, with their full address and PIN code. Before you post it, look up what each part of the PIN code stands for. Ask them to tell you how many days it took to arrive.
