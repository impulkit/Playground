Why do sea otters hold hands?

They link paws while they sleep so they don't drift apart.

The short answer

Sea otters sleep floating on their backs in the sea. Waves and currents could easily carry a sleeping otter away from its group. So otters sometimes hold paws while they snooze, or wrap themselves in long ribbons of seaweed called kelp, which is anchored to the seabed.

Groups of floating otters are called rafts. Some rafts are just a handful of otters. Others have dozens, or even hundreds, bobbing together like a floating mat.

Mothers and pups stay especially close. A pup sleeps on its mother's belly, and when she dives for food, she may tie it up in kelp so it can't float off.

Surprising but true

Sea otters have the thickest fur of any animal, up to about 150,000 hairs on a patch the size of your fingertip.

Warm without fat

Most sea mammals, like seals and whales, stay warm with thick blubber. Sea otters have hardly any. Instead they have the thickest fur of any animal. A patch the size of your fingertip can hold about as many hairs as a whole human head.

The fur traps a layer of air that keeps cold water away from the skin. That only works if it stays clean and fluffy, so otters spend hours each day grooming, blowing air into their fur and rolling about. A newborn pup's fur holds so much air that the pup can't sink, even if it tries.

Otters also eat a lot. Each day they gulp down about a quarter of their own weight in crabs, clams, urchins and other sea creatures, and burning all that food keeps them warm.

Tool users

Sea otters float on their backs with a flat rock on their chest and smash clams and sea urchins against it. They often keep a favourite rock in a pocket of loose skin under their arm. Pups learn the trick by watching their mothers, and some otters keep using the same rock for days. Along parts of the California coast, otters bashing mussels on the same boulders year after year have left worn, polished patches on the stone. They are among the few mammals apart from humans and other primates that use tools, and they share the habit with clever birds like crows.

Guardians of the kelp forest

Kelp grows in tall underwater forests along the coasts of California and Alaska. Sea urchins eat kelp. Otters eat sea urchins. In the 1970s, scientists compared islands in Alaska with and without otters. Where otters had disappeared, urchins had chewed whole forests down to bare rock. Where otters lived, the kelp forests were thick and full of fish.

An animal that holds a whole habitat together like this is called a keystone species, after the top stone that holds up an arch.

Saved from the fur hunters

From the 1700s onward, hunters killed sea otters for their fur all along the North Pacific, from Russia to California. By 1911 only a few small groups were left, and an international treaty finally protected them. In 1938, people spotted a small group off Big Sur in California, which many had thought was empty of otters. California's otters today descend from them.

They still face dangers. Oil ruins their fur. In 1989, the Exxon Valdez oil spill in Alaska killed thousands of them. At the Monterey Bay Aquarium, female otters now foster orphaned pups and teach them to dive and use tools before the pups are released into the wild.

India has no sea otters, but it does have river otters. Smooth-coated otters live along rivers like the Chambal and in the mangroves of the Sundarbans.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Sea otters stay warm under a thick layer of blubber, like seals.

Truth: Sea otters have almost no blubber. Their warmth comes from dense fur and from eating huge amounts of food, which keeps their bodies burning energy like a furnace.

Try this

Put a few drops of cooking oil on a feather or a fluffy piece of wool, then dip it in a bowl of water next to a clean one. Squeeze both gently. The oily one goes flat and soggy and loses its trapped air. That is why an oil spill is so dangerous for a sea otter.
