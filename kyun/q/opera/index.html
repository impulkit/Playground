What is opera?

An opera singer can fill a hall of 3,000 people with sound and be heard over a full orchestra, with no microphone at all.

The short answer

Opera is a play in which the story is sung rather than spoken, with an orchestra playing alongside. It combines singing, acting, music, costumes and scenery, often on a grand scale. Opera began in Italy around 1600 and spread across Europe and then the world.

Opera singers are famous for their huge, unamplified voices. They train for years to sing with power and control, and to be heard over an entire orchestra in large halls without microphones. Some operas are comedies, some are tragedies, and some are fairy tales or myths. The word "opera" simply means "work" in Italian.

Surprising but true

There is a famous American opera about Gandhi's early years in South Africa, and it is sung entirely in Sanskrit.

Born in Florence

In the late 1500s, a group of poets, musicians and thinkers in Florence, Italy, wanted to bring back the drama of ancient Greece. They believed Greek plays had been sung, not spoken. So they began writing plays set entirely to music.

The composer Jacopo Peri wrote some of the first, including Euridice in 1600. In 1607, Claudio Monteverdi composed L'Orfeo, about the Greek musician Orpheus who goes into the underworld to rescue his wife. It is the oldest opera still regularly performed today. Within a few decades, public opera houses opened in Venice, and anyone with a ticket could watch.

Arias and recitatives

Most operas switch between two kinds of singing. In a recitative, characters sing quickly, almost like speech, to move the story on. In an aria, the story pauses and a character sings a long, beautiful song about their feelings. Arias are the parts audiences remember and hum on the way home.

Singers are grouped by how high their voices go. From highest to lowest, they include soprano, mezzo-soprano, tenor, baritone and bass. Heroines are often sopranos, heroes tenors, and villains or old kings basses.

The great composers

Many of the most famous operas come from the 1700s and 1800s. Mozart wrote The Magic Flute and The Marriage of Figaro. Giuseppe Verdi, from Italy, wrote Aida, set in ancient Egypt. Richard Wagner, from Germany, wrote The Ring of the Nibelung, four connected operas that take about 15 hours to perform over several evenings.

Giacomo Puccini's Madama Butterfly and La Bohème still fill opera houses. In New York, the Metropolitan Opera, founded in 1883, is one of the largest opera companies in the world.

How the voice carries

An opera singer's power comes from training, not shouting. Singers breathe deeply from the belly, using the diaphragm, and keep their throat open and relaxed. They learn to boost a band of high frequencies, around 3,000 vibrations per second, that orchestras do not cover much. That ringing quality lets the voice slice through the music to the back row.

Opera and India

Several European operas are set in India, though often in a fanciful way. Léo Delibes's Lakmé (1883) takes place in British India, and its "Flower Duet" is now heard in films and adverts around the world.

In 1980, the American composer Philip Glass wrote Satyagraha, about Gandhi's years in South Africa. Its words come from the Bhagavad Gita, sung in Sanskrit. It has been performed at the Metropolitan Opera in New York. India has its own traditions of musical drama too, from Sanskrit theatre to Parsi theatre and village performances, which use song to tell stories in a similar way.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Opera is always sung in Italian.

Truth: Many famous operas are in German, French, Russian, Czech or English. Philip Glass's opera Satyagraha, about Gandhi, is sung in Sanskrit. Today most opera houses show translations above the stage so audiences can follow.

Try this

Stand at one end of a large room or a school hall and say a sentence in your normal voice. Then take a deep breath from your belly, open your mouth wide and say it again with a tall, open throat, without shouting. Ask a friend at the other end which carried better.
