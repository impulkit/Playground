What do judges do?

A judge's job is to listen to both sides, apply the law, and decide, even when the most powerful person in the room is the one on trial.

The short answer

Judges decide disputes. When two people, two companies, or a person and the government disagree about what the law says or what really happened, they can go to court. A judge listens to both sides, looks at the evidence, and decides according to the law.

The key rule is fairness. A judge must not take sides in advance or decide because of who someone is. In criminal cases, the accused person is treated as innocent until the evidence proves otherwise. A judge's decision is called a verdict or a judgement.

Surprising but true

Judges in India do not use a wooden hammer, or gavel. That is mostly an American habit.

A ladder of courts

Most cases start in a local court. In India, these are district courts. If someone thinks the decision was wrong, they can appeal to a higher court, which checks the reasoning. Above the district courts are 25 High Courts, and at the very top is the Supreme Court in Delhi.

The United States has two ladders side by side, one for each state's laws and one for federal laws. Both lead up to the US Supreme Court in Washington, D.C., where nine justices sit together.

Guarding the rulebook

The highest courts have a special job. They check whether laws and government actions follow the Constitution. If Parliament passes a law that breaks someone's fundamental rights, a court can strike it down. This is called judicial review.

That is why judges need to be independent. In the US, federal judges serve for life unless removed for serious wrongdoing, so they need not fear losing their jobs for an unpopular decision. Indian Supreme Court judges retire at 65, and they too can be removed only through a hard process in Parliament.

Evidence and argument

Judges decide using evidence: documents, witnesses, photographs, and scientific clues like DNA or fingerprints. Lawyers for each side present their case and question the other side's witnesses. The judge keeps the hearing fair, decides which evidence counts, and explains the decision in writing so others can check it.

Many cases are not about crimes at all. Neighbours argue over a wall, a shop refuses to refund a broken fridge, or a family disagrees about who inherits a house. These are civil cases, and the judge's job is to decide who is right and what should be done to fix it, often by ordering payment.

Juries and judges

In the US, many trials use a jury, often 12 ordinary citizens chosen at random, who decide whether the accused is guilty. The judge runs the trial and decides the punishment. India once used juries too, but after a famous Bombay case in 1959, in which a jury's verdict was overturned by the High Court, the country phased them out. Now Indian judges decide on their own.

Robes, symbols and a long queue

Indian judges and lawyers wear black robes with white neckbands, a habit inherited from British courts. A statue of Lady Justice, a woman holding scales, stands in many courts worldwide. Traditionally she is blindfolded, to show the law does not care who you are. In 2024, a new statue in India's Supreme Court library showed her with open eyes, holding the Constitution instead of a sword.

India's courts are very busy. More than 4 crore cases are waiting to be decided, and some take years. People often say "justice delayed is justice denied", and reducing these delays is one of the biggest jobs facing India's judges and lawmakers.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Judges in every country bang a gavel to keep order.

Truth: Gavels are mostly used in American courts and meetings. Judges in India and Britain do not use them. They keep order with their voice and their authority.

Try this

Play judge with a made-up case, like "Who ate the last biscuit?" Give two people a few minutes each to argue their side, with a piece of "evidence" like crumbs. As judge, you may only decide using what was said and shown, not who you like more. Explain your decision out loud.
