What is the Navajo Nation?

Inside the USA is a nation with its own president, courts and language, on land bigger than ten US states.

The short answer

The Navajo Nation is the largest Native American reservation in the United States. It covers about 71,000 square kilometres of desert, canyons and mesas where Arizona, Utah and New Mexico meet. That is bigger than ten of the fifty US states, and a little larger than Sri Lanka.

The Navajo people call themselves the Diné, which means "the People". They are a sovereign nation, which means they govern themselves within the USA. They have their own elected president, a council, police, courts and flag. About 4 lakh people were enrolled as Navajo citizens in 2021, though many live outside the reservation. Navajo citizens are also American citizens, and they vote in US elections too.

Surprising but true

In 2013, the film Star Wars was dubbed into the Navajo language, so that Luke Skywalker and Darth Vader could speak Diné bizaad.

Land between four mountains

In Diné tradition, the homeland is bounded by four sacred mountains, one in each direction. The land inside holds famous places. Monument Valley, with its tall red rock towers, is a Navajo tribal park that has appeared in countless Western films. Canyon de Chelly has cliffs streaked dark red, with ruins of old stone houses tucked into alcoves.

The traditional Navajo home is the hogan, a round or eight-sided house of logs and earth. Its doorway faces east, so the first thing to enter each morning is the rising sun. Many families still keep a hogan for ceremonies beside their modern house.

Life here can be hard. Many homes are far from shops and hospitals, and some estimates suggest about a third of households lack running water. Families often haul water in tanks from distant wells, so getting water to every home is one of the Nation's big goals.

The Long Walk

In 1864, the US Army forced thousands of Navajo from their homeland. They were made to march hundreds of kilometres to a camp called Bosque Redondo in New Mexico, a journey remembered as the Long Walk. Many people died on the way and in the camp, from cold, hunger and disease.

In 1868, Navajo leaders signed a treaty with the United States that let them return home. Very few of the Native nations forced off their land were ever allowed to go back. The Navajo were, and their reservation grew larger in the years that followed.

A language that won a war

The Navajo language, Diné bizaad, is complex, with tones and verbs that change in many ways. During the Second World War, the US Marines recruited Navajo men to send radio messages in a code built from their language. About 400 Navajo code talkers served in the Pacific, and the enemy never broke their code.

For years the code was secret, and the men could not talk about it. They were honoured with medals by the US Congress in 2001.

Fewer children grow up speaking Navajo today, so schools and families work to keep it alive. In 2013, the original Star Wars film was dubbed into Navajo, and audiences packed cinemas to hear it.

Weaving, silver and sheep

Sheep came to the region with the Spanish in the 1500s, and the Navajo became expert herders. Navajo weavers spin and dye the wool and weave rugs and blankets on upright looms. Each rug can take weeks or months, with bold patterns of zigzags and diamonds.

Navajo silversmiths make jewellery set with blue turquoise. And in a state that ignores daylight saving time, the Navajo Nation does change its clocks, so that it keeps the same time across its land in Arizona, Utah and New Mexico.

Myth or fact?

Myth: All Native Americans are one people with one language and one culture.

Truth: The US government recognises over 570 separate tribal nations, and they speak many different languages. The Navajo language is as different from Cherokee or Lakota as Tamil is from Punjabi.
