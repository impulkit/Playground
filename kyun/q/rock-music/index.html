How did rock and roll start?

Before Elvis, a gospel singer named Sister Rosetta Tharpe was already playing electric guitar in a way that would shape rock and roll.

The short answer

Rock and roll took off in the USA in the early to mid 1950s. It mixed rhythm and blues, the dance music of Black America, with gospel and country music. The result was loud, fast and built for dancing, with a pounding beat and the twang of the electric guitar.

Teenagers loved it. For the first time, young people had money to spend on records and radios, and they wanted music of their own. Within a few years, stars like Chuck Berry, Little Richard and Elvis Presley had made rock and roll the most exciting sound in the country, and soon in much of the world.

Surprising but true

Before the name

Many of the ingredients of rock and roll were already there in the 1940s. Rhythm and blues bands played hard-driving dance music with honking saxophones. Gospel singers in Black churches sang with huge energy.

Sister Rosetta Tharpe, a gospel singer from Arkansas, played electric guitar with a style and power that astonished audiences in the 1930s and 1940s. Later stars named her as an influence. In 1951, a record called Rocket 88, with a fuzzy, distorted guitar, became a hit. Some people call it the first rock and roll record, though others pick different songs.

The beat that made people move

Count "1, 2, 3, 4" along with almost any rock song. The drummer hits the snare drum hardest on 2 and 4. This is called the backbeat, and it is the engine of rock and roll. Clapping on 2 and 4 is part of gospel music too.

The electric guitar gave the music its bite. Chuck Berry's guitar opening to Johnny B. Goode, from 1958, is one of the most copied riffs in music.

A music that crossed lines

In the 1950s, much of the USA was segregated: Black and white Americans were kept apart by law and custom, as you can read in civil rights. Radio stations and record shops often split music into "Black" and "white".

Rock and roll crossed those lines. White teenagers listened to Black artists, and concerts sometimes had mixed crowds, which upset some adults. But it was not fair for everyone. Black musicians often earned far less than white singers who recorded "cover" versions of their songs, and many never got full credit. Historians still point to this as part of rock and roll's story.

The King and the critics

Elvis Presley, a young singer from Memphis, Tennessee, became the biggest star of all. His first records, made in 1954 at a small studio called Sun Records, mixed blues and country. His hip-shaking moves on television shocked some parents and thrilled their children.

Many adults thought rock and roll was noisy and a bad influence. Some radio stations refused to play it. That only made teenagers like it more.

Rock goes around the world

In the 1960s, British bands like the Beatles and the Rolling Stones took rock and roll, added their own ideas, and sent it back across the Atlantic. In 1965, Beatle George Harrison played sitar on a Beatles song. He later studied with the Indian sitar player Ravi Shankar.

In 1968, the Beatles spent weeks at an ashram in Rishikesh, learning meditation and writing songs, as you can read in the Beatles in India. Rock went on to split into many styles, from heavy metal to punk. India has its own rock scene too, with bands singing in English, Hindi and many other Indian languages.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Elvis Presley invented rock and roll.

Truth: Elvis became its biggest star, but rock and roll grew from the music of Black artists such as Sister Rosetta Tharpe, Chuck Berry and Little Richard, as well as country musicians. Elvis himself named some of them as influences.

Try this

Put on any rock or pop song you like. Count "1, 2, 3, 4" along with it, and clap only on 2 and 4. Then try clapping on 1 and 3 instead. Notice how different the song feels, and which way makes you want to move.
