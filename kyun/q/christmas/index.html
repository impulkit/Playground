How did Christmas traditions begin?

Nobody knows the actual day Jesus was born. The date of 25 December was first recorded in Rome more than 300 years later.

The short answer

Christmas is the Christian festival that celebrates the birth of Jesus. Most churches mark it on 25 December, with special services, carols, decorations and family meals. It has also become a holiday enjoyed by many people who are not Christian.

Its customs come from many places and times. The date was fixed in Rome in the 300s. Christmas trees came from Germany, cards from Victorian London, and much of today's Santa Claus from the USA in the 1800s. Over centuries, each country has added its own food, songs and traditions.

Surprising but true

Many Orthodox Christians, in countries such as Russia, Ethiopia and Serbia, celebrate Christmas on 7 January, because their churches still use the older Julian calendar.

Choosing a date

The Bible does not say on what day Jesus was born. The earliest record of a Christmas celebration on 25 December comes from a Roman calendar for the year 336 CE.

Historians have different ideas about why that day was chosen. Some think it was meant to replace Roman winter festivals held around the same time. Others point to early Christian calculations about Jesus's life. Many Orthodox churches still follow the older Julian calendar, so their 25 December falls on what most of the world calls 7 January.

Trees, cards and carols

By the 1500s, people in Germany were bringing fir trees into their homes and decorating them with apples and sweets. Evergreen trees, which stay green through winter, had long been symbols of life. In 1848 a London newspaper printed a picture of Queen Victoria's family around a decorated tree, and the custom spread quickly in Britain and America.

In 1843, Henry Cole had the first Christmas cards printed in London. Carols, songs sung at Christmas, are much older. Many popular ones, such as "Silent Night", first sung in Austria in 1818, are still sung around the world in hundreds of languages.

Why do calendars have 12 months?September means "seventh month", but it's the ninth. That clue goes back to an old Roman calendar that started in March.

From Saint Nicholas to Santa

Santa Claus began with Saint Nicholas, a bishop who lived in Myra, in what is now Turkey, in the 300s. Many stories describe him as kind and generous to the poor. In the Netherlands, his feast became a day for children's gifts, and his Dutch name, Sinterklaas, later became Santa Claus.

Dutch settlers brought the custom to New York. In 1823, an American poem, "A Visit from St. Nicholas", described Santa landing on rooftops with eight reindeer. From the 1860s, the cartoonist Thomas Nast drew Santa as a round, bearded man for a popular magazine, and that picture stuck.

Christmas in India

About 2 to 3 per cent of Indians are Christian, which is still millions of people. Christmas, sometimes called Bada Din, "the big day", is a national holiday. In Goa and Kerala, churches hold midnight Mass and homes hang paper star lanterns.

In Nagaland, Mizoram and Meghalaya, where most people are Christian, whole towns decorate for weeks, as northeast India describes. In Kolkata, Park Street is strung with lights, and bakeries sell rich plum cake. Many families of every faith buy a cake or visit the lights.

Christmas in the USA

In the United States, Christmas has been a federal holiday since 1870. Families decorate trees, hang stockings and leave cookies and milk for Santa. Cities put up giant trees, such as the one at New York's Rockefeller Center, lit every year since the 1930s.

For many families, Christmas is a religious celebration; for others, it is mostly a time for family, food and giving. Both kinds of celebration sit side by side. The days that follow lead straight into the New Year, which is why late December is holiday season in so many countries.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Coca-Cola invented Santa Claus's red suit.

Truth: Santa was often shown in red long before Coca-Cola's famous advertisements of the 1930s. The adverts did help fix the image of a big, jolly Santa in red and white in many people's minds.
