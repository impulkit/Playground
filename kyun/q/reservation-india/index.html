What is reservation in India?

India's Constitution sets aside seats in Parliament, colleges and government jobs for groups that were shut out for centuries. Few policies are argued about more.

The short answer

Reservation is India's system of setting aside a share of places for groups that faced discrimination for a very long time. It applies to government jobs, seats in public colleges, and seats in Parliament, state assemblies and village councils.

The main groups are Scheduled Castes, communities once treated as "untouchable" under the caste system, and Scheduled Tribes, many of them Adivasi communities. Other Backward Classes and, since 2019, poorer families outside these groups have reserved places too. The idea is that equal rules are not enough when some people start the race far behind the line.

Surprising but true

In India's Lok Sabha, 131 of the 543 seats are reserved, and only candidates from Scheduled Castes or Scheduled Tribes can stand for them, though everyone in the constituency votes.

Why the Constitution included it

When India's Constitution was written in the late 1940s, millions of Dalits were still barred from temples, wells and schools. B. R. Ambedkar, who chaired the drafting committee and was himself born into a Dalit family, argued that freedom meant little without a fair share of power and opportunity.

So the Constitution allowed the state to make "special provision" for these groups. Seats in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies were reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, at first for ten years. Parliament has extended that time limit again and again ever since.

Who was B.R. Ambedkar?As a boy he wasn't allowed to drink from the school tap. As a man he led the team that wrote India's Constitution.

How the numbers came about

Reserved shares for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes roughly follow their share of the population. In central government jobs and colleges, that means 15% and 7.5%.

In 1980 the Mandal Commission recommended reservation for Other Backward Classes, a large group of castes that were poor but not "untouchable". When the government brought in 27% for them in 1990, there were huge protests, especially by students. In 1992 the Supreme Court upheld the policy, but said reservation should usually not go above half of all places. It also said the better-off families within these groups, called the "creamy layer", should not benefit.

In 2019, a constitutional amendment added 10% for poorer families from groups that had no reservation before.

In practice, take a public engineering college that fills its seats through one national exam. Everyone sits the same paper. The open seats go to the top scorers of any background, and the reserved seats go to the top scorers within each category. Many states run their own reservation lists for state jobs and colleges, with different shares, so the rules in Tamil Nadu are not the same as those in Punjab.

Women in the village council

One form of reservation has changed rural India quietly. Since 1993, at least a third of seats in panchayats must go to women, and many states have raised this to half. As a result, lakhs of women now serve as village councillors and sarpanches. In 2023 Parliament passed a law to reserve a third of Lok Sabha and assembly seats for women. It is due to take effect after a new census and a redrawing of constituencies.

Why people disagree

Supporters point out that caste still shapes who gets good jobs, who owns land and who is treated with respect. They say reservation has created teachers, doctors and officers from families that had none, and that it is still needed.

Critics argue that seats should go by merit alone, or by income instead of caste. Others worry that benefits go mostly to the better-off families within each group. In 2024 the Supreme Court allowed states to divide reserved quotas into sub-groups, partly for this reason.

Other countries face similar questions. The United States used affirmative action in college admissions for decades, until its Supreme Court ended race-conscious admissions in 2023.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Reservation means anyone from a reserved group gets a seat, even without the marks.

Truth: Candidates in reserved categories still sit the same exams and must meet a minimum standard. They compete among themselves for the reserved seats, and they can also win open seats on merit.

Try this

Run a fair race with a twist. Ask one friend to start five steps behind the line and see who wins. Then talk about what would make the race fair. Does everyone start at the same line, or do some people get a head start to make up the difference? Notice how different people answer.
