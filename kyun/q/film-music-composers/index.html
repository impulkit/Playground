Who are India's great film composers?

Ilaiyaraaja grew up in a Tamil village with no formal music training at first and went on to compose music for about 1,000 films.

The short answer

India makes more films than almost any country, and most of them have songs. The composer, often called the music director, writes those songs and the background music. Over a hundred years, a handful of composers have shaped how India sounds.

Among the most admired are Naushad, S.D. Burman, Salil Chowdhury, R.D. Burman, Ilaiyaraaja, A.R. Rahman and M.M. Keeravani. Each mixed Indian classical and folk music with sounds from around the world, and each changed what a film song could be.

Surprising but true

Alam Ara, India's first sound film, came out in 1931 with seven songs. Indian films have been full of songs from the very first day they could talk.

Ragas on the big screen

In 1931 Alam Ara, India's first talking film, came out with seven songs. Ever since, music has been at the heart of Indian films. The 1950s and 60s are often called the golden age of Hindi film music.

Naushad drew deeply on Hindustani classical music. For Mughal-e-Azam (1960) he built grand songs around ragas, with large choirs and orchestras. S.D. Burman, from a royal family in Tripura, brought in the folk tunes of Bengal and the boatmen's songs of its rivers. Salil Chowdhury mixed Bengali folk with Western harmony, inspired by the classical music he loved. Many of these songs were sung by Lata Mangeshkar, whose voice reached almost every home in India.

The rhythm man

S.D. Burman's son, Rahul Dev Burman, known as R.D. or Pancham, loved experimenting. He mixed rock, jazz and Latin American rhythms with Indian melodies. He is said to have made sounds by blowing across bottles and rubbing a comb, just to get a new texture.

His music for Sholay (1975) and dozens of other films made the 1970s sound young and daring. Many musicians today still borrow his beats.

How did Bollywood begin?India's first feature film had no sound, no colour and no actresses. The queen was played by a man.

A village boy and a symphony

In Tamil Nadu, Ilaiyaraaja grew up in the village of Pannaipuram, singing folk songs with his brothers. He later studied Western classical music and became a master of both. His first film as a composer came out in 1976.

He went on to score music for about 1,000 films, mostly in Tamil, Telugu, Kannada and Malayalam. He often wrote parts for every instrument himself, quickly and by hand. In 2025 he premiered his own symphony in London. His work helped make South Indian cinema famous for its music.

The sound of the 1990s and beyond

In 1992 a young keyboard player from Chennai, A.R. Rahman, wrote the music for the Tamil film Roja. It sounded like nothing before it, with crisp electronic beats, folk voices and huge, clean sound. Its songs became hits across India.

Rahman went on to win two Oscars in 2009 for Slumdog Millionaire. In 2023 M.M. Keeravani won an Oscar for "Naatu Naatu", the dance song from the Telugu film RRR. Its fast, stomping beat had people dancing in cinemas from Hyderabad to Los Angeles.

How a film song gets made

A composer rarely works alone. The director explains the scene, perhaps a wedding, a chase or a sad goodbye. The composer hums or plays a tune, a lyricist writes the words, and an arranger decides which instruments play when. In the old days, dozens of musicians crammed into one studio and recorded together, so one mistake meant starting again from the top.

Many more names

Lists like this always leave people out. Shankar-Jaikishan wrote dozens of hits for Raj Kapoor's films. The duo Laxmikant-Pyarelal worked on hundreds of films over about 35 years. In the 1930s Saraswati Devi became one of the first women to compose for Hindi films, at Bombay Talkies. Every language has its heroes, from M.S. Viswanathan in Tamil to Hemant Kumar in Bengali.

Ask three people for India's greatest film composer and you may get three answers. That's a sign of how many good ones there have been.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The actors in Indian films sing their own songs.

Truth: Almost always, professional playback singers record the songs and actors lip-sync. A few actors, like Kishore Kumar, were also singers, but they are the exception.

Try this

Pick a song you love from any film and listen with your eyes closed. Count how many different instruments you can pick out, such as a flute, tabla, guitar or violins. Then listen to a song from your grandparents' time and compare.
