Why is MS Dhoni called Captain Cool?

He once checked tickets on trains at Kharagpur station. A few years later he was lifting the World Cup with a six.

The short answer

Mahendra Singh Dhoni is called Captain Cool because he stayed calm in the tensest moments of cricket. When others panicked, he looked as if he was solving a puzzle. He led India to win the T20 World Cup in 2007, the 50-over World Cup in 2011 and the Champions Trophy in 2013. He is the only captain to win all three of these ICC trophies.

He was also a powerful batter and one of the quickest wicketkeepers ever. Fans especially loved the way he finished matches, often waiting until the last overs and then hitting the winning runs himself.

Surprising but true

Dhoni started as a football goalkeeper at school. His coach asked him to try keeping wicket for the cricket team because he was good at catching.

From Ranchi to the railways

Dhoni was born in Ranchi in 1981. The city was then in Bihar and is now in Jharkhand. Few big cricketers had come from there. At school he played badminton and was the goalkeeper of the football team. When the cricket team needed a wicketkeeper, he was asked to try.

He was good, but it took years to break through. From about 2001 to 2003 he worked as a travelling ticket examiner for Indian Railways at Kharagpur station in West Bengal. He played cricket for the railway team and kept practising.

The long hair and the big hits

In 2004 he made his India debut. In 2005 he hit 148 against Pakistan in Visakhapatnam and then 183 not out against Sri Lanka in Jaipur. Fans noticed his long, flowing hair as much as his sixes. He also had a special shot called the "helicopter", flicking yorkers over the leg side with a fast twist of the wrists.

Behind the stumps he was lightning quick. His stumpings sometimes happened faster than viewers could see without a replay, which tells you a lot about his reflexes.

What is a reflex?When you touch something hot, your hand jerks away before your brain has even worked out that it hurts.

Three trophies

In 2007 a young Indian team with Dhoni as captain won the first T20 World Cup in South Africa. In the final against Pakistan he gave the last over to Joginder Sharma, a surprising choice that worked.

On 2 April 2011, at the Wankhede Stadium in Mumbai, India needed four runs to win the World Cup final against Sri Lanka. Dhoni hit Nuwan Kulasekara for six. He had promoted himself up the batting order that night and scored 91 not out. In 2013 India also won the Champions Trophy in England. In between, in 2009, he led India to the top of the Test rankings for the first time. Many of the young players he backed, like Rohit Sharma and Virat Kohli, went on to become captains themselves.

Why so calm?

Dhoni rarely shouted at players or argued with umpires. He has said he tried to focus on the next ball, not the final score. Teammates said this helped young players feel safe under pressure.

He also made bold decisions quietly. He retired from Test cricket halfway through a series in Australia in 2014, and announced his international retirement on Instagram on 15 August 2020, India's Independence Day.

Thala of Chennai

In the IPL, Dhoni has captained Chennai Super Kings to five titles. Fans in Chennai call him "Thala", a Tamil word meaning head or leader. Yellow-shirted crowds fill stadiums across India just to watch him walk out to bat.

The Indian Army made him an honorary Lieutenant Colonel in the Territorial Army in 2011. He loves motorbikes, and keeps a large collection at his home in Ranchi, the city that now has a cricket stadium where he once dreamed of playing.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Dhoni never felt nervous during big matches.

Truth: Dhoni has said he did feel pressure, but focused on the process of each ball rather than the result. Staying calm on the outside was a skill he worked on.
