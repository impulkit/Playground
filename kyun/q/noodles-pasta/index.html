Who made the first noodles?

The oldest noodles ever found were made from millet about 4,000 years ago, and they were discovered still sitting in an upturned bowl.

The short answer

The oldest noodles ever found come from China. In 2005, scientists reported a bowl of thin, yellow noodles at Lajia, a site on the Yellow River in northwest China. An earthquake and flood about 4,000 years ago had buried the village. The bowl was upside down in the mud, and the noodles had stayed sealed underneath.

Tests suggested they were made from millet, a small grain that grew well in northern China. Later, Chinese cooks made noodles mostly from wheat. In Italy and the Arab world, people made their own kinds of pasta, probably without any help from China.

Surprising but true

Instant noodles were invented in 1958 by Momofuku Ando in Japan, who experimented in a shed in his back garden.

Long noodles, long life

Noodles became central to Chinese food over thousands of years. By about 2,000 years ago, people in China were making wheat noodles. In northern China, cooks still stretch and fold dough by hand into hundreds of thin strands, called lamian.

In China and other parts of East Asia, long noodles are a symbol of long life. People eat them on birthdays and try not to cut them. Noodles spread to Japan, Korea and Southeast Asia, each place making its own kind, from Japanese ramen to Vietnamese pho.

Pasta in Italy

Italian pasta has its own story. A geographer named al-Idrisi, writing in 1154 for the king of Sicily, described a town near Palermo where people made dried pasta and shipped it around the Mediterranean. Sicily had been ruled by Arabs for about 200 years, and they knew how to dry noodles for long journeys.

Dried pasta is made from durum wheat, a very hard wheat that holds its shape when cooked. Italy now has hundreds of shapes, from spaghetti, meaning "little strings", to bow-shaped farfalle, meaning "butterflies".

The Marco Polo myth

Many people believe Marco Polo brought pasta back from China. But he returned in 1295, and pasta was already being made in Italy long before. Historians traced the story to an article in an American pasta trade magazine in 1929.

What did Marco Polo see?A young Venetian spent about 17 years in China, and Europeans refused to believe half of what he told them, including that people paid with paper money.

Instant noodles

In 1958, Momofuku Ando, a businessman in Osaka, Japan, invented instant noodles after a year of experiments in a shed behind his house. He found that frying cooked noodles dried them out and left tiny holes. Hot water could then soften them in minutes.

In 1971, he launched Cup Noodles, sold in a cup with a lid. Instant noodles are now eaten around the world, with more than 100 billion servings eaten each year (2020s figures). In 2005, a version was made for the Japanese astronaut Soichi Noguchi to eat on the Space Shuttle, as you can read in space food.

Noodles in India

India has noodles of its own. Seviyan, thin wheat strands, are cooked with milk and sugar as a sweet, especially at Eid. In Kerala and Tamil Nadu, idiyappam, made by pressing rice dough into fine strings and steaming it, is a breakfast dish. Crispy sev, made from gram flour, is a snack.

Chinese immigrants who settled in Kolkata more than two centuries ago helped create Indo-Chinese food, including hakka noodles and chilli chicken. Instant noodles arrived in India in the 1980s and quickly became an after-school favourite.

Millets come back

The first noodles were made from millet, and millets are becoming popular again. They grow with little water and are packed with fibre and minerals. Some Indian companies now make noodles from ragi, jowar and other millets, which would not have looked strange to cooks at Lajia 4,000 years ago.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Marco Polo brought pasta to Italy from China.

Truth: Marco Polo returned to Italy in 1295, but dried pasta was already made in Sicily by 1154, and a document from Genoa mentions pasta in 1279. The Marco Polo story was spread by an American magazine in the 1920s.

Try this

Mix half a cup of flour with a little water to make a firm dough. Roll it into a thin sheet on a clean surface, then cut it into strips with a butter knife. Compare your noodles with dried pasta from a packet. Let yours dry overnight and see how they change.
