How do you measure area and volume?

Double the size of a cube and it needs four times the paint to cover it, but it holds eight times as much water.

The short answer

Area tells you how much flat surface something covers, like the floor of your classroom. You measure it by counting how many squares of a set size would cover it, such as square centimetres (cm²) or square metres (m²). For a rectangle, you multiply length by width.

Volume tells you how much space something takes up, or how much it can hold, like a water tank. You measure it in cubes, such as cubic centimetres (cm³) or cubic metres (m³). For a box, you multiply length by width by height. A cube 10 cm on each side holds exactly 1 litre.

Surprising but true

An Olympic swimming pool holds at least 2.5 million litres of water, enough to fill roughly 12,500 bathtubs.

Counting squares

Suppose your bedroom floor is 4 m long and 3 m wide. Picture square tiles, each 1 m by 1 m. You could fit 4 in a row, and 3 rows, so 12 tiles. The area is 12 m².

Not every shape is a rectangle. A triangle covers half the area of the rectangle drawn around it, so its area is half the base times the height. A circle's area is π times the radius times the radius. That is where pi, about 3.14, comes in.

Land is often measured in bigger units. A hectare is 10,000 m², a square 100 m on each side. In India, many people buy and sell land in older local units too, like the bigha or gaj (a square yard). A bigha varies from state to state, so two families in villages in different states might mean quite different sizes by the same word.

Counting cubes

Volume works the same way, but in three directions. A box 5 cm long, 4 cm wide and 3 cm high holds 5 × 4 × 3 = 60 little cubes, each 1 cm on every side. Its volume is 60 cm³, which is also 60 millilitres.

An Olympic swimming pool is 50 m long and 25 m wide, and at least 2 m deep. That gives at least 2,500 m³. Since each cubic metre holds 1,000 litres, the pool needs at least 2.5 million litres of water.

Measuring a strange shape

How do you find the volume of a stone, a toy or your own hand? You can't multiply length by width by height. But you can drop it into water. The water level rises by exactly the volume of the object, because the object pushes that much water out of the way.

The Greek thinker Archimedes understood this more than 2,000 years ago. He also worked out why objects float: a floating ship pushes aside water that weighs as much as the whole ship.

Why size changes everything

Here is a surprising rule. If you double every length of a shape, its area grows four times, but its volume grows eight times. Make it ten times bigger and the area grows 100 times, but the volume 1,000 times.

This explains a lot about living things. An animal's weight depends on its volume, but the strength of its bones and legs depends on their cross-section, which is an area. So a giant animal needs very thick legs. That is why an elephant has legs like pillars, while a mouse can have thin ones. It is also why a giant insect the size of a car, from a science-fiction film, would collapse under its own weight.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Archimedes shouted "Eureka" and ran naked through the streets after discovering how to measure volume in his bath.

Truth: The story was written down by the Roman writer Vitruvius about 200 years after Archimedes lived, and may be a legend. Archimedes certainly understood how objects push aside water, but nobody knows if the bath scene really happened.

Try this

Fill a small box, like a matchbox or a juice carton, with water or dry rice. Measure its length, width and height in centimetres and multiply them. Then pour the contents into a measuring jug. Is the number of millilitres close to your answer? It should be, because 1 cm³ is 1 ml.
