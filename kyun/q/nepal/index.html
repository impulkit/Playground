What is Nepal like?

Nepal is the only country whose flag is not a rectangle, and its clocks run 15 minutes ahead of India's.

The short answer

Nepal is a small country tucked between India and China. Most of it is mountains. In the far north are the high Himalayas, including Mount Everest. In the middle are green hills, terraced fields and river valleys. In the south is a strip of flat, warm land called the Terai, which looks much like the plains of Bihar and Uttar Pradesh next door.

Nepal is about 3 crore people strong and very diverse, with over a hundred ethnic groups and many languages. Nepali is the most widely spoken. The country is famous for its mountains, its temples and stupas, and for being the birthplace of the Buddha.

Surprising but true

In a country only about 200 km wide from north to south, the land climbs from under 100 m above sea level to the top of Mount Everest at 8,849 m.

A staircase to the sky

Eight of the world's fourteen mountains over 8,000 m stand in Nepal or on its borders, including Everest, Kanchenjunga and Annapurna. In 2020, Nepal and China announced together that Everest is 8,848.86 m tall, after a joint survey.

The climb from the Terai to the peaks happens in a very short distance, so Nepal has a bit of everything. There are jungles with tigers and one-horned rhinos in Chitwan National Park, pine forests in the hills, and glaciers and snow leopards at the top. Walking for a week in Nepal can feel like walking from Kerala to the Arctic.

Which is the tallest mountain on Earth?Everest is the highest. But measured from its base on the sea floor, a mountain in Hawaii is taller.

Temples, stupas and the Buddha

In about the 5th or 6th century BCE, Siddhartha Gautama, who became the Buddha, was born at Lumbini, in what is now southern Nepal. In 249 BCE the emperor Ashoka visited and set up a stone pillar with an inscription marking the spot. It still stands, and Lumbini is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The Kathmandu Valley is crowded with temples and old palace squares. Pashupatinath, on the banks of the Bagmati, is one of the most important Hindu temples to Shiva. The great white domes of Boudhanath and Swayambhunath stupas are painted with watchful eyes. Hindu and Buddhist traditions mix closely here, and many families honour both.

Neighbours with an open door

India and Nepal share a border of about 1,750 km, and it is open. Citizens of the two countries can cross without a passport or visa, and many Nepalis live and work in India, and Indians in Nepal. Gorkha soldiers from Nepal have served in the Indian Army for over a century.

Nepal is also known for never having been ruled by a European power. It was a kingdom for most of its history, until it became a republic in 2008. Its clocks are set 5 hours 45 minutes ahead of world time, 15 minutes ahead of India. You can find out how countries choose their time in time zones.

When the mountains shake

The Himalayas are still rising, because India keeps pushing north into Asia. That slow crash builds up stress in the rocks, which is released as earthquakes.

On 25 April 2015, a magnitude 7.8 earthquake struck central Nepal. Nearly 9,000 people died, and many old temples in Kathmandu's squares collapsed. Since then, many have been carefully rebuilt using the original methods, and new buildings are meant to follow stronger safety rules.

A flag like no other

Every other country's flag is a rectangle, or in two cases a square. Nepal's is two stacked crimson pennants with a blue border. The shape is often linked to Himalayan peaks and to the triangular flags long flown at temples.

The moon and sun on it stand for the hope that the nation will last as long as they do. That is the kind of meaning you can explore in flag colours.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Mount Everest is entirely in Nepal.

Truth: The summit sits right on the border between Nepal and China (Tibet). Climbers can approach it from either side, though most go up from the Nepal side.
