Why can you float in the Dead Sea?

The Dead Sea is so salty that you can lie back and read a newspaper on the water. Its shore is the lowest dry land on Earth.

The short answer

The Dead Sea is a salt lake in the Middle East, with Jordan on its eastern shore and Israel and the West Bank on its western shore. Its water is so salty that about a third of its weight is dissolved salts. That makes it much heavier, or denser, than ordinary water.

Your body is about as dense as fresh water, so in a pool you just barely float. In the Dead Sea, the water is around a quarter heavier than you are. It pushes you up so strongly that you bob on top like a cork, with your feet and shoulders out of the water. Trying to swim face down is hard, and splashing is a bad idea, because the water stings your eyes badly.

Surprising but true

The Dead Sea's surface is dropping by about a metre every year, and thousands of sinkholes have opened along its shores.

Why it is so salty

The Dead Sea lies at the bottom of a long, deep valley formed where two pieces of the Earth's crust are sliding past each other. The water's surface is about 440 m below sea level (2025), lower than any other dry land on Earth.

Water flows in, mostly from the Jordan River, but it has nowhere to go. No river flows out. In the hot, dry desert, water evaporates fast and leaves its salts behind. Over thousands of years, those salts have built up to roughly ten times the saltiness of the ocean. It is among the saltiest lakes on Earth, although a few, such as Lake Assal in Djibouti and Don Juan Pond in Antarctica, are even saltier.

Floating, the science

Things float when they push aside, or displace, a weight of water equal to their own weight. This is called buoyancy. A steel ship floats this way, by pushing aside a lot of water with its wide hull.

How do huge steel ships float?A steel nail sinks in a bucket of water. A steel ship about as long as four football fields floats. The secret is shape.

In fresh water, your body has to sink almost completely before it has pushed aside enough water. In Dead Sea water, a litre weighs about 1.24 kg instead of 1 kg. You only need to sink part of the way before the water holds you up, so you float high. Visitors lie back, legs in the air, and some cover themselves in black mud from the shore. The minerals and mud are also sold around the world for skincare.

A sea that is shrinking

Since the 1960s, the Dead Sea has fallen by more than 30 m. In recent decades it has dropped by about a metre a year. Most of the Jordan River's water is now taken for farms and cities before it reaches the lake. Factories also pump Dead Sea water into shallow ponds to collect minerals like potash, which is used in fertiliser.

As the water retreats, fresh groundwater dissolves buried layers of salt under the old shore. The ground above collapses into sinkholes. Thousands have formed, swallowing parts of roads, beaches and farms. Hotels once built on the beach now sit a long walk from the water.

Scrolls and salt

The land around the Dead Sea, less than an hour's drive from Jerusalem, has long attracted people. In 1947, a young shepherd exploring caves at Qumran, near the northern shore, found clay jars holding ancient scrolls. The Dead Sea Scrolls, about 2,000 years old, include some of the oldest known copies of books of the Hebrew Bible.

On the southern shore, salt forms crusts, pillars and white rings around rocks. India has salt landscapes too. In the Rann of Kutch in Gujarat, a seasonal shallow sea dries into a vast white salt desert every winter. Neither place is a sea you can swim in for long, but only one lets you float like a beach ball.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Nothing at all lives in the Dead Sea.

Truth: There are no fish or plants, but tiny microbes live in the salty water. After very wet winters, when fresh water forms a layer on top, red algae and microbes have bloomed and turned parts of the sea reddish.

Try this

Put an egg in a glass of tap water and watch it sink. Take it out, stir in salt a spoonful at a time until no more will dissolve, then gently put the egg back. It should float. You have made a tiny Dead Sea.
