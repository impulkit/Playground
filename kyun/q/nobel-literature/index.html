What is the Nobel Prize in Literature?

Leo Tolstoy, author of War and Peace, never won it. Bob Dylan, a singer, did.

The short answer

The Nobel Prize in Literature is one of the world's best-known awards for writers. It has been given almost every year since 1901, to a novelist, poet, playwright or essayist whose work the judges consider outstanding.

It was set up by the will of Alfred Nobel, a Swedish chemist who invented dynamite and became very rich. When he died in 1896, he left most of his fortune to fund prizes in physics, chemistry, medicine, literature and peace. The literature prize is chosen by the Swedish Academy in Stockholm. The winner receives a gold medal, a diploma and a large sum of money, about 11 million Swedish kronor in recent years.

Surprising but true

The youngest person to win the Nobel Prize in Literature, Rudyard Kipling, was born in Bombay. He won in 1907, aged 41.

How the winner is chosen

Each year, the Swedish Academy invites nominations from qualified people: literature professors, members of similar academies, past winners and heads of writers' organisations. Writers can't nominate themselves. There are usually a few hundred nominations.

A small committee narrows these down to a shortlist of about five. Over the summer, Academy members read the shortlisted writers' works, often in translation. In October they vote, and the winner is announced at a press conference in Stockholm. The names of nominees are kept secret for 50 years.

The prize is handed over on 10 December, the anniversary of Nobel's death, at a ceremony in Stockholm. Each winner also gives a Nobel lecture, and many of these speeches are famous in their own right.

India and the Nobel

In 1913, Rabindranath Tagore became the first writer from outside Europe to win, mainly for his book of poems Gitanjali. His prize made news around the world and brought Indian literature to many new readers.

Rudyard Kipling, author of The Jungle Book, was born in Bombay in 1865 and spent his early childhood there. He won in 1907, at 41, and is still the youngest literature winner. V. S. Naipaul, a writer of Indian descent born in Trinidad, won in 2001.

Surprises and arguments

The Academy has often surprised people. In 1953, it gave the prize to Winston Churchill, Britain's wartime prime minister, for his history writing and speeches. In 2016 it went to Bob Dylan, the American singer, "for having created new poetic expressions within the great American song tradition". Some people cheered. Others said songs aren't literature.

Two writers turned the prize down. Jean-Paul Sartre declined it in 1964, saying a writer shouldn't become an institution. Boris Pasternak, author of Doctor Zhivago, was forced by the Soviet government to refuse it in 1958.

In 2018, no prize was announced at first, after a scandal inside the Academy. The next year two winners were named, one for 2018 and one for 2019.

Who gets left out

Critics point out that most winners have been men from Europe and North America. Great writers like Leo Tolstoy, Anton Chekhov and Virginia Woolf never won. Writers in less widely translated languages have a harder time getting noticed.

That has slowly been changing. Toni Morrison became the first Black woman to win in 1993, and the South Korean writer Han Kang became the first Asian woman to win in 2024. Like the Nobel Peace Prize and the science prizes that Marie Curie won twice, the literature prize gets more attention than almost any other award in its field. For many writers, it changes everything overnight.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Nobel Prize in Literature goes to the best book of the year.

Truth: It is given to a writer for their work as a whole, often after a long career. Many winners are not famous outside their own country until they win.
