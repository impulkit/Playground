How do robots drive on Mars?

A message to Mars can take 20 minutes to arrive, so the rovers have to think for themselves.

The short answer

Mars is so far away that a radio message takes between about 3 and 22 minutes to get there. It depends on where the two planets are in their orbits. So nobody can steer a rover live, the way you would steer a remote-control car.

Instead, every day scientists send the rover a list of goals: drive to that rock, take pictures, drill here. The rover uses its cameras and computer to map the ground in front of it, spot rocks and slopes, and choose a safe path by itself. Then it reports back with pictures, and the team plans the next day.

Surprising but true

In 2021, a tiny helicopter called Ingenuity made the first powered flight on another planet, on Mars.

Thinking for itself

The newest rovers are careful drivers. NASA's Perseverance takes pictures, builds a 3D map of the ground, picks a route and checks it again as it moves. It is slow on purpose. Its top speed is about 150 m an hour, slower than a walking tortoise. A stuck rover is a disaster, because there is no tow truck on Mars.

These are the same problems that self-driving cars on Earth face, with one big difference. A Mars rover can't stop and ask for help.

A family of rovers

The first Mars rover, Sojourner, landed in 1997. It was about the size of a microwave oven. In 2004, two solar-powered rovers arrived, named Spirit and Opportunity. Opportunity was meant to work for 90 days. It kept going for almost 15 years, until a giant dust storm covered its solar panels in 2018.

Curiosity landed in 2012 and has spent years slowly climbing a mountain inside Gale Crater. China landed its own rover, Zhurong, in 2021. Each mission taught engineers something new about robots that must work far from any mechanic.

Perseverance

Perseverance landed in February 2021 in Jezero Crater, which held a lake billions of years ago. Rivers once flowed into it and dropped mud and sand, the kind of place where tiny living things might have left traces. The rover is about the size of a car and runs on a nuclear battery that keeps it powered day and night.

It drills into rocks and seals cores the size of a piece of chalk inside metal tubes. Plans to bring those samples to Earth are still being worked out. It also carries microphones, and has recorded the sound of Martian wind.

A helicopter on Mars

Perseverance carried a small helicopter called Ingenuity, which weighed under 2 kg. Mars's air is about one hundredth as thick as Earth's, so its blades had to spin very fast, around 2,400 times a minute. In April 2021 it made the first powered, controlled flight on another planet. It was planned to fly five times. It flew 72 times before a damaged blade grounded it in 2024.

Landing is the hardest part

Getting a rover onto Mars safely is a challenge of its own. Curiosity and Perseverance came down using a heat shield, a parachute, rockets, and finally a "sky crane" that lowered each rover on cables and then flew away. Engineers called the last stretch the "seven minutes of terror", because it all had to happen automatically.

India reached Mars too. Mangalyaan went into orbit in 2014, on its first try, and studied the planet from above. Rovers see Mars from the ground, including its strange blue sunsets.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Mars is red, so it must be a hot planet.

Truth: Mars is red because its dust is full of iron oxide, the same stuff as rust. It is actually freezing, with an average temperature of about −60 °C.

Try this

Play rover. One person is the rover and keeps their eyes closed. The "mission control" team must write down every command first ("3 steps forward, turn right"), then read the whole list out at once and stay silent while the rover follows it. Use a clear room with cushions as rocks, and see how many tries it takes to reach a target.
