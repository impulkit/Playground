How are animated films made today?

In a modern animated film, every hair on a monster can be its own tiny object, and computers can spend hours drawing a single frame.

The short answer

Most big animated films today are made with computers. Artists build characters and worlds as 3D models, a bit like digital puppets. Animators then pose those puppets frame by frame, and powerful computers turn each frame into a finished picture with light, colour and shadow.

It is slow, careful work. A 90-minute film shown at 24 frames a second needs about 1,30,000 frames. Big studios such as Pixar and Disney in the USA, and smaller ones in India, Japan and Europe, may spend four years or more on one film.

Surprising but true

The furry monster Sulley in Monsters, Inc. (2001) had more than two million separate hairs, and Pixar had to write new software to make them move.

Story first, pictures later

Every film starts with a story, and the story changes many times. Artists draw thousands of quick sketches called storyboards, a bit like a comic of the whole film. These are filmed with rough voices to make a test version called a story reel.

If a joke falls flat or a scene is confusing, it is cheap to fix now and very expensive to fix later. Whole characters are sometimes cut. Then actors record the real voices, often alone in a sound booth, months or years before they see the finished film.

Building digital puppets

Modellers sculpt each character on a computer, out of a mesh of thousands of tiny flat shapes. Next, riggers give the model a hidden skeleton, with hundreds of controls for the eyebrows, lips, fingers and tail. Pulling one control might make a character smile.

Other artists paint the surfaces. Is the skin shiny or rough? Is the shirt made of wool or plastic? These choices change how light will bounce off later. Every chair, tree and cloud in the background needs the same treatment.

Animators as actors

Animators move the rigged puppets. They set a few key poses, and the computer fills in the frames between them. The animator then adjusts those in-between frames by hand, because computers move things too smoothly and evenly.

Good animators study how real things move. They film themselves acting a scene, watch animals at the zoo and think about weight. A heavy character lands with a thump and a slow recovery. A light one bounces. This is why some animators read up on falling and gravity as much as on drawing.

Hair, water and rendering

Some things are too complicated to animate by hand, such as flowing hair, splashing water, fluttering clothes or a crowd of thousands. Here, software simulates the physics. When Pixar made Monsters, Inc., it had to create new tools just to make Sulley's two million hairs sway naturally.

Finally comes rendering, when computers work out the colour of every pixel in every frame. A single frame can take hours. Studios use render farms, rooms full of thousands of linked computers working day and night. This is why the computers at a big studio are among the busiest anywhere.

Animation in India

India has a large animation industry. Studios in cities such as Mumbai, Hyderabad and Bengaluru do work for American and European films and series. Green Gold Animation in Hyderabad created Chhota Bheem, first shown in 2008, which became one of India's most popular children's cartoons.

Hand-drawn and stop-motion films have not disappeared either. Some directors still prefer the look of paint or clay, and audiences keep watching. The tools have changed, but the old idea is the same: still pictures, shown fast enough, start to breathe.

Myth or fact?

Myth: In computer animation, the computer does all the work.

Truth: The computer handles the drawing and the maths, but people decide every pose, expression and movement. A skilled animator might finish only a few seconds of film in a whole week.

Try this

Make a stop-motion film with a phone and some clay or toy blocks. Move your character a tiny bit, take a photo, and repeat at least 24 times. Play the photos quickly, one after another, in a free stop-motion app or the phone's gallery, and your character will walk.
