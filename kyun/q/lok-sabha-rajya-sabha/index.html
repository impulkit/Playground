What are the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha?

The Lok Sabha is dissolved and re-elected every five years. The Rajya Sabha never is. It has sat without a break since 1952, swapping members a third at a time.

The short answer

India's Parliament has two houses. The Lok Sabha, or House of the People, is directly elected by voters. The Rajya Sabha, or Council of States, represents India's states and union territories, and most of its members are elected by state legislators.

Both must pass a bill for it to become law. But the Lok Sabha is more powerful. The government needs its support to stay in office, and on money matters it has the final word. The Rajya Sabha is often described as the house of second thoughts. It checks, questions and sometimes slows down what the Lok Sabha passes. Both houses first met in May 1952.

Surprising but true

When the two houses of India's Parliament deadlock over a bill, they can meet together in a joint sitting. It has happened only three times since 1950.

The House of the People

The Lok Sabha has 543 elected members, each representing one constituency. Bigger states have more seats, so Uttar Pradesh has 80 and small states like Sikkim and Mizoram have one each. Members must be at least 25 years old. The number of seats each state gets is still based on the 1971 census, so that states which slowed their population growth would not lose seats. It is due to be redrawn after the first census taken after 2026.

The Lok Sabha is elected for five years, unless it is dissolved early. Whichever party or alliance has a majority, at least 272 seats, forms the government. It is chaired by the Speaker, who keeps order and decides who may speak. The Speaker is expected to be fair to all sides, even though they are elected as a member of a party.

The Council of States

The Rajya Sabha has up to 250 members. At present 233 are elected by the members of state and union territory assemblies, and 12 are nominated by the President for their work in art, literature, science or social service. The singer Lata Mangeshkar and the cricketer Sachin Tendulkar have both been nominated members.

Members must be at least 30 and serve six-year terms. The house is never dissolved. Instead, a third of its members retire every two years and are replaced, so it changes gradually. The Vice-President of India chairs it.

Who has the last word

For most bills, both houses must agree. If they disagree, the President can call a joint sitting, where members of both houses vote together. Since the Lok Sabha is more than twice as big, it usually wins. This has happened only three times: in 1961, 1978 and 2002.

Money bills, such as those about taxes, work differently. They can only start in the Lok Sabha. The Rajya Sabha can suggest changes within 14 days, but the Lok Sabha can ignore them.

The Rajya Sabha has its own special powers. With a two-thirds vote, it can allow Parliament to make laws on a subject normally left to the states, if it is in the national interest.

Two houses elsewhere

Many democracies have two houses. America's Congress has a House of Representatives, elected by population, and a Senate, with two senators from every state. Britain has the elected House of Commons and the House of Lords, whose members are appointed or inherit their seats.

India's system borrows from both. Like the US Senate, the Rajya Sabha speaks for the states, though bigger states send more members. Like Britain's Commons, the Lok Sabha decides who governs. Having two houses means laws get a second look before they reach everyone in the country.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Rajya Sabha is just a less important copy of the Lok Sabha.

Truth: It has a different job. It represents the states, reviews bills more slowly, and has some special powers, such as allowing Parliament to make laws on a state subject in the national interest.
