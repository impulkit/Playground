What is a trade deficit?

In October 2026, India sold more goods to the world than in any month in its history. Yet it still bought far more than it sold. How can a country break a record and fall behind at the same time?

The short answer

A trade deficit is when a country buys more from other countries than it sells to them. What a country sells abroad are its exports. What it buys are its imports. If imports are bigger, the difference is the trade deficit.

India usually has a trade deficit in goods, mainly because it buys a lot of oil, gold and electronics. But it sells more services, like software, than it buys, which shrinks the overall gap.

Surprising but true

The USA has had the biggest trade deficit in the world for years, buying far more than it sells, and it is still the richest large country on Earth. A trade deficit is not the same as being poor.

How it works

Trade is counted month by month. Officials add up the value of everything that leaves the country to be sold, and everything that arrives. Because much of world trade is priced in dollars, India's figures are usually given in dollars too.

Exports minus imports gives the trade balance:

  • - If exports are bigger, it is a trade surplus.
  • - If imports are bigger, it is a trade deficit.

There are two kinds of trade. Goods are things you can drop on your foot, like oil, phones, rice and cars. Services are things people do, like writing software, running call centres, teaching online or welcoming tourists. India runs a deficit in goods and a surplus in services.

To pay for extra imports, a country needs foreign money, or foreign currency. It can get dollars from foreign companies investing, from Indians abroad sending money home, or from its own savings, called foreign exchange reserves. If a deficit grows very large for a long time, the rupee can weaken, making imports even dearer.

Think of a family that runs a small tiffin service. It earns money selling lunches, and spends money buying vegetables, a new stove and school books. If it spends more than it earns, that is a deficit. Whether that is a problem depends on what it bought. A new stove that helps it cook more lunches may pay for itself. Spending savings on things that do not help later is riskier.

Where it came from

Hundreds of years ago, many European rulers believed exports were good and imports were bad. They wanted to pile up gold by selling more than they bought. This idea was called mercantilism.

In 1776, the Scottish thinker Adam Smith argued that both sides gain from trade, because each country can focus on what it does best. Most economists since then say a deficit is not good or bad on its own. It depends on why it happens and how it is paid for.

India learned this the hard way in 1991. It had borrowed heavily and had so little foreign money left that it could pay for only about three weeks of imports. India had to pledge its gold to get loans, and then opened its economy with big reforms.

India's story

India imports most of its crude oil, as the story of where India gets its oil explains. It also buys large amounts of electronics, machinery, coal and gold. Its biggest goods exports include engineering goods, refined petrol and diesel, medicines, gems and jewellery, and rice.

In October 2026, India's goods exports reached 49.47 billion dollars, the highest for any month ever. But imports rose even faster, to 89.35 billion dollars. Gold imports more than tripled to 5.51 billion dollars as families bought before Diwali, and oil imports cost much more after a crisis in West Asia. So the goods trade deficit was 39.88 billion dollars, the largest in a year.

Services saved the day. India sold 40.51 billion dollars of services and bought 20.72 billion dollars, a surplus of almost 20 billion dollars. Counting goods and services together, India's exports were a record 89.99 billion dollars, and the overall gap was about 20 billion dollars.

The numbers that matter

  • - 49.47 billion dollars: India's goods exports in October 2026, a monthly record.
  • - 39.88 billion dollars: the goods trade deficit that month, the biggest in a year.
  • - 5.51 billion dollars: gold imports in October 2026, more than triple the month before.
  • - About 20 billion dollars: India's services surplus that month, which roughly halved the overall gap.
  • - About 3 weeks: how many weeks of imports India's foreign money could pay for during the 1991 crisis.

What people argue about

Some say India should cut its trade deficit by making more at home, such as electronics and solar panels, and by putting higher taxes called tariffs on some imports. Others say tariffs make goods dearer for families and factories, and that India should focus on selling more abroad instead.

Gold is another debate. India mines very little gold of its own. Indians love gold as savings and for weddings, but importing so much widens the deficit. The government has tried gold bonds and other schemes so people can save in gold without bringing in as much metal. Abroad, some leaders, especially in the USA, see a deficit with another country as unfair. Many economists disagree.

Myths and mix-ups

"A trade deficit means the country is poor." The USA has run the world's biggest deficit for decades.

"Trade deficit and fiscal deficit are the same." The fiscal deficit is when the government spends more than it collects in taxes. The trade deficit is about the whole country's buying and selling with the world.

"Only goods count." Services, like India's software exports, count too, and they are one of India's great strengths.

What it means for you

The trade balance affects the value of the rupee. When the deficit is large, the rupee can weaken, which makes imported things like phones, laptops and petrol dearer, and makes studying or travelling abroad cost more. A strong export sector creates jobs in factories, farms and offices across India.

So the next time your family buys gold for a festival or a new phone, remember you are part of India's trade story. Learning why countries trade and how big India's economy is helps make sense of that monthly number.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A trade deficit means a country is losing money and getting poorer.

Truth: A deficit only means a country is buying more than it sells. If it buys machines and materials that help it grow, it can get richer. Many strong economies run deficits for years.

Try this

Keep a "family trade book" for a week. On one side, write money that comes into your home, like salaries. On the other, write money that goes out. Is there a surplus or a deficit? Then think. Did the family buy something useful, like a bicycle, that will help later? Countries ask the same question about their deficits.
