What does a state government do?

The police officer at a crossing, the teacher in a government school and the doctor at a district hospital in India are usually all employed by a state government.

The short answer

Big countries like India and the USA are federal, which means power is shared between a national government and state governments. Each state has its own government with its own leader, lawmakers and civil servants.

State governments run much of what touches daily life: police, hospitals, most schools, state roads, farming support, water and land records. In India, the state's elected head is the Chief Minister, who leads a council of ministers. The Governor, appointed by the President, is the formal head of the state. In the USA, each state elects its own governor, who is the real head of the state government, a bit like a president for that state.

Surprising but true

An Indian state's Governor is appointed by the President, but an American state's governor is elected directly by the voters of that state.

Who does what in India

India's Constitution lists who controls what in three lists. The Union List, for the central government, includes defence, railways, foreign affairs and currency. The State List includes police, public health, agriculture, prisons and local government. The Concurrent List, shared by both, includes education, forests and marriage laws. If the two clash on a shared subject, the central law usually wins.

States pass some of their own work further down. Since 1992, the Constitution has required states to hand certain powers and funds to elected panchayats in villages and to municipalities in towns. How much they actually hand over varies a lot from state to state.

India has 28 states and 8 union territories. Union territories are run more directly by the central government, though Delhi, Puducherry, and Jammu and Kashmir have their own elected assemblies.

Chief Ministers and assemblies

Every Indian state has a legislative assembly, or Vidhan Sabha, elected every five years. The party or alliance with a majority chooses the Chief Minister. Six states, including Maharashtra, Karnataka and Uttar Pradesh, also have an upper house, the Legislative Council.

The Governor's job is mostly formal, but it matters at certain moments. When no party has a clear majority, the Governor invites someone to try to form a government. If a state's government breaks down, the President can impose President's Rule, and the state is run from Delhi for a time. Governors and elected state governments sometimes clash, and some of these disputes have reached the Supreme Court.

America's 50 governors

In the USA, the original 13 states existed before the national government, and all 50 states keep wide powers. Each has its own constitution, its own courts and its own laws on things like driving ages, school rules and criminal punishments.

Every state elects a governor, usually for four years. The governor proposes the state budget, signs or vetoes bills, commands the state's National Guard and can pardon prisoners. Every state except Nebraska has a two-house legislature. Nebraska's single house, set up in 1937, is also officially non-partisan, so members don't run under party labels.

Why states matter

States let a huge country be run closer to the people. India redrew many of its state borders by language in 1956, so that people could deal with government in their own language, like Tamil, Telugu or Marathi. That is one reason India's languages remain so strong.

States also try new ideas. In the USA, some states let women vote decades before the whole country did. Tamil Nadu's school lunch programme helped inspire India's national mid-day meal scheme. When one state tries something that works, others often copy it.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Governor runs an Indian state.

Truth: The Governor is the formal head, but the elected Chief Minister and council of ministers run the state government. Like the President at national level, the Governor usually acts on their advice.
