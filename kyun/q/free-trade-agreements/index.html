What is a free trade agreement?

A shirt made in Tiruppur can cost more in another country just because it crossed a border. A free trade deal is a promise to take that extra cost away.

The short answer

When goods cross a border, governments often charge a tax on them, called a tariff or customs duty. It makes foreign goods more expensive, which can protect local makers, but it also makes it harder to sell abroad.

A free trade agreement is a promise between countries to cut or remove these taxes on each other's goods. That makes it cheaper and easier for businesses on both sides to buy and sell.

Surprising but true

India and New Zealand first started talking about a trade deal in 2010. The talks stalled in 2015 and only restarted in 2025, so it took about 16 years from first meeting to final deal.

Why countries make these deals

No country can make everything it needs cheaply, so countries trade. India is good at making clothes, medicines and machine parts. New Zealand is known for fruit, wool and dairy. Lower border taxes let each sell what it is best at, and shoppers get more choice.

India began opening up to world trade in a big way with its 1991 reforms. Since then it has signed deals with many countries, including the UAE, Australia, the UK and New Zealand.

Not every door opens

Most deals come with a list of protected goods. In its deal with New Zealand, India kept out milk, cheese and yoghurt, along with onions, sugar, spices and edible oils. India's dairy is made mostly by small farmers working together in groups like Amul, and cheap imports could hurt them.

Some goods get in only partly. New Zealand apples could enter India only in limited amounts, above a minimum price and in certain seasons. That is because apple growers in Himachal Pradesh worried about competition. Deals can also cover people, such as letting students work for a while after studying abroad.

What each side usually says

Supporters say deals help businesses export more, create jobs and bring in investment. Critics worry that cheaper imports can hurt local farmers and small makers, and that promises of investment depend on what private companies actually choose to do.

Trade figures over the following years show whether a deal is working. Between India and New Zealand, trade was about USD 1.15 billion a year when their deal began, tiny next to the size of India's economy.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A free trade deal means everything between two countries becomes completely free.

Truth: The goods still cost money. What changes is the tax charged at the border. And most deals keep a list of products that are protected, like India's dairy, which still pay the full tax.
