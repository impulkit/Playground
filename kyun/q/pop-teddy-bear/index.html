Why is it called a teddy bear?

In 1902 a US president refused to shoot a bear tied to a tree. A newspaper cartoon later turned that moment into the world's favourite toy.

The short answer

The teddy bear is named after Theodore Roosevelt, the 26th president of the United States, whose nickname was Teddy. In November 1902 he went bear hunting in Mississippi. After days with no luck, his guides chased down a black bear and tied it to a tree so the president could shoot it.

Roosevelt refused. He said shooting a tied-up animal would be unsporting. A cartoonist named Clifford Berryman drew the scene for The Washington Post, and the story spread across the country.

A shopkeeper in New York saw the cartoon, sewed a toy bear, and asked to call it "Teddy's bear". The name stuck, and within a few years teddy bears were a craze across America and Europe.

Surprising but true

Teddy bears were invented twice in the same year, by a shop owner in New York and a toy company in Germany, apparently without either knowing about the other.

A cartoon goes viral, 1902 style

Berryman's drawing was called "Drawing the Line in Mississippi". In it, the president turns away from a small, frightened bear with his hand raised. Newspapers in other cities copied it. Berryman kept drawing the little bear in later cartoons about Roosevelt, and readers grew fond of it.

The hunting guide that week was Holt Collier, a famous hunter who had been born into slavery. He had tracked the bear for the president. His name is often left out of the story, but historians now give him credit.

Roosevelt reportedly never liked the nickname Teddy. He became stuck with it on millions of toys anyway. You can read about other surprises from the White House in US presidents' oddest facts.

Two bears, two countries

In Brooklyn, New York, Morris and Rose Michtom ran a small shop selling sweets and stationery. Rose sewed a plush bear and Morris put it in the window beside the cartoon. People wanted to buy it. The bears sold so well that the couple started the Ideal Novelty and Toy Company.

Across the ocean in Germany, the Steiff company had begun as a workshop run by Margarete Steiff, who made felt toys. Her nephew Richard designed a bear with arms and legs that moved. At a toy fair in Leipzig in 1903, an American buyer ordered 3,000 of them.

One of the early Michtom bears now sits in the Smithsonian museum in Washington, DC. It has lost much of its fur from being loved. Teddy bears soon became part of Roosevelt's public image, and his supporters used them at rallies and on badges.

Bears that became characters

Once the teddy existed, writers gave bears personalities. Winnie-the-Pooh appeared in 1926. The real Christopher Robin had a teddy, and its name came from Winnipeg, a black bear living at London Zoo. Paddington Bear, from "darkest Peru", arrived in 1958. In India, many children grew up with Jambavan, the wise bear king of the Ramayana, long before teddies reached the shops.

Real bears are nothing like these soft, round friends. Wild bears are strong, fast and best watched from a distance. India has four kinds, from the shaggy sloth bear to the small sun bear, as you can see in India's bears.

Why we hug them

Doctors and psychologists call a cuddly toy a comfort object. Many young children carry one through big changes, like starting school or moving house. Police officers and ambulance crews in several countries keep teddy bears in their vehicles to calm frightened children.

Teddies are not just for little kids, either. Plenty of adults keep their childhood bear for life. Some old Steiff bears have sold at auction for huge sums, because collectors love the early designs.

And the strangest part? The cartoon that started it all was made by a political cartoonist, not a toymaker. His craft lives on in the world's best-loved cartoonists.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The teddy bear was invented in Germany and later renamed in America.

Truth: Both countries made bears in 1902 and 1903. The name "Teddy's bear" came from the American shop owner Morris Michtom and the Roosevelt cartoon, while the German Steiff company made its own jointed bear at the same time.
