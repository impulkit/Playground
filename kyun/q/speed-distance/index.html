How do you work out speed?

When Usain Bolt set the 100-metre world record in 2009, he averaged about 37 km/h. A cheetah could overtake him in seconds.

The short answer

Speed tells you how far something goes in a certain amount of time. To work it out, divide the distance by the time.

If a bus travels 60 km in 2 hours, its speed is 60 ÷ 2 = 30 km per hour. If you walk 100 metres in 50 seconds, your speed is 100 ÷ 50 = 2 metres per second.

You can turn the formula around too. Distance equals speed times time. Time equals distance divided by speed. If you know any two of the three, you can find the third. That's how a train timetable is made, and how a map app guesses when you'll arrive.

Surprising but true

Standing still at the equator, you are moving at about 1,670 km/h as the Earth spins, and over 1,00,000 km/h as it travels round the Sun.

Average speed and top speed

The speed you work out this way is an average. A bus that covers 30 km in an hour probably didn't go 30 km/h the whole time. It stopped at signals, crawled in traffic and sped up on open road. The number on its speedometer at any moment is its speed at that instant.

Usain Bolt ran 100 metres in 9.58 seconds at the World Championships in Berlin in 2009. That's an average of about 10.4 metres per second, or about 37.6 km/h. But he started from standing still. In the fastest part of the race, he reached nearly 45 km/h.

Different units, same idea

Speed can be measured in many units. Cars and trains in India use kilometres per hour. Cars in the United States use miles per hour. Scientists often use metres per second. Ships and planes use knots, which are nautical miles per hour.

To change metres per second into kilometres per hour, multiply by 3.6. That works because an hour has 3,600 seconds and a kilometre has 1,000 metres. So a sprinter at 10 m/s is going 36 km/h.

How fast things go

A cheetah can reach around 100 km/h in a short burst. India's Vande Bharat trains are built for speeds up to about 160 km/h on suitable tracks. Japan's bullet trains run at up to 320 km/h.

Sound travels through air at about 343 metres per second, which is about 1,235 km/h. That's why you see lightning before you hear thunder. Light is far faster: about 3 lakh km every second. It could go round the Earth more than seven times in one second.

How do bullet trains go so fast?Japan's bullet train got its long pointed nose partly from a bird. An engineer who loved birdwatching copied the beak of the kingfisher.

Speeds you can't feel

The Earth spins once a day. At the equator, the ground travels about 40,000 km in 24 hours, which is about 1,670 km/h. In Bengaluru it's a little less, and at the poles almost zero.

At the same time, the Earth travels round the Sun at about 1,07,000 km/h. You don't feel any of this, because the motion is smooth and you move along with it. You only notice changes in speed, like a train braking suddenly.

Speed in space

The International Space Station circles the Earth at about 28,000 km/h, roughly 7.7 km every second. At that speed it goes all the way round in about 90 minutes. Astronauts on board see about 16 sunrises and sunsets every day.

Space probes go faster still. The fastest, NASA's Parker Solar Probe, has flown past the Sun at more than 6 lakh km/h. Even at that speed, it would take about nine days to cross the distance from the Earth to the Sun.

Marathon runners use the same maths as space scientists. A runner who wants to finish the 42.195 km of a marathon in 3 hours must average about 14 km/h the whole way.

Myth or fact?

Myth: You can't feel the Earth moving because it moves slowly.

Truth: The Earth moves very fast, both spinning and travelling round the Sun. You don't feel it because the motion is smooth and steady, and you, the air and everything around you move along together. We only feel changes in speed, not speed itself.

Try this

Measure out 20 metres in a park or playground with a grown-up. Time a friend walking it, then running it. Work out each speed by dividing 20 by the number of seconds. Multiply by 3.6 to change metres per second into kilometres per hour.
