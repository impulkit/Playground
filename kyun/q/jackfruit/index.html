What is jackfruit, the giant fruit that grows on tree trunks?

Jackfruit is the biggest fruit that grows on trees. One can weigh more than a sack of rice, and it hangs straight off the trunk.

The short answer

Jackfruit is a huge, green, bumpy fruit that grows on a tall evergreen tree. It is the largest fruit that grows on trees anywhere. Many weigh 5 to 20 kilograms, and the biggest have topped 50 kilograms, about as much as a sack of rice. Because they are so heavy, they grow directly from the trunk and the thickest branches.

Inside the spiky skin are hundreds of yellow, sweet pods, each wrapped around a large seed. Ripe jackfruit tastes a bit like banana, mango and pineapple mixed together. Unripe jackfruit is cooked as a vegetable, and the seeds are roasted or added to curries.

Surprising but true

The wood of the jackfruit tree is used to make the body of the veena, one of India's oldest string instruments.

A fruit from the Western Ghats

Jackfruit trees first grew wild in the rainforests of the Western Ghats, along India's southwest coast. People have grown them for thousands of years, and they spread to Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Southeast Asia and later to Africa, Brazil and Florida.

The English name came through Portuguese traders, who called the fruit "jaca". That word probably came from "chakka", its name in Malayalam. In 2018, Kerala made jackfruit its official state fruit. It is also the national fruit of Bangladesh, where it is called kathal.

Opening one is a job

Cutting a jackfruit is messy. The fruit is full of a sticky white sap, a kind of latex, that clings to knives and hands like glue. Cooks rub oil on their hands and blades first, and spread newspaper on the floor. Usually an adult cuts the fruit into big wedges, and then the family pulls out the pods together.

Jackfruit has a strong smell when it is ripe, sweet and a bit like overripe banana. Some people love it and some can't stand it. It is often confused with durian, a Southeast Asian fruit that smells far stronger, but the two are not related.

One tree, many uses

In Kerala and Karnataka, jackfruit season brings chips, jams, sweet halwa, payasam and steamed cakes wrapped in leaves. Raw jackfruit is cooked in spicy dishes. In Bengal it is made into a curry so meaty in texture that it is sometimes called "tree mutton".

Unripe jackfruit has become popular in Britain and the USA as a vegetarian swap for pulled pork in burgers and tacos. The tree's golden-brown wood is strong and doesn't warp easily. Carpenters use it for furniture, and instrument makers use it for the body of the veena.

The rice tree

In Sri Lanka, the jackfruit tree is sometimes called "bath gasa", meaning rice tree, because its fruit has helped feed families when rice was short. A big jackfruit can be cooked like a vegetable while young, eaten as a sweet fruit when ripe, and its seeds kept for later.

Those seeds are worth saving. Roasted or boiled, they taste a little like chestnuts, and they can be ground into flour for rotis and cakes. In South India the main season runs roughly from March to July, and in good years there is so much fruit that some of it goes to waste. Food scientists and small companies are now drying, freezing and packing jackfruit so it can be eaten all year.

A cousin of the fig

Jackfruit belongs to the same plant family as figs, mulberries and the banyan. That is why it has sticky sap like a fig. What looks like one fruit is really hundreds of tiny flowers that have fused together, with each yellow pod grown from a single flower.

One tree can produce more than 100 fruits a year. Because the trees are tough and need little care, scientists see jackfruit as a useful food for a warming world. In many villages, a single old jackfruit tree feeds a family and their neighbours all season.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Young jackfruit is a meat replacement that is full of protein like chicken.

Truth: Cooked unripe jackfruit can feel stringy and chewy like pulled meat, which is why it is popular in vegan dishes. But it has very little protein. Its seeds have more, and beans and dal have much more.

Try this

At a market, ask if you can lift a whole jackfruit, with a grown-up close by. Then lift a 1 kg packet of rice or sugar at home. Guess how many packets your jackfruit weighed, and check your guess on a bathroom scale if you buy one.
