What are the animal kingdom's record-breakers?

The fastest animal on Earth reaches over 300 km/h, as fast as the quickest bullet trains, and it does it by falling out of the sky.

The short answer

Nature's record-breakers come in every size. The blue whale is the largest animal known, up to about 30 m long, as long as three city buses. The peregrine falcon is the fastest, diving at more than 300 km/h. A tiny dung beetle can pull over a thousand times its own weight, and the Greenland shark may live for hundreds of years.

Each record is a clue about how animals have adapted to survive. But records are hard to measure in the wild. Scientists can't easily clock a wild cheetah or weigh a whale, so many numbers are estimates, and some get revised as better tools come along.

Surprising but true

A Greenland shark studied in 2016 was estimated to be at least 272 years old, and possibly much older.

Biggest and tallest

The blue whale is probably the heaviest animal that has ever lived, heavier than even the largest dinosaurs as far as we can tell. A big one can weigh well over 100 tonnes. Its heart alone can weigh about 180 kg, as much as two grown men, and its main food is krill, shrimp-like animals only a few centimetres long.

On land, the African elephant is the heaviest animal, and the giraffe is the tallest, reaching more than 5 m. In India, the Asian elephant is the biggest land animal, and the gaur, or Indian bison, is the largest wild cattle in the world. The king cobra, found in India's forests, is the longest venomous snake, sometimes over 5 m long.

Fastest

The peregrine falcon hunts other birds by diving on them from high above, folding its wings into a teardrop shape. Measurements suggest it can top 300 km/h, faster than many bullet trains run.

On land, the cheetah holds the record, at around 100 km/h. In 2012, a cheetah named Sarah at the Cincinnati Zoo in the United States ran 100 m in 5.95 seconds. India's wild cheetahs disappeared in the 1950s, and in 2022, cheetahs were brought from Africa to Kuno National Park in Madhya Pradesh.

Smallest and farthest

At the other end of the scale, some frogs from Papua New Guinea are smaller than 1 cm when fully grown, short enough to sit on your fingernail with room to spare. The bee hummingbird of Cuba, the smallest bird, is about 5–6 cm long, beak and all.

The Arctic tern makes the longest known migration. It flies from the Arctic to the Antarctic and back every year, and tracking tags show some birds cover more than 70,000 km in a single round trip.

Strongest and toughest

For its size, one of the strongest animals ever measured is a horned dung beetle. In one study, a male pulled a weight more than 1,000 times its own body weight. That is like a person pulling six double-decker buses.

Other animals are record-breaking survivors. Tardigrades, tiny animals less than 1 mm long, can survive being dried out for years and have even survived being exposed to space. Bar-headed geese fly over the Himalaya, where the air has less than half the oxygen found at sea level.

Oldest

In 2016, scientists in Denmark worked out the ages of Greenland sharks by studying the lenses of their eyes. The lens contains proteins that form before birth and never change. The biggest shark they studied was estimated to be at least 272 years old, and possibly closer to 400.

Some other animals live astonishingly long too. Giant tortoises can pass 150 years, and some bowhead whales seem to have lived more than 200 years. People also love keeping world records, but in the animal kingdom, the record books keep changing as scientists measure more.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A cheetah can keep running at top speed for a long way.

Truth: A cheetah's top-speed sprint usually lasts less than a minute and covers a few hundred metres. Then it must stop to cool down and catch its breath, because sprinting makes its body overheat.

Try this

Using a tape measure, mark 30 m along a corridor, playground or pavement with chalk. That is roughly the length of a big blue whale. Then walk it and count your steps. For comparison, a city bus is about 12 m long, and a cricket pitch is about 20 m.
