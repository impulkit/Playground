What are the weirdest sports in the world?

In one sport you play chess, then box, then play chess again. In another, people chase a big round cheese down a hill so steep they tumble.

The short answer

Every sport looks strange if you have never seen it. Cricket can last five days and still end in a draw, which amazes many Americans. American football stops every few seconds, which baffles many Indians. But some sports are odd by any standard.

There is chess boxing, where brains and fists take turns. There is cheese rolling, wife carrying, bog snorkelling and underwater hockey. India has its own surprises, like buffalo racing through muddy rice fields. Most weird sports started as a joke, a village festival or a bet, and then refused to die.

Surprising but true

Tug of war was an Olympic sport for five Games, from 1900 to 1920.

Brains, then fists

Chess boxing began as an idea in a French comic book. In 2003, a Dutch artist organised the first real match. Players alternate short rounds of chess and boxing, up to eleven rounds in all. You win by checkmate, by knockout, or if your opponent runs out of time on the chess clock.

The hard part is thinking clearly with your heart pounding. Try planning five moves ahead a minute after being punched. Fans say it is the perfect test of body and mind. Most top chess players are happy to skip it.

Chasing cheese down a cliff

Every spring at Cooper's Hill in Gloucestershire, England, someone rolls a round cheese weighing about 3 to 4 kg off the top of a hill. A crowd of runners chases it. The hill is so steep, in places nearly as steep as a staircase, that most competitors fall and roll most of the way down. The first to cross the finish line wins the cheese.

The cheese gets a head start and is almost never caught. In some years a foam copy has been rolled instead, for safety, with the real cheese handed to the winner at the bottom. Ambulances wait at the foot of the hill every year. Nobody knows exactly when the race began, but it has been run for at least 200 years.

Mud, water and wives

In Finland, the Wife Carrying World Championships began in 1992. A man carries his partner, often upside down on his back, through an obstacle course with a water pit. In Wales, bog snorkellers swim two lengths of a muddy trench in a peat bog, using flippers but no normal swimming strokes.

Underwater hockey was invented in England in 1954 by divers who wanted to keep fit in winter. Players wear snorkels and push a heavy puck along the bottom of a pool with short sticks, holding their breath for each dive.

Kicking a ball over a net

Sepak takraw looks like volleyball, but players may not use their hands. They kick a woven ball over a net, often with bicycle kicks in mid-air. It is hugely popular in Thailand, Malaysia and other parts of South-East Asia and has been part of the Asian Games since 1990. India won its first medal in the sport, a bronze, in 2018.

In the USA, a version of quidditch from the Harry Potter books began at a college in Vermont in 2005. Players run with broomsticks between their legs. It is now called quadball.

India's weird and wonderful

In coastal Karnataka, Kambala racers sprint through flooded rice fields beside pairs of buffaloes, holding the reins. The water splashes as high as their heads. In 2020, one racer's time made headlines when people compared it to Usain Bolt's, though the two races cannot really be compared.

Kambala is centuries old and tied to the farming year, held after the harvest in the cool months. Then there is mallakhamb, where gymnasts climb, twist and balance on a wooden pole, or even on a hanging rope. And weird sports are nothing new. The ancient Olympics had an event where athletes raced in full armour.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Olympics have only ever had serious, ordinary sports.

Truth: Tug of war was an Olympic sport from 1900 to 1920, and early Games also had live pigeon shooting and a swimming obstacle race. Sports come and go as tastes change.
