Who first sailed around the world?

About 270 men set out to sail around the world in 1519. Only 18 made it home on the first try, and their famous captain was not one of them.

The short answer

The first voyage around the world was led by Ferdinand Magellan, a Portuguese captain sailing for Spain. It left in 1519 with five ships and about 270 men.

The fleet crossed the Atlantic, found a passage around the bottom of South America and crossed the vast Pacific. In the Philippines, Magellan was killed in a battle in 1521. The remaining crew sailed on to the Spice Islands in Indonesia, then home across the Indian Ocean and around Africa. In September 1522, one battered ship, the Victoria, reached Spain with 18 men, led by Juan Sebastián Elcano. They had completed the first circumnavigation of the Earth.

Surprising but true

When the survivors got home, they found their careful logbook was a whole day behind the calendar on land.

Chasing cloves

The goal was spices, not exploration for its own sake. Cloves, nutmeg and mace grew only on a few islands in what is now Indonesia, the Moluccas, and in Europe they were worth a fortune.

Portugal controlled the route east around Africa and India. Spain wanted its own way to the Spice Islands. Magellan promised to reach them by sailing west, through a passage he believed existed somewhere in South America.

A strait at the end of the world

Finding the passage took more than a year. The fleet sailed down the coast of South America, through cold and storms. One ship was wrecked, and the crew of another turned back to Spain. Magellan crushed a mutiny along the way.

In late 1520, they entered a narrow, twisting channel near the tip of the continent, now called the Strait of Magellan. Getting through took 38 days. Along the way they saw penguins, and a species of South American penguin is named after him today.

Starving on the Pacific

No one in Europe knew how wide the Pacific was. The crossing lasted over three months, and they saw almost no land. Food ran out. Pigafetta wrote that they ate biscuit crawling with worms, sawdust, rats and leather strips soaked in seawater.

Many men fell ill with scurvy, a disease that makes gums swell and bleed. We now know it is caused by lack of vitamin C, found in fresh fruit and vegetables. In March 1521 they reached Guam, and then the Philippines.

Death at Mactan

In the Philippines, Magellan made allies and pushed local leaders to accept Christianity and Spanish rule. On the island of Mactan, a chief named Lapulapu refused.

On 27 April 1521, Magellan attacked with a small force and was killed in the shallows. In the Philippines, Lapulapu is honoured as a hero who resisted foreign rule. Magellan's interpreter, a man from Southeast Asia known as Enrique, had travelled west with him from Europe. If he got home afterwards, he may have been the first person to circle the globe, though no one knows what became of him.

The missing day

Only the Victoria completed the voyage, carrying a cargo of cloves. When the survivors stopped at the Cape Verde islands in July 1522, they discovered that their logbook was one day behind the local date.

They had kept perfect records. But by sailing west with the Sun all the way around the world, they had seen one fewer sunrise than people who stayed at home. Today, the International Date Line and time zones deal with exactly this puzzle. In 2017–18, six women officers of the Indian Navy sailed around the world on the small sailing boat INSV Tarini, following a long tradition of ocean voyagers.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Magellan was the first person to sail around the world.

Truth: Magellan was killed halfway, in the Philippines. The voyage was finished by Juan Sebastián Elcano and 17 others.
