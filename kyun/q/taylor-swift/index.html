Why is Taylor Swift so popular?

During two Taylor Swift concerts in Seattle in 2023, fans jumped and danced so hard that earthquake sensors nearby picked up the shaking.

The short answer

Taylor Swift is popular mainly because of her songwriting. She writes about everyday feelings, like a friendship going wrong or a first crush, with small, specific details that make listeners feel she is describing their own lives. She has also changed her sound several times, from country to pop to quiet folk, bringing old fans along and winning new ones.

She talks to fans directly and hides clues for them in her songs, videos and posts. Her Eras Tour, from 2023 to 2024, earned more than US$2 billion, more than any concert tour before it.

Surprising but true

Seismologists measured the shaking from fans at her July 2023 Seattle shows, which some news reports compared to a small earthquake of about magnitude 2.3.

A teenager in Nashville

Swift was born in 1989 in Pennsylvania and grew up on a Christmas tree farm. She loved country music, the storytelling style made famous in Nashville, Tennessee. When she was about 14, her family moved near Nashville so she could pursue music.

She signed a deal as a songwriter while still in school, and released her first album in 2006, at 16. Many of the songs came from her own diary, about high school and boys she liked.

Changing her sound

Most country singers stay country. Swift slowly mixed in pop, and with her 2014 album 1989 she switched to pop completely. In 2020, during the COVID-19 lockdowns, she surprised fans with two quiet, folk-style albums made largely by sending files back and forth with other musicians.

She has won the Grammy for Album of the Year four times, more than any other artist.

Words that feel personal

Many of her songs work like short stories or poems. She names exact things: a scarf left at a sister's house, a car ride, a particular colour. Listeners fill in the rest with their own memories.

Her fans, called Swifties, love searching for hidden meanings. She puts secret messages in album notes, numbers in videos, and dates in songs. Finding them has become a game that keeps fans talking for months.

The fight over her music

When a musician records a song, the recording itself, called the master, is usually owned by the record company. In 2019, the masters of Swift's first six albums were sold to someone she didn't want to own them.

Her answer was unusual. She recorded those albums again, note for note, and released them as "Taylor's Version". Many fans and radio stations switched to the new versions. In 2025 she bought the original masters back. Her fight made many young musicians think harder about who owns their work.

The Eras Tour

The Eras Tour ran from 2023 to 2024, with 149 shows on five continents. Each three-hour show travelled through all her musical "eras". Fans made and swapped friendship bracelets with song lyrics on them.

At her July 2023 concerts in Seattle, the dancing crowd shook the ground enough for scientists at the Pacific Northwest Seismic Network to record it. News reports called it the "Swift Quake", and some compared it to a small earthquake of about magnitude 2.3.

Criticism and debate

Not everyone is a fan. Some critics find her music too polished. Others have criticised her heavy use of private jets, which release a lot of carbon dioxide. Her team has said she buys carbon credits to make up for the flights, and the debate continues.

She has never toured India, but her songs are among the most streamed in the country, and fan groups in Indian cities hold listening parties when a new album comes out. Like many stars of the streaming era, she reaches fans in places she has never visited.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Taylor Swift sings songs written for her by other people.

Truth: She writes or co-writes every song on her albums. She wrote her third album, Speak Now (2010), entirely by herself.
