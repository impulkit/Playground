What are England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland?

The United Kingdom is one country made of four countries, which is why it sends four separate teams to the football World Cup but one team to the Olympics.

The short answer

The United Kingdom, or UK, is a country in north-west Europe made up of four countries: England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. They share one king, one army, one currency (the pound) and one main parliament in London.

Each also has its own identity. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own parliaments or assemblies that make many decisions about schools, hospitals and local laws. Each has its own flag, its own patron saint and, often, its own sports teams. People may call themselves Scottish or Welsh first, and British second.

Surprising but true

A village in Wales has a name 58 letters long, Llanfairpwllgwyngyllgogerychwyrndrobwllllantysiliogogogoch, and its railway station sign is a tourist attraction.

Islands and names

The names can be confusing. Great Britain is the biggest island, holding England, Scotland and Wales. Ireland is the island to its west. Most of Ireland is a separate country, the Republic of Ireland, while the north-eastern part, Northern Ireland, belongs to the UK. The British Isles is a geographical name for all these islands together, though many people in Ireland dislike it.

There are smaller places too. The Isle of Man and the Channel Islands, such as Jersey, belong to the British Crown but are not officially part of the UK. They make most of their own laws.

How four became one

England and Wales were joined by law in the 1500s. In 1603 the King of Scotland, James VI, also became King of England. In 1707 England and Scotland formally united into the Kingdom of Great Britain. Ireland joined in 1801, but most of it broke away to become independent in the 1920s, leaving Northern Ireland in the UK.

The Union Jack records some of that story. It overlays the crosses of England, Scotland and Ireland.

Four countries, four flavours

Scotland has its own legal system, its own banknotes and Gaelic, along with mountains called the Highlands. Edinburgh's castle stands on top of an extinct volcano. Wales has its own language, Welsh, which more than half a million people speak. Road signs there are written in both Welsh and English.

Northern Ireland has the Giant's Causeway, a coast of about 40,000 six-sided stone columns made by cooling lava. Its recent history includes a long conflict known as the Troubles, which largely ended with the Good Friday Agreement in 1998. England is the largest country by far, with London, the capital of the UK.

Sport: one country, many teams

In football, England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland each play as separate national teams. England and Scotland played the first international match in 1872. In rugby, Northern Ireland's players join the Republic's in a single team for the whole island of Ireland. In cricket, the England team represents both England and Wales, and plays some of the oldest Test cricket rivalries in the world.

At the Olympics, though, all four compete together as one team, Great Britain. So a Welsh cyclist and a Scottish swimmer may be rivals in one sport and teammates in another.

The UK and India

The East India Company, founded in London in 1600, began as a trading business and ended up ruling much of India. The British government took over direct rule in 1858, which lasted until India's independence in 1947. That long, often painful history explains why English is so widely spoken in India, and why cricket is so loved.

Today about 1.8 million people of Indian heritage live in the UK, one of its largest communities. Chicken tikka masala is often listed among Britain's favourite dishes. Many words travelled the other way too. English took "shampoo", "bungalow" and "pyjamas" from Indian languages, and the plays of Shakespeare are now performed in Hindi, Bengali and Tamil.

Myth or fact?

Myth: England and Britain mean the same thing.

Truth: England is one of four countries in the United Kingdom. Great Britain is the island that holds England, Scotland and Wales. The United Kingdom adds Northern Ireland, on the island of Ireland.
