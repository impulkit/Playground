Who invented canned food?

Canned food began with a French sweet-maker sealing food in glass bottles to win a prize for feeding Napoleon's army, and the can opener came decades after the can.

The short answer

Canned food was invented in France in the early 1800s by Nicolas Appert, a confectioner, someone who makes sweets. He found that if he sealed food in glass bottles and heated them in boiling water, the food stayed good for months.

The tin can came soon after. In 1810, an Englishman named Peter Durand patented the idea of using metal containers instead of glass. Within a few years, a factory in London was canning meat and vegetables for the Royal Navy. Ever since, cans have let people carry food across oceans, deserts and even into space.

Surprising but true

The first tin cans were so thick that soldiers opened them with a hammer and chisel, or even a bayonet. Handy can openers came along about 45 years later.

A prize for a general

At the end of the 1700s, France was at war, and its armies were often hungrier than their enemies. Soldiers and sailors lived on salted meat and hard biscuits, and many fell ill on long voyages. In 1795, the French government offered a prize of 12,000 francs to anyone who could find a better way to keep food.

Appert spent about fifteen years experimenting in his workshop near Paris. He packed soups, meat, vegetables and fruit into thick glass bottles, corked them tightly, tied the corks with wire and boiled the bottles for hours. In 1810, under Emperor Napoleon, he received the prize and published a book explaining his method.

From glass to tin

Glass was heavy and broke easily on bumpy roads and rolling ships. In Britain, Peter Durand patented the use of tin-plated iron containers in 1810. Bryan Donkin and John Hall bought the idea and opened a canning factory in London around 1812 or 1813. Their cans of beef and vegetables went on navy ships and Arctic expeditions.

These early cans were heavy and thick. A label from the time told people to open them with a chisel and hammer. Proper can openers only arrived in the 1850s, and the simple turning-wheel opener most kitchens use came later still.

Who was Napoleon?Napoleon sold a huge chunk of North America to the United States for 15 million dollars, roughly doubling the size of the young country overnight.

Why it works

Appert had no idea why his method worked. Food rots because of microbes, tiny living things like bacteria and moulds that feed on it. In the 1860s, the French scientist Louis Pasteur showed that heating kills many of them, and that food sealed away from the air stays fresh because new microbes can't get in.

Today, factories heat sealed cans to about 121 °C under steam pressure, much hotter than boiling water. The idea is the same as a pressure cooker, which also cooks at higher temperatures by trapping steam. This kills even tough germs, including the one that causes botulism, a rare but dangerous food poisoning.

Cans everywhere

During the American Civil War in the 1860s, canned food fed soldiers on both sides, and many brought the habit home. Canned soups, beans and fruit became everyday food in the USA. Lighter cans made of steel and aluminium replaced the old heavy tin-plate, and drink cans arrived in the 1930s.

In India, canned food is less common at home than in the USA, but tins of rasgullas, gulab jamuns and ready-to-eat curries travel with Indian families all over the world. Astronauts, soldiers and mountaineers have long depended on canned and sealed food too.

Safety and recycling

A can that is badly dented, rusty or bulging should be thrown away, because the seal may be broken and germs may have grown inside. Otherwise, many canned foods last for years.

Empty cans are among the easiest things to recycle. Steel and aluminium can be melted down and made into new cans again and again, without losing quality. A can thrown in a recycling bin today can be back on a shop shelf as a new can within a couple of months.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Canned food has almost no nutrition left in it.

Truth: Canning destroys some vitamin C and a few other vitamins, but much of the protein, fibre and minerals remains. Canned beans, fish and tomatoes can be healthy foods.

Try this

Look at the labels of three tins or packets in your kitchen, such as beans, fruit or soup. Find the "best before" date on each. Work out how many months each one can be kept. Then compare with fresh fruit in your house, which might last only a few days.
