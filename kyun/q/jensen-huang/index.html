How did Jensen Huang build Nvidia?

As a teenager he washed dishes at a roadside diner. Years later, at a table in a diner of the same chain, he planned a company that would become one of the most valuable in the world.

The short answer

Jensen Huang is a Taiwan-born American engineer and businessman. In 1993, he and two friends started Nvidia, a technology company that designs computer chips.

Nvidia's chips, called GPUs, were made for video games, but they turned out to be just what artificial intelligence needed. Under Huang, Nvidia grew into one of the most valuable companies in the world. He led it for 33 years.

Surprising but true

Jensen Huang and his two co-founders started Nvidia with just 600 dollars, 200 dollars each, according to an account of its early days. By 2025 it was the first company ever worth 5 trillion dollars.

A long way from home

Huang was born in Taiwan in 1963. His father was a chemical engineer and his mother a school teacher. When he was five, the family moved to Thailand.

In 1973, when he was nine, his parents sent him and his older brother to live with an uncle in the United States. By mistake, the boys ended up at a strict boarding school in a small town in Kentucky. Huang has said life there was tough and that he had to clean toilets. About two years later, the boys joined their parents in Oregon. Stories like his are part of the bigger story of why people move to new countries.

In high school he was very good at table tennis, good enough to play in national contests. He also skipped ahead in school and finished at 16. As a teenager, he worked at a Denny's restaurant, washing dishes, clearing tables and serving food.

Learning to build chips

Huang studied electrical engineering at Oregon State University, graduating in 1984. There he met Lori Mills, who later became his wife. He then designed chips at two companies, AMD and LSI Logic, while taking evening classes at Stanford University for a master's degree, which he finished in 1992.

At LSI Logic he worked with two engineers from another company, Sun Microsystems: Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem. All three believed that one day, computers would need special chips just for drawing pictures.

A plan at a diner

The three friends planned their company over coffee at a Denny's in East San Jose, California. Nvidia was officially set up on 5 April 1993, and Huang, then 30, became its chief executive. The name comes from invidia, the Latin word for envy.

The early years were hard. By 1997, the company was nearly out of money, with about a month of salaries left, before a new chip called RIVA 128 sold well and saved it. In 1999, Nvidia sold shares to the public for the first time and launched the GeForce 256, which it called the world's first GPU. Its chips helped computers show millions of pixels of smooth 3D action.

Why is Silicon Valley famous?Fewer than a hundred years ago, the valley south of San Francisco was famous for apricot and plum orchards. Now it's home to Apple, Google and Nvidia.

From games to AI

Huang bet that GPUs could do more than games. In 2006, Nvidia released software that let scientists use GPUs for all kinds of maths. In 2012, researchers used Nvidia GPUs to teach a computer to recognise pictures far better than before, and the AI boom began.

As companies raced to build AI, they bought Nvidia chips by the thousands. In October 2025, Nvidia became the first company ever worth 5 trillion dollars. Time magazine named Huang among its Persons of the Year for 2025, as one of the "Architects of AI", and in 2026 he received the IEEE Medal of Honor, a top prize for engineers.

What his story teaches

Huang's big bet took more than 15 years to pay off fully. When Nvidia opened its GPUs to scientists in 2006, few people used them that way, and for years investors asked why the company was spending so much on it. The lesson founders take from Nvidia is that a company can survive near-failure, keep one long bet going, and be ready when the world suddenly needs it. Myth or fact?

Myth: Jensen Huang invented the computer chip.

Truth: Computer chips were invented in the late 1950s, before he was born. His company's big idea was a special chip for drawing graphics, the GPU, which later turned out to be ideal for AI.
