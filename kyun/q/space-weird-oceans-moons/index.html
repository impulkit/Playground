Which moons might have hidden oceans?

Under the ice of Jupiter's moon Europa there may be more liquid water than in all of Earth's oceans put together.

The short answer

Earth is the only world we know with oceans on its surface. But several moons in the outer Solar System seem to have oceans hidden underground, beneath thick shells of ice.

The strongest candidates are Jupiter's moons Europa and Ganymede, and Saturn's moons Enceladus and Titan. A few others, like Callisto and even little Mimas, might have them too. These oceans stay liquid because the moons are squeezed and heated by the gravity of their giant planets.

Water is one of the things life needs. That makes these hidden seas some of the most exciting places to explore in the whole Solar System.

Surprising but true

Saturn's small moon Enceladus sprays water from cracks near its south pole, and the Cassini spacecraft flew right through the spray.

Europa, the ice world

Europa is a little smaller than our Moon. Its surface is a shell of ice crisscrossed by cracks and ridges. Scientists think that under the ice, perhaps 15 to 25 km down, lies an ocean of salty water 60 to 150 km deep.

That would mean Europa holds about twice as much water as all of Earth's oceans, even though the moon is far smaller than Earth. In 2019, telescope studies found signs of ordinary table salt on its surface, perhaps carried up from the ocean below.

In the 1990s, NASA's Galileo spacecraft found that Europa disturbs Jupiter's magnetic field in a way best explained by a salty, electrically conducting ocean.

Enceladus, the spraying moon

Enceladus, a moon of Saturn, is only about 500 km across. It would fit inside Rajasthan. In 2005, the Cassini spacecraft spotted fountains of water vapour and ice shooting out of long cracks near its south pole, nicknamed tiger stripes.

Cassini later flew right through these plumes and tasted them. It found salt, silica and simple carbon-based molecules. In 2023, scientists reported phosphates in ice grains from Enceladus, a key ingredient for life as we know it.

Tiny bits of silica suggest that hot water is reacting with rock on the ocean floor. On Earth, places like that, called hydrothermal vents, are packed with life that never sees sunlight.

Ganymede and Titan

Ganymede is the largest moon in the Solar System, bigger than the planet Mercury. In 2015, the Hubble Space Telescope watched its auroras. A salty ocean inside Ganymede would push back on Jupiter's magnetic field, damping the auroras' swing. That's exactly what Hubble saw. The ocean may be sandwiched between layers of ice.

Titan, Saturn's biggest moon, is stranger still. It has lakes and seas on its surface, but they are filled with liquid methane and ethane, not water. Measurements by Cassini suggest a deep water ocean hidden far below the surface too.

Missions on the way

Several spacecraft are heading to these moons right now. The European Space Agency's JUICE launched in April 2023 and should reach Jupiter in 2031, focusing on Ganymede. NASA's Europa Clipper launched in October 2024 and is due to arrive in 2030. It will fly past Europa dozens of times, studying its ice and what lies beneath.

NASA is also building Dragonfly, a flying drone that will hop across Titan's surface. It is planned to launch in 2028.

No one knows whether anything lives in these oceans. Scientists are careful to say they are looking for places that could support life, not life itself. Ocean water on Earth is salty because rivers carry minerals from rocks, as explained in why the sea is salty. The same chemistry might be happening on these moons.

And the strangest part? If life is ever found in one of these dark seas, it would mean life might be common in the universe, a question at the heart of the search for alien life.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Only Earth has oceans in the Solar System.

Truth: Earth is the only world with oceans on its surface. But several moons of Jupiter and Saturn very probably have oceans hidden beneath thick shells of ice.

Try this

Freeze a small plastic tub of salty water and another of plain water for the same time. Check which freezes first. Salt lowers the freezing point of water, one reason scientists think salty oceans can stay liquid under the ice of cold moons.
