Why did so many tech companies start in garages?

A small wooden garage in Palo Alto, California, is an official historic landmark, because two friends started a company called Hewlett-Packard there in 1939.

The short answer

Garages are cheap, roomy and right next to home. In American suburbs, especially in California, almost every house has one. For young inventors with little money, a garage offered a free workshop where they could spread out tools, make noise and work late without renting an office.

Several famous companies began this way: Hewlett-Packard in 1939, Amazon in 1994 and Google, briefly, in 1998. Apple is linked to a garage in Los Altos too, though its co-founder says the story has grown in the telling. Over time, "started in a garage" became a kind of legend, a way of saying a company began small and humble.

Surprising but true

Steve Wozniak, who built Apple's first computer, has called the Apple garage story "a bit of a myth". Most of his design work was done at his desk at Hewlett-Packard and at home.

The garage that started it

In 1939 two young Stanford University graduates, Bill Hewlett and David Packard, rented a house at 367 Addison Avenue in Palo Alto. In the small garage behind it, they built their first product, an audio oscillator, a machine that makes test sounds. One of their early customers was the Walt Disney studio, which used them while making the film Fantasia.

Hewlett-Packard grew into one of the world's biggest technology companies. In 1989 the state of California named the garage "the Birthplace of Silicon Valley" and made it an official historic landmark.

Apple's garage, and the truth

In 1976 Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak founded Apple. Wozniak had designed a computer, the Apple I, small enough to sit on a desk at a time when the first computers had filled whole rooms. A local shop, the Byte Shop, ordered 50 of them, and the pair rushed to build them. Some of that assembling and testing happened in the garage of the Jobs family home in Los Altos.

But in a 2014 interview, Wozniak said the garage story was "a bit of a myth". He did most of his design work at home and at his day job at Hewlett-Packard. The garage was where they sometimes worked, and it made a good story. In 2013 the town of Los Altos listed the house as a historic site.

Amazon and Google

In 1994 Jeff Bezos started Amazon in the garage of a rented house in Bellevue, near Seattle. It sold only books at first. In 1998 Larry Page and Sergey Brin rented a garage and rooms in a house in Menlo Park, south of San Francisco, for their new search company, Google. They stayed only a few months before moving into an office.

Long before these, in 1923, Walt Disney set up his first Hollywood cartoon studio in his uncle's garage in Los Angeles.

India's version

Indian homes rarely have garages, so Indian startups began in other cramped spaces. In 1981, seven engineers founded Infosys in Pune, starting with about ₹10,000 from the savings of Sudha Murty, wife of co-founder N.R. Narayana Murthy. Flipkart began in 2007 in a small flat in Bengaluru. Many of India's startups were born in rented rooms, college hostels and the corners of family homes.

Why the garage story sticks

The garage legend is partly true and partly a good tale. What these places offered was freedom: room to make a mess, try things that might fail, and start again the next day. California also had something special nearby: Stanford University, electronics companies, cheap spare parts, and people willing to invest in risky ideas.

Today many founders start with nothing but a laptop at a kitchen table. The garage hasn't vanished, though. Makers still build robots, drones and electric bikes in garages, sheds and balconies around the world, and a few of those projects will one day have a landmark plaque of their own.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Apple's computers were designed and built in Steve Jobs's garage.

Truth: Steve Wozniak designed the Apple I mostly at home and at his job at Hewlett-Packard. The garage was used for some assembly, testing and as a business address, and it became a famous symbol of how Apple began.

Try this

Choose a corner of a room, a balcony or a table and turn it into a "maker space" for a week. Collect cardboard, tape, string, old bottles and caps. Each day, build something that solves a tiny problem, like a phone stand or a pencil holder, and keep a notebook of what worked.
