Who are some of India's greatest sportswomen?

In November 2025 India's women's cricket team won the ODI World Cup for the first time, at a packed stadium in Navi Mumbai, after decades of near misses.

The short answer

India has many great sportswomen, across very different sports. Boxer Mary Kom, badminton star PV Sindhu, shooter Manu Bhaker, cricketer Mithali Raj, sprinter PT Usha and chess players Koneru Humpy and Divya Deshmukh are a few of the best known.

They have won Olympic medals, world titles and World Cups. Many came from small towns and families with little money, and some trained with poor equipment for years before anyone noticed them.

In recent years their success has drawn huge crowds and TV audiences. In 2025 India's women's cricket team won the 50-over World Cup, one of the biggest moments in Indian sport.

Surprising but true

PT Usha missed an Olympic bronze medal in the 400 m hurdles at Los Angeles in 1984 by just one hundredth of a second, less than the blink of an eye.

Pioneers on the track and in the gym

PT Usha, from Kerala, was the queen of Indian athletics in the 1980s. She won a string of gold medals at the Asian Games and Asian Championships. At the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics she finished fourth in the 400 m hurdles, missing bronze by one hundredth of a second.

In 2000, weightlifter Karnam Malleswari, from Andhra Pradesh, won bronze at the Sydney Olympics. She became the first Indian woman to win an Olympic medal. In 2003 long jumper Anju Bobby George won bronze at the World Championships in Paris, India's first medal at that event.

Punches and shuttles

Mary Kom grew up in a farming family in Manipur. She took up boxing as a teenager and went on to win six world championships. In 2012, when women's boxing joined the Olympics, she won bronze in London, even though she had to box in a heavier class than her usual one.

Badminton gave India two Olympic stars. Saina Nehwal won bronze in 2012. Then PV Sindhu, from Hyderabad, won silver in 2016 and bronze in 2021, becoming the first Indian woman with two Olympic medals. Sindhu is also a world champion, from 2019.

Cricket's big night

For years women's cricket in India got little attention. Mithali Raj helped change that. She scored more runs in women's one-day internationals than any other player and led India to two World Cup finals, in 2005 and 2017, losing both.

On 2 November 2025, at the DY Patil Stadium in Navi Mumbai, India finally won. Captained by Harmanpreet Kaur, the team beat South Africa by 52 runs in the final. Fans celebrated late into the night across the country, and the players became household names.

On target and over the board

Shooting has brought India many medals. At the Paris Olympics in 2024, Manu Bhaker won two bronze medals, one alone and one in a mixed team event. She became the first Indian to win two medals at a single Olympics since independence.

Weightlifter Mirabai Chanu won silver at Tokyo in 2021. Boxer Lovlina Borgohain and wrestler Sakshi Malik have also won Olympic medals.

In chess, a game with roots in India, Koneru Humpy has been one of the world's top women players for over two decades. In 2025, two Indians, Humpy and 19-year-old Divya Deshmukh, met in the final of the Women's World Cup. Divya won and earned the title of grandmaster.

More than medals

Many of these athletes faced extra hurdles because they were women. Some were told sport wasn't for girls. Others had to fight for equal pay, decent coaching or safe places to train.

Their wins have changed things. More girls now play cricket in school, box in local clubs and enter chess tournaments. In the USA, stars like Serena Williams and Simone Biles inspired a generation. In India, a girl watching Sindhu or Harmanpreet on TV can now see someone who looks like her lifting a trophy.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Indian women only started doing well in sport recently.

Truth: Indian women have been winning on the world stage for decades. PT Usha won many Asian titles in the 1980s, and Karnam Malleswari won an Olympic medal in 2000. What has grown recently is the attention, money and number of girls taking part.
