Who was Lal Bahadur Shastri?

Lal Bahadur Shastri was India's prime minister for only about 19 months, yet his slogan "Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan" is still shouted at rallies today.

The short answer

Lal Bahadur Shastri was India's second full-time prime minister, after Jawaharlal Nehru. He led the country from June 1964 until his sudden death in January 1966. In that short time, India fought a war with Pakistan and faced a serious shortage of food.

Shastri was small, quiet and famously honest. He came from a poor family and lived simply even in high office. His slogan "Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan", meaning "Hail the soldier, hail the farmer", honoured the two groups he believed India depended on most. He also backed changes in farming and dairy that helped India grow far more food in the years after his death.

Surprising but true

In 1965, during a food shortage, Shastri asked Indians to skip one meal a week, and his own family did it first.

A childhood by the Ganga

Shastri was born on 2 October 1904, sharing a birthday with Gandhi, in Mughalsarai, near Varanasi. His father died when he was a baby, and his mother raised him with help from relatives. Money was tight.

An often-told story says he sometimes swam across the Ganga to get to school, because he couldn't spare the money for the boat. Whether or not every detail is true, it fits what people knew of him: stubborn, hard-working and not one to complain.

His family name was Srivastava, but he dropped it as a young man because he disliked the caste system. "Shastri" was a title he earned in 1925 after graduating from Kashi Vidyapith, a college set up by nationalists in Varanasi.

Freedom fighter and minister

At 16, Shastri left his studies to join Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement. Over the next 25 years he was jailed several times by the British, spending years of his life in prison.

After independence, he became a minister in Uttar Pradesh and then in Nehru's government in Delhi. As railway minister, he made a decision people still talk about. In 1956, after a deadly train accident at Ariyalur in Tamil Nadu, he resigned, saying he took moral responsibility. The railways were vast and he hadn't caused the crash, but he felt the minister should answer for it.

Soldier and farmer

When Nehru died in 1964, Shastri became prime minister. A year later, war broke out with Pakistan. Shastri's calm leadership during the fighting earned him great respect across India.

At the same time, monsoon rains failed and food ran short. India was relying on wheat shipped from the USA. Shastri asked Indians to skip one meal a week to save grain, and he had his own family do it first. In 1965 he gave the slogan "Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan", linking the soldiers at the border with the farmers in the fields.

He supported new seeds and farming methods that later grew into the Green Revolution, which made India far better at feeding itself.

Milk from Anand

In 1964, Shastri visited the dairy cooperative in Anand, Gujarat, that made Amul butter and milk. He was so impressed by how village farmers ran it together that he wanted the idea copied across India. In 1965 his government set up the National Dairy Development Board, led by Verghese Kurien.

That decision helped launch the White Revolution, which turned India into the world's biggest milk producer.

How did farmers build Amul?In 1946, angry dairy farmers in Gujarat refused to sell their milk for two weeks. Out of that strike grew one of India's best-known brands.

Tashkent

In January 1966, Shastri travelled to Tashkent, then part of the Soviet Union, for peace talks with Pakistan's president, Ayub Khan. On 10 January they signed the Tashkent Declaration, agreeing to pull back their armies. In the early hours of the next morning, Shastri died. The official cause was a heart attack, though some people, including members of his family, have long raised questions.

His body was flown home, and huge crowds lined the streets of Delhi. He was given the Bharat Ratna that year, the first person to receive it after death. His memorial in Delhi is called Vijay Ghat, meaning "place of victory".

Myth or fact?

Myth: Shastri was a weak leader who only filled the gap after Nehru.

Truth: Many people expected that at first, because he was small, soft-spoken and modest. But he led India through a war with Pakistan in 1965 and a serious food crisis, and his support for dairy and farming helped shape India's future.
