What is the Smithsonian?

America's biggest museum group was paid for by a British scientist who never once set foot in the United States.

The short answer

The Smithsonian Institution is a group of museums, a zoo and research centres run for the people of the United States. Most of its museums stand along the National Mall, the long grassy park in the middle of Washington, DC, and all of those are free to enter.

Between them they hold nearly 157 million things. There are dinosaur bones, moon rocks, aeroplanes, paintings, the flag that inspired the American national anthem and a pair of ruby slippers from a famous film. Most of the collection, about 148 million items, is scientific specimens like insects, fossils and minerals kept at the natural history museum. Only a tiny share is ever on display.

Surprising but true

When the Hope Diamond was given to the Smithsonian in 1958, it arrived through the ordinary post, wrapped in brown paper.

A gift from a stranger

James Smithson was a British chemist who studied minerals. When he died in 1829, his will said that if his nephew died without children, his fortune should go to the United States "to found at Washington... an establishment for the increase and diffusion of knowledge". Nobody knows exactly why. He had never visited.

The nephew died in 1835. In 1838 the money crossed the Atlantic as 105 bags of gold coins. Congress then argued for eight years about what to do with it, before setting up the Smithsonian in 1846. Its first building, a red sandstone castle with towers, still stands on the Mall. Smithson's remains were moved there in 1904.

Things you can see

At the National Air and Space Museum, you can stand under the Wright brothers' 1903 Flyer, the first successful powered aeroplane. Nearby is the capsule that brought the Apollo 11 astronauts home from the Moon. A sister museum in Virginia holds the space shuttle Discovery.

The National Museum of Natural History has a huge Tyrannosaurus rex skeleton, an elephant in its main hall and the Hope Diamond, a deep blue stone of about 45 carats. For anyone who loves gems and crystals, the mineral hall nearby can easily take an afternoon.

How did the Wright brothers learn to fly?The first powered aeroplane flight lasted 12 seconds and covered less distance than the length of a big jet's wings today.

Stories of a country

Some Smithsonian museums tell the history of particular people. The National Museum of the American Indian shows art and objects from Native nations across the Americas. The National Museum of African American History and Culture, opened in 2016, traces the story from slavery to civil rights to music and sport.

The National Museum of American History keeps everyday things too: lunchboxes, early computers, a kitchen used by a famous TV chef. The idea is that ordinary objects can explain a country as well as royal treasures.

Most of the Smithsonian's money comes from the US government, with the rest from gifts and its own businesses, such as shops and magazines. That has a side effect. When Congress and the president fail to agree a budget and the government shuts down, the museums can close too, as happened for weeks in 2019 and again in 2025.

Behind the doors

Most Smithsonian staff never work in a gallery. Scientists study coral reefs, track birds and name new species. The Smithsonian even runs a research institute in Panama, where scientists study tropical rainforests and the sea creatures on both sides of the Panama Canal. In storerooms the size of aircraft hangars, millions of specimens sit in drawers, waiting for researchers. Some fossils collected a century ago are still revealing new things, the way bones in dinosaur museums around the world keep surprising experts.

The National Zoo, also part of the Smithsonian, has bred rare animals and run research on giant pandas for decades. Its pandas have come and gone as agreements with China changed. All of this began with one man's wish to spread knowledge to a country he had only read about.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Smithsonian is one big museum.

Truth: It's a group of 21 museums, a zoo and several research centres. Most sit along the National Mall in Washington, but a few are in New York, Virginia and elsewhere.

Try this

Start a mini museum at home. Pick five small objects from around the house, like an old coin, a shell, a ticket or a toy. Write a label card for each with what it is, where it came from and why it matters. Ask a family member to tour your museum and guess which object you think is most precious.
