What is the frozen river trek of Ladakh?

Every winter in Ladakh, the Zanskar river freezes into a sheet of ice, and people walk along it for days at temperatures that can drop below -25 °C.

The short answer

The Chadar trek is a winter walk on the frozen Zanskar river in Ladakh. In January and February, the river freezes along its edges and often across, into a sheet called the chadar, which means blanket. For days, walkers follow the river through a deep, narrow gorge, sliding and shuffling over the ice.

Nights often fall below -20 °C, and sometimes below -25 °C. Walkers sleep in tents or caves along the bank. Today the trek draws adventurers from across India and abroad, but for centuries it was simply how the people of Zanskar travelled in winter, when snow closed the mountain passes.

Surprising but true

For centuries, the frozen Zanskar river was the only winter road out of the Zanskar valley, and families sent children and butter along it to Leh.

A road made of ice

Zanskar is a high valley, cut off for much of the winter by deep snow on the passes. For centuries, the frozen river was its only way out. Families walked the chadar to reach Leh, the main town of Ladakh, to trade butter, cheese and other goods, and to take children to school and back.

Villagers knew every bend and every dangerous stretch. They walked in felt boots or wrapped their feet, carrying loads on their backs. Some still do. In recent years a road has been built through the gorge, and work on it has changed parts of the river.

Why the river freezes on top

Ice is lighter than water, so when a river freezes, the ice forms on top, like a lid. That lid keeps the cold air off the water underneath, so the river keeps flowing below. Explaining why ice floats is one of the odd tricks of water. Most liquids get heavier when they turn solid.

So walkers on the chadar are often standing on ice with water rushing beneath it. The ice can be glassy blue, snowy white or covered in a film of flowing water. Guides tap it with sticks and listen. A dull thud or a hollow ring can mean very different things.

Why does ice float?Almost every solid sinks in its own liquid. Water is the weird one.

Frozen waterfalls and caves

The most famous sight is near the village of Nerak, where a waterfall freezes into a huge curtain of ice, hanging down the cliff. Walkers camp nearby, and some cross a bridge over the river close to it.

Along the way, the gorge walls rise hundreds of metres. In places, the sun reaches the river only for a short time each day. Walkers pass caves where travellers have sheltered for generations, cooking tea and keeping warm around small fires.

Staying alive in the cold

The cold here is dangerous. Fingers, toes and noses can freeze. Walkers wear many layers, keep moving and drink hot drinks. Many local guides and porters from Zanskar villages lead the trek, carrying food, fuel and tents.

The trek starts at about 3,200 m above sea level, where air holds less oxygen. The Ladakh authorities now require visitors to spend days in Leh first to adjust to the height and to get a medical check. The body needs time, as the story of the cold body shows.

A trek under threat

In some recent winters, the river did not freeze properly. Warmer spells left gaps in the ice, forcing walkers onto steep riverbanks or making the trek impossible for days. Road building in the gorge has also changed how and where the river freezes.

For the people of Zanskar, a thin chadar is more than a problem for tourists. It is a sign of changing winters, linked to the warming of the Himalaya and its glaciers, which feed the very rivers that freeze into this road of ice.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Chadar is a thick, solid road of ice that is safe to walk anywhere.

Truth: The ice changes every day. In places it is thin, cracked or covered with flowing water, and in some stretches there is no ice at all. Local guides read the colour and sound of the ice to choose a safe path, and sometimes walkers must climb along the rocky banks.

Try this

Put a cup of water in the freezer and check it every 30 minutes. Notice where ice forms first. It starts at the top and the edges, and floats on the water below, just like the chadar on the Zanskar.
