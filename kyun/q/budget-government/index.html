How does a government spend money?

Before India's budget is printed, officials stir a giant pot of halwa. Then many of them stay locked inside the ministry until the budget is out.

The short answer

A government spends money the way a family does, but with far bigger numbers and many more people arguing over it. Each year it writes a budget: a plan of how much it expects to collect, mostly through taxes, and how much it will spend, and on what.

In India, the Finance Minister presents the Union Budget in Parliament on 1 February. In the United States, the President proposes a budget, but Congress decides how much each department actually gets. In both countries, elected members must vote before most money can be spent. That rule is old, and it exists so that rulers cannot take and spend people's money just as they please.

Surprising but true

Most big governments spend more than they collect in taxes almost every year, and borrow to cover the gap.

Where the money goes

Some spending is fixed before anyone starts arguing. Pensions, salaries and interest on old loans have to be paid. In India, interest on past borrowing is one of the largest single items. Defence, roads, railways, farm support and help for states come next.

Then there are smaller lines with big effects on children: school lunches through the mid-day meal scheme, vaccines, scholarships. The US federal government spends most on Social Security pensions and on health programmes for older and poorer people. Much of America's school money comes from states and towns instead. A budget is a list of choices. Every rupee given to a new highway is a rupee not given to a hospital.

Spending more than you earn

Big governments almost always spend more than they collect. The gap is called the deficit. To fill it, they borrow by selling bonds, which are promises to repay with interest. Banks, pension funds and ordinary savers buy them.

Borrowing is not always bad. A road or a power station can help the economy grow for decades, a bit like a family taking a loan for a house. But if debt grows faster than the economy year after year, interest payments start eating the budget. Economists keep a close eye on that balance.

What was the Great Depression?In October 1929 share prices in New York crashed. Within three years, about one in four American workers had no job, and the trouble had spread around the globe.

Halwa and red folders

Budget day in India comes with traditions. A few days before, officials hold a halwa ceremony, sharing the sweet from a huge pan. After it, staff involved in preparing the documents stay inside the ministry, cut off from phones and visitors, so that no numbers leak early.

For decades, Finance Ministers carried the budget into Parliament in a leather briefcase, a habit inherited from Britain. In 2019, Nirmala Sitharaman swapped it for a red cloth folder like a traditional bahi-khata, or account book. From 2021 the budget went paperless, read from a tablet.

The very first budget of independent India was presented on 26 November 1947, barely three months after freedom, by R. K. Shanmukham Chetty. The country was still dealing with the upheaval of Partition, and close to half of the planned spending went on defence.

When the money stops

In America, if Congress and the President cannot agree on funding by the start of the financial year on 1 October, parts of the government shut down. Museums close, some workers are sent home and others work without pay until a deal is reached. One shutdown in late 2025 lasted about six weeks.

India's system works differently, because the government that controls the Lok Sabha nearly always gets its budget passed. The fights happen in speeches and on television, and the lights in government offices stay on.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A government can never run out of money because it can just print more.

Truth: Printing lots of new money usually makes prices rise fast, so each rupee or dollar buys less. Germany in 1923 and Zimbabwe in 2008 printed money to pay their bills, and prices rose so quickly that savings became almost worthless.

Try this

Plan a pretend budget for your class with 100 counters or buttons. Split them between things like books, sports, a trip and repairs. Then a friend announces an emergency, say a broken window, that costs 20. Decide what to cut, or whether to borrow from next year.
