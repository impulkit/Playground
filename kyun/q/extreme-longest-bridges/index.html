What are the longest bridges in the world?

The longest bridge in the world is so long that a bullet train takes about half an hour to cross it, and most of it isn't over water at all.

The short answer

The longest bridge on Earth is the Danyang–Kunshan Grand Bridge in China, 164.8 kilometres long. It opened in 2011 and carries the high-speed railway between Beijing and Shanghai. It's a viaduct, a long chain of short spans resting on pillars, crossing rice paddies, canals, rivers and a lake. A bullet train at full speed needs about half an hour to cross it.

Other bridges hold other records. The Lake Pontchartrain Causeway in Louisiana, USA, runs about 38 kilometres over water without a break. The Çanakkale 1915 Bridge in Turkey has the longest gap between two towers, 2,023 metres. In India, Mumbai's Atal Setu is the country's longest sea bridge, at about 21.8 kilometres.

Surprising but true

Turkey's Çanakkale 1915 Bridge has a main span of exactly 2,023 metres, chosen to mark the 100th birthday of the country in 2023.

Why build a bridge over land?

China's bullet trains need tracks that are very flat and very straight. On the ground, that would mean flattening hills, filling hollows and cutting through villages and farms. Lifting the track onto pillars solves all of that. It also protects the line from floods and earthquakes, and leaves the fields underneath for farming.

Many of the world's longest bridges are railway viaducts in China, built this way in the 2000s and 2010s. Each section is made in a factory and lifted into place by huge machines, a bit like laying giant Lego bricks.

Out of sight of land

The Lake Pontchartrain Causeway is two side-by-side bridges across a lake near New Orleans. In the middle, drivers can travel for several kilometres and see no land at all, only water and the road ahead.

China's Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau link, opened in 2018, runs about 55 kilometres across the sea. Part of it dives into an undersea tunnel between two artificial islands, so that big ships can sail over the top.

How did a puzzle about seven bridges start a branch of maths?People in an old German city liked to try a Sunday walk crossing each of their seven bridges exactly once. Nobody ever managed it, and in 1736 a mathematician proved why.

The longest leap

A suspension bridge hangs its road from thick steel cables draped over tall towers. It's the best design for crossing a wide gap without pillars in the middle. When San Francisco's Golden Gate Bridge opened in 1937, its 1,280-metre main span was the longest in the world.

Today the record belongs to the Çanakkale 1915 Bridge over the Dardanelles strait in Turkey, opened in 2022. Its main span is 2,023 metres, about 20 football fields end to end. The number was chosen on purpose: 2023 was the 100th anniversary of the Turkish Republic.

Heat, wind and earthquakes

Long bridges grow and shrink with the weather. Steel and concrete expand on hot days and contract on cold nights, so engineers leave gaps called expansion joints. When a car thumps over a metal strip on a bridge, it's usually crossing one of them.

Wind is a bigger danger for long suspension bridges. In 1940 a new bridge in Washington state, USA, nicknamed Galloping Gertie, began twisting in strong wind and tore itself apart only four months after it opened. Since then, engineers test models of big bridges in wind tunnels before building them.

In earthquake zones, bridges are designed to sway and slide a little without falling. While Japan's Akashi Kaikyō Bridge was being built, a huge earthquake in 1995 shifted its two towers, making the main span nearly a metre longer.

India's record breakers

Atal Setu, opened in January 2024, links Mumbai to Navi Mumbai across the sea. It's nearly four times as long as the Bandra–Worli Sea Link. Engineers had to protect flamingos in the mudflats below, so parts of the bridge carry noise barriers.

India also has one of the world's most dramatic bridges. The Chenab Bridge in Jammu and Kashmir, opened to trains in 2025, carries a railway 359 metres above the river, higher than the Eiffel Tower. And the strangest part? Some of India's toughest bridges aren't built at all. In Meghalaya, people guide the roots of rubber fig trees across streams until they become living root bridges.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The longest bridges are the ones that hang from giant cables.

Truth: Suspension bridges have the longest single gaps between towers, but the longest bridges overall are viaducts, long chains of short spans on pillars. Each part is ordinary. There are just thousands of them.

Try this

Build a bridge between two stacks of books, 20 cm apart, using only one sheet of paper. A flat sheet sags. Now fold the paper into a zigzag like a fan and try again. Add coins one by one and count how many each design holds. Folding is one of engineers' oldest tricks for making things stiff.
