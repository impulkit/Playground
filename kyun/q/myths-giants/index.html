Why do stories have giants?

Ancient Greeks may have looked at the skull of a dwarf elephant, seen one huge hole in the middle and pictured a one-eyed giant.

The short answer

Giants turn up in stories nearly everywhere. Greece had Titans and the one-eyed Cyclopes. Norse myth had frost giants. India has Kumbhakarna, who slept for months at a time, and the rakshasa Hidimba from the Mahabharata. The Bible has Goliath. America has Paul Bunyan, a lumberjack so big his footprints made lakes.

Nobody knows for sure why. Historians and psychologists point to a few likely reasons. People found enormous bones in the ground. They saw landscapes and ruins that looked too big for humans to have made. And every child spends years looking up at adults who seem huge and powerful. Giants turn all that into a story.

Surprising but true

In Norse myth, the whole world was made from the body of a frost giant called Ymir, with his blood becoming the sea and his skull the sky.

Bones in the hillside

Picture a farmer in ancient Greece breaking ground and hitting a skull the size of a cart wheel. On islands like Crete and Sicily, people did find skulls of extinct dwarf elephants. An elephant skull has a big hole in the middle of the face where the trunk joins. It looks like one huge eye socket.

In 1914, a scientist named Othenio Abel suggested these skulls inspired the Cyclopes. In 2000, the historian Adrienne Mayor went further in her book The First Fossil Hunters. She argued that Greeks and Romans collected giant bones and treated them as remains of heroes and monsters. Not everyone agrees, but bones of mammoths and other big animals were certainly found and displayed in ancient temples.

Walls too big for people

Some ruins look as if giants built them. At Mycenae in Greece, the walls are made of boulders weighing several tonnes each. Later Greeks couldn't believe ordinary people had lifted them, so they said the Cyclopes did it. Builders still call this style "Cyclopean masonry".

In Ireland, legend says the giant Finn McCool built the Giant's Causeway as a stepping-stone road across the sea. The rock columns are real, and the true story of how lava made them is just as strange.

How did the Giant's Causeway form?On the coast of Northern Ireland, about 40,000 stone columns fit together like a honeycomb, and most of them have six sides.

Giants who made the world

In many myths, giants were there first. Norse stories say the world was built from the body of the frost giant Ymir. His flesh became land, his bones became mountains and his skull became the sky. In Greek myth, the Titans ruled before Zeus and the Olympian gods overthrew them.

Giants in these stories stand for wild, old forces like frost, fire and storms. The gods who defeat them bring order. You can read it as a story about people taming a dangerous world.

The sleeping giant of Lanka

In the Ramayana, Ravana's brother Kumbhakarna was so large that waking him took an army. In one telling, soldiers blew conches, beat drums and drove elephants over him. He slept for six months, woke for a single day to eat mountains of food, then slept again. When he finally joined the battle, he was fierce but also honest. He told Ravana he was wrong to kidnap Sita, and fought anyway out of loyalty.

Many giants are like that: frightening, but with a sad or funny side. In the Mahabharata, the rakshasi Hidimbi falls in love with the hero Bhima, and their son Ghatotkacha grows into a giant warrior who fights on the side of the Pandavas.

Why we still love them

Giants keep appearing in new stories, from Roald Dahl's The BFG to the Titans in Attack on Titan. Psychologists note that small children live in a land of giants. Adults are two or three times their height and control almost everything.

A giant can be a bully like Goliath or a gentle friend like the BFG. And giants have company. Almost every culture also tells of dragons, and some historians think the same buried bones lie behind those stories too.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Giant skeletons tens of metres tall have been dug up and hidden by scientists.

Truth: Photos of giant human skeletons online are edited images. No giant human bones have ever been found. The tallest people known, with good records, were under 3 metres tall.

Try this

Draw a giant from your own imagination. Then add a small person at its feet for scale. Write a short story about where your giant lives and why nobody has found it yet. Notice which real things, such as hills, big rocks or old bones, you used to make the story feel true.
