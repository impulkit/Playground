Why are flamingos pink?

Flamingo chicks hatch grey. They turn pink slowly, because of what they eat.

The short answer

Flamingos are pink because of what they eat. Their food, tiny algae and small creatures such as brine shrimp, contains pigments called carotenoids. These are the same kind of colour chemicals that make carrots orange and tomatoes red.

As a flamingo digests its food, its body turns these pigments into pink and red colours, which end up in its new feathers, skin and legs. Flamingos that eat more of them are brighter. Chicks hatch grey and take two or three years to turn fully pink.

Surprising but true

Flamingo parents feed their chicks a red "milk" made in their throats. Both mothers and fathers make it.

Eating upside down

A flamingo feeds with its head upside down. It wades into shallow, often salty water, dips its head and sweeps its bent beak from side to side.

Its tongue works like a pump, sucking water in and pushing it out. The edges of the beak are lined with comb-like plates that act as a strainer. Mud and water flow out. Algae, shrimp and other tiny food stay in. Whales like the blue whale feed in a similar way, using plates of baleen.

The lesser flamingo eats mostly tiny blue-green algae and is a deeper pink. The greater flamingo, which is taller and paler, eats more small shrimp, worms and insect larvae.

Red milk

Flamingo chicks can't strain food at first. Instead, both parents feed them a bright red liquid called crop milk, made in their throats. It is rich in fat and protein, and also full of the red pigment.

Feeding a chick for months drains a parent's colour. Many flamingo parents look noticeably paler by the end of the breeding season. A bright pink flamingo is usually a well-fed, healthy one, and brighter birds tend to attract partners more easily.

Flamingos in India

Every winter, tens of thousands of flamingos gather on the mudflats of Thane Creek in Mumbai, within sight of busy roads and tall buildings. In 2015, part of the creek was declared the Thane Creek Flamingo Sanctuary.

The biggest breeding grounds in India are in the Rann of Kutch in Gujarat. After good monsoon rains, the salty flats fill with shallow water, and huge colonies of greater flamingos build mud-mound nests there. The place is so famous that it is nicknamed Flamingo City. Flamingos depend on wetlands like these, which are easily lost to building and pollution.

Standing on one leg

Flamingos often stand, and even sleep, on one leg. Why they do it has been debated for years. One idea is that tucking a leg into their feathers keeps them warm, since less skin is exposed to cold water.

Another idea comes from a 2017 study in the USA. Researchers found that a flamingo's body can balance on one leg almost without using its muscles, locking into place like a folding stand. Both reasons may be true.

A pink icon

Flamingos are found in Asia, Africa, Europe and the Americas, and there are six species in all. The American flamingo lives in the Caribbean and parts of Florida.

In 1957, an American designer named Don Featherstone created a plastic pink flamingo for gardens. Millions have been sold, and the plastic bird is now far more common in American yards than real ones. A group of flamingos, by the way, is called a flamboyance.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Flamingos are born pink.

Truth: Flamingo chicks hatch with grey or white fluffy down. They slowly turn pink over two to three years as they eat food rich in carotenoid pigments.

Try this

Time how long you can stand on one leg with your eyes open. Then try with your eyes closed. Then try it with your knee bent and your other foot tucked up, like a flamingo. Flamingos can sleep standing this way.
