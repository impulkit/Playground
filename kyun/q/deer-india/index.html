What kinds of deer live in India?

In Manipur, a rare deer lives on islands of floating plants and steps so lightly that people call it the dancing deer.

The short answer

India has several kinds of deer, from tiny, barking forest deer to big, shaggy stags that weigh more than a motorbike. The most common is the chital, or spotted deer, often seen in herds in national parks. The biggest is the sambar. Others include the barasingha, or swamp deer, the hog deer, the barking deer, the sangai of Manipur and the hangul of Kashmir.

All male deer grow antlers, branching bony crowns that fall off every year and grow back bigger. India also has musk deer and mouse deer, which look like deer and are close cousins but belong to separate families. Many of India's deer are prey for tigers and leopards, so they live on high alert.

Surprising but true

The blackbuck and the nilgai, two of India's best-known wild grazers, are not deer at all. They are antelopes, with horns that stay on for life.

The spotted crowd

Chital are the deer most visitors see first. They have reddish-brown coats with neat white spots that they keep all their lives, unlike many deer whose spots fade. Herds of dozens graze in open forest clearings in parks like Kanha, Corbett and Bandipur.

Chital often feed beneath trees where langur monkeys sit. The monkeys drop leaves and fruit, which the deer eat. The monkeys watch from above, the deer sniff and listen below, and when one spots a tiger, both give alarm calls. Wildlife guides follow these calls to find big cats, which is why chital and langurs are called a team.

The big and the barking

The sambar is India's largest deer. A big stag can weigh more than 250 kilograms and stand about as tall at the shoulder as a grown-up. It lives in thick forest, comes out mostly at night and loves wallowing in water. When frightened, it stamps a foot and gives a loud, honking bell.

The Indian muntjac, or barking deer, is small, about the size of a large dog. Its alarm call sounds like a dog's bark and can carry a long way through the forest at dusk. Males have short antlers and two small tusks that poke down from the upper jaw.

Saved from the brink

The barasingha, or swamp deer, is named for its antlers. "Bara singha" means twelve tines, and a big stag can have twelve or more points. A hard-ground kind of barasingha lives only in central India. By 1970, only about 66 remained, all in Kanha in Madhya Pradesh.

Forest staff protected their meadows, relocated some villages with compensation, and fenced off breeding areas. The herd recovered, and recent counts have found more than 1,000 in Kanha, with some moved to other reserves to start new herds.

Deer of the valleys and lakes

The hangul, also called the Kashmir stag, is a kind of red deer found mainly in Dachigam National Park near Srinagar, in Kashmir. It is the official animal of Jammu and Kashmir. Its numbers fell to a few hundred, and a 2025 census counted 323, up from 289 in 2023.

The sangai, a kind of Eld's deer, lives only around Keibul Lamjao National Park, on Loktak Lake in Manipur. The park is the world's only floating national park, made of thick mats of floating plants called phumdis. The deer walk delicately across these soft islands, which may be why they are called dancing deer. The sangai is Manipur's state animal, and only a few hundred survive.

Antlers, not horns

Antlers are among the fastest-growing bone tissue of any animal. While growing, they are covered in soft, furry skin called velvet, full of blood vessels. When fully grown, the velvet dries and the stag rubs it off on trees, leaving hard, bare bone for fighting rivals in the breeding season.

Afterwards the antlers drop off, and a new set starts. This is a quick way to tell deer from antelopes like the blackbuck and nilgai, whose horns stay on for life. Spotted coats, sharp ears and lightning-fast reflexes all help deer stay alive in forests full of predators. Even a tiger's stripes are designed to fool a deer's eyes.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The blackbuck and the nilgai are kinds of deer.

Truth: Both are antelopes. Antelopes have permanent horns, while deer have antlers that fall off and regrow every year. The nilgai's name means blue cow, and it is Asia's largest antelope.
