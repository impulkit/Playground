What are the Paralympics?

The Paralympics began in 1948 as an archery contest for 16 injured ex-servicemen and women on a hospital lawn in England.

The short answer

The Paralympics are the world's biggest sporting event for athletes with disabilities. They are held every four years, soon after the Summer and Winter Olympics, in the same city and venues. Athletes compete in sports from swimming and athletics to wheelchair basketball and blind football.

The name comes from the Greek word "para", meaning beside or alongside. The Games stand side by side with the Olympics. At the Paris 2024 Paralympics, about 4,400 athletes from more than 160 teams took part.

Surprising but true

Since 1988, the Paralympics have been held in the same city as the Olympics, using the same stadiums, a few weeks later.

A doctor and a hospital lawn

The story begins with Dr Ludwig Guttmann, a Jewish doctor who fled Nazi Germany in 1939 and settled in England. During the Second World War, he ran a unit for people with spinal cord injuries at Stoke Mandeville Hospital, north-west of London.

At the time, most patients with such injuries were expected to stay in bed and many died young. Guttmann believed sport could help them get stronger and feel hopeful. On 29 July 1948, the same day the London Olympics opened, he held an archery competition for 16 wheelchair users, most of them injured ex-servicemen and women. He called it the Stoke Mandeville Games.

From hospital games to world stage

The Stoke Mandeville Games grew each year, and athletes from the Netherlands joined in 1952. In 1960 the games were held in Rome, just after the Olympics there, with about 400 athletes from 23 countries. This is now counted as the first Paralympic Games.

The first Winter Paralympics followed in Sweden in 1976. Since the Seoul Games of 1988, the Paralympics have been held in the Olympic host city. The International Paralympic Committee was founded in 1989.

Making competition fair

Paralympic sport uses a system called classification. Athletes are grouped with others whose impairment affects their sport in a similar way. A swimmer with one arm will race others with similar limits, not someone with a visual impairment.

Some sports exist only at the Paralympics. Goalball, for blind and partially sighted players, uses a ball with bells inside. In boccia, athletes with severe impairments throw or roll leather balls towards a target ball, sometimes using a ramp. Technology helps too. Carbon-fibre running blades and light sports wheelchairs are designed with the help of engineers and sports scientists.

India's Paralympic stars

India's first Paralympic gold came in 1972, when Murlikant Petkar won the 50 metres freestyle swim in Germany, setting a world record. He was a soldier who had been badly wounded in the 1965 war.

Javelin thrower Devendra Jhajharia won gold in 2004 and 2016. High jumper Mariyappan Thangavelu won gold in Rio. At Tokyo, shooter Avani Lekhara became the first Indian woman to win a Paralympic gold, and she won again in Paris. India won 19 medals at Tokyo and 29 in Paris, far more than at its Olympic appearances.

The symbol and the spirit

The Paralympic symbol is three curved shapes in red, blue and green, called agitos, from a Latin word meaning "I move". The motto is "Spirit in Motion".

Guttmann hoped the Games would change how people saw disability, and over the decades crowds and television audiences have grown. At London 2012 the stadium was full night after night. Away from sport, inventions such as braille have opened up reading and work in the same spirit. The Paralympics showed millions that a wheelchair or a missing limb is not the end of a sporting dream.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Paralympics and the Special Olympics are the same thing.

Truth: They are separate. The Paralympics are elite competitions for athletes with a range of physical, visual and some intellectual impairments. The Special Olympics are for people with intellectual disabilities and focus on taking part at every level.

Try this

With an adult's help, try a sound game. One person rolls a ball with a small bell, or a plastic bottle with rice in it, gently across the floor. The other, with eyes closed, tries to stop it by listening. Notice how much harder you have to concentrate.
