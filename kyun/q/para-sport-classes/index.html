What do the letters and numbers in para sport mean?

At a para athletics meet, the results board says T64, F57 and T11. Each code tells you exactly who is racing whom, and why it is fair.

The short answer

In para athletics, every event has a code made of a letter and a number. The letter tells you the kind of event: T for track, F for field. The number tells you the kind of impairment and how much it affects the sport.

Athletes with the same code compete against each other. So a race is never "everyone against everyone". It is runners whose bodies work in a similar way racing each other, and the best-trained one wins.

Surprising but true

In races for blind runners, a sighted guide runs alongside, joined by a short tether. If they win, the guide gets a medal too.

Reading the code

The first number shows the group. Numbers in the 10s are for athletes with a vision impairment, the 20s for an intellectual impairment, and the 30s to 60s for different physical impairments.

The second number shows how strongly the sport is affected. A lower number usually means a bigger effect. So a T11 runner is blind and races with a guide, while a T13 runner can see a little.

For example, T64 is for runners with a leg missing below the knee who race on a carbon-fibre prosthetic blade. F57 is for throwers who compete sitting down, because legs give a big push to a javelin throw.

What are the Paralympics?The Paralympics began in 1948 as an archery contest for 16 injured ex-servicemen and women on a hospital lawn in England.

Who decides?

Before competing, athletes are checked by trained experts called classifiers, usually doctors and coaches. They test strength, movement or sight, and watch the athlete do their sport, to classify them correctly. An athlete can be checked again later if their body changes.

It is not perfect. Bodies are all different, and people still argue about where some lines should be drawn. But the aim is simple: win because you trained hardest, not because of your body.

Codes in action

The same system runs from the Paralympics to the Asian Para Games, a tournament that began in 2010 and are held soon after the Asian Games. India won 114 medals at the 2026 Games in Japan, its best ever, and many of its para athletes are now household names.

Every class also sends a message about inclusion: when the rules are fair, everyone can take part.

Quick facts

T11

T64
runners with a leg missing below the knee, on a blade
F57
field athletes who compete seated

Myth or fact?

Myth: Para athletes are not as serious about sport as other athletes.

Truth: Para athletes train for years, follow strict diets and break world records. Some para javelin throwers hurl it farther than many non-disabled club athletes.

Try this

Try throwing a soft ball as far as you can while standing, then while sitting on a chair. Measure both throws. Notice how much your legs help. Now you can see why seated throwers compete in their own class.
