What is a haiku?

The most famous haiku in the world is about a frog jumping into a pond. That's it, and people have been talking about it for more than 300 years.

The short answer

A haiku is a very short poem from Japan. In Japanese, it has 17 sound units arranged in three parts of 5, 7 and 5. It usually captures one small moment in nature, like a frog, a falling leaf or the first snow.

A traditional haiku has a season word, called a kigo, which tells you the time of year. It also has a "cut", a pause that sets two images side by side and lets the reader feel the connection. A good haiku doesn't explain itself. It shows you something and stops.

Surprising but true

In 2013, NASA collected more than 12,000 haiku from the public and sent over 1,100 of them towards Mars on a disc carried by its MAVEN spacecraft.

The frog in the old pond

The most famous haiku was written in 1686 by Matsuo Bashō. In Japanese it reads: furu ike ya / kawazu tobikomu / mizu no oto. In English that is roughly: "An old pond. A frog jumps in. The sound of water."

The word ya is the cut. First comes the old, still pond. Then, suddenly, a frog and a splash. Then silence again. The frog is the season word, because in Japanese poetry frogs belong to spring. Readers have argued for centuries about what it means: stillness and movement, old and new, or simply a splash heard on a quiet day.

Where haiku came from

Haiku grew out of a group game. In old Japan, poets met to write linked verse, called renga, taking turns to add lines to a long chain. The opening verse, the hokku, had to set the season and the scene. Over time, poets began writing hokku on their own.

Bashō turned them into serious art. He lived simply, wandered across Japan on foot and wrote travel diaries mixed with poems. The most famous, The Narrow Road to the Deep North, describes a journey of about five months in 1689. At Yamadera, a temple high on a mountain, he wrote of the cicadas' cry seeping into the rocks.

Other masters followed, including Buson, who was also a painter, and Issa, who wrote tenderly about snails, flies and sparrows. In the 1890s, the poet Masaoka Shiki gave the form its modern name, haiku.

Counting sounds, not syllables

Japanese poets count "on", sound units that are shorter than English syllables. That is why a 17-syllable English haiku says much more than a Japanese one. Many poets writing in English now aim for about 10 to 14 syllables, in three short lines.

Haiku are often written by hand with a brush, and in Japan the calligraphy can matter as much as the words. Schoolchildren in Japan write haiku, and newspapers there print readers' haiku every week.

Haiku around the world

In the early 1900s, haiku spread to Europe and the Americas. In India, writers in Bengali, Hindi, Tamil and other languages have tried it. The short form suits many languages, because nearly every place has seasons, weather and small creatures worth noticing.

Seasons are the heart of a haiku, though which ones depends on where you live. A poet in Mumbai might choose the monsoon frogs or the first mangoes. A poet in Minnesota might choose the first snowfall. The tilt of the Earth that causes seasons gives every haiku its clock.

In 2013, NASA invited people to send haiku to Mars. More than 12,000 were submitted, and over 1,100 of them, including the most popular, were put on a disc aboard the MAVEN spacecraft, which went into orbit around Mars in 2014. Those 17 sound units travelled further than Bashō ever walked.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A haiku is any poem with exactly 5, 7 and 5 syllables in English.

Truth: The 5–7–5 count comes from Japanese sound units, which are shorter than English syllables. Many haiku poets writing in English use fewer syllables, and care more about a season, a sharp image and a moment of surprise.

Try this

Go outside or to a window and find one small thing happening right now, like an ant carrying a crumb or rain dripping off a leaf. Write it in three short lines, with one word that tells the season. Don't explain how you feel. Let the picture do it.
