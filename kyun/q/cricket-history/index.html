How did cricket become India's favourite sport?

The first cricket match recorded in India was played by sailors on a Gujarat beach.

The short answer

Cricket came to India with the British. Indians soon made it their own. It spread from army camps and clubs to schools, princely states and city streets, and after India won the World Cup in 1983, it became a national passion.

Gully cricket needs just a bat, a ball and some imagination. A wall can be the wicket, a rubber ball replaces the leather one, and every street has its own rules.

Today, India has more cricket fans than any other country. Its cricket board is the richest in the sport, and its stars are among the most famous people in the country.

Surprising but true

Cricket in India was first recorded in 1721, before the USA even existed as a country.

Sailors on a beach

In 1721, British sailors from an East India Company ship played cricket near Khambhat, in Gujarat. A sailor named Clement Downing wrote about it in a book in 1737. It is the first recorded cricket in India.

For a long time, cricket was a game for British soldiers and officials. The Calcutta Cricket Club was founded in 1792. Then the Parsis of Bombay, a community of traders, took it up keenly. They formed clubs in the 1840s and toured England in 1886. Hindu, Muslim and other community teams followed.

Cricket and caste

One of India's early cricket heroes was Palwankar Baloo, a left-arm spinner who played in the 1890s and 1900s. He was a Dalit, from a community treated as "untouchable", and was often forced to eat and drink apart from his teammates. His skill made him famous anyway. In 1937 he stood for election in Bombay against B.R. Ambedkar, the great leader who fought against caste discrimination.

At the same time, a prince named Ranjitsinhji was dazzling crowds in England with his stylish batting. India's national championship, the Ranji Trophy, started in 1934, is named after him.

From Lord's to the world

India played its first Test match in 1932, at Lord's in London, led by C.K. Nayudu. It took 20 years and 25 Tests to win one, against England in Madras in 1952.

The big turning point came in 1983. India's men, led by Kapil Dev, were outsiders at the World Cup in England, but they beat the mighty West Indies in the final at Lord's. Colour TV had arrived in India the year before, and millions watched. Children everywhere started playing in streets and gullies.

India won the World Cup again in 2011, with the final in Mumbai, and the T20 World Cup in 2007, 2024 and 2026. In 2008, the Indian Premier League began, turning cricket into a TV show watched by crores.

Women's cricket

Indian women have played Test cricket since 1976. For years they got little money or attention. Stars like Mithali Raj, who played international cricket for over 20 years, helped change that. In 2025, India's women won their first ODI World Cup, beating South Africa in the final in Navi Mumbai.

America's lost game

Cricket was once popular in the USA. The first international match ever played was between the USA and Canada in New York in 1844. Then baseball took over, and cricket faded.

Now it is returning. Many South Asian families in America play, the USA co-hosted the T20 World Cup in 2024, and cricket will be part of the Olympics in Los Angeles in 2028. A game that left India's shores as a British habit is now being carried back across the world by Indian fans.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Cricket was invented in India.

Truth: Cricket began in south-east England, probably by the 1500s. It came to India with British sailors, soldiers and traders, and Indians later made it their own.

Try this

Play a game of gully cricket with "home rules", like "one tip one hand" (a catch after one bounce, caught one-handed, is out) or "hit the neighbour's wall and you're out". Write your rules down, then compare them with the official Laws of Cricket. Which of your rules would make the real game more fun?
