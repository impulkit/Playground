What was the Second World War?

The largest war in history involved more than 100 million soldiers, and it was fought on land, at sea, in the air and in secret code rooms.

The short answer

The Second World War was fought from 1939 to 1945 between two groups of countries. The Axis was led by Germany, Italy and Japan. The Allies were led by Britain, the Soviet Union, the USA and China. It was fought across Europe, Asia, Africa and every ocean.

It was the deadliest conflict in history. Roughly 7 to 8.5 crore people died, more of them civilians than soldiers. The war ended with the defeat of Germany in May 1945 and Japan in August 1945. Afterwards, the world's map, its powerful countries and its ideas about keeping the peace all changed.

Surprising but true

British India raised about 25 lakh soldiers during the war, often described as the largest volunteer army in history.

How it began

Many Germans were angry and poor after losing the First World War. In 1933, Adolf Hitler and his Nazi party came to power. They blamed Jewish people and others for Germany's troubles, built up a huge army and began taking over neighbouring lands.

On 1 September 1939, Germany invaded Poland. Two days later, Britain and France declared war. By mid-1940 Germany had conquered much of western Europe, including France. Britain held out, and in 1941 Germany invaded the Soviet Union.

In Asia, Japan had been at war with China since 1937. In December 1941 it attacked the US base at Pearl Harbor, and the USA joined the war.

The Holocaust

The Nazis set out to murder Europe's Jewish people. About 60 lakh Jewish men, women and children were killed, along with Roma people, disabled people and others. This is called the Holocaust. The diary of Anne Frank, a Jewish girl who hid with her family in Amsterdam, has helped millions of readers understand what was lost.

A war of secrets

Codes and code-breaking mattered enormously. At Bletchley Park in England, thousands of people, many of them young women, worked to read German messages. Alan Turing helped design machines to crack the German Enigma code. In the Pacific, US Marines used Navajo speakers to send messages the Japanese could not understand.

Who were the Navajo code talkers?In the Second World War, a secret code built from a Native American language was never cracked by the enemy.

India in the war

India was still ruled by Britain, and the British declared India at war without asking Indian leaders. Congress protested, and in 1942 launched the Quit India movement. Meanwhile, about 25 lakh Indians volunteered for the army and served in Africa, Italy and Southeast Asia.

In 1944 Japanese forces, with soldiers of Subhas Chandra Bose's Indian National Army, pushed into Manipur and Nagaland. At Imphal and Kohima in northeast India, Allied troops, most of them Indian, stopped them after months of fighting. Many Naga and Manipuri villagers helped carry supplies.

In 1943, a terrible famine struck Bengal, and around 30 lakh people died. Historians agree that war, rising prices and government decisions made it far worse.

How it ended

On 6 June 1944, D-Day, Allied soldiers landed on the beaches of Normandy in France. Soviet armies pushed in from the east. Germany surrendered on 8 May 1945.

Japan fought on. In August 1945 the USA dropped atomic bombs on the cities of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, killing many tens of thousands of people at once. Japan surrendered days later and signed the formal surrender on 2 September 1945.

What came after

The war left Europe and much of Asia in ruins. In 1945, countries founded the United Nations to try to stop such a war happening again. The USA and the Soviet Union came out as the two most powerful nations, and soon became rivals in the Cold War.

Britain was exhausted, and its empire began to end. Two years later, in 1947, India became independent. At Kohima, a war cemetery stands on the hill where the tennis court once was.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The USA was in the Second World War from the very start.

Truth: The USA stayed out of the fighting for more than two years. It joined only after Japan attacked the American naval base at Pearl Harbor in Hawaii on 7 December 1941.
