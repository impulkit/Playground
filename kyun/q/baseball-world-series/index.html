What is the World Series?

It is called the World Series, but almost every team that has ever played in it comes from the United States.

The short answer

The World Series is the championship of Major League Baseball (MLB), the top professional baseball league in North America. Every October, the winner of the American League plays the winner of the National League. The first team to win four games takes the title.

The first modern World Series was played in 1903. Since then it has been held almost every year. It is one of the oldest championships in American sport, and baseball fans call it the "Fall Classic", because it happens in autumn.

Surprising but true

The Chicago Cubs went 108 years between World Series wins, from 1908 to 2016.

How a season gets there

An MLB season is long. Each of the 30 teams plays 162 games from spring to the end of September. The best teams then enter the playoffs, a set of knockout rounds. Only two teams are left standing for the World Series.

The series is best of seven. The first two games are played at the home of the team with the better season, then two or three at the other team's park, then back again if needed. A best-of-seven reduces the role of luck. One bad day can lose a single match, but the stronger team usually wins four out of seven, which is a lesson in probability.

The first World Series

In 1903 the champions of two rival leagues agreed to play each other. The Boston Americans, later the Red Sox, beat the Pittsburgh Pirates. That series was best of nine.

In 1904 the New York Giants refused to play, saying the American League was not good enough, so there was no series. In 1994 a strike by players cancelled the end of the season, and again there was no World Series. Those are the only two missing years.

Curses and droughts

Fans love stories of bad luck. In 1920 the Boston Red Sox sold their star, Babe Ruth, to the New York Yankees. The Yankees became the most successful team in baseball, with 27 titles. The Red Sox did not win again until 2004. Fans blamed "the Curse of the Bambino", after Ruth's nickname.

The Chicago Cubs had an even longer wait. They won in 1908 and then not again until 2016, 108 years later. When they finally won, in a tense seventh game that went to extra innings, millions of people filled the streets of Chicago to celebrate.

Is it really a world championship?

Only one team from outside the USA has ever won: the Toronto Blue Jays of Canada, in 1992 and 1993. But many players come from other countries, especially the Dominican Republic, Venezuela, Cuba, Japan and South Korea.

In 2024 the Los Angeles Dodgers beat the New York Yankees with help from Japanese stars like Shohei Ohtani, who both pitches and bats. Baseball is also huge in Japan, where crowds fill stadiums for the national high school tournament.

Baseball and cricket

Baseball and cricket are distant cousins, both bat-and-ball games that grew from older English games. In both, a bowler or pitcher throws to a batter, and fielders try to catch the ball or get runners out.

The differences are big too. A baseball bat is round, and the ball can only be hit forwards into a wedge-shaped field. There are three strikes, not one wicket. Still, the World Series final innings can feel as tense as the last over of a World Cup final.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The World Series is named after the New York World newspaper.

Truth: This story is often repeated but there is no evidence for it. The phrase "World's Championship Series" was in use in the 1880s, before any link with that newspaper.
