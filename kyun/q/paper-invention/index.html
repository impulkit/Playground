Who invented paper?

The first people to make paper weren't humans. Wasps have been chewing wood into paper nests for millions of years.

The short answer

Paper was invented in China. For a long time, people gave the credit to Cai Lun, an official at the emperor's court, who presented a method for making paper in 105 CE. He used tree bark, hemp, old rags and fishing nets.

Archaeologists have since found paper in China that is a few hundred years older than Cai Lun. So papermaking probably grew slowly, with Cai Lun improving it and making it official. From China, paper travelled to Korea, Japan, Central Asia, the Arab world, India and finally Europe.

Surprising but true

In 1719, a French scientist watched wasps build their nests and suggested that people could make paper from wood too. It took more than a century before anyone did it on a large scale.

What makes paper paper

Paper is a thin sheet made of plant fibres. To make it, the plant material is soaked and beaten until the fibres separate. They float in water like a thin soup. A papermaker dips a fine screen into the soup and lifts it out. The water drains away, and the fibres are left behind in a tangled mat. When it dries, the fibres hold together, and you have a sheet.

That is different from papyrus in Egypt, palm leaves in India, or parchment in Europe, which was made from animal skin. Those were written on for centuries, but none of them is paper.

Cai Lun's paper

The early Chinese wrote on bamboo strips, which were heavy, and on silk, which was expensive. Paper was light and cheap. According to an official history written centuries later, Cai Lun presented his papermaking method to the Emperor He in 105 CE, and the emperor praised it.

Chinese papermakers kept improving their craft. Paper was used for books, letters, wrapping, umbrellas, kites and even clothing. By about the 700s CE, the Chinese were using woodblock printing on paper, and later they made the first paper money.

Along the trade routes

Paper reached Central Asia along the Silk Road. A famous story says that Chinese papermakers were captured at the Battle of Talas in 751 and taught the craft in Samarkand. Historians doubt this tale, because paper was probably already known in the region. But Samarkand did become famous for its paper.

By the late 700s, Baghdad had paper mills. The Arab world made huge amounts of paper, which helped books and learning spread. Paper reached Spain with Arab rulers, and by the 1100s it was being made at Xàtiva, near Valencia. From there it spread across Europe, ready for the printing press in the 1450s.

In India, paper became common during the time of the Delhi Sultanate, in the 1200s and 1300s, though palm leaves and birch bark were still used for long after. Towns like Sanganer near Jaipur and Kalpi in Uttar Pradesh became known for handmade paper, and some still make it.

From rags to wood

For centuries, paper in Europe was made from old cotton and linen rags. As more people learned to read and newspapers grew, there weren't enough rags.

In 1719, the French scientist René de Réaumur wrote that wasps make paper from wood, and suggested people try it. In the 1840s, inventors in Germany and Canada finally worked out how to grind wood into pulp for paper. That made paper cheap enough for daily newspapers, schoolbooks and, eventually, toilet paper.

Today most paper comes from wood pulp, and much of it is recycled. Each time paper is recycled, the fibres get shorter, so the same fibres can only be reused a handful of times. Some papermakers in India still use cotton rags, the way their ancestors did.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The ancient Egyptians invented paper, because they wrote on papyrus.

Truth: Papyrus is made by laying strips of a plant stem crosswise and pressing them. Paper is made from fibres broken down in water and matted into a thin sheet. The word paper comes from papyrus, but the inventions are different.

Try this

Make recycled paper with an adult. Tear old newspaper into tiny pieces, soak them in water overnight, then mash them into a pulp. Spread a thin layer on a piece of mesh or an old cloth, press out the water with a sponge and let it dry for a day or two.
