What are tennis's Grand Slams?

Winning all four Grand Slam tournaments in a single year is so hard that only two men and three women have ever done it in singles.

The short answer

The Grand Slams are the four biggest tournaments in tennis: the Australian Open, the French Open, Wimbledon and the US Open. They are the oldest, offer the most prize money and ranking points, and are the titles players want most.

Each one is played on a different kind of court, in a different city, at a different time of year. A player who wins all four in the same calendar year has "won the Grand Slam", one of the hardest feats in sport. Winning all four over a whole career is called a career Grand Slam.

Surprising but true

The name "Grand Slam" was borrowed from the card game bridge, where it means winning every trick in a hand.

Four tournaments, four surfaces

The season starts in January with the Australian Open in Melbourne, played on hard courts in the middle of the Australian summer. Temperatures sometimes pass 40 °C, so there are rules for stopping play in extreme heat.

In late May comes the French Open, or Roland-Garros, in Paris. It is played on clay made of crushed brick, which slows the ball and makes it bounce high. Long rallies and sliding players are part of its style. Next is Wimbledon in London, in late June and July, on grass, where the ball skids and stays low. Players must wear almost all white. The year ends with the US Open in New York in late August, on hard courts, often under floodlights late at night.

Where the name came from

Wimbledon is the oldest. It was first held in 1877 at a croquet club that had just added lawn tennis. The US championships began in 1881, the French in 1891 (open to foreigners from 1925) and the Australian in 1905.

In bridge, a card game, a "grand slam" means winning every trick in a hand. In 1933 an American sportswriter borrowed the phrase for the Australian player Jack Crawford, who had won three of the four big titles that year and nearly won the fourth.

The hardest feat in tennis

Only two men have won all four singles titles in a calendar year. Don Budge of the USA did it in 1938, and Rod Laver of Australia did it twice, in 1962 and 1969. Three women have done it: Maureen Connolly in 1953, Margaret Court in 1970, and Steffi Graf in 1988. Graf also won Olympic gold that year, a feat called the Golden Slam.

Until 1968, the Grand Slams were only for amateurs, and the best professionals were shut out. Since then, in what is called the Open Era, records have kept climbing. Novak Djokovic has won 24 men's singles titles, the most ever (as of 2026). Margaret Court won 24 women's singles titles, though many came before 1968. Serena Williams won 23, the most of the Open Era.

India at the Slams

India's best Grand Slam results have come in doubles. In 1997, Mahesh Bhupathi and Rika Hiraki of Japan won the French Open mixed doubles, making Bhupathi the first Indian Grand Slam champion. He and Leander Paes went on to win many doubles titles, together and with other partners.

Sania Mirza won six Grand Slam doubles titles, three in women's doubles and three in mixed. In 2024, Rohan Bopanna won the Australian Open men's doubles at the age of 43, the oldest man to win a Grand Slam title in the Open Era. In singles, Ramanathan Krishnan reached the Wimbledon semi-finals in 1960 and 1961.

Changing with the times

The Slams have changed a lot. The US Open became the first Slam to use electronic line-calling replays, called Hawk-Eye, in 2006. In 2025, Wimbledon replaced its line judges with electronic line-calling for the first time in its history. Some fans missed the human voices shouting "Out!" The grass, the white clothes and the strawberries and cream, though, are still there.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A "Grand Slam" means winning any one of the four big tournaments.

Truth: Each of the four is called a Grand Slam tournament. But strictly, "winning the Grand Slam" means winning all four in the same calendar year. Winning all four at different times in a career is called a career Grand Slam.

Try this

Bounce a tennis ball, or any small rubber ball, on three surfaces at home or school, such as tiles, a rug and grass or soil. Drop it from the same height each time and see how high it comes back. Then think about how each surface would change the way a player has to hit.
