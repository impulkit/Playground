Who were the Greek gods?

The ancient Greeks believed their gods lived on a real mountain, argued like a big family and sometimes turned people into spiders.

The short answer

The ancient Greeks worshipped many gods. The most important were the twelve Olympians, who were said to live on Mount Olympus, the highest mountain in Greece. Zeus, god of the sky and thunder, was their king. His brother Poseidon ruled the sea, and another brother, Hades, ruled the land of the dead.

The Greeks told stories, called myths, about these gods and about heroes like Heracles and Odysseus. The myths explained storms, seasons and stars, and taught what people thought was brave or foolish. For more than a thousand years, the gods were part of everyday religion in Greek temples, homes and festivals.

Surprising but true

Pandora never had a box. In the oldest Greek telling she opened a large jar, and the "box" crept in through a translation about 2,000 years later.

A quarrelsome family

The Greek gods were powerful but far from perfect. They fell in love, held grudges, played favourites and fought among themselves. Zeus's wife Hera was queen of the gods and protector of marriage, and she was often furious with her husband.

Athena, goddess of wisdom and craft, was said to have sprung fully grown from Zeus's head. The city of Athens took her name, and its great temple, the Parthenon, was built for her. Apollo was god of music, light and prophecy. His twin Artemis ruled hunting and the Moon. Hermes, with winged sandals, carried messages. Hephaestus worked a forge, and Aphrodite was goddess of love.

In one story, a weaver called Arachne boasted that she was better than Athena. She lost the contest that followed, and Athena turned her into a spider. That is why spiders, scorpions and their relatives belong to a group called arachnids. Scientists still use names from Greek myths for new species and space missions.

Where the stories come from

Myths were told aloud long before they were written. The earliest written versions we have come from two poets of about 700 BCE. Homer is credited with the Iliad, about the war at Troy, and the Odyssey, about one hero's ten-year voyage home. Hesiod wrote the Theogony, which tells how the gods were born.

In Hesiod's version, the world began with Chaos, a yawning emptiness. Then came the earth, Gaia, and a race of giants called Titans. Zeus led his brothers and sisters in a war against the Titans and won. Different Greek cities told the stories in different ways, so there was never one official version.

How old are the Olympic Games?They began in ancient Greece about 2,800 years ago, and came back in 1896.

Gods all around us

The Olympic Games began at Olympia, a sanctuary of Zeus, and the traditional start date is 776 BCE. When the Romans conquered Greece, they matched the Greek gods with their own. Zeus became Jupiter, Aphrodite became Venus and Ares became Mars. Those Roman names now belong to the planets.

You can find the myths in the night sky, where Orion, Perseus and Andromeda have their own star patterns. They live on in words like echo, atlas and museum, which once meant a home of the Muses. A sportswear brand is named after Nike, the winged goddess of victory.

Still being retold

Few people worship the Olympians today, but the stories keep finding new readers. Rick Riordan's Percy Jackson books, first published in 2005, follow the gods' children at an American summer camp. Indian readers often compare Homer's epics with the Ramayana and the Mahabharata, which also have great wars, long journeys and gods who take sides.

The ruins of ancient Greece still stand in Athens, Delphi and Olympia. Visitors walk where pilgrims once came to ask Apollo's oracle about the future. His answers were famous for being so puzzling that people could read them more than one way.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Pandora opened a box and let all the troubles out into the world.

Truth: The poet Hesiod describes a pithos, a big storage jar. In the 1500s the scholar Erasmus mistranslated it as a box, and the box stuck.
