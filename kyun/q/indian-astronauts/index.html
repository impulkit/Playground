Who are India's astronauts?

In 1984 Rakesh Sharma looked down from space and told Prime Minister Indira Gandhi that India looked "saare jahan se achha", better than the whole world.

The short answer

Only a few people of Indian birth or citizenship have been to space. The first was Rakesh Sharma, an Indian Air Force pilot who flew on a Soviet spacecraft in 1984. The second Indian citizen was Shubhanshu Shukla, also an air force pilot, who spent about 18 days on the International Space Station in 2025.

Two women born in India or to Indian families flew with NASA. Kalpana Chawla grew up in Karnal, Haryana, and became an American astronaut. Sunita Williams, whose father came from Gujarat, spent 608 days in space. A few Indian-born travellers have also made short trips to the edge of space on private rockets.

India is now preparing to launch its own astronauts on its own rocket. Four air force pilots have been chosen and are training for the Gaganyaan mission.

Surprising but true

Forty-one years passed between India's first and second astronauts. Rakesh Sharma flew in 1984, and Shubhanshu Shukla went to the International Space Station in 2025.

Rakesh Sharma, 1984

In the 1980s the Soviet Union invited friendly countries to send a guest astronaut to its space station. India picked two air force pilots, Rakesh Sharma and Ravish Malhotra, to train in Russia. Sharma flew, and Malhotra was his backup.

On 3 April 1984, Sharma launched with two Soviet cosmonauts from Baikonur. He spent nearly eight days aboard the space station Salyut 7. He did experiments on how the body copes with weightlessness, and took photographs of India from orbit to help map its land.

During a live TV call, Prime Minister Indira Gandhi asked him how India looked from space. He replied, in Hindi, "saare jahan se achha", a line from a famous patriotic song. It means "better than the whole world". Many Indians still remember that moment.

Kalpana Chawla and Sunita Williams

Kalpana Chawla studied aeronautical engineering in Chandigarh and then in the USA, where she became a citizen. She flew on the space shuttle Columbia in 1997, the first woman born in India to go to space.

In 2003 she flew again. On the way home, Columbia broke apart as it came back through the atmosphere, and all seven crew members died. Schools, a hostel and a satellite in India have been named after her.

Sunita Williams was born in Ohio, USA, to an Indian father and a Slovenian-American mother. She made three long stays on the space station. Her last mission, in 2024 and 2025, was meant to last about a week, but problems with her spacecraft stretched it to nine months. She retired from NASA in December 2025 after 608 days in space.

Shubhanshu Shukla, 2025

In June 2025 Group Captain Shubhanshu Shukla, from Lucknow, flew to the International Space Station on a private mission called Axiom-4, launched on a SpaceX rocket from Florida. He was the mission's pilot.

He became the first Indian to visit the station and the second Indian citizen in space. Over about 18 days he ran experiments chosen by Indian scientists, including growing seeds such as methi and moong in weightlessness to see how they sprouted. He returned in July 2025, splashing down off the coast of California.

Gaganyaan's crew

In February 2024 ISRO named four astronaut-designates for India's first crewed spaceflight: Prasanth Nair, Ajit Krishnan, Angad Pratap and Shubhanshu Shukla. All four are test pilots in the Indian Air Force.

Gaganyaan aims to launch two or three astronauts on an Indian rocket from Sriharikota, keep them in orbit for up to three days and bring them safely back to the sea. First come uncrewed test flights. One of them will carry Vyommitra, a humanoid robot that sits in a crew seat and reports what a human would feel.

When the first crewed flight happens, India will become only the fourth country to send people to space on its own rocket, after Russia, the USA and China.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Kalpana Chawla was the first Indian in space.

Truth: Rakesh Sharma was first, in 1984. Kalpana Chawla, who flew in 1997, was the first woman born in India to go to space, and she flew as an American astronaut with NASA.
