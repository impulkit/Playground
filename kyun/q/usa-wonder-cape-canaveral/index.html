What happens at Cape Canaveral?

The launch pad that sent the first people to the Moon in 1969 now shares its swampy coast with alligators, manatees and rockets that fly home and land.

The short answer

Cape Canaveral is a strip of sandy coast on the eastern side of Florida, in the USA. It is one of the busiest places in the world for launching rockets. People were sent to the Moon from here, along with Space Shuttles, Mars rovers and thousands of satellites.

Two neighbouring sites share the area. NASA's Kennedy Space Center sits on Merritt Island, and Cape Canaveral Space Force Station sits on the cape itself. Both government and private rockets launch from here, so a single week can see several launches. Around the launch pads lies a wildlife refuge, so rockets roar into the sky above alligators, sea turtles and wading birds.

Surprising but true

Each star in the American flag painted on NASA's giant rocket-building hall is about 1.8 m across, as tall as a grown man.

Why Florida?

Earth spins eastwards, and places near the equator move fastest. At Cape Canaveral, the ground is already moving east at about 1,470 km/h. A rocket launched eastwards keeps that speed, which saves fuel.

Florida also faces the Atlantic Ocean. Rockets fly east over open water, so any falling stages or failures land in the sea, far from towns. India uses the same idea at Sriharikota, which launches eastwards over the Bay of Bengal, as the story of ISRO launches explains. Engineers choose launch sites all over the world with these two rules in mind.

From captured rockets to the Moon

The first launch from the Cape was on 24 July 1950. It was a rocket called Bumper 8, built partly from a German V-2 captured after the Second World War. Soon the Cape was busy with missile tests and then the space race.

On 16 July 1969, a Saturn V rocket lifted off from Launch Complex 39A carrying Neil Armstrong, Buzz Aldrin and Michael Collins. Four days later, two of them walked on the Moon. The Saturn V was 110 m tall, about as tall as a 36-storey building. You can follow that flight in Apollo and the Moon.

The giant hall

Rockets for those missions were stacked upright inside the Vehicle Assembly Building, about 160 m tall. It is so big inside that, on humid days, wisps of cloud can form near the ceiling. Its doors are among the tallest in the world.

A huge American flag is painted on its side. Each star is about 1.8 m across, and each stripe is nearly as wide as a road lane. Rockets were carried out to the pad on giant tracked crawlers moving at about walking pace, or slower.

How do rockets land themselves?A rocket booster taller than a 12-storey building can fall back from the edge of space and land upright on a ship's deck about the size of a football field.

Rockets that come back

For most of history, rockets were thrown away after one flight. Now the boosters of SpaceX's Falcon 9 often fly back and land upright, on pads near the coast or on ships at sea. Watching one return, with a double sonic boom, is a sight unique to the past decade. Reusable rockets have made launches cheaper and much more frequent.

In 2025, Florida's Space Coast saw more than 100 launches, a record. The year before it had 93. Many of these carried batches of Starlink internet satellites.

Back to the Moon

On 1 April 2026, NASA's giant SLS rocket launched from Launch Complex 39B carrying the Artemis II crew. The four astronauts, three American and one Canadian, flew around the Moon and splashed down in the Pacific about nine days later. They were the first people to travel to the Moon since 1972.

Space and swamp

Most of the land around Kennedy Space Center is a protected wildlife refuge. It is home to manatees, bobcats, sea turtles and hundreds of bird species. Bald eagles nest near launch pads. Alligators sometimes wander onto roads and runways, and staff have to wait for them to move. To learn how to tell them from their cousins, see crocodiles and alligators.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Cape Canaveral and Kennedy Space Center are the same place.

Truth: They are neighbours. Kennedy Space Center, run by NASA, is on Merritt Island. Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, run by the US Space Force, is on the cape next door. Together people call the whole area the Space Coast.
