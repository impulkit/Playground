How tall can a human grow?

The tallest person ever measured, Robert Wadlow, stood 2.72 metres tall, so he had to duck by more than half a metre to walk through an ordinary door.

The short answer

Healthy adults rarely grow taller than about 2.1 metres. People taller than that almost always have a condition where the body makes too much growth hormone. Usually the cause is a small, harmless lump on the pituitary gland, a pea-sized gland at the base of the brain.

The tallest person ever reliably measured was Robert Wadlow, from Alton in Illinois, USA. In 1940 he stood 2.72 metres, taller than a standard doorway with an extra half-metre on top. Scientists think the human body can't safely go much beyond this. The heart, bones and joints struggle to cope with the extra size and weight.

Surprising but true

You are about 1 cm taller when you wake up than when you go to bed, because the soft discs in your spine get squashed during the day.

The boy who kept growing

Robert Wadlow was born in 1918 at a normal size. By the age of 8, he was taller than his father. By 13, he was over 2.2 metres. His body made too much growth hormone, and in those days doctors had no way to stop it.

He wore size 37 American shoes, about 47 cm long, nearly twice the length of an average man's foot. He needed leg braces to walk. A blister from a brace became infected, and he died in 1940, aged 22, still growing. More than 40,000 people attended his funeral in Alton.

Why bodies have a limit

Size causes problems because of a simple bit of maths. If you double a person's height, their body gets roughly eight times heavier, but their bones only get about four times stronger. Bones are strong in proportion to their cross-section, not their length.

The heart has extra work too. It must pump blood higher up to the brain against gravity. The giraffe solves this with a huge, thick-walled heart and very high blood pressure. Humans aren't built that way. Very tall people often have heart trouble, joint pain and difficulty walking.

How you grow taller

Children grow at the ends of their long bones, in soft areas called growth plates. These are made of cartilage, which slowly turns into bone. Growth hormone and other signals keep the plates busy through childhood and the big spurt of the teenage years.

By the late teens, the growth plates close and turn to solid bone. After that, you can't get taller, however much you eat or stretch. Doctors can see on an X-ray whether a child's bones are still growing.

Nations getting taller

Around the world, people have grown taller over the last century, mostly because of better food and fewer childhood illnesses. Dutch men now average about 1.83 metres, the tallest in the world, up by about 20 cm since the 1850s. South Korean women grew about 20 cm in a century, one of the biggest jumps measured.

Indian adults have also grown taller, though more slowly, and in some recent decades average height barely changed. Scientists see height as a mirror of how well a country feeds its children. Short height across a whole population usually points to poor food or illness in childhood, not to genes.

The tallest people today

As of 2026, the tallest living man is Sultan Kösen of Türkiye, at 2.51 metres. Surgery in 2010 stopped his growth. In the NBA, players over 2.2 metres are rare, and the tallest players in history have been around 2.31 metres.

Studies of twins suggest that about 80% of the differences in height between people come from their genes. That explains why tall parents often have tall children. But even with the tallest parents and the best food, the human body runs into its limits long before it reaches the height of a giraffe, which stands about 5 metres tall.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Hanging from a bar or stretching every day can make you grow much taller.

Truth: Stretching can improve posture, which may make you stand a little taller, but it can't lengthen your bones. Height depends mostly on your genes, with food, sleep and health in childhood playing a part.

Try this

Measure yourself twice in one day. Stand against a wall without shoes first thing in the morning and have someone mark your height with a pencil and a book held flat on your head. Do the same just before bed. Compare the two marks with a ruler.
