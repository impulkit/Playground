How do fireflies make light?

Light without heat. Engineers are still jealous.

The short answer

Fireflies make light by bioluminescence, like deep-sea fish.

A firefly's light is "cold": nearly all the energy becomes light, not heat.

Inside the last few segments of a firefly's belly is a special light organ. There, a chemical called luciferin combines with oxygen, helped by an enzyme called luciferase. The reaction gives off light. By controlling how much air reaches the light organ, the firefly can switch its glow on and off in a fraction of a second.

Before the monsoon, fireflies light up villages like Purushwadi in Maharashtra.

Surprising but true

A firefly's light wastes almost no energy as heat. An old light bulb wasted about 90%.

Beetles with lanterns

Fireflies are not flies at all. They are beetles, with hard wing covers like a ladybird's. There are about 2,000 known species, living on every continent except Antarctica. Most fly in warm, damp places near forests, fields, streams and mangroves.

Not every firefly flashes as an adult. Some glow steadily, and some don't light up at all. But almost all firefly larvae, the young ones that live in soil and leaf litter, glow. Scientists think the glow first began as a warning. Many fireflies taste bitter to birds and lizards, and the light says "don't eat me".

A code of flashes

For flashing fireflies, light is a language. A male flies through the dusk flashing his species' pattern. A female watching from the grass answers with her own flash after a precise delay. If the timing is right, he flies down to her.

Some fireflies cheat. Females of a group called Photuris, found in the Americas, copy the reply of another species. When a hopeful male lands, she eats him. That's why scientists call them "femme fatale" fireflies.

Flashing in time

In a few places, thousands of fireflies flash together, all at once, like strings of festival lights. Every June in Great Smoky Mountains National Park, in Tennessee, USA, one species does this in the forest at night. The show is so popular that the park runs a lottery for visitor passes.

In India, the village of Purushwadi in the Sahyadri hills of Maharashtra holds a firefly festival each May and June. Visitors come to see trees blinking with thousands of fireflies on warm nights before the rains. Firefly watchers are encouraged to keep torches off and voices low, so the insects are not disturbed. Soon after, the monsoon arrives, along with the frogs.

Cold light

An old-style light bulb wasted about 90% of its energy as heat, which is why it got too hot to touch. A firefly's light organ gives off very little heat, because the chemical reaction turns energy straight into light. LED lights do something similar, which is why they stay cool.

Scientists also borrowed the firefly's tools. The gene for luciferase is used in labs around the world. When it is added to cells, they glow, letting researchers watch diseases and new medicines at work.

Fading lights

Many people say they see fewer fireflies than when they were young. Scientists who study them think the main reasons are the loss of wild, damp places, pesticides, and light from streetlamps and buildings. Bright lights at night can drown out firefly signals, so males and females fail to find each other. Keeping some gardens and fields dark at night, with long grass and leaf litter, gives fireflies a better chance.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Fireflies are flies.

Truth: Fireflies are beetles. Like all beetles, they have a pair of hard wing covers that open up to reveal the thin wings they fly with. In some species, the females have no wings at all and glow from the ground.

Try this

Talk in firefly code. On a dark evening, with an adult, give each friend a small torch. Each person makes up a secret flash pattern, such as two short flashes and one long one. One person flashes a pattern from across the room or garden, and whoever has that pattern must flash back the reply. Notice how hard it is to pick out one signal when everyone flashes at once.
