Can a car run on fuel made from sugarcane?

The same sugarcane that gives you a glass of fresh juice can be turned into a fuel that drives a car.

The short answer

Yes. Sugarcane can be turned into ethanol, a kind of alcohol that burns well in car engines. Most petrol in India already contains some ethanol.

Special cars called flex-fuel vehicles can run on petrol, on pure ethanol, or on any mix of the two. A sensor checks what is in the tank, and the engine adjusts itself.

Surprising but true

Henry Ford's famous Model T car, sold from 1908, could run on petrol or on alcohol fuel. Ford called ethanol "the fuel of the future".

From cane to tank

First, sugarcane is crushed to squeeze out its sweet juice. The juice, or the sticky molasses left over after making sugar, is mixed with yeast. The yeast eats the sugar and gives off ethanol and carbon dioxide. This is called fermentation, the same process that makes bread rise.

The mixture is then heated, and the ethanol is separated by distillation, because it boils at a lower temperature than water. Ethanol can also be made from maize, broken rice and other grains.

How engines use it

A car engine burns fuel mixed with air to push pistons. Ethanol burns cleanly and gives off less smoke than petrol, but it holds less energy in each litre, so cars need a little more of it. It also attracts water and can wear out ordinary rubber parts.

That is why blends are labelled. E10 has 10% ethanol, E20 has 20%, and E100 is almost pure ethanol. Ordinary cars are built for low blends. Flex-fuel cars have tougher pipes and seals and smarter computers so they can handle anything up to E100.

How does a car engine work?A petrol engine makes thousands of tiny controlled bursts of burning every minute, and each one shoves a piston down.

Why countries use it

Petrol comes from crude oil, which India mostly buys from other countries. Ethanol can be made at home from crops, so it saves money and supports farmers. Plants also soak up carbon dioxide as they grow, which can make ethanol cleaner overall.

Brazil started a big ethanol programme in 1975, and most new cars there are flex-fuel. India moved to E20 petrol in 2025, and in 2026 allowed certified vehicles to use E85 and E100. Critics point out that sugarcane needs a lot of water, and that farmland used for fuel cannot grow food. Like electric cars and hydrogen, ethanol is one of several ways people are trying to move beyond oil.

Myth or fact?

Myth: You can pour pure ethanol into any petrol car.

Truth: Ordinary engines are built for petrol or low ethanol blends like E20. High ethanol can damage their seals and pipes and confuse their fuel systems. Only flex-fuel vehicles, made with special parts and sensors, can safely use E85 or E100.

Try this

Mix a teaspoon of sugar and a pinch of dried yeast into a cup of warm water in a bottle, and stretch a balloon over the top. Leave it somewhere warm for an hour. The balloon swells with gas. The yeast is fermenting the sugar, the same first step used to make ethanol.
