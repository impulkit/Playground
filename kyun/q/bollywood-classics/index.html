What are some classic Hindi films everyone knows?

One Hindi film from 1995 has been playing every morning at the same Mumbai cinema for more than 30 years.

The short answer

Some Hindi films are so well known that their lines, songs and characters turn up in everyday talk, even among people who have never watched them. They include Mother India (1957), Mughal-e-Azam (1960), Sholay (1975), Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge (1995) and Lagaan (2001). Many families add their own favourites, from Guide and Anand to Mr India and 3 Idiots.

These films are classics for different reasons. Some broke records. Some won awards abroad. Others had songs that every wedding band still plays. Together, they tell the story of how Hindi cinema, often called Bollywood, grew up.

Surprising but true

The village of Ramgarh in Sholay was not in the north at all. It was a set built among the rocky hills of Ramanagara, near Bengaluru.

The early giants

India's first feature film, Raja Harishchandra, was made by Dadasaheb Phalke in 1913. It was silent. The first Indian talkie, Alam Ara, came in 1931, and from then on songs became part of almost every Hindi film.

In the 1950s, a golden age began. Raj Kapoor's Awaara (1951) was a huge hit in India and also in the Soviet Union, where people sang its songs in Russian. Guru Dutt's Pyaasa (1957) told the story of a struggling poet. Mother India (1957), directed by Mehboob Khan, showed a village woman raising her sons through hardship. It became the first Indian film nominated for an Oscar for Best Foreign Language Film.

Mughal-e-Azam

K. Asif spent more than ten years making Mughal-e-Azam, a love story set at Akbar's court. It came out in 1960. For one song, a Sheesh Mahal, or palace of mirrors, was built on a studio floor, and filming its reflections was a huge technical challenge.

The film was mostly black and white, with a few scenes in colour. In 2004, it was fully coloured using computers and released in cinemas again. A new generation went to see it.

Who was Dadasaheb Phalke?India's first full-length feature film was made in 1913 by a printer and part-time magician, and a man had to play the queen.

Sholay

Sholay (1975), directed by Ramesh Sippy, is an action film about two small-time crooks hired to catch a bandit, Gabbar Singh. It starred Dharmendra, Amitabh Bachchan, Hema Malini, Jaya Bhaduri and Sanjeev Kumar.

It started slowly at the box office, then grew by word of mouth. It ran for five years at Mumbai's Minerva cinema. Lines such as Gabbar's "Kitne aadmi the?" (How many men were there?) are still quoted everywhere. Ramgarh, the film's village, was built among the granite hills of Ramanagara in Karnataka.

Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge

Better known as DDLJ, this 1995 romance starring Shah Rukh Khan and Kajol follows two young Indians who meet on a trip across Europe. It mixed foreign locations with family values, and audiences in India and abroad loved it.

DDLJ opened at Maratha Mandir, a cinema in Mumbai, in October 1995. It has played there ever since, usually in a morning show. In October 2025 it completed 30 years, the longest run of any film in an Indian cinema.

Lagaan and beyond

In Lagaan (2001), villagers in British India play a cricket match against their rulers to escape a heavy tax. The film was nominated for an Oscar. It is nearly four hours long, but many children remember it as the first long film they watched to the end.

Later classics include Taare Zameen Par (2007), about a boy with dyslexia, and 3 Idiots (2009), a comedy about the pressure on engineering students.

Songs that outlast films

Many classics are remembered for their music as much as their stories. Singers such as Lata Mangeshkar, Mohammed Rafi and Kishore Kumar recorded songs that people still sing at parties and weddings. A film can fade, but a good song from it can live for generations.

India makes more feature films each year than any other country, in many languages. Only a few become classics. Those are the ones that grandparents, parents and children can all watch together and still argue about.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Bollywood is the name for all Indian films.

Truth: Bollywood means the Hindi film industry based in Mumbai. India also makes huge numbers of films in Telugu, Tamil, Malayalam, Kannada, Bengali, Marathi and other languages, and several of these industries are as big as Bollywood or bigger.

Try this

Hold a family song quiz. Each person picks one song from an old Hindi film and plays or hums the first few lines. Everyone else has to guess the film, the hero or the decade. Grandparents usually win, which is half the fun.
