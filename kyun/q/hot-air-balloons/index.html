How does a hot-air balloon float?

The first passengers in a hot-air balloon were a sheep, a duck and a rooster.

The short answer

A hot-air balloon floats because hot air is lighter than cold air. When air is heated, its tiny particles move faster and spread out. So a balloon full of hot air holds fewer particles than the same space filled with cooler air. It weighs less.

The cooler air around the balloon pushes it upwards, the same way water pushes up on a beach ball. This upward push is called buoyancy. If the push is bigger than the weight of the balloon, basket and passengers, the balloon rises. A burner roars above the passengers' heads to keep the air hot.

Surprising but true

A hot-air balloon has no steering wheel. Pilots steer by rising or sinking to find winds blowing the way they want to go.

The numbers

Air is heavier than it seems. A cubic metre of air at about 20 °C weighs about 1.2 kg. Heat it to around 100 °C, and the same space holds only about 0.95 kg. Each cubic metre of hot air can therefore lift roughly 0.25 kg.

That sounds tiny. So balloons have to be huge. To lift a basket, burner, fuel and three or four people, maybe 500 kg in all, you need roughly 2,000 cubic metres of hot air. That's a balloon about as tall as a six-storey building, holding almost as much air as an Olympic swimming pool holds water.

A sheep, a duck and a rooster

In 1782, two French brothers who ran a paper factory, Joseph-Michel and Jacques-Étienne Montgolfier, noticed that hot smoke made paper bags rise. They built bigger and bigger bags of cloth and paper.

On 19 September 1783, at the palace of Versailles, they sent up a balloon carrying a sheep, a duck and a rooster, watched by King Louis XVI. The animals landed safely after about eight minutes. Two months later, on 21 November, two men floated across Paris for about 25 minutes. It was the first time people had flown freely.

Up, down and sideways

The pilot controls height with the burner. A blast of flame heats the air and the balloon rises. Let the air cool, or open a vent at the top, and it sinks.

There is no way to steer sideways. The balloon goes wherever the wind blows. But winds often blow in different directions at different heights. A skilled pilot climbs or sinks to find a layer heading roughly the right way. A chase car follows on the ground to collect everyone at the end.

Balloons usually fly just after sunrise or before sunset, when winds are calm and the air is steady.

Around the world

In 1999, the Swiss balloonist Bertrand Piccard and the British pilot Brian Jones flew all the way around the world without stopping, in about 20 days. Their balloon used both helium and hot air. They rode fast winds high up called jet streams for much of the way.

Scientists use balloons too. Every day, weather services in India, the USA and around the world launch hundreds of small helium balloons carrying instruments. They rise through the clouds and radio back temperature, wind and moisture for weather forecasts.

A sky full of balloons

Every October, the Albuquerque International Balloon Fiesta in New Mexico, USA, fills the sky with hundreds of balloons, some shaped like animals and cartoon characters. It is the biggest balloon festival in the world. The same idea also lifts the paper sky lanterns released at some festivals, although these can start fires and harm animals, so many places now discourage them. The physics is the same as in a huge steel ship that floats on water: if something weighs less than the stuff it pushes aside, up it goes.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Hot-air balloon pilots can steer wherever they want.

Truth: Balloons drift with the wind. Pilots can only go up or down. They choose a direction by finding winds at different heights, and a chase team on the ground follows them to wherever they land.

Try this

Stretch a deflated balloon over the mouth of an empty plastic bottle. Stand the bottle in a bowl of warm tap water and watch the balloon slowly lift and swell as the air inside warms and spreads out. Move it to a bowl of cold water and watch it shrink.
