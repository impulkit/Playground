What was the Swadeshi movement?

In 1905, people in Bengal lit bonfires of British cloth in the streets and began buying Indian-made goods instead, to protest against their province being cut in two.

The short answer

The Swadeshi movement was a protest that began in 1905, when the British government divided the province of Bengal into two. Swadeshi means "of one's own country". Its idea was to hit Britain where it hurt, in trade, by refusing British goods and buying Indian ones.

People burned foreign cloth, salt and sugar in public. Shops refused to sell British goods. Students left government schools. Indians started their own factories, banks, schools and shipping companies. Songs, poems and paintings spread the message. It was one of the first times the freedom struggle went beyond meetings and petitions to change what ordinary people bought and wore every day.

Surprising but true

In 1906 a lawyer in Tuticorin, V.O. Chidambaram Pillai, started an Indian shipping company to compete with British steamships, and he bought two steamships for it.

Why Bengal was angry

In 1905, Lord Curzon, the British viceroy, announced the partition of Bengal. The province was huge, and the official reason was that it was too big to run well. But many Bengalis believed the real aim was to weaken them by splitting the region along religious lines.

On 7 August 1905, a large meeting at the Calcutta Town Hall agreed to boycott British goods. Soon students were picketing shops that sold foreign goods, standing outside and urging customers to turn away. In some towns, people who kept buying British goods found that neighbours stopped inviting them to weddings and feasts. When the partition came into force on 16 October, many people fasted and marched. Rabindranath Tagore asked Hindus and Muslims to tie rakhis on each other's wrists as a sign that Bengal was still one.

Who was Rabindranath Tagore?He wrote the national anthems of two countries, won Asia's first Nobel Prize, and only started painting seriously in his sixties.

Making it in India

Boycotting was only half the plan. The other half was building. The chemist Prafulla Chandra Ray's company, Bengal Chemicals, grew quickly as people chose Indian medicines and soaps. Indian textile mills and handloom weavers found new customers.

In 1906, V.O. Chidambaram Pillai, a lawyer in Tuticorin in Tamil Nadu, started the Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company to run ships to Sri Lanka against a British firm. In 1907, Tata Iron and Steel raised its money from Indian investors in just a few weeks, in the Swadeshi spirit. A National Council of Education set up schools and colleges taught by Indians.

Songs and pictures

The movement had a soundtrack. Tagore wrote songs, including "Amar Sonar Bangla", which later became Bangladesh's national anthem. The painter Abanindranath Tagore painted Bharat Mata, showing India as a calm woman in saffron robes holding a book, rice, cloth and a rosary.

The movement spread well beyond Bengal. In Maharashtra, Bal Gangadhar Tilak led bonfires of foreign cloth. In Punjab, Lala Lajpat Rai took up the call. In the south, crowds gathered to hear speakers in Tamil and Telugu.

Three leaders became so linked with the movement that people called them Lal-Bal-Pal: Lala Lajpat Rai from Punjab, Bal Gangadhar Tilak from Maharashtra and Bipin Chandra Pal from Bengal. They wanted bolder action than older Congress leaders, who preferred polite petitions. Since 2015, India has marked 7 August as National Handloom Day, remembering the day the boycott was declared.

How it ended

By 1908 the movement had slowed. The British arrested leaders, including Tilak, who was sent to prison in Burma. Chidambaram Pillai was jailed too, and his company collapsed. The Congress split between moderates and those who wanted bolder action. Some young people, frustrated, turned to secret groups using violence.

Swadeshi also had weaknesses. Indian goods often cost more, which hurt poor families. Many Muslim peasants in eastern Bengal, who had gained from the new province, stayed away. Still, in 1911 the British reversed the partition. The idea of Swadeshi stayed alive, and Gandhi later made homespun khadi its symbol across India.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Gandhi started the Swadeshi movement.

Truth: Swadeshi began in Bengal in 1905, when Gandhi was still living in South Africa. He admired it and later made homespun khadi the heart of his own campaigns.
