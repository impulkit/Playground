Which grains feed the world?

Three kinds of grass seed, rice, wheat and maize, give humanity about half of all the food energy it eats.

The short answer

Most of the world eats grains every day. A grain is the dry seed of a grass plant, packed with starch. Three grains matter most: maize (corn), wheat and rice. According to the UN's Food and Agriculture Organization, these three provide around half of the food energy people eat around the world.

Other grains matter a lot too. Barley, sorghum, oats and millets feed hundreds of millions of people, especially in dry places. In India, a plate might hold rice in Kolkata, wheat rotis in Lucknow and ragi balls in rural Karnataka. Across the world, grain is cheap, keeps for months and fills you up. That is why so many great civilisations were built on it.

Surprising but true

Maize is the most-grown grain on Earth, but most of it is never eaten by people. It feeds farm animals or is turned into fuel.

Rice, the grain of Asia

Rice was first farmed along the Yangtze River in China, about 9,000 years ago. It grows best in warm, wet fields, often flooded with water. Today about nine out of every ten bowls of rice are grown and eaten in Asia.

India became the world's largest rice grower in 2024–25, with about 150 million tonnes, just ahead of China. India is also the biggest rice exporter, sending basmati and other rice to the Middle East, Africa and beyond. In the United States, rice grows mostly in Arkansas, California and Louisiana.

Wheat, from the Fertile Crescent

Wheat was first farmed around 10,000 years ago in the Fertile Crescent, an arc of land across what is now Iraq, Syria and Turkey. It likes cooler, drier weather than rice. Ground into flour, it becomes bread, rotis, pasta, noodles and cakes.

China grows the most wheat, and India is second. In the 1960s, India was importing wheat to avoid famine. New high-yield seeds, fertilisers and irrigation during the Green Revolution, especially in Punjab and Haryana, changed that within a few years. Kansas and North Dakota are among the big wheat states in the US.

How did farming begin?The ancestor of corn was a skinny wild grass with a few rock-hard seeds. It took thousands of years of farmers' choices to turn it into a cob.

Maize, the giant

Maize began as a wild grass called teosinte in what is now Mexico, around 9,000 years ago. Its ears were tiny, about the size of your little finger. Over thousands of years, farmers picked seeds from the best plants, until it became the big cob we know.

Today maize is the most-grown grain on Earth, at well over a billion tonnes a year. The United States grows the most, mainly in the Corn Belt of Iowa, Illinois and nearby states. But most maize is fed to cows, pigs and chickens, or made into ethanol fuel, corn syrup and starch. Only a part of it ends up as tortillas, cornflakes or bhutta roasted on a street corner.

The comeback of millets

Before rice and wheat spread across India, many families ate millets such as jowar (sorghum), bajra (pearl millet) and ragi (finger millet). Millets grow with little water and handle heat well. India is the world's biggest millet grower.

The UN named 2023 the International Year of Millets, after a proposal from India. Millets are now showing up again in school meals, breakfast cereals and city cafes. As the climate warms, crops that cope with drought may become more important.

Why so few grains

There are thousands of edible plants, so it is odd that we lean so heavily on a handful. Grains are easy to store, carry and trade, and they produce a lot of food per field. But depending on a few crops is risky. One disease or drought can hurt harvests in many countries at once.

That is why scientists keep seeds of thousands of grain varieties in seed banks, including a vault dug into a frozen mountain in Svalbard, Norway. If a crop fails, an old variety stored there might hold the answer.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Brown rice and white rice come from different plants.

Truth: They come from the same plant. Brown rice still has its outer bran layer. White rice has been polished to remove the bran and germ, which makes it cook faster but takes away some fibre and vitamins.

Try this

Look through your kitchen and collect a spoonful of each grain you can find, such as rice, wheat flour, oats, cornflakes, ragi or bajra. Lay them out on a plate and label them. Then check a packet or two and see which countries or states they came from.
