Which deserts are the biggest?

The biggest desert on Earth is not hot or sandy at all. It is Antarctica, a continent of ice where almost no snow falls.

The short answer

A desert is a place that gets very little rain or snow, usually less than about 250 mm a year. That is less than many Indian cities get in a single monsoon month. By that measure, the biggest desert on Earth is Antarctica, at about 14 million square kilometres, followed by the Arctic.

The biggest hot desert is the Sahara, in North Africa, at about 9.2 million square kilometres. After that come the Arabian Desert, the Gobi in Mongolia and China, the Kalahari in southern Africa, and the great deserts of Australia. Deserts cover roughly a third of all the land on Earth. Many of them are rocky, not sandy, and some are bitterly cold.

Surprising but true

Some weather stations in Chile's Atacama Desert have recorded almost no rain for years on end, and NASA tests Mars rovers there because the ground is so dry.

Why some places get no rain

Deserts form for a few reasons. Many, like the Sahara and Arabian, lie along belts about 20 to 30 degrees north and south of the equator. There, air that rose over the hot, wet tropics sinks again, warming up and drying out as it falls. Sinking dry air means clear skies and very little rain.

Some deserts sit behind tall mountains, which block rain clouds. Others, like the Atacama in Chile, are next to cold ocean currents that cool the air and stop it from forming rain clouds. Antarctica's air is so cold it can hardly hold any moisture at all.

Cold deserts

Antarctica gets so little snow inland that it is drier than much of the Sahara. Parts of it, called the Dry Valleys, have had no snow or ice cover for a very long time, and the ground is bare rock and gravel.

The Gobi Desert in Asia is cold too. Summers can be hot, but winters fall below minus 30 °C. Explorers in the 1920s found some of the first known dinosaur eggs in the Gobi, a story told in dinosaur eggs. Ladakh, in India, is often called a cold desert, because it gets little rain and has freezing winters.

The driest place

The Atacama Desert, a narrow strip along the coast of Chile and Peru, is the driest hot desert on Earth. Some weather stations there have gone years without measurable rain. Its soil is so dry and lifeless in places that NASA has tested equipment for Mars rovers there, because it is one of the most Mars-like spots on our planet.

Yet the Atacama is not completely empty. Fog from the sea rolls in on some mornings. People have set up big nets to catch fog droplets for water. When rare rains do come, the desert can burst into flower, carpeting the sand in pink and purple.

The Thar, India's desert

India's largest desert, the Thar, covers much of western Rajasthan and stretches into Pakistan. It is home to the cities of Jaisalmer, Bikaner and Jodhpur, and is one of the most crowded deserts in the world, with many towns and villages.

People there have adapted carefully. They store rainwater in tanks, build homes with thick walls to stay cool, and raise camels, goats and hardy crops like millet. Folk music, bright turbans and mirrorwork cloth are part of desert life.

American deserts

The south-west United States has several deserts, including the Mojave and the Sonoran. The Sonoran Desert is home to the saguaro cactus, which can grow over 12 m tall and live for over 150 years.

Death Valley, in the Mojave Desert, holds the record for the hottest air temperature ever officially measured. It shows that deserts can be extreme in both directions: burning by day, and sometimes surprisingly cold at night.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Deserts are always hot and sandy.

Truth: A desert is any place that gets very little rain or snow. Many are rocky or gravelly, and some are freezing. The Gobi Desert in Mongolia and China can drop below minus 30 °C in winter.

Try this

Make a simple rain gauge. Put a straight-sided jar or glass outside where nothing drips into it, and tape a ruler to the side. After each rain, note how many millimetres collected. A desert gets less than about 250 mm in a whole year.
