Why is the Super Bowl so big?

For one Sunday each February, more than 100 million Americans watch the same game, and many tune in mainly for the adverts.

The short answer

The Super Bowl is the championship game of the National Football League (NFL), the top American football league. It is played on a Sunday in early February and is the most-watched television broadcast in the USA most years. In 2025, about 128 million Americans watched.

It is big for more reasons than the football. There is a huge halftime music show, famous adverts that cost millions of dollars, and parties with mountains of snacks. For many families, Super Bowl Sunday is almost a holiday.

Surprising but true

The name "Super Bowl" was inspired by a bouncy toy, the Super Ball, that belonged to the children of a team owner.

Two leagues, one game

In the 1960s there were two rival professional football leagues, the NFL and the newer American Football League (AFL). They agreed to merge, and from 1967 their champions met in a final game.

The first game, on 15 January 1967, was called the AFL-NFL World Championship Game. The Green Bay Packers beat the Kansas City Chiefs 35–10 at the Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum, and thousands of seats were empty. Lamar Hunt, who owned the Chiefs, had joked about calling it the "Super Bowl" after his children's Super Ball toy. The name caught on.

The game is now played in a different city each year, chosen years in advance. Warm places like Miami, New Orleans and Los Angeles, and cities with roofed stadiums, are favourites, because February can be freezing in much of the USA. Cities compete to host it, hoping for millions of dollars from visitors.

Why the Roman numerals?

Each Super Bowl is numbered with Roman numerals. Super Bowl I was in 1967. Super Bowl LIX, which means 59, was played in 2025. Because the season starts in one year and ends in the next, Roman numerals avoided confusion over which year's champion it was. They also make the game sound grand.

The winners receive the Vince Lombardi Trophy, a sterling silver football on a stand, named after the coach who won the first two Super Bowls. Each winning player also gets a large ring.

How do adverts try to persuade you?The first official TV advert in the USA, in 1941, showed a watch ticking over a map of the country for about ten seconds. It cost the company $9.

The adverts

Because so many people watch, companies pay huge sums for advertising time. In 2025, a 30-second slot cost around 8 million US dollars. Brands try to make funny, surprising or emotional adverts, and people discuss them at school and work the next day.

Some adverts became famous on their own. In 1984 Apple showed a cinema-style advert for its new Macintosh computer, and people still talk about it. Today many companies release their Super Bowl adverts online before the game.

The halftime show

At halftime, a major music star performs a short, spectacular show on a stage built in minutes on the field. Michael Jackson's show in 1993 helped make halftime a must-watch event. Since then, singers from Beyoncé to Rihanna have performed.

Performers are not paid their usual concert fees, but the exposure is enormous. For some viewers the halftime show is the main event. Others watch only for the game and the famous last-minute finishes.

Food, money and comparisons

Americans eat huge amounts of food on Super Bowl Sunday. The National Chicken Council estimates that well over a billion chicken wings are eaten around the game. Host cities, such as New Orleans in 2025, fill their hotels and restaurants.

India's biggest cricket matches attract even larger audiences. An India–Pakistan World Cup game or an IPL final can draw hundreds of millions of viewers on TV and streaming. The Super Bowl is unusual because almost all of its audience is in one country, watching together on one evening.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Super Bowl has always been the biggest event in American sport.

Truth: It began as a championship between two rival leagues and was not even called the Super Bowl officially at first. It took years of television growth for it to become the giant event it is now.

Watch a few TV or YouTube adverts with your family and score each one out of ten for being funny, surprising or memorable. Then ask everyone which product they remember an hour later. Super Bowl advertisers spend millions trying to win this exact game.
