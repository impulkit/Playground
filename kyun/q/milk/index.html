Where does milk come from?

India makes more milk than any other country on Earth, and more than 40% of it comes from buffaloes, not cows.

The short answer

Milk is food that mammals make in their bodies to feed their babies. A cow, buffalo or goat makes milk after giving birth, in special glands in her udder. The milk has water, fat, protein, vitamins, minerals and a sugar called lactose, everything a newborn needs to grow.

Farmers milk the animals once or twice a day, by hand or with machines. The milk is cooled, collected and sent to dairies. There it is usually heated to kill germs, packed, and trucked to shops before dawn. In India, much of it still arrives in a steel can on the back of a bicycle or motorbike.

Surprising but true

Most adults in the world make less of the stomach chemical needed to digest milk sugar than they did as babies.

The country with the most milk

India makes about a quarter of all the milk in the world, more than any other country. Unusually, a big share of India's milk, about 44% in 2024, comes from buffaloes. Buffalo milk is thicker and richer in fat than cow's milk, which is why it makes such creamy curd and paneer.

Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh produce the most. In the USA, California makes the most milk, and Wisconsin calls itself "America's Dairyland".

How India got its milk

In the 1950s and 1960s, India was short of milk. Then a young engineer named Verghese Kurien began working with a cooperative of farmers in Anand, Gujarat. Each farmer owned a few animals and brought milk to a village centre twice a day. The cooperative, later known by its brand name Amul, collected it, tested it and paid fairly.

In 1970, the government spread this model across the country in a programme called Operation Flood. It grew into the White Revolution, and by the late 1990s India had become the world's biggest milk producer. Kurien's birthday, 26 November, is now National Milk Day.

Making milk safe

Fresh milk can carry harmful bacteria. In the 1860s, the French scientist Louis Pasteur showed that gentle heating kills many germs, and the idea was later applied to milk. Pasteurised milk is typically heated to about 72°C for 15 seconds, then cooled fast.

Many Indian families boil milk at home before drinking it, which also kills germs. Watch a pot of milk heat up and you'll see a skin form on top. That is protein and fat cooking at the surface.

Can everybody drink milk?

Babies make plenty of lactase, a chemical in the gut that breaks down milk sugar. In most of the world, people make much less of it after childhood. If they drink a lot of milk, they may get tummy aches. This is called lactose intolerance.

But some groups whose ancestors kept cattle, in northern Europe, parts of Africa and north-western India, often keep making lactase as adults. It is one of the best examples of a change in human genes spreading because of food. Curd and cheese have less lactose, which is one reason people everywhere turned milk into them.

Milk in your body

Milk has plenty of calcium, a mineral your body uses to build bones and teeth. It also has protein for muscles. That is why it is part of many school meal programmes. People who don't drink milk can get calcium from foods such as ragi, sesame, leafy greens and some pulses.

Milk is also the starting point for a long list of foods: curd, butter, ghee, paneer, khoa and most Indian sweets. A few litres of milk on an Indian kitchen counter can become a whole meal by evening.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Chocolate milk comes from brown cows.

Truth: All cow's milk is white, whatever the colour of the cow. Chocolate milk is ordinary milk with cocoa and sugar added. In a 2017 US survey, about 7% of American adults said they believed the brown-cow idea.

Try this

Put a few drops of milk into a clear glass of water and shine a torch through it from the side in a dark room. The beam looks bluish from the side and orange-yellow when you look straight into it, for the same reason the sky is blue and sunsets are orange.
