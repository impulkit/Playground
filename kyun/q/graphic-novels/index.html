What is a graphic novel?

In 1992, a comic book about mice and cats won a special Pulitzer Prize. It told a true story of the Holocaust.

The short answer

A graphic novel is a book-length story told in the form of comics: a series of drawn panels with speech bubbles, captions and pictures working together. Unlike a monthly comic book, which might tell one short episode, a graphic novel usually tells a complete story, or a big part of one, between two covers.

Graphic novels can be about anything. Some are superhero adventures or fantasy. Others are memoirs, history, science or school stories. Some are for young children, and some are written for adults. What they share is the way they tell the story, through pictures and words together, which is the art of comics.

Surprising but true

The empty white strip between comic panels has a name, the gutter, and it is where your brain does the work of imagining what happened between one picture and the next.

Panels and gutters

A comic page is divided into panels, the boxes that each hold one moment. Between them are gutters, the gaps. In his book Understanding Comics (1993), the cartoonist Scott McCloud pointed out that the gutter is where the reader's imagination fills in what happened between two pictures.

If one panel shows a raised bat and the next shows a ball flying over a fence, nobody draws the hit. Your brain supplies it. This is why reading comics is an active skill. A reader has to follow the order of panels, read expressions and body language, and connect words with images, all at once.

Where the name came from

Long comic stories existed well before anyone called them graphic novels. Hergé's Tintin adventures, first published from 1929, were collected as albums of about 60 pages. In Japan, manga stories ran to thousands of pages.

The term "graphic novel" became popular after 1978, when the American cartoonist Will Eisner put it on the cover of A Contract with God. The book collected stories about life in a poor New York neighbourhood. He wanted bookshops to treat it as a serious book, not a throwaway comic.

Maus and beyond

The book that changed many minds was Maus, by Art Spiegelman. It tells how his father, a Polish Jew, survived the Holocaust. In it, Jewish people are drawn as mice and Nazis as cats. The simple drawings make the true story easier to approach without hiding its horror. In 1992 it received a special Pulitzer Prize.

Graphic novels for young readers have boomed since. Raina Telgemeier's Smile (2010), about her teenage dental disasters, has sold millions of copies. Jerry Craft's New Kid is about a Black boy starting at a mostly white private school. In 2020 it became the first graphic novel to win the Newbery Medal, America's top prize for children's books.

Indian graphic novels

India has a long history of comics, from Amar Chitra Katha to Tinkle. Graphic novels for older readers grew in the 2000s. Sarnath Banerjee's Corridor (2004), set around a second-hand bookshop in Delhi, is often described as one of India's first.

One of the most striking is Bhimayana (2011), about the life of B. R. Ambedkar and the caste discrimination he faced as a child and adult. It was drawn by Durgabai Vyam and Subhash Vyam in the style of Gond art, from central India. Instead of square panels, the pages flow with patterns, and some speech bubbles take the shape of birds. It showed that a graphic novel can borrow its look from India's own art traditions as well as from Tintin or superheroes.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A "graphic" novel is one with violent or disturbing content.

Truth: In this name, "graphic" means visual. Many graphic novels are gentle, funny or written for young children. The name describes how the story is told, not what it is about.

Try this

Fold a sheet of paper into six boxes. Tell a short true story from your day, one moment per box, using as few words as possible. Leave a big jump between two boxes and ask a friend to tell you what happened in the gap. That gap is the gutter at work.
