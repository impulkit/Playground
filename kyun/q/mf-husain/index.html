Who was M.F. Husain?

He started out painting giant film posters in Bombay and became the most famous Indian painter of his time, often walking barefoot.

The short answer

Maqbool Fida Husain (1915–2011), usually called M.F. Husain, was one of the most famous painters of modern India. He painted bold, colourful pictures of horses, gods, villages, cities and people, using strong outlines and shapes that seem to burst off the canvas.

He began by painting cinema posters in Bombay and became a founder of a group that changed Indian art after independence. He was also known for walking barefoot, even into fancy hotels. Late in life his paintings of Hindu goddesses caused angry protests, and he spent his final years living abroad.

Surprising but true

Husain's first film, a short documentary called Through the Eyes of a Painter, won a Golden Bear award at the Berlin Film Festival in 1967.

From Pandharpur to the cinema hoardings

Husain was born in Pandharpur, a pilgrimage town in Maharashtra. His mother died when he was very young, and he grew up partly with his grandfather. As a boy he loved drawing and watching the crowds at the town's temple fairs.

In 1937 he moved to Bombay with almost no money. To survive, he painted huge film hoardings, the giant posters that hung outside cinemas, and designed toys and furniture. The work was hard and paid little, but it taught him to paint big and fast. That love of cinema never left him.

A new kind of Indian art

In 1947, the year India became free, Husain joined a small group of young painters in Bombay. They called themselves the Progressive Artists' Group. Members included F.N. Souza and S.H. Raza. They wanted an art for a new nation, one that did not copy old Indian styles or simply follow Europe.

Husain mixed ideas from European modern art, especially the broken shapes of Picasso, with Indian folk art, temple sculpture and miniature paintings. His people often have no detailed faces at all. Their bodies, colours and poses tell you what you need to know.

Horses, cities and saints

Horses were his favourite subject. He painted them rearing, galloping and twisting, sometimes with just a few powerful lines. He also painted the Mahabharata and Ramayana, the cities of India, and a long series about Mother Teresa, showing her in her blue-bordered sari but with no face.

He worked very quickly and made thousands of works in his lifetime. The Government of India gave him three of its highest awards: the Padma Shri, the Padma Bhushan and, in 1991, the Padma Vibhushan. He also made films, and his short film Through the Eyes of a Painter won a top prize in Berlin.

A painter at the centre of a storm

From the 1990s, some of Husain's paintings of Hindu goddesses, painted years earlier, became widely known. Many people found them offensive and disrespectful. There were protests, attacks on exhibitions and many court cases filed against him.

Husain's supporters, including many artists, said he had painted from love of Indian traditions, pointing to temple sculptures that also show gods without clothes. His critics said a famous artist should respect what millions hold sacred. Both sides felt strongly, and people still disagree today.

In 2006 Husain left India. In 2010 he accepted citizenship of Qatar.

His last years

Husain kept painting into his nineties, working on huge projects about Arab and Indian history. He died in London in 2011, aged 95.

He remains a puzzle for many Indians: a painter who loved India and its stories deeply, yet whose work caused real hurt. His paintings now sell for very high prices, and they hang in museums in India and around the world.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Husain was trained at a famous art school from childhood.

Truth: He had very little formal art training. He taught himself, partly by painting cinema hoardings and furniture to earn a living in Bombay.
