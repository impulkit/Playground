Why do different places have different times?

The Sun rises in Arunachal Pradesh nearly two hours before it rises in Gujarat, yet both read the same time on the clock.

The short answer

The Earth spins once every 24 hours, so the Sun can't be overhead everywhere at once. When it is noon in Mumbai, it is early morning in London and the middle of the night in New York.

To make clocks match the Sun's position roughly, the world is divided into time zones. Each full turn of the Earth is 360 degrees, and 360 divided by 24 hours makes 15 degrees an hour. So, in theory, every 15 degrees of longitude east, clocks go forward an hour. In practice, countries draw their zones along borders and choose what suits them, so the lines on a time-zone map zigzag a lot.

Surprising but true

Until the 1880s, almost every town in the United States set its clocks by its own local noon, so a train timetable could list dozens of different local times.

When every town had its own time

For most of history, people set their clocks by the Sun. When it was highest in the sky, it was noon. Because the Sun moves across the sky, noon in one town arrived a few minutes earlier or later than in the next town east or west.

This did not matter when travel was slow. Then came the railways, in America and later across India, as told in India's railways. A train crossing a country needed a timetable that made sense. In the United States in the 1800s, railways juggled dozens of local times. On 18 November 1883, US and Canadian railways switched to a few standard time zones. People called it "the day of two noons", because many towns reset their clocks at midday.

Choosing a starting line

In 1884, delegates from 25 countries met in Washington, DC, for the International Meridian Conference. They chose the meridian, or north-south line, running through the Royal Observatory at Greenwich, in London, as the starting line for longitude and world time.

Today world time is called Coordinated Universal Time, or UTC. Time zones are described as hours ahead of or behind it. New York is 5 hours behind in winter, and Tokyo is 9 hours ahead.

One India, one clock

India covers about 30 degrees of longitude, which should mean roughly two hours of Sun-time difference. Yet the whole country uses one time zone, India Standard Time, which is 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of UTC. It is based on a line at 82.5 degrees east, passing near Mirzapur in Uttar Pradesh. The official time is kept by the CSIR-National Physical Laboratory in New Delhi.

Bombay and Calcutta kept their own local times for many years, and Bombay Time lasted until 1955. Today, one clock helps trains, flights and offices run smoothly across India. But in the north-east, the Sun can rise before 5 am in summer and set by around 4.30 pm in winter. Tea gardens in Assam have long kept their own unofficial "tea garden time", an hour ahead, and some people argue India should have two time zones.

The United States and its zones

The US mainland has four main time zones: Eastern, Central, Mountain and Pacific. Alaska and Hawaii have their own. When a family in Los Angeles sits down for breakfast at 7 am, friends in New York are already at 10 am.

Most of the US also uses daylight saving time, moving clocks forward one hour in spring and back in autumn, to give longer light evenings in summer. India does not.

Where the date changes

If you keep going east, adding an hour every zone, you eventually have to drop a whole day. That happens at the International Date Line, in the middle of the Pacific. Cross it heading west and you jump forward to tomorrow.

The line bends around islands and countries. In 1995, the island nation of Kiribati moved the line so its whole territory shared the same date. Some of its islands are now among the first places on Earth to greet each new year. Exact time matters more than ever today: your phone's GPS works by comparing time signals from satellites that are accurate to billionths of a second.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Every country's clocks differ from world time by a whole number of hours.

Truth: Many do, but not all. India is 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of world time, Nepal 5 hours 45 minutes, and the Canadian province of Newfoundland 3 hours 30 minutes behind.
