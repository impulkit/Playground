Why is Jaisalmer called the Golden City?

In the Thar Desert stands a fort made of yellow stone, and a few thousand people still live inside its walls, more than 850 years on.

The short answer

Jaisalmer is built almost entirely from one kind of rock: a yellow sandstone dug from the desert around it. Houses, temples, mansions and the great fort on the hill are all the same warm colour. In the morning the city looks pale yellow. At sunset it glows like honey, and that is why people call it the Golden City.

The fort is called Sonar Qila, the Golden Fort. Rawal Jaisal, a Bhati Rajput ruler, founded it in 1156 on a hill called Trikuta. More than 850 years later, a few thousand people still live inside its walls.

Surprising but true

Jaisalmer Fort is one of the few forts in the world where people still live, cook, run shops and go to school inside the walls.

A fort with people in it

Most old forts are quiet places full of tourists. Jaisalmer's fort has narrow lanes, cows, shops, small hotels, Jain temples from the 12th to 15th centuries and families who have lived there for generations. Its walls have 99 bastions, round towers that let guards see across the sand.

Living forts face a modern problem. Water from taps and drains seeps into the hill, and the soft rock underneath can crumble. Parts of the walls have collapsed after heavy rain. In a city that usually gets under 200 mm of rain a year, while Mumbai gets more than ten times that, the buildings were never designed for much water.

Camels, caravans and rich traders

Jaisalmer sat on the caravan routes linking India with Persia, Arabia and Central Asia, branches of the old Silk Road network. Long lines of camels carried spices, silk, opium and cloth across the desert, and the city taxed them as they passed.

The traders grew rich and built havelis, tall mansions with stone carved as finely as lace. Patwon ki Haveli is a cluster of five mansions begun in 1805 by a merchant named Guman Chand Patwa. Its windows, called jharokhas, have carved stone screens. These let in breeze and light while keeping out the harsh sun.

Then trade moved to the sea. Once ships sailed from Bombay, caravans faded, and after the border was drawn in 1947 the old routes west were cut. Jaisalmer grew quiet for decades.

How did the Thar Desert form?Every summer, monsoon winds blow right over the Thar Desert carrying plenty of moisture, and still most of it falls somewhere else.

Stone with a deep past

The sandstone here formed from sand and mud laid down when parts of this region lay near an ancient sea, many millions of years ago. Not far from the city, at Akal, lie fossil tree trunks thought to be about 180 million years old. They grew in forests at a time when dinosaurs walked the Earth, in a place that is now a desert.

So when you touch a Jaisalmer wall, you touch rock that formed long before the Himalaya even began to rise.

Made famous by a film

In 1974, the director Satyajit Ray made a children's detective film called Sonar Kella, "The Golden Fortress". In it, a boy remembers a past life in a golden fort, and the hero Feluda hunts for it across Rajasthan. Crowds of Bengali visitors still arrive asking for the fort from the film.

Today the city lives on tourism. The Sam sand dunes, about 40 km away, roll in waves several metres high, and every winter there is a Desert Festival with camel races and folk music.

Water in the dry land

Desert cities live or die by water. Jaisalmer's rulers built Gadisar Lake in the 14th century, a large tank that once held the whole city's water supply. Today the Indira Gandhi Canal carries Himalayan river water hundreds of kilometres into the Thar. Before canals, towns like this depended on clever tanks and deep stepwells, some cut so deep that people climbed down many storeys to reach the water.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Jaisalmer Fort is an empty monument, like a museum.

Truth: A few thousand people still live inside it, in houses that families have kept for generations. That makes it one of the world's very few living forts, and also one of the hardest to protect.

Try this

In a dark room, hold a sheet of yellow paper and shine a torch straight down on it, then from a low angle. Now put a thin orange or red cloth over the torch. Notice how the yellow looks richer and more golden, like sandstone at sunset.
