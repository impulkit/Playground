Who was the Flying Sikh?

He survived Partition as a boy refugee, failed to join the army three times, and then became the fastest 400-metre runner in Asia.

The short answer

"The Flying Sikh" was Milkha Singh, one of India's greatest athletes. He was a 400-metre runner who won gold at the 1958 Commonwealth Games and four Asian Games golds. At the 1960 Rome Olympics he came fourth, missing a medal by a fraction of a second. That race is still one of the most famous in Indian sport.

The nickname came from Pakistan, of all places. In 1960 he raced in Lahore and beat Pakistan's star sprinter. Pakistan's president, Ayub Khan, is said to have told him he had flown rather than run.

Surprising but true

Milkha Singh missed an Olympic medal in 1960 by about a tenth of a second, and India's national 400-metre record he set there stood for 38 years.

A childhood torn apart

Milkha Singh was born in Govindpura, a village in Punjab that became part of Pakistan in 1947. His exact birth date is unknown, but it was probably 1929. During the violence of Partition, several members of his family were killed. He escaped to Delhi as a teenage refugee, alone and frightened.

For a time he lived in refugee camps and struggled to survive. His elder brother encouraged him to join the army, which offered food, a uniform and a future. He was rejected three times before being accepted, around 1951 or 1952.

The army runner

In the army, a cross-country race offered extra milk to the top runners. Milkha came sixth and was picked for special training. He practised so hard that he sometimes collapsed or was sick after runs.

He made the Indian team for the 1956 Melbourne Olympics but did not get past the first round. There he met the American champion Charles Jenkins, who shared his training methods. Milkha went home determined to do better.

Gold in Cardiff

At the 1958 Commonwealth Games in Cardiff, Wales, Milkha won the 440 yards, beating South Africa's world-class runner Malcolm Spence. It was independent India's first gold medal at the Commonwealth Games. The same year he won the 200 metres and 400 metres at the Asian Games in Tokyo.

Prime Minister Nehru asked what reward he wanted. Milkha asked for a national holiday, and one was declared.

In 1960 he was invited to race in Lahore, in the country where his family had died. He did not want to go, and agreed only after Nehru persuaded him. Huge crowds watched him beat Abdul Khaliq, Pakistan's fastest man. In 1962 he won two more Asian Games golds in Jakarta, in the 400 metres and the 4 × 400 metres relay.

Fourth in Rome

At the 1960 Olympics in Rome, Milkha was one of the favourites for the 400 metres. In the final he started very fast, then eased slightly and glanced back. Three runners passed him. He finished fourth in about 45.6 seconds, a fraction behind the bronze medallist.

His time was a national record that lasted 38 years, until Paramjeet Singh broke it in 1998. Milkha later said that losing in Rome haunted him, and that he would give anything to see an Indian win an Olympic athletics medal.

A life remembered

Milkha married Nirmal Kaur, a former captain of India's women's volleyball team, and their son Jeev Milkha Singh became a top golfer. The family lived in Chandigarh. In 2013 the film Bhaag Milkha Bhaag told his story to a new generation. He sold the film rights for one rupee.

He died in June 2021 from complications of COVID-19, a few days after his wife. Weeks later, Neeraj Chopra won javelin gold in Tokyo and dedicated it to him.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Milkha Singh won a medal at the 1960 Olympics.

Truth: He came fourth in the 400 metres, just missing bronze. He later said he looked back at the other runners during the race, a mistake he regretted for the rest of his life.

Try this

Measure out 40 metres in a park or playground with an adult and time how long you take to run it. A 400-metre race is ten times that distance. Work out how fast you would need to run each 40 metres to match Milkha's 45.6 seconds, then see how far you get before you slow down.
