What is the Bible?

The Bible isn't really one book. Its name comes from a Greek word meaning "the books", and inside are dozens of them, written over many centuries.

The short answer

The Bible is the holy book of Christianity, the world's largest religion. Its first and longer part, often called the Old Testament, is also sacred to Jews, who call it the Tanakh or Hebrew Bible. The second part, the New Testament, is about the life and teachings of Jesus and his early followers.

It isn't one book written in one go. It is a library of dozens of books: laws, histories, poems, songs, wise sayings, prophecies and letters. Most were written in Hebrew or Greek, with some passages in Aramaic. Christians around the world read it in prayer and worship, and many believe it carries the word of God.

Two parts, many books

The Old Testament tells of the creation of the world, of Noah and the flood, and of Abraham, whom Jews, Christians and Muslims all honour. It follows Moses leading the Israelites out of slavery in Egypt, and young David defeating the giant Goliath. It includes the Psalms, a book of 150 poems and songs, and the words of prophets such as Isaiah.

The New Testament begins with four Gospels, each telling the story of Jesus in its own way. Then come the Acts of the Apostles and letters written to early Christian groups, many by a traveller and teacher named Paul. It ends with Revelation, a book of visions.

Different churches include slightly different books. Most Protestant Bibles have 66, Catholic Bibles have 73 and some Orthodox Bibles have a few more.

Old scrolls in desert caves

In 1947, Bedouin shepherds exploring caves at Qumran, near the Dead Sea, found clay jars holding ancient scrolls. Over the next years, searchers found fragments of hundreds of manuscripts. Some had lain hidden for more than 2,000 years.

These Dead Sea Scrolls include the oldest known copies of books of the Hebrew Bible. A long scroll of Isaiah is almost complete. Scholars were struck by how closely it matches copies made about a thousand years later, showing how carefully scribes worked. Many fragments are now kept in Jerusalem, in a museum building whose white roof is shaped like the lid of one of the jars. Thousands of pieces have been photographed in high detail and can be viewed online by anyone.

From handwritten to printed

For centuries every Bible was copied by hand, which took months or years. Then, around 1455, Johannes Gutenberg in Germany printed a Latin Bible using movable metal type. It was the first major book printed that way in Europe, and his printing press soon made books far cheaper.

How did the printing press change the world?Before the printing press, a single book could take a scribe months to copy by hand. Within fifty years of Gutenberg, Europe had printed millions.

Printing helped people read the Bible in their own languages rather than Latin. The English King James Version of 1611 gave the language phrases still used today, like "the writing on the wall". Centuries later, Martin Luther King Jr. quoted the prophet Amos in his "I Have a Dream" speech: "let justice roll down like waters".

The Bible in India

Christians in Kerala have a tradition that Thomas, one of Jesus's twelve apostles, arrived there in 52 CE. Historians can't confirm this, but Christianity has certainly been in India for well over 1,500 years.

In 1714, a German missionary, Bartholomäus Ziegenbalg, printed the New Testament in Tamil at Tranquebar, one of the earliest Bible translations printed in an Indian language. Later, William Carey and his team at Serampore near Kolkata worked on translations into Bengali and many other languages.

The Bible has been translated more than almost any other book. By 2024, the full Bible existed in more than 700 languages, and parts of it in thousands more. In India, there are complete Bibles in Hindi, Tamil, Malayalam, Mizo, Khasi and dozens of other languages.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Bible was written all at once by a single author.

Truth: It is a collection of many writings, including history, laws, poems, songs, stories and letters. Many different writers produced them over a long period, and they were gathered together much later.
