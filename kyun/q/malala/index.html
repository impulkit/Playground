Who is Malala Yousafzai?

At 11, she wrote a secret diary about wanting to go to school. At 17, she won the Nobel Peace Prize.

The short answer

Malala Yousafzai is a Pakistani campaigner for girls' education. She grew up in the Swat Valley, where an armed group called the Taliban tried to stop girls going to school. As a child she spoke out against it, on the radio, on television and in a diary for the BBC.

In 2012, when she was 15, a gunman from the group attacked her on her school bus. She was badly hurt but survived after treatment in Pakistan and England.

Instead of going quiet, she spoke louder. In 2014, aged 17, she became the youngest person ever to win the Nobel Peace Prize.

Surprising but true

Malala's first writing for the BBC was published under a made-up name, Gul Makai, so that nobody would know a schoolgirl had written it.

A valley and a school

Swat is a green valley of rivers and mountains in northern Pakistan, once popular with holidaymakers. Malala's father, Ziauddin Yousafzai, ran a school there and believed girls should learn as much as boys.

From about 2007, the Taliban took control of parts of Swat. They destroyed many schools and ordered girls to stay home. Malala, who loved school and came top of her class, refused to accept it. She was named after Malalai, a brave young woman in Pashtun folk stories who rallied fighters in a battle in Afghanistan in 1880.

The secret diary

In early 2009, the BBC's Urdu service wanted a schoolgirl to describe life under these rules. Malala, then 11, wrote short entries under the name Gul Makai. She wrote about fear, about hiding her uniform, and about missing her friends.

A teenage diary written in danger has power. Decades earlier, Anne Frank had kept one while hiding during the Second World War.

A girl with a microphone

Even before the diary, Malala and her father spoke at press clubs and on local radio about the school closures. In 2009, an American journalist made a short documentary about the family for The New York Times, filming Malala on what was meant to be her last day of school.

That year, Pakistan's army fought to push the Taliban out of Swat. Malala's family, like hundreds of thousands of others, had to leave home for several months. When they returned, schools slowly reopened.

In 2011 she won Pakistan's first National Youth Peace Prize. The government named a school after her. She was becoming well known, which also made her a target.

The attack and after

On 9 October 2012, a gunman stopped Malala's school bus and shot her. Doctors in Pakistan saved her life, and she was flown to a hospital in Birmingham, England, to recover. Her family settled there.

On her 16th birthday in 2013, she spoke at the United Nations in New York. "One child, one teacher, one book and one pen can change the world," she said. The UN called the day Malala Day.

The Nobel and beyond

In 2014, Malala shared the Nobel Peace Prize with Kailash Satyarthi, an Indian campaigner against child labour. With her father, she started the Malala Fund, which pays for education projects for girls in many countries. In 2020, she graduated from the University of Oxford.

People in Pakistan have mixed feelings about her. Many see her as a hero. Others are suspicious of how much attention she gets from Western countries. Around the world, about 12 crore girls are still out of school, so her work is far from done.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Malala only became known after she was attacked.

Truth: She was already speaking out in Pakistan for years before. She wrote her BBC diary in 2009, appeared in a documentary, and won Pakistan's first National Youth Peace Prize in 2011, a year before the attack.

Try this

Keep a one-week school diary like Malala's. Each day, write three lines about something you learned and how it felt. At the end of the week, read it back and pick the one thing you would most hate to lose if school stopped tomorrow.
