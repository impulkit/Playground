What is the lowest place on land?

On the shore of the Dead Sea you stand more than 430 metres below sea level, and the ground drops about another metre every year.

The short answer

The lowest dry land on Earth is the shore of the Dead Sea, a salt lake between Jordan, Israel and the West Bank. In the 2020s its surface lay more than 430 metres below sea level. That is deeper than the Eiffel Tower is tall.

And it is getting lower. The lake loses about a metre of depth each year. People take water from the Jordan River before it can reach the lake, and the hot sun dries out what is left. Every year, the lowest place you can walk on land moves a little further down.

Surprising but true

Farmers in Kuttanad, Kerala, grow rice on fields that lie up to about 2 metres below sea level, held back from the water by mud walls.

Why is it so low?

The Dead Sea sits in a long crack in Earth's crust called the Jordan Rift Valley. Two pieces of crust are sliding past each other here, and the land between them has dropped. It is part of the same system that runs south into Africa, where the Great Rift Valley is slowly splitting the continent.

Water flows in, but no river flows out. The only way out is up, as water evaporates in the desert heat. The salt is left behind, so the lake has grown saltier for thousands of years. Today it is almost 10 times saltier than the ocean. In 1947, a young shepherd exploring caves near the lake's north-west shore found ancient writings in clay jars. These Dead Sea Scrolls are more than 2,000 years old, and the dry desert air helped them survive.

Floating without trying

That salt makes the water very dense. Your body is less dense than the salty water, so it pushes you up. Swimmers bob on the surface like corks and can lie back as if on a sofa. That push is called buoyancy. Crusts of white salt build up on rocks along the shore, sometimes in shapes like mushrooms.

It is not all fun. The water stings badly in eyes and cuts, and swallowing it can make you very ill. Lifeguards watch closely, and visitors are told never to dunk their heads.

A shore full of holes

As the water falls, it leaves behind dry land with salt hidden underground. Fresh rainwater and groundwater seep in and dissolve that salt, leaving empty spaces. The ground above then collapses. Thousands of sinkholes have opened along the shore since the 1980s, swallowing roads, fields and even buildings.

The ruins of old beach resorts now sit far from the water's edge. Some places where people once swam are now hundreds of metres from the lake.

The lowest spots on other continents

Every continent has its own low point. In the United States, Badwater Basin in Death Valley lies 86 metres (282 feet) below sea level. A sign on a nearby cliff marks where sea level would be, high above visitors' heads. Death Valley is also one of the hottest places on Earth. Lake Assal in Djibouti, Africa's lowest point, sits about 155 metres down.

India's lowest point is Kuttanad in Kerala. Its rice fields lie up to about 2 metres below sea level, protected by mud walls, and farmers pump water out to plant their rice.

Even lower under the ice

If you could peel away the ice, Antarctica would beat them all. In 2019, scientists mapping the rock beneath the ice found a canyon under the Denman Glacier about 3,500 metres below sea level. It is packed with ice, so no one can visit.

Under water, things get far deeper. The bottom of the Mariana Trench in the Pacific is nearly 11,000 metres below the surface. And the strangest part? If you dropped Mount Everest into it, its peak would still be more than 2 kilometres underwater, as you'll see in the story of the deepest part of the ocean.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Nothing at all lives in the Dead Sea.

Truth: No fish or plants can survive there, but scientists have found tiny microbes, such as some bacteria and algae, living in the salty water and in springs on the sea floor.

Try this

Fill two glasses with water. Stir lots of salt into one until no more dissolves. Gently drop a fresh egg into each. The egg sinks in plain water but may float in the salty one, just as people float in the Dead Sea.
