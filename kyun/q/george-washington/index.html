Who was George Washington?

He won a war, could probably have ruled for life, and chose to go home to his farm instead.

The short answer

George Washington led the American army that won independence from Britain, and then became the first President of the United States. Americans often call him the "Father of His Country".

He was not a brilliant general who won every battle. He lost quite a few. But he kept a ragged army together through eight hard years and never gave up. After winning, he did something rulers rarely do: he gave power back. He resigned from the army in 1783, and as president he stepped down after two terms. That habit of leaving office peacefully became one of the foundations of American democracy.

Surprising but true

Washington's false teeth were not wooden. They were made from materials such as hippopotamus ivory, metal and human teeth.

A Virginia surveyor

Washington was born in 1732 in Virginia, then a British colony. His father died when he was 11, so he never went to university. As a teenager he worked as a surveyor, measuring and mapping land in the wild country to the west. It taught him to travel, read landscapes and live rough. He kept careful notebooks of measurements, a habit of record-keeping he never lost. As a boy he also copied out 110 rules of good manners, about how to behave politely at the table and in company.

At 21 he joined the Virginia militia and fought for Britain against the French. He was tall, around 1.88 m, and calm under fire.

Leading the army

When the American Revolution began in 1775, Congress chose Washington to command its army. His soldiers were farmers, shopkeepers and boys, often without proper uniforms or boots.

The darkest time came in late 1776. The army had lost New York and was shrinking. On Christmas night, Washington led his men across the icy Delaware River and surprised an enemy camp at Trenton. The win gave the army fresh hope. The war ended with victory at Yorktown in 1781.

The first president

In 1787 Washington chaired the meeting in Philadelphia that wrote the US Constitution. Two years later he was elected the first president. He remains the only president to win every single electoral vote, and he did it twice.

He was sworn in on 30 April 1789 in New York City, the capital at the time. Everything he did set an example, even what to call the president. Some people suggested grand titles. He accepted the plain "Mr. President".

Going home

In 1796 Washington announced he would not run for a third term. He went back to Mount Vernon, his farm by the Potomac River. Presidents followed his two-term custom for nearly 150 years, and in 1951 it became law.

Washington's life had a hard side too. Mount Vernon ran on the labour of enslaved people. More than 300 were living there when he died. In his will, he ordered that the people he personally owned be freed after his wife Martha's death. Martha freed them in 1801.

His face everywhere

Washington suffered from bad teeth for most of his life, and by the time he became president he had only one of his own left. He wore dentures that hurt and changed the shape of his face in his portraits.

The capital city, Washington, D.C., is named after him. The Washington Monument there, a stone obelisk about 169 m tall, was the tallest structure in the world when it was finished in 1884, until the Eiffel Tower beat it in 1889. His face is also carved on Mount Rushmore, and it appears on the US one-dollar note and the quarter.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Young George chopped down a cherry tree and said, "I cannot tell a lie."

Truth: The story was invented by an author called Mason Locke Weems, who added it to his book about Washington in 1806, after Washington had died. There is no evidence it happened.
