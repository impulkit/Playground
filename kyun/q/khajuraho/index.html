What are the Khajuraho temples?

A thousand years ago, kings in central India built about 85 stone temples in one town. About 25 still stand, covered in carved dancers, gods and elephants.

The short answer

Khajuraho is a small town in Madhya Pradesh with one of the finest groups of medieval temples in India. They were built between about 950 and 1050 CE by the Chandela kings, who ruled this part of central India.

Most are Hindu temples to Shiva and Vishnu, but a cluster of them belongs to the Jain faith. Nearly every outside wall is covered in carvings: gods, heavenly dancers, musicians, elephants, warriors and ordinary people going about their day. In 1986 the temples became a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Surprising but true

For hundreds of years the temples were half-hidden by forest, and most of the outside world heard of them only in 1838, when a British army engineer was led there.

Mountains made of sandstone

Each big temple sits on a high stone platform. Above the main shrine rises a curved tower called a shikhara, and around it smaller towers climb up like foothills. The idea was to copy Mount Kailash, the home of Shiva in the Himalaya. From a distance, the Kandariya Mahadeva temple looks like a cluster of peaks about 31 metres tall, roughly the height of a ten-storey building.

The builders used buff and pink sandstone from quarries near the Ken river. Blocks were shaped to fit tightly together, and the weight of the stone holds much of the building in place.

A crowd of stone people

Walk around Kandariya Mahadeva and you pass hundreds of figures, some no taller than your arm. Many are apsaras, heavenly women caught in the middle of a movement: one pulls a thorn from her foot, one looks into a mirror, one writes a letter. Sculptors carved them with bent hips and turned heads, so the stone seems to sway.

Elephants, horses and soldiers march along the lower bands. Higher up are gods and their attendants. A small share of the carvings show couples, and these get most of the attention from tourists. Historians have different ideas about why they are there. Some link them to Tantric beliefs, some to ideas about life's four goals, and others think they were simply part of showing the whole of human life.

Three groups of temples

The temples are usually split into three groups. The western group is the largest and holds the most famous Hindu temples, including Kandariya Mahadeva and the Lakshmana temple, one of the earliest, finished around 954 CE.

The eastern group includes the Jain temples. The biggest, the Parshvanath temple, has some of the most delicate carving on the whole site, and Jain pilgrims still worship there today. A short drive south, the southern group has a couple of later temples standing alone among fields.

Hindu and Jain temples were built side by side, by the same kinds of craftsmen, during the same years. The Chandela kings were Hindu, but rich Jain merchants in their kingdom paid for temples of their own, and the two styles look like close cousins.

Lost in the forest

After the Chandela kingdom faded in the 1200s and 1300s, Khajuraho stopped being a royal centre. Local people still worshipped at some temples, but the forest grew back around the others.

In 1838 a British army engineer, T. S. Burt, was taken to the site by local guides and wrote about what he saw. Later the Archaeological Survey of India cleared the plants, repaired the platforms and began protecting the temples. The name is usually explained by the word khajur, the date palm, which once grew here.

Dance among the temples

Every February since the 1970s, the Khajuraho Dance Festival has brought performers of Kathak, Bharatanatyam, Odissi and other forms of classical dance to an open-air stage in front of the western temples. Some of the poses the dancers strike can be found, a thousand years older, on the walls just behind them.

Today the town has a small airport and a railway station, and visitors from around the world come to walk the stone platforms at sunrise, when the sandstone turns gold.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Khajuraho temples are mostly covered in carvings of couples.

Truth: Only a small share of the carvings show couples. Most show gods and goddesses, musicians, dancers, soldiers, animals and scenes of daily life such as women writing letters or putting on make-up.

Try this

Build a temple tower from boxes or cups. Put the biggest at the bottom and stack smaller ones on top, then crowd little "peaks" around the main one so the whole thing rises like a mountain range. That is how a Khajuraho tower is meant to look, a stone copy of the Himalaya.
