What is democracy?

In a democracy, the people in charge can be sent home by ordinary voters. That simple idea took thousands of years to spread.

The short answer

Democracy is a way of running a country where the people choose who governs them, and can replace those leaders at the next election. Power comes from the voters, not from a king, an army or one family.

The word is Greek. Demos means "the people" and kratos means "rule", so democracy means "rule by the people". Today most countries call themselves democracies, though some hold elections that are not really free. A working democracy needs more than voting. It needs fair rules, a free press, courts that are independent, and the freedom to disagree without being punished.

Surprising but true

In two small Swiss cantons, citizens still gather in the town square once a year and make laws by raising their hands.

Two ways to rule by the people

In a direct democracy, citizens vote on the laws themselves. That works in a small place. In the Swiss cantons of Glarus and Appenzell Innerrhoden, voters still meet in an open-air square once a year and decide by a show of hands.

A country of crores of people cannot fit into one square. So most democracies are representative: you elect someone to make laws on your behalf, and if you dislike what they do, you vote for someone else next time. India's MPs and America's members of Congress are representatives. Some countries mix both ways and hold a referendum, a direct public vote on a single question.

Where it began

Around 508 BCE, the people of Athens began meeting on a hillside called the Pnyx to argue and vote on laws. Thousands could gather at once. But only free men born to citizen parents counted as citizens. Women, enslaved people and foreigners had no vote at all.

For most of history after that, kings and emperors ruled most of the world. Ideas of people's rule came back slowly, through parliaments in Britain, the American Revolution and the French Revolution.

How did democracy begin in Athens?In ancient Athens, many officials were chosen by a machine that worked like a lottery, because the Athenians thought elections favoured the rich.

Who gets a vote

Early modern democracies were not open to everyone either. In the United States, women won the vote nationally only in 1920, and many Black Americans in the South were kept from voting until the Voting Rights Act of 1965.

India made a bold choice. Its Constitution gave every adult the vote from the very start, whatever their religion, caste, wealth or gender. In the first general election of 1951–52, most voters could not read, so each party had a picture symbol on the ballot. Indian ballots still carry symbols today.

Words that stuck

In 1863, during the American Civil War, President Abraham Lincoln described democracy as "government of the people, by the people, for the people". The phrase from his Gettysburg Address is still quoted around the world.

India is the largest democracy on Earth. In 2024, about 97 crore people were registered to vote, more than the entire population of Europe. The United States has one of the oldest written national constitutions still in use, from 1789. The two countries elect their leaders in very different ways, but both rest on the same promise: the people in power must ask the rest of us for permission, again and again.

Harder than it looks

Democracy can be slow and noisy. People argue, parties compete and decisions take time. Supporters say that is the point. Arguing in public lets mistakes be spotted, and elections let a country change direction without anyone fighting in the streets. Many thinkers have pointed out that a democracy lasts only as long as its citizens keep taking part.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Democracy means the majority can do whatever it likes.

Truth: Most democracies have a constitution that protects everyone's basic rights, so a bigger group cannot simply vote away the rights of a smaller one. Courts can strike down laws that break those rules.

Try this

Run a vote at home or in class on something small, like which game to play. First let everyone vote directly. Then pick two "representatives" and let only them decide. Talk about which way felt fairer, and which was faster.
