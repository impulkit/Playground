What are the Youth Olympic Games?

Some of the world's Olympic stars first competed on the big stage as teenagers, at an Olympics made just for them.

The short answer

The Youth Olympic Games are the Olympics for teenagers. Athletes aged about 15 to 18 from around the world compete in many of the same sports as the grown-up Olympics.

They are held every four years, with summer and winter editions. The first Summer Youth Olympics took place in Singapore in 2010. The fourth, Dakar 2026 in Senegal, was the first Olympic event ever held in Africa.

Surprising but true

India's first ever swimming medal at the Youth Olympics, won in 2026, came by just 0.38 seconds. That is less time than it takes to blink twice.

Why have a teenage Olympics?

The idea came from Jacques Rogge, who led the International Olympic Committee. He wanted young athletes to get a taste of the Olympics early, and to learn about health, fair play and other cultures along the way.

Many stars of the main Olympics first competed at the Youth Games. They get used to big crowds, new countries and huge pressure while they are still young. The Games also try out new events, such as mixed teams of boys and girls, and sports like breaking and 3x3 basketball before they reach the main Olympics.

How old are the Olympic Games?They began in ancient Greece about 2,800 years ago, and came back in 1896.

India at the Youth Games

India's young athletes have won medals in sports like shooting, weightlifting, wrestling and archery. In 2026, 16-year-old Dhinidhi Desinghu from Bengaluru won India's first Youth Olympics swimming medal, a bronze in the 100 m freestyle. At 14, she had been the youngest member of India's team at the Paris 2024 Olympics.

She joins a growing list of young women athletes making history for India.

The science of a fast swim

Freestyle is usually front crawl, the fastest swimming stroke. Swimmers stretch out long and flat to cut down drag, the push of water against the body. Strong kicks and arm pulls need power and stamina.

Breathing matters too. Sprinters turn their heads only every few strokes, because each breath costs a tiny bit of speed. Learning to hold your breath calmly is part of swimming training.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Great swimmers are born loving the water.

Truth: Many champions start out nervous. Dhinidhi Desinghu was afraid of water as a small child, and her parents got into the pool to help her. Practice turned fear into skill.

Try this

Stand in a pool or a full bucket and sweep your hand through the water, first flat and then sideways like a knife. Feel how much harder the flat hand is to push. Swimmers keep their bodies long and slim to slice through water the same way.
