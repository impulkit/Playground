Who are some famous women in space?

The first woman in space was a factory worker who loved parachuting. Valentina Tereshkova orbited Earth 48 times in 1963, twenty years before any American woman flew.

The short answer

Hundreds of people have now been to space, and women have been part of the story almost from the beginning. Valentina Tereshkova of the Soviet Union went first, in 1963. The USA's first woman, Sally Ride, flew in 1983.

Since then, women have commanded the International Space Station, repaired telescopes, done spacewalks and, in 2026, flown around the Moon. India's story includes Kalpana Chawla, who flew twice on the Space Shuttle, and Sunita Williams, an American astronaut with family roots in Gujarat.

Each of them had to be a scientist, engineer or pilot first. Getting picked is hard for anyone. For women in the early decades, it was harder still.

Surprising but true

In April 2026, NASA astronaut Christina Koch became the first woman to travel around the Moon, on the Artemis II mission.

A parachutist in orbit

Tereshkova worked in a textile factory and spent her spare time jumping out of planes with a local club. The Soviet space programme wanted parachutists, because its capsules didn't land gently. Cosmonauts had to eject and float down on their own.

On 16 June 1963 she launched on Vostok 6. She circled Earth 48 times in almost three days, more time in orbit than all of America's astronauts had managed together until then. Her call sign was Chaika, which means seagull.

Another woman didn't fly for nineteen years, until Svetlana Savitskaya in 1982. In 1984, Savitskaya became the first woman to walk in space.

America catches up

When NASA picked its first astronauts in 1959, it chose only military test pilots, and the armed forces didn't let women fly jets. Women weren't selected until 1978. Sally Ride, a physicist from California, was one of six women in that group.

She flew on the Space Shuttle Challenger in June 1983. Reporters asked whether she would cry if things went wrong. She answered calmly and got on with the job. In 1992, Mae Jemison, a doctor and engineer, became the first African American woman in space.

Behind them were women who never flew but made flight possible, such as the mathematicians who calculated orbits by hand.

Who were the women who did the maths for the space race?Before John Glenn would fly around the Earth in 1962, he asked for one person to check the computer's numbers by hand. She was Katherine Johnson.

From Karnal to orbit

Kalpana Chawla grew up in Karnal, Haryana, and loved aeroplanes. She studied aeronautical engineering in Chandigarh, then moved to the USA. In 1997 she flew on the Space Shuttle Columbia, becoming the first woman born in India to go to space. She died with her six crewmates when Columbia broke apart on its return in 2003. Schools, scholarships and a satellite have been named after her.

Sunita Williams has done nine spacewalks. In 2024 she flew to the space station on a test flight of Boeing's Starliner capsule. Problems with the capsule meant she stayed about nine months instead of about a week, returning in March 2025.

Records in orbit

Christina Koch spent 328 days in space in 2019 and 2020, the longest single trip by a woman. During that stay, she and Jessica Meir made the first spacewalk with only women outside.

Peggy Whitson, a biochemist from Iowa, has spent about 695 days in space over her career, more than any other American or any woman. She was the first woman to command the space station.

Around the Moon

In April 2026, Koch flew on Artemis II with three male crewmates. Their Orion capsule looped around the far side of the Moon, and the crew travelled farther from Earth than any humans before them. She became the first woman to leave low Earth orbit and see the Moon up close.

India is preparing its own crewed flights under Gaganyaan. Its first uncrewed test flight, still waiting to launch in late 2026, is due to carry a humanoid robot with a woman's face called Vyommitra, which means "space friend".

Myth or fact?

Myth: Sally Ride was the first woman in space.

Truth: Sally Ride was the first American woman in space, in 1983. Valentina Tereshkova of the Soviet Union flew twenty years earlier, in 1963, and Svetlana Savitskaya flew in 1982.
