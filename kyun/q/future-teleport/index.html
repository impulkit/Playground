Is teleportation possible?

Scientists teleport things every year, but only the information inside single particles of light, and in 2017 they beamed it up to a satellite 1,400 km away.

The short answer

Teleportation in the Star Trek sense, where you vanish here and appear there, is not possible today, and no one knows how to make it possible. But a real thing called quantum teleportation happens in laboratories all the time. It moves the information that describes a particle, not the particle itself.

In 2017, Chinese scientists used it to send the state of photons (particles of light) between a station in Tibet and a satellite called Micius, about 1,400 km apart. That is roughly the distance from Delhi to Mumbai. Three physicists, Alain Aspect, John Clauser and Anton Zeilinger, shared the 2022 Nobel Prize partly for the work that made this possible.

Surprising but true

Star Trek's famous transporter was partly a money-saving trick, because showing a spaceship land on a new planet every week cost too much to film.

What actually gets teleported

The trick uses entanglement, a strange link between two particles that Einstein called "spooky action at a distance". Measure one entangled particle and you instantly learn something about its partner, however far apart they are.

Scientists use a pair like this as a bridge. They mix a third particle with one half of the pair, measure them, and send the result to the far end by ordinary radio or laser. The person there uses those results to turn the other half into an exact copy of the third particle's state. The original state is wiped out, so nothing is ever duplicated, and nothing goes faster than light.

What is a quantum computer?Some quantum computers have to be kept colder than outer space, just so their tiny parts can follow the strange rules of the atomic world.

Why people are much harder

A person isn't one particle. An adult body has about 7 followed by 27 zeros of atoms. If you listed each atom's type, position and movement, the description would dwarf every hard disk on Earth put together. Rough estimates suggest that sending it with today's technology would take longer than the universe has existed.

Then comes a creepier problem. Because the original state is destroyed, a teleporter would take you apart here and build someone new over there from fresh atoms. Would that person be you? Philosophers have argued about this for decades, and no experiment can settle it. Some call it the teleporter problem, and it turns up in classrooms as a favourite puzzle about what makes you, you.

A transporter born from a budget

When Star Trek began on American television in 1966, showing a spaceship landing on a different planet each week needed costly models and effects. Beaming the crew down with a shimmer of light was quicker and cheaper. The idea became so famous that "Beam me up" is now an everyday joke, even though nobody in the original series said exactly those words.

Science fiction has a habit of giving scientists ideas, which is a big part of the Star Wars effect on real research.

Teleporting without teleporting

There is a cheat that already works. In 2021, a NASA doctor and his team appeared as live 3D images aboard the International Space Station, using a camera system Microsoft calls "holoportation". The astronaut wore a headset and could see and talk to them as if they were floating in the cabin. No atoms moved, of course. The images were built from cameras on Earth and sent up as data, about 400 km to the station, in a few seconds. It is a very fancy version of a video call.

Where it could lead

Quantum teleportation may never carry people, but it could carry secrets. Messages sent this way can't be copied without leaving a trace, which makes them a dream for encryption. Scientists hope to link quantum computers in different cities into a quantum internet, and satellites like Micius are the first test runs.

So the honest answer is that we already teleport, just not the way films promised. And if you think moving through space is hard, physicists have even stranger ideas about moving through time.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Quantum teleportation means scientists can already zap small objects from place to place.

Truth: Only the quantum state of particles such as photons and atoms has been teleported. Not a single grain of sand, let alone a cat, has ever been sent this way.

Try this

Play "fax teleporter" with a friend. Build a small shape from 10 Lego bricks, then describe it in words only, while your friend copies it out of sight. Count how many sentences you needed. Now think about describing every atom in a human body instead of 10 bricks.
