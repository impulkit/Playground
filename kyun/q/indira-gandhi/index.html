Who was Indira Gandhi?

Indira Gandhi led India for about 16 years, longer than any prime minister except her father, and people still argue about what she did.

The short answer

Indira Gandhi was the prime minister of India from 1966 to 1977, and again from 1980 until 1984. She was the country's first woman prime minister and is still the only one. She was also the daughter of Jawaharlal Nehru, India's first prime minister.

She was one of the most powerful leaders India has had. Supporters remember her for winning a war, helping India grow more of its own food and standing up to big countries. Critics remember her for the Emergency, when she suspended many of India's freedoms for nearly two years. Both memories are true, which is why she is still argued about.

Surprising but true

As a girl of about 12, she started a "monkey army" of children who ran messages and kept watch for freedom fighters.

Growing up in the freedom struggle

Indira was born in 1917 in Allahabad, in a house that was a meeting place for leaders of the freedom movement. Her father and grandfather were often in prison. As a child she organised a Vanar Sena, a "monkey army" of children who carried messages and made flags.

She studied at Rabindranath Tagore's school in Santiniketan and later at Oxford. Her mother, Kamala, was often ill and died in Switzerland in 1936, when Indira was 18. After independence she lived with her father in Delhi and worked beside him, meeting world leaders long before she held any job of her own.

Prime minister

When Prime Minister Lal Bahadur Shastri died suddenly in 1966, Congress party leaders chose Indira. Some of them thought she would be easy to control. They were wrong. Within a few years she had split the party and taken charge.

In 1969 her government took over 14 large private banks, so that farmers and small businesses could borrow more easily. She won the 1971 election with the slogan "Garibi hatao" (remove poverty). That same year India fought the 1971 war, which ended with the birth of Bangladesh. Her government also backed the Green Revolution, and in 1974 India tested its first nuclear device at Pokhran.

She was given the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian award, in 1971. In 1973 her government started Project Tiger, setting up nine special reserves to protect India's falling number of tigers. Abroad, she kept India out of both Cold War camps. In 1983 she hosted a summit in New Delhi of the Non-Aligned Movement, a large group of countries that refused to side with either the USA or the Soviet Union.

The Emergency

In 1975 a court found her guilty of breaking election rules, and protests against her grew. On 25 June 1975 she declared a state of Emergency. Elections were put off. Thousands of opposition leaders were jailed without trial. Newspapers had to show their stories to government officers before printing. In some places, people were forced into operations to stop them having children.

She ended the Emergency in 1977 and called an election. Voters threw her out. It was the first time Congress had lost power at the centre since independence. Three years later, with the new government falling apart, voters brought her back.

The end

In the early 1980s, armed separatists in Punjab took over buildings inside the Golden Temple in Amritsar. In June 1984 she ordered the army to remove them. Many people died, including soldiers and pilgrims, and the holy site was damaged. Many Sikhs felt deeply hurt.

On 31 October 1984 she was shot by two of her own bodyguards at her home in Delhi. Days of violence against Sikhs followed in Delhi and other cities, and thousands of innocent people were killed. Her son Rajiv Gandhi became prime minister. Her home is now a museum, where the path she walked that morning is marked with crystal.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Indira Gandhi was related to Mahatma Gandhi.

Truth: She wasn't. She was the daughter of Jawaharlal Nehru and took the name Gandhi when she married Feroze Gandhi.
