What is DNA?

A four-letter instruction book inside almost every one of your cells.

The short answer

DNA is a very long molecule inside your cells. It carries the instructions for building and running your body, written in a code of just four chemical "letters": A, T, C and G. The order of the letters spells out the instructions.

Your DNA has about 3 billion pairs of letters. Printed as books, it would fill a big library shelf.

A section of DNA that holds the instructions for one job is called a gene. Humans have about 20,000 genes that carry recipes for proteins, the building blocks and tiny machines of your body.

Surprising but true

If you stretched out all the DNA in just one of your cells, it would be about 2 m long.

The twisted ladder

DNA is shaped like a twisted ladder, called a double helix. Each rung is a pair of letters: A always pairs with T, and C with G. In 1953, James Watson and Francis Crick worked out this shape, using X-ray pictures taken by Rosalind Franklin.

Franklin's sharpest picture, known as Photo 51, was shown to Watson without her knowing. She died in 1958, aged 37. Watson, Crick and her colleague Maurice Wilkins won the Nobel Prize in 1962, and today many scientists argue her part was not fairly recognised at the time.

How a cell copies itself

The pairing rule makes copying easy. When a cell divides, the ladder unzips down the middle. Each half then builds a new partner, putting a T opposite every A and a G opposite every C. You end up with two identical ladders, one for each new cell.

The copying is extremely accurate, but now and then a letter changes. Most changes do nothing. A few cause illness, and a very few turn out to be useful. Over many generations, these small changes are how living things evolve.

Why do children look like their parents?You carry a long set of instructions, written in a chemical code. Half came from your mother, half from your father, shuffled into a mix no one has ever had before.

Same and different

All people share about 99.9% of their DNA. The tiny differences help make each of us unique. You got half your DNA from each parent, which is why you may have your mother's nose and your father's laugh.

We also share a lot with other living things. DNA is how scientists know birds are dinosaurs and that mammoths were closest to Asian elephants. Identical twins start with almost exactly the same DNA, because they grow from one fertilised egg that split in two.

Catching criminals with DNA

In 1984, the British scientist Alec Jeffreys made a discovery. A few stretches of DNA vary so much between people that they can identify a person, a bit like a fingerprint. Police soon began using this DNA fingerprinting to solve crimes and free people who had been wrongly jailed.

In India, the biologist Lalji Singh developed DNA fingerprinting methods at the Centre for Cellular and Molecular Biology in Hyderabad. It's now used in Indian courts and to reunite lost children with their families.

Reading the whole book

In 2003, scientists from many countries finished the Human Genome Project. They had read nearly all the letters of human DNA, a job that took 13 years and cost billions of dollars. Today a person's whole DNA can be read in about a day, for a tiny fraction of that cost.

A code like a computer's

DNA uses four letters. Computers use just two: 0 and 1. Both store huge amounts of information in long strings. Scientists have even stored books and short films inside man-made DNA, because a few grams of it could in theory hold more data than a whole room of computers.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Your DNA decides everything about you.

Truth: DNA affects many things, such as your eye colour and blood group. But food, health, the place you grow up and the things you learn and practise shape you too. Even identical twins with almost the same DNA grow into different people.

Try this

Build a paper DNA ladder. Cut strips of four colours of paper for A, T, C and G, and glue them as rungs between two long paper strips, always pairing A with T and C with G. Write a secret word using your own colour code, then give a friend one side of the ladder and see if they can rebuild the other side.
