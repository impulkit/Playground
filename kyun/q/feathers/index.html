Why did some dinosaurs have feathers?

Feathers were around long before anything used them to fly.

The short answer

The first feathers were simple fuzz, like a chick's down. They probably kept small dinosaurs warm.

Later feathers grew branches and flat vanes. Dinosaurs likely used them for showing off, covering eggs and balancing as they ran.

Much later, some feathered dinosaurs began to glide and flap. Flying came second. Most feathered dinosaurs never left the ground, and some were far too big to fly, even with a full feathered coat.

Surprising but true

Feathers didn't start for flying. They were dinosaur fashion and blankets first.

What a feather is

Feathers are made of keratin, the same stuff as your hair and nails. A flight feather has a central shaft with hundreds of branches that zip together with tiny hooks. The branches on the front edge are shorter than those at the back, so the vane is lopsided. That shape helps the feather slice through the air.

Simple feathers are different. They are just hollow threads, or tufts of threads, without hooks. Scientists think feathers evolved in steps, from threads to tufts to branched feathers to flat vanes. Living birds show the same order as they grow. A newly hatched chick is covered in fluffy down, and its stiff, flat feathers come in later. Feathers also wear out, so birds shed and regrow them, usually once or twice a year. Scientists are still searching fossils for clear signs that feathered dinosaurs moulted too.

Clues from China

Fine-grained volcanic ash in Liaoning, China, preserved dinosaurs with their feathers in extraordinary detail. Some even kept their colour clues. The first feathered one, Sinosauropteryx, was found in 1996, covered in short fuzz along its back and tail.

Since then, dozens of feathered kinds have been found there. In 2012, scientists named Yutyrannus, a tyrannosaur about 9 m long, covered in long fluffy filaments. Its home had cool winters, so the coat may have kept it warm. A feathered tail tip has even turned up trapped in amber from Myanmar.

Many jobs

Warmth was probably the first job. Small, active animals lose heat quickly, and fuzz holds a layer of warm air close to the skin. Later, colourful feathers became useful for display, to attract mates or warn rivals, like a peacock's tail.

Feathers also kept eggs warm. Fossils of Oviraptor relatives show them crouched over nests, with their feathered arms spread around the eggs. And fast runners may have used feathered arms to help them balance and turn.

From fuzz to flight

Microraptor, a crow-sized dinosaur from China, had long flight feathers on both its arms and its legs. It probably glided between trees. Velociraptor had bumps on its arm bones where big feathers attached, even though it was too heavy to fly. Its ancestors may once have flown, or its feathers may have been for show.

Over time, some small feathered dinosaurs used their wings to flap and fly. Their descendants are today's birds.

Did all dinosaurs have feathers?

That is still debated. A plant-eater from Siberia called Kulindadromeus, far from the bird branch, had fuzzy threads as well as scales. That suggests the ability to grow feather-like coverings might go back to the earliest dinosaurs. But skin prints from many big dinosaurs, including T. rex, show only scales. Perhaps some lost their fuzz as they grew large, the way elephants have very little hair.

Today, feathers keep birds warm and dry, help them fly, and help them show off. Owls even have soft-edged feathers that let them fly in silence.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Any dinosaur with feathers could fly.

Truth: Most feathered dinosaurs could not fly at all. Their feathers kept them warm, helped them show off or shielded their eggs. Flight came later, in only a few small kinds.

Try this

Look closely at a bird feather with a magnifying glass. Gently pull two branches apart, then stroke them back together between your fingers. They zip up again. Tiny hooks hold them, just like the feathers of many dinosaurs.
