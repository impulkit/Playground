Why do fingers wrinkle in water?

If the nerve to a finger is cut, that finger stays smooth in the bath, which tells us wrinkling is something your body does on purpose.

The short answer

Your fingers wrinkle in water because your nervous system tells them to. After a few minutes of soaking, nerves signal the tiny blood vessels under the skin of your fingertips to narrow. The pad of each fingertip loses a little volume, and the skin on top, which stays the same size, folds into wrinkles.

For a long time, people thought it happened because skin soaked up water like a sponge. Water is part of the trigger, but nerves do the real work. Why our bodies do this is still debated. One popular idea is that wrinkles help us grip wet things, like tyre treads on a rainy road.

Surprising but true

Doctors have used the bath test as a simple check. If a finger doesn't wrinkle after soaking, the nerve to it may be damaged.

The cut-nerve clue

In the 1930s, doctors noticed something odd. People whose finger nerves had been cut in an accident didn't get wrinkly fingers in water. The skin was the same. Only the nerves were missing.

That showed wrinkling isn't just soggy skin. It is an action the body takes. Doctors later turned it into a simple test: soak the hand and see if each finger wrinkles. A finger that stays smooth may have a damaged nerve.

How the wrinkles form

The outer layer of your skin is made of tough dead cells full of keratin, the same protein as hair and nails. On your fingertips, palms and soles, this layer is extra thick and has many sweat pores.

When you soak, water slips in through those pores and changes the balance of salt in the skin. Nerves seem to pick up on this. They send a signal that narrows the blood vessels beneath. The fingertip shrinks slightly, but the thick outer skin can't shrink with it, so it buckles into ridges and valleys.

Wrinkles like tyre treads?

In 2011, scientist Mark Changizi pointed out that finger wrinkles look like river channels running downhill. He suggested they might drain water away from the fingertips when we grip, like the grooves on a car tyre.

In 2013, a team at Newcastle University in England tested the idea. Volunteers moved wet marbles from one box to another, with smooth or wrinkled fingers. Wrinkled fingers did the job faster. Dry marbles made no difference. But some later studies did not find this advantage. So the grip idea is popular, but not proven.

Why do tyres have patterns?The grooves on a car tyre are drains. At highway speed in heavy rain, a tyre can push away several litres of water every second.

Only in some places

Wrinkles appear only on the hairless skin of the fingers, toes, palms and soles. These are the parts that touch the ground or grab things. That supports the grip idea: our ancestors may have needed to hold wet branches or walk on wet rocks.

People aren't the only animals with pruney fingers. Some macaque monkeys get them too. Scientists are still checking how common it is in other animals.

Warm water works faster

Wrinkling starts after about 5 minutes in warm water, and it takes longer in cold water. Salty water slows it down too, so swimmers in the sea may wrinkle later than people in a bath.

Once you dry off, the blood vessels relax and the fingertips fill out again. Within about 10 to 20 minutes, your fingers are smooth. A 2023 study found something else odd: the wrinkles form in nearly the same pattern every time you soak, because they follow the way your skin is anchored to the tissue underneath.

What wrinkles leave behind

Even when wrinkled, your fingertip ridges, the tiny swirls of your fingerprints, stay the same. And the strangest part? Scientists think those swirling ridges may also help with grip and touch, which means your fingertips may carry two separate grip systems, one permanent and one that switches on in water.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Fingers wrinkle because the skin soaks up water and swells.

Truth: Water does seep into the outer skin, but the main cause is your nerves. They tell blood vessels under the skin to narrow, so the fingertip shrinks a little and the skin on top puckers.

Try this

Fill two bowls, one with warm tap water and one with warm water with three spoons of salt stirred in. Put a finger of each hand in a different bowl and time how long each takes to wrinkle. Then dry your hands and time how long they take to go smooth again.
