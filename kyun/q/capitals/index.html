What is a capital city?

Some of the world's capitals were built from scratch in empty countryside, simply because two bigger cities couldn't stop arguing over which should win.

The short answer

A capital city is the place where a country's or state's government is based. It is usually where the parliament meets, where the president, prime minister or king has an official home, and where the top court sits. Embassies of other countries cluster there too.

The word comes from the Latin "caput", meaning head, because a capital is the head of a country's government. India's capital is New Delhi. The United States' capital is Washington, DC. Each Indian state and each US state also has its own capital, where its own assembly or legislature meets. Some capitals are ancient cities that grew important over centuries. Others were planned on empty land and built for the job.

Surprising but true

Chandigarh is the capital of two Indian states at once, Punjab and Haryana, and it isn't part of either of them.

Capitals built from scratch

When a country argues over which big city should lead, building a new one can settle it. In 1790, the US Congress agreed to create a new capital on the Potomac River, between the northern and southern states. Washington, DC, was laid out with wide avenues, and the government moved there in 1800.

Australia had the same problem. Sydney and Melbourne both wanted to be the capital, so the government built Canberra between them. It was founded in 1913. Brazil went further. In 1960, it moved its capital from Rio de Janeiro to Brasília, a futuristic new city built in just a few years in the middle of the country.

What does a government do?A government decides which side of the road you drive on, what's in your textbooks, and who fixes the streetlight outside your home.

How India's capital moved

During much of British rule, Calcutta (now Kolkata) was the capital of British India. In 1911, at a grand ceremony called the Delhi Durbar, King George V announced that the capital would move to Delhi. Delhi had been the seat of powerful rulers for centuries, from the Delhi Sultanate to the Mughals, as told in Delhi.

The architects Edwin Lutyens and Herbert Baker designed a new city beside old Delhi. New Delhi was inaugurated in 1931. Its broad avenues lead to Rashtrapati Bhavan, the President's house, and close by stands Parliament, where India's laws are made.

Two capitals, three capitals

Some countries split their capital's jobs. South Africa has three: Pretoria for the government, Cape Town for parliament, and Bloemfontein for the top appeals court. Bolivia's government sits in La Paz, while its constitution names Sucre as the capital.

In the Netherlands, Amsterdam is the official capital, but the government and parliament work in The Hague. In British India, the government moved up to Shimla in the hills every summer to escape the heat of the plains, making it a summer capital.

State capitals

Every Indian state has its own capital, from Thiruvananthapuram in Kerala to Itanagar in Arunachal Pradesh. Chandigarh, designed by the Swiss-French architect Le Corbusier in the 1950s, was planned as a modern city with numbered sectors, gardens and lakes. It serves as capital for both Punjab and Haryana, a story connected to how India's states were drawn.

US state capitals are often not their state's biggest city either. The capital of New York State is Albany, not New York City. California's is Sacramento, not Los Angeles.

Why location matters

Rulers often chose capitals for safety, for trade or to unite a country. Some capitals sit on rivers or coasts for easy travel. Others were placed in the centre to be fair to all regions.

Countries still move capitals today. Indonesia has been building a new capital on the island of Borneo, partly because Jakarta is crowded and parts of it are sinking.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A country's capital is always its biggest city.

Truth: Often it isn't. Washington, DC, is much smaller than New York, Canberra is smaller than Sydney, Ottawa is smaller than Toronto, and Brasília is smaller than São Paulo.
