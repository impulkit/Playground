What is Yakshagana?

In coastal Karnataka, travelling troupes still perform myths from dusk till dawn, in crowns so tall and wide they look like glittering fans.

The short answer

Yakshagana is a traditional theatre from coastal Karnataka that mixes dance, music, singing and spoken dialogue. Its name means "songs of the yakshas", magical beings from Hindu stories. Performances tell tales from the Mahabharata, the Ramayana and the Puranas, with gods, kings, heroes and demons.

Actors wear bright costumes, large wooden ornaments and towering, decorated crowns. A singer called the Bhagavata leads the show from the back of the stage, singing the story while drummers pound out the rhythm. The actors dance to the songs, then step forward and speak, making up much of their dialogue on the spot. Traditionally, shows run all night in the open air, ending at sunrise.

Surprising but true

Yakshagana actors make up much of their dialogue on the spot, so the same story can be told differently every night.

The Bhagavata and his band

The Bhagavata is the heart of Yakshagana. He sits on a low platform with the musicians and sings the verses of the play. He also keeps the beat with small cymbals and signals the actors when to move, when to speak and when to stop.

Beside him sit the drummers. The chande is a tall drum played with sticks, loud enough to be heard across the fields at night. The maddale is a two-sided drum played with the hands, giving softer, deeper sounds. A harmonium or a drone instrument holds the main note.

Talking gods and demons

Unlike many classical dance-dramas, Yakshagana actors talk. After the Bhagavata sings a verse, an actor might argue, joke, boast or plead, in Kannada, sometimes for many minutes. The best actors are admired for their quick wit and knowledge of the epics as much as for their dancing.

That is why no two shows are the same. The story stays fixed, but each actor finds new things to say. A comic character, the hasyagara, often teases the audience and other actors, bringing laughter into even the most serious tale.

What is the Mahabharata?It has about 100,000 verses, making it one of the longest poems ever composed. Hidden inside it is the Bhagavad Gita.

Costumes that tower

The costumes are among the most striking in Indian theatre. Kings and heroes wear a tall crown called a kireeta. Demons and fierce characters wear the huge kedage mundale, a fan-shaped headdress that can be wider than the actor's shoulders.

Ornaments are carved from light wood, covered with gold foil and set with small pieces of mirror and coloured glass. Faces are painted in bold colours, with patterns that show each character's nature. Fully dressed, an actor looks almost twice their normal size, which makes sense for a show watched by crowds sitting on the ground in the dark.

Touring troupes

Yakshagana troupes are called melas, and many are linked to temples. From about November to May, the dry season after the monsoon, they travel from village to village, setting up a stage and lights in open fields. Families sponsor shows to give thanks or to fulfil a vow.

Thousands of people may come, bringing mats to sit on. Children fall asleep and wake up to see a demon in full fury at 3 a.m. There are two main styles: Tenkutittu in the south, closer to Kerala, and Badagutittu in the north, around Udupi.

A version with no costumes

Yakshagana also has a seated form called talamaddale. The actors sit in a row in everyday clothes, and the Bhagavata sings as usual. There is no dancing at all. Instead, the performers debate the story in long, clever arguments, almost like a courtroom.

In the 20th century, the writer K. Shivarama Karanth experimented with Yakshagana, shortening shows and taking troupes abroad. Today it is also performed in Tulu, the other major language of the coast. Its mix of music, dance and improvised theatre still draws large crowds.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Yakshagana is a kind of Kathakali.

Truth: They are separate traditions from neighbouring states. Both use big costumes and stories from the epics, but Yakshagana actors speak and improvise dialogue in Kannada, while Kathakali actors never speak.

Try this

Play "Bhagavata" with friends. One person sings or reads a single line of a story out loud, such as "The king enters, angry". The actor then has to make up what the king says and does, with no script. Swap roles and see how differently each person tells the same line.
