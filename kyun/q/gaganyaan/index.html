What is India's Gaganyaan mission?

India has sent probes to the Moon, Mars and the Sun. Gaganyaan is its plan to launch its own astronauts on its own rocket.

The short answer

Gaganyaan, meaning "sky vehicle" in Sanskrit, is India's human spaceflight programme. Its aim is to launch two or three astronauts on an Indian rocket, from Sriharikota in Andhra Pradesh, into orbit about 400 km above Earth. They would circle the planet for up to three days, then return in a capsule that splashes down in the sea.

If it succeeds, India will become the fourth country to send people into space on its own, after the Soviet Union in 1961, the United States in 1961 and China in 2003. ISRO is testing each part carefully first: the rocket, the capsule, the parachutes and an escape system to save the crew if something goes wrong.

Surprising but true

Before any astronaut flies, a half-humanoid robot called Vyommitra, meaning "space friend", is meant to ride an uncrewed test flight and report how the capsule behaves.

The astronauts

In February 2024, India named the four astronaut-designates for Gaganyaan. All four are test pilots from the Indian Air Force: Prasanth Balakrishnan Nair, Ajit Krishnan, Angad Pratap and Shubhanshu Shukla. They trained in Russia and in Bengaluru, learning everything from spacecraft systems to how to survive if the capsule lands somewhere unexpected.

In June 2025, Shukla flew to the International Space Station on a private mission called Axiom-4, launched from the United States. He became the first Indian to visit the station, and the experience he brought home is feeding into Gaganyaan.

Safety first

The most important part of any crewed rocket is the part you hope never to use: the crew escape system. If the rocket fails, small powerful motors pull the capsule away and parachutes bring it down.

On 21 October 2023, ISRO tested this with a special test rocket from Sriharikota. At about 12 km up, the escape system fired, pulled away a test capsule, and brought it down under parachutes into the Bay of Bengal, where the Navy recovered it. ISRO has also dropped capsules from helicopters to test the parachutes.

The rocket chosen to carry the crew is a strengthened version of LVM3, India's heaviest launcher, the same type that sent Chandrayaan-3 to the Moon.

A robot goes first

Before any astronaut flies, ISRO plans uncrewed test flights. One is meant to carry Vyommitra, a half-humanoid robot with a head, arms and a body but no legs. Vyommitra can speak, watch the capsule's control panels and report back on temperature, air and other conditions, just as a crew member would.

The Soviet Union and the United States sent dogs and chimpanzees into space before people. Today, robots and sensors can do that job instead.

From Rakesh Sharma to Gaganyaan

India's first astronaut, Rakesh Sharma, flew to the Soviet Salyut 7 space station in April 1984, on a Soviet Soyuz rocket. Prime Minister Indira Gandhi spoke to him by video link and asked how India looked from space. He replied, "Saare jahan se achha", better than the whole world, a line from a famous patriotic song. He spent nearly eight days in orbit and did yoga exercises to study how the body copes without weight.

India's space programme was started in the 1960s by Vikram Sarabhai, who believed space technology should help ordinary people through weather forecasts, communication and education. Human spaceflight was a later dream.

ISRO has said it aims to fly the first Gaganyaan crew in 2027. By November 2026, though, the first uncrewed test flight, with Vyommitra on board, had not yet launched. India's longer-term plans include its own space station, the Bharatiya Antariksh Station, by 2035, and an Indian astronaut on the Moon by 2040.

Myth or fact?

Myth: India has already launched astronauts on its own rockets.

Truth: Not yet. Rakesh Sharma flew on a Soviet rocket in 1984, and Shubhanshu Shukla on an American one in 2025. Gaganyaan is India's plan to launch astronauts from Indian soil, on an Indian rocket, in an Indian capsule.
