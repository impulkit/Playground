What is the White House?

George Washington chose the spot and approved the design for the White House, but he is the only US president who never lived in it.

The short answer

The White House is the home and office of the President of the United States. It stands at 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue in Washington, D.C., the US capital. Every president since John Adams in 1800 has lived there.

It is also a working office, a museum and a symbol. When news reports say "the White House said today", they mean the president and their staff. Its picture appears on the back of the US $20 note, so most Americans carry it around without thinking about it.

Surprising but true

It takes about 2,100 litres of white paint to cover the outside of the White House.

Building the president's house

In 1792, a competition was held to design a home for the president. It was won by James Hoban, an architect born in Ireland. His plan was for a large, elegant house of pale sandstone, inspired by country houses in Ireland and Britain.

George Washington chose the site and approved the design, but the house was not ready until 1800, after his term ended. It was built by a mix of workers: hired labourers, skilled stonemasons from Scotland and enslaved African Americans, whose work is now recognised by historians and by the White House itself. The outside was coated with a lime-based whitewash to protect the stone.

Burned and rebuilt

In 1814, during the War of 1812, British troops marched into Washington. They set fire to the president's house and the Capitol. First Lady Dolley Madison is famous for making sure a large portrait of George Washington was saved before she fled.

Only the blackened outer walls survived. Hoban returned to rebuild it, and by 1817 a president was living there again. People had called it the "White House" for years because of its colour, and in 1901 President Theodore Roosevelt made the name official by printing it on his writing paper.

Inside the house

The main residence has 132 rooms, 35 bathrooms and six levels, including two basement floors. It also has 28 fireplaces and 147 windows. The ground and first floors include famous rooms open to visitors, such as the Blue Room, the Red Room and the long East Room, used for big ceremonies. The president's family lives on the floors above.

The West Wing, added in 1902, holds offices for the president's staff. The Oval Office, the president's own office, was added to it in 1909. Its oval shape echoes oval rooms in the main house. Between 1949 and 1952, the inside of the main house was almost completely rebuilt, because its old wooden beams were close to collapse. President Harry Truman and his family moved across the street while workers kept only the outer walls and built a steel frame inside them.

Changes over time

Every president changes something. Some have added tennis courts, a swimming pool or a vegetable garden. In October 2025, the East Wing was demolished to make room for a large new ballroom, a project that drew both support and criticism.

Some traditions last. Since 1878, children have raced to push eggs across the South Lawn with spoons at the Easter Egg Roll. Presidents have hosted Diwali celebrations at the White House since 2003, and the lighting of diyas there is now a regular event for many Indian Americans.

A house for the whole country

In India, the President lives in the Rashtrapati Bhavan in New Delhi, which is far larger than the White House. The White House was meant to be grand but not a palace, a house fit for a citizen rather than a king. That idea, a leader who lives among the people and leaves after their term, is part of what the building stands for.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The White House was painted white to hide the burn marks after the fire of 1814.

Truth: Its stone walls were first coated with white lime wash in 1798, years before the fire, to protect them from rain and frost. People were already calling it the White House before 1814.
