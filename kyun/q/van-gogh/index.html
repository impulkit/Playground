Who was Vincent van Gogh?

Van Gogh painted for only about ten years and sold very few pictures, yet his swirling night sky is now one of the best-known paintings on Earth.

The short answer

Vincent van Gogh (1853–1890) was a Dutch painter famous for thick, swirling brushstrokes and strong, bright colours. He painted sunflowers, wheat fields, starry skies, his bedroom, and dozens of portraits of himself and the people around him.

During his short life he was poor, often lonely, and struggled with periods of serious illness that affected his mind. He sold very little. His brother Theo supported him with money and letters for years. After Vincent's death, his paintings slowly became world famous. Today they are among the most valuable and best-loved pictures anywhere.

Surprising but true

Van Gogh didn't start painting seriously until he was 27, and he made most of his famous works in his last few years.

A late start

Van Gogh grew up in the south of the Netherlands, the son of a minister. Before he became an artist he tried many jobs. He worked for an art dealer, taught at a school in England, sold books and trained to be a preacher among coal miners in Belgium.

At 27 he decided to become an artist. He taught himself largely by copying, practising drawing again and again. His early paintings were dark and earthy, full of farmers and weavers. A famous one, The Potato Eaters, shows a family eating supper by the light of a single lamp.

Paris and colour

In 1886 he moved to Paris to live with Theo. There he saw the work of the Impressionists and other new painters, and his colours exploded. He also collected Japanese woodblock prints and loved their flat areas of bright colour and bold outlines.

He began placing colours that are opposite each other on the colour wheel, like blue and orange, side by side. These pairs make each other look brighter.

Why is the night sky dark?If the universe were endless, old and full of stars, every point in the sky should blaze with starlight. It doesn't, and that tells us the universe had a beginning.

The yellow house in Arles

In 1888 van Gogh moved to Arles, in the sunny south of France. He rented a house painted yellow and dreamed of starting a community of artists there. In just over a year he made around 200 paintings, including his famous Sunflowers.

The painter Paul Gauguin came to stay, but the two argued fiercely. Van Gogh then had a severe breakdown and hurt his own ear. Afterwards he spent a year in a hospital in Saint-Rémy, where he kept painting the gardens, olive trees and views from his window.

The Starry Night

In June 1889, from that hospital room, van Gogh painted The Starry Night. A village sleeps under a sky full of swirling wind, glowing stars and a crescent moon, with a dark cypress tree rising like a flame.

He painted it partly from memory and imagination, not exactly as he saw it. Astronomers have pointed out that the bright "star" near the horizon may be the planet Venus, which was visible in the early morning sky then. The painting now hangs in the Museum of Modern Art in New York.

Letters to Theo

Vincent wrote hundreds of letters, most of them to Theo. More than 800 survive. In them he describes what he is painting, which colours he is using and why, and how he feels. They are one reason we know so much about his life and thinking.

Van Gogh died in July 1890, aged 37, after a long period of illness. Theo died just six months later. Theo's widow, Jo van Gogh-Bonger, kept the paintings and letters together, organised exhibitions and published the letters. Without her, the world might never have known his work. Today the Van Gogh Museum in Amsterdam holds the largest collection of his paintings, and it is one of the most visited museums in Europe.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Van Gogh sold only one painting in his whole life.

Truth: The famous story says he sold just The Red Vineyard. In fact records show a few other sales and trades, though he still earned very little and depended on his brother Theo for money.

Try this

Mix a little flour into some poster paint to make it thick, like toothpaste. Paint a night sky using short, curving strokes and let the ridges of paint stay. When it dries, shine a torch across it from the side and watch the shadows of your brushstrokes appear.
