Where do dolphins live in India?

India's national aquatic animal is a nearly blind dolphin that lives in muddy rivers and finds its food by sound.

The short answer

Dolphins live in three kinds of places in India: big rivers, coastal lagoons and the open sea. The most famous is the Ganges river dolphin, which lives in the Ganga, the Brahmaputra and their branches in states such as Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Assam and West Bengal.

India's first full count, published in 2025, found about 6,300 river dolphins. Almost all were Ganges river dolphins. Along the coast, Irrawaddy dolphins live in Chilika Lake in Odisha and in the Sundarbans. Out at sea, bottlenose, spinner and humpback dolphins swim off both coasts, and you can sometimes spot them from boats in Goa, Kerala and the Andaman Islands.

Surprising but true

The Ganges river dolphin often swims on its side, trailing one flipper along the riverbed to feel its way through the murky water.

A dolphin that cannot see

The Ganges river dolphin looks quite unlike the smiling sea dolphins in cartoons. It has a long, thin beak packed with sharp teeth, tiny eyes and a rounded forehead. Its eyes have no proper lens. It can tell light from dark, and not much more.

In muddy water, sight is not much use anyway. Instead, the dolphin sends out fast clicks and listens for the echoes bouncing back from fish and stones. This is called echolocation. It often swims on its side, sweeping its head and trailing a flipper along the riverbed, hunting for prawns and fish. It must come up to breathe every minute or two, and the sound of its breath, "susu", gave it its local name.

India's national aquatic animal

In 2009, the Government of India named the Ganges river dolphin the national aquatic animal. Scientists call it an indicator species. If the dolphins are doing well, the river is probably healthy too, with enough water, fish and clean stretches.

Bihar has the Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary, a stretch of about 60 kilometres of the Ganga near Bhagalpur, set up in 1991. Uttar Pradesh had the largest number of dolphins in the 2025 survey, followed by Bihar.

Dolphins in the Beas

A close cousin, the Indus river dolphin, lives mainly in Pakistan. In India, a very small group survives in the Beas river in Punjab. The 2025 survey counted only three. Indus river dolphins were thought to have vanished from India decades ago, so their rediscovery in the Beas in 2007 was a pleasant shock. The stretch of river where they live is part of a protected wetland, and Punjab's wildlife department now watches them closely.

The two river dolphins look almost the same. For a long time scientists thought they were one species. Studies of their skulls and genes later showed they had been apart long enough to count as two.

Lagoon dolphins of Chilika

Chilika Lake, a huge salty lagoon on the Odisha coast, is home to Irrawaddy dolphins. They have round heads with no beak, which makes them look a little like small whales. Recent counts put the number at about 150.

Boats take visitors out to watch them near the mouth of the lake, where it meets the Bay of Bengal. Too many noisy motorboats can disturb them, so local rules limit how close boats can go. Irrawaddy dolphins also live in the rivers and creeks of the Sundarbans, alongside crocodiles and tigers.

Trouble in the water

River dolphins face many dangers. Dams and barrages split rivers into pieces, so dolphin groups get cut off from one another. Some stretches dry up in summer as water is taken for farms and cities. Dolphins get tangled in fishing nets, and pollution from towns and factories harms the fish they eat.

In 2020 India launched Project Dolphin to protect both river and sea dolphins, and it now marks National Dolphin Day on 5 October. A second, wider count began in 2026. If the numbers hold steady, it will be good news for the rivers as well as the dolphins.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Dolphins live only in the sea.

Truth: Several kinds of dolphin live in rivers. India has the Ganges river dolphin in the Ganga and Brahmaputra, and a tiny group of Indus river dolphins in Punjab's Beas river. Some dolphins, like the Irrawaddy dolphin, live in coastal lagoons and river mouths.

Try this

Fill a large bowl with water, close your eyes and tap the side of it gently with a spoon. Then put a small object in the water and tap again. Can you hear any change? River dolphins can't see much in muddy water, so they send out clicks and listen for echoes to find fish.
