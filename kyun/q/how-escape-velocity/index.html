How fast must a rocket go to escape Earth?

To throw a ball so hard it never comes back down, you would need to hurl it at about 40,000 km/h.

The short answer

Throw a ball up and gravity drags it back. Throw it harder and it goes higher before falling. Escape velocity is the speed that is just enough to never fall back, because gravity can slow the ball down but never quite stop it.

For Earth, that speed is about 11.2 km per second, or roughly 40,000 km/h. At that pace you could cross from Mumbai to Delhi in under two minutes. A bullet from a rifle travels only about a tenth as fast.

But here is the twist. Escape velocity is for something thrown once and left to coast, like a cannonball. A rocket keeps firing its engines, so in theory it could climb away slowly. In practice, slow climbing wastes fuel fighting gravity, so rockets go fast anyway.

Surprising but true

A rocket does not actually need to reach escape velocity. If it could keep its engines running, it could leave Earth at the speed of a bicycle.

Newton's giant cannon

More than 300 years ago, Isaac Newton drew a famous picture. It showed a cannon on top of a very tall mountain, firing balls sideways. A slow ball curves down and lands nearby. A faster one lands farther away. Fire one fast enough, and the ground curves away beneath it as fast as it falls. The ball never lands. It is in orbit.

For low orbit around Earth, that speed is about 7.8 km per second. Push faster still, to 11.2 km per second, and the orbit stretches into a path that never comes back. That is escape.

Why heavy and light need the same speed

Escape velocity does not depend on what you throw. A pebble and the space station need the same speed. Heavier things take more energy to get there, but the number is the same. That's the same reason a feather and a hammer fall together on the Moon, where there is no air to slow the feather. Gravity pulls harder on heavier things, but they are also harder to move, and the two effects cancel out.

What matters is the planet. The bigger and heavier it is, the higher its escape velocity. On the Moon it is only about 2.4 km per second, which is why the Apollo astronauts could blast off from it with a small rocket. On Jupiter, it is about 60 km per second.

Why not just fire a cannon?

In 1865, the French writer Jules Verne wrote about launching people to the Moon from a giant cannon in Florida. It makes a great story but terrible science. The air near the ground is thick. Anything moving at 11 km per second would heat up like a meteor and burn. And the sudden push would crush any passengers.

That's why rockets start slowly, climb out of the thick air first, and then speed up. They burn fuel for several minutes, gaining speed bit by bit.

India's clever way to the Moon

Chandrayaan-3, which landed near the Moon's south pole in August 2023, did not blast straight off at escape speed. Its rocket put it in a long oval orbit around Earth. Then the spacecraft fired its own small engine several times at the closest point of each loop, stretching the orbit bigger and bigger, like a child pumping higher on a swing.

Finally, one last burn flung it towards the Moon. The trip took about 40 days instead of three, but it saved a lot of fuel. Small, carefully timed pushes added up to the speed it needed.

Faster than light

Some objects have escape velocities that are almost beyond belief. To leave the surface of the Sun, you would need about 618 km per second. And for a black hole, the escape velocity is faster than light itself, about 300,000 km per second. Since nothing can go that fast, nothing that falls in ever gets out. Not even the light, which is why it looks black.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Once a rocket gets high enough, there is no gravity in space.

Truth: Gravity reaches out forever, getting weaker with distance. Astronauts on the space station feel weightless because they are falling around Earth all the time, while gravity there is still about 90 per cent as strong as on the ground.

Try this

Throw a soft ball straight up gently, then harder, and count how many seconds it takes to come down each time. Every extra bit of speed buys more time in the air. Escape velocity is the speed at which the ball would never come back at all.
