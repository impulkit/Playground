Why is Michael Jordan a legend?

He was left off his school's top team as a 15-year-old. He went on to win six NBA titles and turn a basketball shoe into a worldwide brand.

The short answer

Michael Jordan is often called the greatest basketball player of all time. He won six NBA championships with the Chicago Bulls in the 1990s, and was named the most valuable player of the Finals all six times. His career scoring average of about 30 points a game is the highest in NBA history.

He was famous for flying dunks, clutch shots in the last seconds and a fierce will to win. His Nike shoe, the Air Jordan, became one of the best-selling sports products ever. To many people around the world in the 1990s, he was the face of America's NBA.

Surprising but true

At the height of his fame, Michael Jordan left basketball to play minor-league baseball for a season, then came back and won three more NBA titles.

Not good enough, yet

Jordan was born in Brooklyn, New York, in 1963 and grew up in Wilmington, North Carolina. At Laney High School, at 15, he tried out for the varsity team and was sent to junior varsity instead. Coaches wanted a taller player.

He went home upset and then practised harder. He grew taller too, and made the varsity team the next year. In 1982, as a first-year student at the University of North Carolina, he hit the winning shot in the national college championship final.

Air Jordan

The Chicago Bulls picked him third in the 1984 draft. That year he also won Olympic gold in Los Angeles. In 1985 Nike launched his shoe, the Air Jordan. At first the NBA objected to its red-and-black colours, and the story of Jordan being fined for wearing it became part of the shoe's legend.

People called him "Air Jordan" because of his leaps. He seemed to hang in the air, though gravity pulls on everyone the same way. His jumps simply lasted a little longer, and he could change hands or twist his body before landing.

Six rings

For years Jordan scored huge numbers of points, but the Bulls could not win the championship. Under coach Phil Jackson, with teammate Scottie Pippen, they finally won in 1991, then again in 1992 and 1993.

In 1992 Jordan played on the US Olympic "Dream Team" in Barcelona, with Magic Johnson and Larry Bird. They won every game by big margins, and children around the world wanted to play basketball. It was the first Olympics where NBA professionals were allowed to play, and opponents sometimes asked the Americans for photos after losing to them.

A season of baseball

In October 1993, after his father was killed in a robbery, Jordan retired from basketball. He then played minor-league baseball for the Birmingham Barons, a dream his father had shared. He worked hard but struggled as a hitter.

In March 1995 he returned to the Bulls with a two-word message: "I'm back." The Bulls won 72 of 82 games in 1995–96, a record at the time. They went on to win three more titles from 1996 to 1998. In the 1997 Finals he played while sick with what was called flu and still scored 38 points.

Life after the Bulls

Jordan retired again in 1999, returned to play for the Washington Wizards from 2001 to 2003, then stopped for good. For more than a decade he was the main owner of the Charlotte Hornets, an NBA team in his home state.

He has been criticised at times for being very harsh on teammates. A 2020 documentary series, The Last Dance, showed both his drive and his toughness, and introduced him to a new generation who had never seen him play live.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Michael Jordan was cut from his high school team.

Truth: He was not thrown off basketball. As a 15-year-old he was left out of the varsity team and played for the junior varsity side instead, where he was the star.

Try this

Test your "hang time". Stand next to a wall, reach up and mark the highest point with a sticky note. Then jump and touch the wall as high as you can, with an adult watching. Measure the difference. Most top basketball players can jump 70 centimetres or more.
