How are twins made?

Identical twins begin as one tiny ball of cells that splits in two. Non-identical twins start as two separate eggs on the same day.

The short answer

A baby starts when one egg cell from the mother joins with one sperm cell from the father. That single cell divides again and again, becoming a tiny ball of cells that grows into a baby. Twins happen in two different ways.

Sometimes, in the first days, that tiny ball of cells splits into two. Each half grows into a whole baby. These are identical twins. They come from the same egg and sperm, so they share the same genes. Other times, the mother releases two eggs at once, and each one is joined by a different sperm. Two separate babies grow side by side. These are non-identical twins, also called fraternal twins. They are as alike as any brother and sister, just born on the same day.

Surprising but true

The nine-banded armadillo of the Americas almost always gives birth to four identical babies, all grown from one split egg.

Same genes, different people

Identical twins are always both boys or both girls, and they usually look very alike. Even so, they aren't copies. Their fingerprints differ. One may be left-handed and the other right-handed. They can have different heights, likes and personalities.

That's because genes aren't the only thing that shapes a person. Small differences in the womb, different experiences and pure chance all play a part. Some identical twins are mirror images. One twin's hair may swirl clockwise and the other's anticlockwise, or a birthmark may sit on opposite sides.

Twin towns

Rates of non-identical twins vary around the world. They are highest in parts of central Africa, and in Nigeria among the Yoruba people. The town of Igbo-Ora in Nigeria calls itself the twin capital of the world. Non-identical twins are less common in East Asia.

In India, the village of Kodinhi in Malappuram district, Kerala, has become famous for having many more twins than usual. Scientists have visited to study it, but nobody yet knows why. Identical twins are different: they turn up at about the same rate everywhere, roughly 3 or 4 in every 1,000 births.

What makes Kerala unique?Kerala is the first place in India to feel the monsoon each year, and its pepper drew Roman ships across the sea about 2,000 years ago.

More twins than before

Twin births have become more common over the last 50 years in many countries, including the USA. One reason is that more women are having babies when they're older, and older mothers are more likely to release two eggs at once. Another is fertility treatment, which can lead to more than one baby.

Triplets, quadruplets and more can also happen, from extra eggs, extra splits or both. Nine-banded armadillos, which live from South America to the southern USA, almost always have four identical babies from one split egg. The ancient Greeks named the constellation Gemini, meaning twins, after the twin brothers Castor and Pollux, and you can find it among the constellations on winter nights.

Twins and science

Because identical twins share genes, scientists study them to learn what genes do and what the world around us does. If identical twins are much more alike in something than non-identical twins, genes probably play a big part.

In 2015, astronaut Scott Kelly spent nearly a year on the International Space Station, while his identical twin, Mark, also an astronaut, stayed on Earth. NASA scientists compared their bodies before, during and after. They found changes in Scott's genes' activity, his eyes and his gut microbes, most of which went back to normal on Earth.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Twins always skip a generation.

Truth: There's no such rule. A tendency to release two eggs at once can run in families through mothers, which makes non-identical twins more likely. Identical twins mostly happen by chance and don't run in families.

Try this

Ask your class or your extended family how many twins they know, and whether each pair is identical or non-identical, a boy-girl pair or two boys or two girls. Make a tally chart. Boy-girl pairs are always non-identical, so see how many of each kind you find.
