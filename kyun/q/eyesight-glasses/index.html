Why do some people need glasses?

Scientists estimate that by 2050 about half the people on Earth could be short-sighted. Spending time outdoors seems to help protect children's eyes.

The short answer

You see clearly when light entering your eye is focused sharply on the retina, the light-sensing layer at the back. The cornea at the front and the lens just behind it do the focusing, a bit like the lens in a camera.

If the eye is a little too long or too short, or if the cornea is curved unevenly, the light focuses in the wrong place and the picture is blurry. Glasses add an extra lens in front of the eye that bends the light by just the right amount, so the image lands sharply on the retina again. Doctors call these focusing problems refractive errors, and they are very common.

Surprising but true

The earliest spectacles appeared in northern Italy around 1286, but they only helped with reading. Glasses for seeing far away came well over a century later.

Three common problems

In short-sightedness, or myopia, the eyeball is usually slightly too long. Nearby things look clear, but the board at school or a bus number looks fuzzy. Glasses with lenses thinner in the middle fix it.

In long-sightedness, or hyperopia, the eye is slightly too short, so close-up things are harder to focus on. Lenses thicker in the middle help. In astigmatism, the cornea is curved more in one direction than the other, a bit like the surface of a rugby ball or an American football instead of a round ball. Some lines then look sharper than others, and glasses with specially shaped lenses correct it.

After about the age of 40, almost everyone's lens gets stiffer. That's why many grandparents hold menus at arm's length and need reading glasses.

How we test eyes

In 1862, the Dutch eye doctor Herman Snellen designed a chart with rows of letters getting smaller. Versions of it still hang in clinics around the world. "20/20 vision", a phrase used in the USA, means you can read at 20 feet what a typical eye can read at 20 feet. In metric countries the same thing is written 6/6, for 6 metres.

Children don't always know their eyes are blurry, because they think everyone sees that way. School eye checks are one way of catching problems early.

The invention of spectacles

The first glasses were made in northern Italy around 1286. A friar from Pisa, preaching in Florence in 1306, said that the art of making them had been discovered less than 20 years earlier. Those early glasses used lenses thicker in the middle, so they helped older people read. Lenses for short-sighted people appeared in the 1400s.

Lens-making skill also led to telescopes. In 1609, Galileo built his own and turned it to the sky.

Why are more children short-sighted?

Short-sightedness has become much more common in recent decades, especially in cities. In parts of East Asia, the large majority of young adults are now short-sighted. A 2016 study estimated that about half of the world's population could be myopic by 2050.

Genes play a part, since short-sighted parents are more likely to have short-sighted children. But the fast rise points to the way children live. Studies, including school trials in China and Taiwan, suggest that spending more time outdoors in daylight lowers children's risk. Long hours of close-up work, such as reading and screens, may add to it.

If the board looks blurry or you often squint or get headaches, tell a parent or teacher. An eye test is quick and painless, and glasses can make an immediate difference.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Reading in dim light or sitting close to the TV will permanently damage your eyes.

Truth: These can tire your eyes and give you a headache, but there's no good evidence they cause lasting damage. Children often sit close to screens because they can focus up close easily, or because they are short-sighted and need an eye test.

Try this

Fill a clear, smooth glass with water and look at a printed page through it from the side. Move it closer and further away. The curved water acts like a lens and makes the letters bigger, smaller or upside down, just like the lenses used in glasses.
