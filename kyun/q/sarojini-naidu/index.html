Who was Sarojini Naidu?

She wrote poems that made readers in London sit up, then swapped verse for the salt march and a jail cell.

The short answer

Sarojini Naidu was a poet and one of the best-known leaders of India's freedom struggle. Her poems, written in English, were so musical that people called her the Nightingale of India.

She later put much of her writing aside to work with Gandhi. She gave fiery speeches across India and abroad, marched in the Salt Satyagraha, and went to prison. She became the first Indian woman to lead the Indian National Congress, and after independence the first woman to govern an Indian state.

Surprising but true

Sarojini Naidu passed the matriculation exam of Madras University when she was just 12.

A child genius in Hyderabad

Sarojini Chattopadhyay was born in Hyderabad in 1879, the eldest of eight children. Her father, Aghorenath, was a scientist who helped found the city's Nizam College. Her mother wrote poetry in Bengali.

Sarojini was quick. She passed her matriculation exam at 12 and wrote a long poem at 13. The Nizam of Hyderabad gave her a scholarship to study in England, at King's College London and then Girton College, Cambridge.

Poems of India

In England, the critic Edmund Gosse read her early poems, which copied English styles about robins and snow. He told her to write about India instead: its temples, markets and people.

She took the advice. Her first book, The Golden Threshold, came out in London in 1905 and was named after her family home. Her poems described bangle sellers, palanquin bearers and the spice and silver of Hyderabad's bazaars. They were full of rhythm and colour, meant to be read aloud.

At 19 she married Dr Govindarajulu Naidu, a doctor from another region and community. Marriages across communities were rare at the time and drew disapproval. The couple went ahead anyway and had four children.

Into the struggle

Meetings with leaders such as Gopal Krishna Gokhale and Gandhi drew her into politics. She travelled across India speaking about freedom and about women's education and rights. In 1925 she was elected president of the Congress.

In 1930, Gandhi began his march to the sea to make salt in defiance of British law. After he was arrested, Naidu led a protest at the Dharasana salt works in Gujarat. Hundreds of volunteers walked forward, unarmed, towards police lines. Reports of their courage spread around the world.

She was jailed several times. She also went with Gandhi to London in 1931 for the Round Table Conference, where Indian leaders and the British government discussed India's future. The talks failed, but her speeches won many friends for India's cause.

Wit and warmth

Naidu was famous for her humour. She was one of the few people who teased Gandhi openly, and she nicknamed him "Mickey Mouse" because of his big ears. Gandhi enjoyed the joke and wrote her warm, playful letters in return.

She also spoke up for women's right to vote and take part in public life. She was part of a delegation in 1917 asking the British for votes for Indian women, part of a worldwide fight for the vote.

Governor and nightingale

When India became free in 1947, she was made governor of the United Provinces, now Uttar Pradesh. She died in office in Lucknow on 2 March 1949.

Her birthday, 13 February, is marked as National Women's Day in India. Her old home in Hyderabad, The Golden Threshold, now belongs to the University of Hyderabad, and students study there.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Sarojini Naidu was India's first woman Prime Minister or President.

Truth: She never held either job. She was the first woman to be governor of an Indian state, the United Provinces (now Uttar Pradesh), from 1947 until her death in 1949.
