Which is the loneliest island in the world?

On Tristan da Cunha, fewer than 300 people live on a volcano in the South Atlantic, and the nearest town is a seven-day boat ride away.

The short answer

The usual answer is Tristan da Cunha, a volcanic island in the South Atlantic Ocean. Fewer than 300 people live there, in one village called Edinburgh of the Seven Seas. The nearest place with people is Saint Helena, about 2,430 km away, roughly the straight-line distance from Delhi to Kanyakumari. South Africa is about 2,800 km away.

There is no airport. Supply and fishing ships come from Cape Town a few times a year, and the trip takes around a week. If you are counting islands where nobody lives, the loneliest of all is Bouvet Island, a frozen speck far to the south.

Surprising but true

In 1961, Tristan's volcano erupted beside the only village, and the whole population was taken to England. Most chose to go home two years later.

Life at the edge of the map

Tristan is a single round volcano rising out of the sea, with a peak called Queen Mary's Peak at about 2,060 m. The village sits on the only flat strip of land, on the north coast.

The islanders grow potatoes in patches outside the village, raise sheep and cattle, and catch crawfish, a kind of lobster that is the island's main export. Money from selling stamps and coins with Tristan's name also helps. There is a school, a small hospital, a shop, a pub and a church.

Many islanders share just a few family names, handed down from the families who settled there in the 1800s. Everyone knows everyone. It is part of the United Kingdom's overseas territories.

The volcano wakes up

In October 1961, the ground started to crack and rumble close to the village. A new volcano vent opened just behind the houses. The islanders were taken off by boat and eventually brought to England.

They lived near Southampton for nearly two years, in a very different, crowded world of cars and shops. Many were unhappy. In 1963, when scientists said it was safe, most of them voted to go home. They found their houses mostly unharmed, though lava had buried their old fish factory.

What makes a volcano erupt?It's a bit like opening a shaken bottle of soda. A very, very hot one.

Bouvet: nobody home

Bouvet Island, which belongs to Norway, lies in the South Atlantic about 1,700 km from the coast of Antarctica. Over nine-tenths of it is covered by a glacier, and it has no harbour. It is often called the most remote island on Earth.

Only scientists and a few expeditions have set foot on it, often by helicopter. Seals and penguins are the main residents. In 1964, a mysterious abandoned lifeboat was found on the island, and nobody ever found out where it came from.

Lonely places closer to home

India has remote islands too. The Andaman and Nicobar Islands lie about 1,200 km from the mainland, closer to Myanmar and Indonesia than to Chennai. Lakshadweep's coral islands sit far out in the Arabian Sea.

The Pacific is full of lonely islands. Easter Island, famous for its giant stone heads, is over 2,000 km from the nearest inhabited island. Pitcairn Island, home to fewer than 50 people, has one of the smallest populations of any territory in the world.

The loneliest point of all

In the South Pacific lies Point Nemo, the spot in the ocean farthest from any land, about 2,700 km from the nearest shore. Space agencies even steer old satellites to crash into the ocean nearby, because nobody lives there.

Here's the strange part. When the International Space Station passes overhead, its crew about 400 km up are often the nearest humans to Point Nemo. The loneliest people on Earth's map may be the ones living on the space station, not on any island at all.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The most remote spot in the ocean must be close to an island somewhere.

Truth: The point of ocean farthest from any land, called Point Nemo in the South Pacific, is about 2,700 km from the nearest shore. The closest humans to it are often astronauts on the space station passing overhead about 400 km up.
