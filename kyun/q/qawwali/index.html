What is qawwali?

A qawwali can begin with a quiet hum and end, an hour later, with the whole crowd clapping, swaying and singing along.

The short answer

Qawwali is a form of devotional music from South Asia, sung by Sufis, Muslims who seek a deep, personal love of God. It is usually performed by a group: one or two lead singers, a chorus that sings back and claps, a harmonium player and a drummer on tabla or dholak.

The songs are poems of love and longing for God, or praise for the Prophet Muhammad and for Sufi saints. A qawwali starts slowly and gently, then builds through repetition, louder clapping and faster rhythm until it reaches a powerful climax. The word comes from the Arabic qaul, meaning a saying. For centuries it has been sung at Sufi shrines in India, Pakistan and Bangladesh, and today it fills concert halls around the world.

Surprising but true

A qawwali singer may repeat a single line dozens of times, changing it a little each time, to lift listeners into a feeling of closeness to God.

Born in Delhi

Qawwali traces its roots to Delhi around 700 years ago, during the time of the Delhi Sultanate. The poet and musician Amir Khusrau was a devoted follower of the Sufi saint Nizamuddin Auliya. Tradition says Khusrau blended Persian, Arabic and Indian music into a new style, and wrote many songs still sung today, such as "Chhap Tilak".

Historians point out that Sufis had held music gatherings, called sama, before Khusrau's time. So qawwali probably grew slowly, with Khusrau as one of its most important shapers. Either way, Nizamuddin's shrine in Delhi is still one of the best places to hear it, especially on Thursday evenings.

How a qawwali builds

A qawwali often begins with an unaccompanied verse, sung freely without a beat. Then the harmonium picks up the tune, the drums come in and the chorus starts clapping. The lead singer takes one line of the poem and repeats it many times, stretching words and decorating the melody.

Each repetition pushes a little higher and a little faster. Listeners call out in appreciation, and some may sway or weep. The aim is to bring the audience into a state of spiritual joy, sometimes called wajd, a kind of ecstasy. In a shrine, people offer money to the singers as a sign of respect.

The words

Qawwali poetry is full of images of love: the lover and the beloved, wine, the moth drawn to a candle. For Sufis, these stand for the soul's love of God. The poems are sung in Urdu, Persian, Punjabi, Hindi, the old Braj language and other tongues. Many were written by famous poets such as Khusrau, Bulleh Shah and Rumi.

Because the lead singer chooses which lines to repeat and how, no two performances of a song are quite the same.

Qawwali goes global

In the 1980s and 1990s, the Pakistani singer Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan took qawwali to audiences across Europe, America and Japan. He worked with Western musicians and film-makers, and people who understood none of the words found themselves moved by his voice. The Sabri Brothers, also from Pakistan, were another famous group.

Hindi films have used qawwali for decades. A.R. Rahman composed "Kun Faya Kun" for the 2011 film Rockstar, and its video was filmed at the Nizamuddin shrine. In the United States, qawwali groups now perform at music festivals and universities.

Music that brings people together

At many Sufi shrines, Hindus, Sikhs, Christians and Muslims sit side by side to listen. The harmonium and tabla are the same instruments heard at a bhajan or a classical concert. Many listeners, of every faith or none, say that qawwali speaks to them even when they cannot follow every word.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Qawwali is just a style of Bollywood film song.

Truth: Qawwali is a centuries-old Sufi devotional tradition sung at shrines. Films borrowed it, and many film qawwalis are loved, but the form is much older than cinema.

Try this

Try call and response with friends or family. One person sings or chants a short line, and everyone else repeats it, clapping on the beat. Each time, the leader changes one small thing. See how long you can keep it going and how the energy changes.
