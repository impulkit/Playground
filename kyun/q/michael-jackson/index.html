Why was Michael Jackson called the King of Pop?

Michael Jackson patented a special shoe so he could lean forward at an impossible angle on stage without falling flat on his face.

The short answer

Michael Jackson was called the King of Pop because, in the 1980s, nobody sold more records, filled more stadiums or changed pop music videos as much. His album Thriller (1982) is often called the best-selling album of all time. He sang, wrote songs and danced with moves that millions of children tried to copy in their living rooms.

The nickname stuck after the actress Elizabeth Taylor called him the true king of pop, rock and soul in 1989. His music is still played at weddings, parties and dance classes from Ohio to Odisha.

His life was also troubled, and parts of it remain painful and disputed.

Surprising but true

In 1993 Michael Jackson and two designers were granted a US patent for a shoe that hooked onto a peg in the stage floor, letting him lean far forward.

A child star

Jackson was born in 1958 in Gary, Indiana, an industrial city in the USA. He was the eighth of ten children. With four of his brothers he formed the Jackson 5, who signed with Motown Records and had four number-one hits in 1969 and 1970.

He was only about 11, but already the lead singer. Later he spoke about how hard his childhood had been, with long hours of rehearsals and a strict father.

Thriller and MTV

As a solo singer, Jackson released Thriller in 1982 with the producer Quincy Jones. Seven of its songs became top-10 hits in the USA. Its sales are usually estimated at more than 60 million copies, though exact totals are hard to check.

Music television had just started. Jackson made videos that were like short films, with stories, costumes and big dance scenes. The "Thriller" video, with dancing zombies, lasted about 14 minutes. At the time, few Black artists were shown on MTV. His videos broke through and helped open the door for others, a small chapter in the long story of civil rights in American entertainment. Within a few years, a big-budget music video had become something every pop star wanted.

The moonwalk

In 1983, on a TV special, Jackson performed "Billie Jean" and suddenly glided backwards while seeming to walk forwards. Viewers were stunned. The step, which he called the moonwalk, depends on smooth shoes and careful control of friction: one foot slides while the other grips.

He kept inventing. For the "Smooth Criminal" lean, he helped design shoes that hooked onto pegs in the stage floor. He received a US patent for them in 1993.

India and the world

Jackson toured the world many times. In November 1996 he performed in Mumbai, his only concert in India. Thousands of fans travelled from across the country.

His influence reached Indian films too. The dancer and choreographer Prabhu Deva was often nicknamed "India's Michael Jackson" for his fast, rubbery moves. Many Bollywood dances of the 1980s and 1990s borrowed his spins, kicks and sharp freezes.

A complicated life

Jackson's appearance changed a lot over the years, and newspapers wrote about his private life constantly. From 1993 he faced serious accusations of harming children. He denied them, and at a trial in 2005 a jury found him not guilty. After his death, new accusations were made in a 2019 documentary, and his family and fans disputed them. People still disagree strongly.

He died in Los Angeles in 2009, aged 50, from an overdose of a medicine given by his doctor, who was later convicted. His songs remain among the most played in the world.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Michael Jackson invented the moonwalk.

Truth: Dancers had performed similar gliding backslides for decades, including Cab Calloway in the 1930s. Jackson made the move world-famous when he performed it on TV in 1983 and gave it the name most people use.

Try this

In socks on a smooth floor, try a simple backslide. Keep one foot flat and slide it back while the toes of the other foot press down, then switch. It feels like walking forwards while you glide backwards, the illusion behind the moonwalk.
