Who was Jules Verne?

Jules Verne's Captain Nemo, the mysterious commander of the submarine Nautilus, turns out to be an Indian prince who fought the British in 1857.

The short answer

Jules Verne was a French writer who lived from 1828 to 1905. He wrote adventure novels packed with science and travel, and he's often called one of the fathers of science fiction.

His characters go under the sea in a submarine, deep into the Earth through a volcano, around the world against the clock, and to the Moon in a giant shell. Verne studied science books, maps and engineering journals to make his adventures feel believable, even when they were impossible.

His books have been translated into dozens of languages. Long lists of the world's most translated authors put him near the very top.

Surprising but true

In 1865, Jules Verne wrote about three men fired to the Moon from Florida. Just over a century later, three real astronauts launched from Florida and became the first humans to fly around it.

A boy from a harbour town

Verne grew up in Nantes, a busy port on the Loire river in western France. He watched sailing ships come and go and dreamed of faraway places. A family story says he once tried to sneak away as a ship's boy, but was caught and brought home.

His father was a lawyer and wanted his son to be one too. Verne studied law in Paris, but spent his time writing plays and reading about geography and science instead.

In 1863 a publisher, Pierre-Jules Hetzel, printed Five Weeks in a Balloon, about a journey across Africa by hot-air balloon. It was a hit. Hetzel signed Verne up to write novels for years to come.

Voyages Extraordinaires

Verne and Hetzel called the series the Voyages Extraordinaires, "extraordinary journeys". Over 40 years, Verne wrote more than 50 novels for it. Most came out first in parts in a magazine, then as handsome books full of detailed engravings.

In Journey to the Centre of the Earth (1864), a professor and his nephew climb down an Icelandic volcano and find an underground sea with prehistoric beasts. Scientists now know what's really inside the Earth: hot rock and metal, no ocean. In Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas (1870), Captain Nemo explores the oceans in his electric submarine, the Nautilus.

How did people walk on the Moon?In July 1969, two astronauts stepped onto the Moon. Their footprints are still there.

Captain Nemo's secret

Nemo means "nobody" in Latin. In Twenty Thousand Leagues he's a mystery, a brilliant man who hates empires. Verne reveals the secret in a later book, The Mysterious Island (1875).

Nemo is Prince Dakkar, son of an Indian raja from Bundelkhand. After the revolt of 1857, in which he fought the British, he escaped and built the Nautilus to live free beneath the sea.

Racing around the world

In Around the World in Eighty Days (1872), the English gentleman Phileas Fogg bets he can circle the globe in 80 days. He crosses India by train and, when a railway line turns out to be unfinished, by elephant.

The book inspired real races. In 1889, the American journalist Nellie Bly set out to beat Fogg's time. She stopped in France to meet Verne on the way and finished in 72 days.

Did he predict the future?

Verne didn't invent the submarine. Real ones existed before him. But his From the Earth to the Moon (1865) had three men launched from Florida. In 1968, the three astronauts of Apollo 8 launched from Florida too, and became the first humans to circle the Moon. Their capsule, like Verne's, splashed down in the Pacific.

One early novel, Paris in the Twentieth Century, imagined glass towers, petrol-driven cars and a worldwide telegraph network. Hetzel turned it down, and it wasn't published until 1994. Still, Verne wasn't really trying to predict anything. He wanted readers to be thrilled by science and curious about the world. Many engineers, explorers and astronauts have said his books made them want to go and see for themselves.

Myth or fact?

Myth: In Around the World in Eighty Days, Phileas Fogg travels by hot-air balloon.

Truth: There's no balloon journey in the book. Fogg uses trains, steamships, an elephant and even a sled with a sail. The balloon was added by a famous 1956 film and has stuck in people's minds ever since.
