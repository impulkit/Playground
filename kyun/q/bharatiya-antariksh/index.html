What is the Bharatiya Antariksh Station?

India plans to launch the first piece of its own space station in 2028 and finish building it in orbit by 2035.

The short answer

The Bharatiya Antariksh Station, or BAS, is India's plan for its own space station: a home and laboratory circling Earth where Indian astronauts can live and work for weeks at a time. "Antariksh" means space in Hindi and Sanskrit.

India's Cabinet approved the first stage in September 2024. ISRO plans to launch the first module, BAS-1, in 2028, and to complete a five-module station by 2035. If it happens, India would join the USA, Russia and China as one of only a few countries to run a space station of its own.

Surprising but true

The full station is planned to weigh about 52 tonnes, roughly as much as a dozen Indian elephants, and every piece of it has to be carried up by rocket.

Built in pieces

A space station is too heavy to launch in one go. The plan is to send up five modules, one at a time, starting with ISRO's most powerful rocket today, the LVM3. Each one will be joined to the others while they orbit Earth at about 28,000 km per hour.

When finished, BAS should weigh around 52 tonnes and circle about 400–450 km above the ground. That is about as far up as Goa is from Mumbai along the coast. The International Space Station, which has been visited by astronauts from many countries since 2000, is much bigger, at about 420 tonnes. BAS is meant to be smaller, simpler and fully Indian-run.

In August 2025, ISRO showed a model of the first module at an exhibition in New Delhi for National Space Day. It was the first time the public could see what the station might look like.

First, a ride to space

Before anyone can live on BAS, India needs a way to get astronauts there and back. That is the job of Gaganyaan, India's crewed spacecraft. ISRO has been testing its crew capsule, escape system and parachutes, and plans uncrewed flights first, including one carrying a humanoid robot called Vyommitra.

In 2025, Indian Air Force pilot Shubhanshu Shukla flew to the International Space Station on a private American mission. He spent about 18 days there and did experiments for ISRO, gaining experience that will feed into Gaganyaan and BAS.

What happens to the body in space?Astronauts come home a few centimetres taller, with weaker bones and puffy faces, and some have to relearn how to walk.

What will astronauts do there?

A space station is a laboratory where gravity seems to vanish. Scientists call this microgravity. In it, flames burn in round balls, water forms floating spheres and plants grow their roots in strange directions.

Indian astronauts on BAS are expected to study how the human body changes in space, test new materials and medicines, and grow plants. Long stays help doctors learn how to protect astronauts' bones and muscles, which weaken without gravity to work against. That knowledge matters for any future trip to the Moon or Mars.

Why India wants its own

Running a space station is hard and costly. Supplies, water and spare parts must be sent up regularly, and the crew must be brought home safely. Some people ask whether the money would be better spent on other things. Supporters argue that the station will train Indian scientists and engineers, build new industries, and let India carry out experiments on its own timetable.

The International Space Station is expected to be retired around 2030. After that, the main stations in orbit may be China's Tiangong, private stations being built by American companies, and, if all goes to plan, BAS.

The long road

ISRO began with small rockets launched from a church in Thumba, Kerala, in the 1960s, under Vikram Sarabhai. Six decades later, it has landed on the Moon and is planning a home in orbit. Space projects often run late, and BAS may too. In January 2026, ISRO invited Indian companies to help build the first module, so the work has already moved from drawings to hardware.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Astronauts on a space station float because there is no gravity up there.

Truth: At 400 km up, gravity is still about 90% as strong as on the ground. Astronauts float because the station and everyone in it are falling around Earth together, all the time.
