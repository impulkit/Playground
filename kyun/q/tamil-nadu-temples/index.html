Why does Tamil Nadu have such tall temple towers?

Some temple gateways in Tamil Nadu are taller than a 20-storey building, and every level is crowded with painted gods, animals and dancers.

The short answer

Tamil Nadu's temple towers are part of a building style called Dravidian architecture. The tallest are usually gopurams, gateway towers over the entrances. Over many centuries, kings and communities kept expanding their favourite temples, adding new walls around the old ones. Each new wall got a bigger, grander gateway than the last.

The towers had several jobs. They marked the boundary of sacred ground. They could be seen from far across flat farmland, guiding pilgrims in. And they showed the power and generosity of whoever paid for them. Their steps are covered with thousands of painted figures of gods, goddesses, animals and stories, so a tower is also a giant picture book.

Surprising but true

In older Tamil temples the tallest tower stands over the shrine, but later the gateway towers grew so big that they dwarfed the shrine inside.

The great temple of Thanjavur

Around the year 1010, the Chola king Rajaraja I completed the Brihadeeswarar Temple at Thanjavur. Its main tower, called a vimana, rises about 66 m over the shrine of Shiva. For its time, it was one of the tallest buildings in India, made mostly of granite, a very hard stone.

At the top sits a massive capstone. A popular story says it was hauled up a long earth ramp, but no clear evidence for the ramp has been found. Some experts think scaffolding and earth slopes were used together, while others suggest ramps built around the tower itself. The temple is still in use, a thousand years later. With two other Chola temples, it forms a UNESCO World Heritage Site called the Great Living Chola Temples.

When gateways grew taller than shrines

In the Chola temples, the tallest tower sat over the shrine. Later, from about the 1200s onwards, things changed. Rulers including the Pandyas, the kings of Vijayanagara and the Nayakas who governed for them poured money into gateways instead.

Temples grew outwards in rings, like the layers of an onion. Each ring had high walls and four gopurams, one on each side. The outermost gopurams became the tallest of all, while the old shrine in the middle stayed comparatively small. Walking in, you pass through gateway after gateway, from the noisy street towards the quiet centre.

Madurai and Srirangam

The Meenakshi Temple in Madurai has 14 gopurams, the tallest about 52 m high. They're covered with brightly painted figures, and are repainted from time to time during big renovation ceremonies. Inside, a hall of carved pillars and a large temple tank draw crowds every day.

At Srirangam, near Tiruchirappalli, the Ranganathaswamy Temple is one of the largest working temple complexes in India, with seven rings of walls. Its main gateway, the Rajagopuram, was left unfinished for centuries and only completed in 1987. It rises about 73 m, roughly as tall as a 20-storey building.

Built to last, and to be used

Gopurams are mostly brick and plaster on a stone base, which kept them lighter than solid stone. Their stepped shape, wide at the bottom and narrow at the top, makes them steady, like a pyramid. The upper levels are often hollow inside. At the very top runs a barrel-shaped roof lined with a row of pot-shaped finials, called kalasams, which catch the sunlight from far away.

These temples are living places. Musicians play, priests carry out daily rituals, and festivals pull huge wooden chariots through the streets. Many famous bronze statues were made to be carried in such processions. Even the state's official emblem shows a gopuram, a sign of how closely the towers are linked to Tamil identity.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The huge stone on top of the Thanjavur temple was raised up a ramp 6 km long.

Truth: This story is popular and often repeated, but there is no clear evidence for it. Nobody knows exactly how the builders lifted the capstone, and historians and engineers have suggested several methods.

Try this

Build a stepped tower from blocks, books or matchboxes, making each level a little smaller than the one below. See how tall you can go before it wobbles. Then try making the bottom levels much wider, and notice how a broad base helps a tower stand tall.
