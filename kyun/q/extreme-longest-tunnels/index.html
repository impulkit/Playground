What are the longest tunnels in the world?

Under the Swiss Alps runs a railway tunnel 57 km long, with up to 2.3 km of solid mountain sitting above the trains.

The short answer

The longest tunnel on Earth is the Delaware Aqueduct, a water tunnel about 137 km long that feeds New York City. It was finished in 1945 and still supplies about half of the city's water.

For trains, the record belongs to the Gotthard Base Tunnel in Switzerland, at 57.1 km. For cars, it is the Laerdal Tunnel in Norway, at 24.5 km. And the Channel Tunnel between England and France runs about 38 km under the sea, the longest undersea stretch of any tunnel.

Surprising but true

The longest tunnel of all carries no trains or cars. It is a 137 km water pipe that keeps New York City's taps running.

A water pipe 137 km long

The Delaware Aqueduct carries water from mountain reservoirs north of New York down to the city. It is about 4 m wide, big enough to drive a car through, though nobody does. Water rushes through it day and night, more than a billion litres a day.

It has also leaked for decades, losing millions of litres every day through cracks. So engineers dug a new 4 km bypass tunnel under the Hudson River to go around the worst part.

Under 2 km of mountain

The Gotthard Base Tunnel took 17 years to build. Four giant tunnel boring machines, each about as long as four football fields, chewed through the Alps. They dug out about 28 million tonnes of rock. That is enough to build five copies of the Great Pyramid of Giza.

At its deepest, the tunnel lies about 2.3 km below the peaks. That deep, the Earth's heat pushed rock temperatures to about 45°C. Fast trains now cross the Alps in about 20 minutes inside the mountain, instead of climbing over it.

Giant metal worms

Most long tunnels today are dug by a tunnel boring machine, or TBM. The front is a spinning steel wheel studded with hard cutting discs. It grinds slowly into the rock while conveyor belts carry the broken stone backwards.

Right behind the cutter, the machine lines the new tunnel with curved concrete pieces, like fitting rings inside a giant pipe. A good day might mean 20 to 40 m of progress. In softer ground under cities, TBMs keep the soil from collapsing by pushing back against it with mud or foam.

Under the sea

Japan's Seikan Tunnel, 53.9 km long, links two islands under a stormy strait. It held the world record for years. The Channel Tunnel, opened in 1994, is slightly shorter at about 50 km, but more of it lies beneath the sea.

Eurostar trains pass through it in about 20 minutes, and shuttle trains carrying cars and trucks take about 35. When the two digging teams met in the middle in 1990, they were only about 36 cm apart, less than the length of a school ruler and a half.

India's tunnels in the mountains

India's own record tunnels are mostly in the Himalaya. The Atal Tunnel under the Rohtang Pass, opened in 2020, is about 9 km long. It is the world's longest highway tunnel above 10,000 feet (about 3,000 m).

The railway to Kashmir, finished in 2025, needed dozens of tunnels. The longest, called T-50, runs about 12.8 km between Khari and Sumber stations. The Pir Panjal tunnel nearby is about 11.2 km. An even longer railway tunnel, about 14.5 km, has been dug in Uttarakhand for the new line to Karnaprayag, which was still being finished in 2026.

Coming soon

The Brenner Base Tunnel between Austria and Italy will be about 55 km long. Joined to an existing tunnel near Innsbruck, it will give trains a 64 km underground run, longer than Gotthard. It is due in the 2030s.

Engineers are also building the Fehmarnbelt Tunnel between Denmark and Germany. It is made from giant concrete pieces, each longer than two football fields, sunk into a trench on the seabed. Tunnel builders and bridge builders race each other across the same seas, and some of the longest bridges stretch even farther than these tunnels.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Long tunnels are dangerous because the air inside runs out.

Truth: Big tunnels have powerful fans and air shafts that keep fresh air moving. Long railway tunnels like Gotthard and the Channel Tunnel also have escape passages and emergency stations every few kilometres.

Try this

Make a mini tunnel boring machine test. Pack damp sand firmly into a shoe box, then try to push a toilet roll tube through it sideways. Next, twist the tube as you push. The twisting tube is a tiny version of how a boring machine's spinning head cuts through rock.
