Who was Mother Teresa?

A schoolteacher from Europe walked out of her convent into the lanes of Kolkata, and spent almost 50 years caring for the poorest people there.

The short answer

Mother Teresa was a Catholic nun who spent her life caring for the sick, the dying and the very poor in Kolkata. She was born in Europe in 1910, came to India as a young teacher, and in 1950 started a group of nuns called the Missionaries of Charity.

Her sisters ran homes for people who had nowhere else to go, including those with leprosy and the dying. The group spread to more than 100 countries.

She won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1979 and India's highest civilian award, the Bharat Ratna, in 1980. After her death, the Catholic Church named her a saint.

Surprising but true

When she won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1979, Mother Teresa asked for the usual celebration banquet to be cancelled and the money given to the poor.

From Skopje to Kolkata

She was born Anjezë Gonxhe Bojaxhiu in Skopje, to an Albanian family. At 18 she joined the Sisters of Loreto, an order of Irish nuns, and took the name Teresa.

In 1929 she arrived in India. For almost 20 years she taught at a girls' school in Kolkata, then called Calcutta, and became its head. Outside the school walls, the city was struggling with famine, and later with the violence and refugees of Partition.

A "call within a call"

On 10 September 1946, she was on a train to Darjeeling. She later said she felt a call from God to leave the convent and live among the poor.

In 1948 she swapped her nun's robes for a simple white cotton sari with a blue border. She learned basic nursing and began teaching children in a slum, writing letters in the dirt with a stick.

What makes Kolkata special?Kolkata has India's oldest metro, a giant bridge held together by rivets instead of bolts, and a festival so grand that UNESCO honoured it.

Homes for the forgotten

In 1952 she opened Nirmal Hriday, a home for dying people, beside the Kalighat temple. People found on the street were brought in, washed, fed and allowed to die with someone holding their hand.

Her sisters later opened homes for children, people with leprosy and the elderly. When she died in 1997, India gave her a state funeral. In 2016, Pope Francis declared her Saint Teresa of Calcutta.

The Missionaries of Charity

In 1950, the Church allowed her to start her own order, the Missionaries of Charity. Its first members were mostly her former pupils. Besides the usual vows of nuns, they promised "wholehearted and free service to the poorest of the poor".

The sisters lived simply. They owned a few saris, a bucket for washing and little else, and they went out each day to find people in need. The headquarters, called Mother House, still stands on a busy road in central Kolkata.

The order grew fast. By the time of her death, thousands of sisters and brothers were working in more than 100 countries, running soup kitchens, orphanages and homes for people with HIV. Many volunteers from around the world, including students, still come to Kolkata to help in the homes for a few weeks.

Praise and questions

To millions, she was a symbol of selfless care. Yet her work also drew real criticism. Some doctors and journalists said her homes gave poor medical care and too little pain relief, even when money was available.

Others objected to her strong views against abortion and birth control, or to donations she accepted. Some in India worried that her work aimed at religious conversion, which her supporters denied. Like many famous people, she is remembered in more than one way, and readers can weigh the evidence for themselves.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Mother Teresa was born in India.

Truth: She was born in Skopje, in Europe, and came to India at 18 to teach. She chose India as her home, took Indian citizenship and is buried in Kolkata.
