What is the Vatican?

The Vatican is a whole country smaller than many college campuses, with its own stamps, flag, radio station and army of Swiss guards.

The short answer

The Vatican, or Vatican City, is the smallest country in the world. It covers about 0.44 square kilometres, roughly the area of 60 football fields, and sits inside the city of Rome in Italy, on the west bank of the river Tiber. It is the home of the pope, the leader of the Roman Catholic Church, which had about 1.4 billion members worldwide in 2023.

The Vatican has its own flag, stamps, coins, radio station and police. Fewer than 1,000 people live there, mostly priests, nuns and guards. But millions visit every year to see St Peter's Basilica, the Sistine Chapel and some of the most famous art on Earth.

Surprising but true

The Swiss Guard, the pope's soldiers, has protected popes since 1506. Its guards must be Swiss Catholic men, and they still carry long spear-axes called halberds.

Why a country so small

For over a thousand years, popes ruled a large area of central Italy called the Papal States. When Italy became one country in the 1800s, it took over those lands, including Rome, in 1870. The popes refused to accept it and stayed inside the Vatican for nearly 60 years.

In 1929, the Lateran Treaty between the pope and the Italian government settled the dispute. Italy recognised Vatican City as an independent country. The pope gave up any claim to the rest of the old Papal States.

St Peter's and the Sistine Chapel

St Peter's Basilica is one of the largest churches in the world. Catholics believe it stands over the burial place of Saint Peter, one of Jesus's twelve apostles and, in Catholic tradition, the first pope. Its great dome was designed by Michelangelo, who worked on it in old age, and the building took over a century to finish.

Next door is the Sistine Chapel. Between 1508 and 1512, Michelangelo painted its ceiling with scenes from the Bible, including the famous picture of God reaching out to touch Adam's finger. He painted standing on scaffolding, with his head tilted back, not lying down as many people think.

What was the Renaissance?In one Italian city, within a single lifetime, artists worked out how to paint depth, and an architect raised the largest brick dome ever built.

A country with a pope as king

The pope is the head of the Catholic Church and also the ruler of Vatican City. He is elected by cardinals, senior church leaders, in a secret meeting called a conclave held in the Sistine Chapel. The doors are locked and the cardinals vote again and again until one person gets two-thirds of the votes. A pope usually serves until he dies, though Benedict XVI chose to step down in 2013.

In May 2025, the cardinals elected Robert Prevost, born in Chicago, who took the name Leo XIV. He was the first pope from the United States, though he had spent many years working in Peru. The previous pope, Francis, came from Argentina and was the first from the Americas.

The Swiss Guard

The Vatican's small army is the Pontifical Swiss Guard, founded in 1506. There are about 135 guards. They must be unmarried when they join, Catholic, Swiss citizens and men between 19 and 30. On duty at the gates, they wear striped blue, red and yellow uniforms and carry halberds, a mix of spear and axe.

Behind the old uniforms, they are trained bodyguards. The colourful clothes are for ceremonies.

Treasures of the Vatican Museums

The Vatican Museums hold one of the world's great art collections, gathered by popes over centuries. There are ancient Roman and Egyptian statues, maps painted on walls, and rooms decorated by the painter Raphael. On a busy day, more than 20,000 people pass through, and a full walk through the galleries is several kilometres long.

The Vatican also has a library with ancient manuscripts and an observatory, with telescopes now in Arizona, US, where its astronomers study the stars.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Vatican is just a part of Rome, like a neighbourhood.

Truth: Vatican City sits inside Rome, but it is a separate country. It has its own government, laws, passports, post office and bank, and Italy has no power over it.
