Who are India's Olympic heroes?

India waited more than 60 years after independence for its first individual Olympic gold, then won another with a javelin throw of 87.58 metres.

The short answer

India's Olympic heroes include the hockey teams that won eight gold medals. Shooter Abhinav Bindra won India's first individual gold in 2008, and javelin thrower Neeraj Chopra became the first Indian to win gold in athletics. Wrestlers, boxers, badminton players, weightlifters and shooters have all added medals.

India has won about 41 medals at the Summer Olympics. That is few for a country of its size, but the number has grown fast. India won 7 medals at Tokyo, its best ever, and 6 in Paris in 2024.

Surprising but true

India's first individual medal after independence came from a wrestler, K. D. Jadhav, whose trip to the 1952 Olympics was paid for partly by donations from people in his home village.

Hockey's golden years

From 1928 to 1956, India's men's hockey team won six Olympic golds in a row, led at first by the great Dhyan Chand. They won again in 1964 and 1980.

The 1948 gold in London, won by beating Great Britain in the final, was India's first as an independent nation. For decades hockey was almost the only sport in which India won Olympic medals. After 1980 the team waited 41 years for another, a bronze at Tokyo.

The first individual medals

In 1952 in Helsinki, wrestler Khashaba Dadasaheb Jadhav won bronze, the first individual medal for independent India. He came from a village in Maharashtra and struggled to raise money for the trip.

India then waited 44 years for another individual medal. Leander Paes won bronze in tennis at Atlanta in 1996. In 2000 in Sydney, Karnam Malleswari became the first Indian woman to win an Olympic medal, a bronze in weightlifting. In Athens in 2004, shooter Rajyavardhan Singh Rathore won silver in double trap, independent India's first individual silver.

Near misses

Two of India's best-loved athletes never won an Olympic medal. At Rome in 1960, Milkha Singh, the "Flying Sikh", finished fourth in the 400 metres. At Los Angeles in 1984, P. T. Usha finished fourth in the 400 metres hurdles, just one hundredth of a second behind the bronze medallist.

Both went on to inspire generations of runners, and their stories are still told in films and schoolbooks.

Gold at last

On 11 August 2008 in Beijing, Abhinav Bindra won the 10 metre air rifle event, India's first individual gold. London 2012 brought six medals, including bronzes for boxer Mary Kom and badminton player Saina Nehwal.

In Rio in 2016, India's only medals came from two women: badminton player P. V. Sindhu won silver and wrestler Sakshi Malik won bronze. Sindhu added a bronze in Tokyo, becoming the first Indian woman with two individual Olympic medals.

In Tokyo, at Games held in 2021, weightlifter Mirabai Chanu won silver on the first day. On 7 August, Neeraj Chopra threw the javelin 87.58 metres to win gold. A javelin flies furthest when it leaves the hand at the right angle and speed, a contest between the thrower's strength and gravity.

Paris and beyond

At Paris 2024, shooter Manu Bhaker won two bronze medals, one alone and one in a mixed team event with Sarabjot Singh. She became the first Indian to win two medals at a single Olympics since independence. Neeraj won silver, the hockey team won bronze, and wrestler Aman Sehrawat and shooter Swapnil Kusale added bronzes.

India's athletes also shine at the Paralympics, where India won 29 medals in Paris. With better training centres and support, many Indian fans hope the next generation will turn fourth places into podiums.

Myth or fact?

Myth: India has never won an Olympic gold medal in athletics.

Truth: Neeraj Chopra won the javelin gold at the Tokyo Olympics in August 2021, India's first ever gold in athletics. He added a silver in Paris in 2024.
