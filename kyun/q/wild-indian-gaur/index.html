What is the Indian gaur?

The biggest wild cattle on Earth can weigh as much as a small car, stand taller than a tall man at the shoulder, and still sneak quietly through Indian forests.

The short answer

The Indian gaur is the largest wild cattle in the world. A grown male can stand over 2 metres at the shoulder, about the height of a door, and weigh up to around 1,500 kg. That is heavier than many cars.

Gaur live in forests and grassy hills across South and Southeast Asia. India is home to most of them, well over 80% of the world's gaur by recent estimates. They are dark brown to almost black, with a ridge of muscle along the back and pale legs that look like they are wearing white socks.

Surprising but true

A big male gaur can weigh up to about 1,500 kg, yet it eats only plants, mostly grass, leaves and bamboo shoots.

Built like a bulldozer

The gaur's most striking feature is the big hump of muscle above its shoulders. It anchors the powerful neck that carries a head with curved horns, which can be longer than a school ruler. Males are about twice as heavy as females.

The white or yellowish "socks" on all four legs help gaur pick each other out in dim forest light. Their bodies look heavy, but they are agile. Gaur climb steep hillsides and push through thick bamboo with ease. They often feed at dawn and dusk and rest in shade during the hottest hours.

A newborn calf is a soft golden brown and can stand soon after birth. Calves darken as they grow. Old bulls become so dark that in the shade of a forest they look almost black, with only their pale socks and horns catching the light.

Plant eaters with a tiger problem

Gaur eat grass, leaves, fruit and fresh bamboo shoots. Like cows, they chew the cud, bringing food back up to chew it again. They live in small herds, usually led by an older female, while big males often wander alone and join herds in the breeding season.

Only one predator regularly tackles an adult gaur: the tiger. Even then it is a big risk, since a gaur can charge and toss a tiger with its horns. Leopards and dholes, India's wild dogs, sometimes take calves. When danger appears, the herd may bunch together, with calves in the middle.

Where to see them

Some of India's best gaur country is in the Western Ghats, in parks like Nagarhole and Bandipur in Karnataka and Periyar in Kerala. They also live in Central India's tiger reserves and in the forests of the northeast. Goa made the gaur its state animal, where it is called gava.

In the Nilgiris, gaur now wander into tea gardens and even the streets of hill towns like Kotagiri and Ooty. A count in the Nilgiris forest division, reported in 2020, found more than 2,000 gaur. Living so close to people can lead to traffic jams, damaged crops and, sometimes, dangerous meetings.

A comeback story

Gaur are listed as Vulnerable. Hunting, disease spread by village cattle and the loss of forests made them disappear from many areas in the last century. In Bandhavgarh Tiger Reserve in Madhya Pradesh, gaur had vanished completely. In 2011 and 2012, about 50 gaur were moved there from Kanha Tiger Reserve, carried in trucks. The new herd has since grown.

Gaur also have a tame cousin. In Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland and other parts of northeast India, families keep the mithun, a gentler animal probably descended from gaur. Mithun roam semi-free in the forest and are prized at weddings and festivals. A wild giant with domestic relatives sounds familiar. India's cows also came from a wild ox, the aurochs, which no longer exists. And on the other side of the world, the American bison, the gaur's look-alike, was once down to a few hundred animals before it made one of the great wildlife comebacks.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The gaur is a kind of buffalo or bison.

Truth: It is a wild ox, a cousin of cattle. Its domestic relative is the mithun, kept by communities in northeast India.

Try this

Use a measuring tape and a pencil to mark 2 metres on a wall or door frame, with an adult's help. That is roughly the shoulder height of a big male gaur. Stand beside it, then remember the head and horns rise even higher.
