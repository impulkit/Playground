Who was Andy Warhol?

In 1962, Andy Warhol showed 32 paintings of soup cans, one for each flavour, and helped turn supermarket shelves into gallery art.

The short answer

Andy Warhol was an American artist who became the best-known face of pop art in the 1960s. Pop art took images from popular culture, such as adverts, comic strips, packaging and film stars, and put them into galleries. Warhol painted soup cans, bottles of Coca-Cola, dollar bills and rows of famous faces.

He liked his art to look as if a machine had made it. Using silkscreen printing, he made many copies of the same picture in different colours, like products rolling off a factory line. He even called his studio "the Factory". Some people thought this wasn't real art. Others thought it showed something true about a world full of brands, screens and celebrities.

Surprising but true

Warhol pointed out that a rich film star and a person on the street both drink exactly the same Coca-Cola, and he found that idea wonderful.

A shy boy from Pittsburgh

Warhol was born Andrew Warhola in 1928 in Pittsburgh, a steel-making city. His parents had moved to the USA from a village in what is now Slovakia. When he was about eight, he fell ill with a disease that affected his nerves and spent a long time in bed. His mother gave him comics, colouring books and magazines with photos of film stars, and he drew constantly.

He studied design at college in Pittsburgh, then moved to New York in 1949. There he became a successful commercial artist, drawing shoes, handbags and other products for magazine adverts.

Soup cans in a gallery

Warhol wanted to be taken seriously as a painter. In 1962, a gallery in Los Angeles showed his Campbell's Soup Cans: 32 canvases, each showing a different flavour of the same cheap canned soup. They were lined up like goods on a shop shelf.

Visitors were puzzled. Why paint something so ordinary? Warhol said he had eaten Campbell's soup for lunch for years. To him, the cans were simply part of everyday American life. The paintings now belong to the Museum of Modern Art in New York and are among the most recognised artworks of the twentieth century.

Art like a factory

Warhol learned that he could make art the way advertising made posters. In silkscreen printing, an image is put onto a fine mesh stretched over a frame. Ink is pushed through the mesh onto canvas, in a way similar to block printing or stencilling. He could print the same photo dozens of times.

His studio, covered in silver foil and paint, was full of assistants, musicians, actors and visitors. Warhol made paintings, prints, films and even a magazine there. Repeating images, he once suggested, can make them feel both more powerful and more empty.

How do adverts try to persuade you?The first official TV advert in the USA, in 1941, showed a watch ticking over a map of the country for about ten seconds. It cost the company $9.

Fame and money

Warhol was obsessed with fame. He made bright, repeated portraits of film stars and singers from news photographs. In 2022, one of his portraits of the actress Marilyn Monroe sold at auction for 195 million dollars. At the time, no work by an American artist had ever fetched more at auction.

He also enjoyed the idea that some things are the same for everyone. In a book published in 1975, he wrote that a president, a film star and a person on the street all drink the same Coke. No amount of money, he said, buys a better one. That is one reason he painted soft drinks and soup.

Not everyone agreed

Many critics at the time felt that copying adverts and photos wasn't real art, and that Warhol was more interested in money and fame than ideas. Others argued that he held up a mirror to modern life, where people see the same images again and again on screens, shelves and billboards. The debate has never fully ended.

His legacy

Warhol died in New York in 1987, aged 58, after a routine operation. The Andy Warhol Museum opened in Pittsburgh in 1994 and is one of the largest museums in the world devoted to a single artist.

Pop art spread far beyond America. In India, the painter Bhupen Khakhar used the bright colours of calendar art and shop signs to paint ordinary people at work. Today, when a brand logo turns up on a T-shirt as a joke, or a meme repeats one face in many colours, it owes something to the man who painted soup.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Andy Warhol definitely said "In the future, everyone will be famous for 15 minutes."

Truth: The line first appeared in a 1968 exhibition catalogue and was credited to him. Whether he said it, or someone else wrote it for him, is still argued over. It became famous anyway.

Try this

Cut a simple shape, such as a star or a fruit, out of the middle of a piece of card to make a stencil. Hold it on paper and dab paint through the hole with a sponge. Repeat it in a row with different colours. Notice how each print comes out a little different, just as Warhol's did.
