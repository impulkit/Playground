What does the Indian Air Force do?

India's first astronaut and the first Indian to visit the International Space Station were both Indian Air Force pilots.

The short answer

The Indian Air Force protects India's skies. Its pilots fly fighter jets that can intercept unknown aircraft, and it runs radars that watch the airspace day and night. In a war, it supports the army on the ground and the navy at sea.

It also does a great deal of work that has nothing to do with fighting. Its helicopters rescue people from floods and landslides. Its giant transport planes fly supplies to soldiers on snowy mountain posts, carry aid to other countries after disasters, and bring Indians home from war zones. It was founded on 8 October 1932, which is why Air Force Day is celebrated every 8 October.

Surprising but true

In Ladakh, the Indian Air Force lands planes at Daulat Beg Oldi, an airstrip about 5,000 metres up, higher than Mont Blanc, the tallest peak in the Alps.

From biplanes to jets

The Air Force began very small. Its first flight, formed in 1933, had a handful of Westland Wapiti biplanes, two-winged planes with open cockpits. During the Second World War it grew quickly and was given the title "Royal". When India became a republic in 1950, the "Royal" was dropped.

Since then it has flown many kinds of aircraft. The MiG-21, a fast Soviet-designed jet, served for more than 60 years before its final flight in September 2025. Today the fighters include the Russian-designed Su-30MKI, the French Rafale and the Tejas, a light jet designed and built in India. The Air Force also flies large transport planes such as the C-17 Globemaster and C-130J, and helicopters including the Dhruv and the Prachand, both made in India by Hindustan Aeronautics Limited.

High and cold

India has some of the hardest flying conditions anywhere. In Ladakh, the airstrip at Daulat Beg Oldi sits about 5,000 metres above sea level. Air that high is thin, so wings make less lift and engines produce less power. Landing a big plane there takes careful skill.

Helicopters fly supplies to soldiers on the Siachen Glacier, among the highest places where people are posted all year round. Pilots must deal with sudden storms, icy winds and narrow valleys. Knowing how planes fly in thin air is a matter of life and death there.

When disaster strikes

In June 2013, floods and landslides trapped thousands of pilgrims and villagers in the mountains of Uttarakhand. The Air Force launched Operation Rahat, flying helicopters and planes for weeks through rain and cloud to lift people to safety. One helicopter crashed during the rescue, killing everyone on board.

The Air Force also flies after cyclones in Odisha and floods in Kerala and Assam, often working with the army, navy and other disaster teams. It has flown aid to Nepal after the 2015 earthquake and evacuated Indians from countries in conflict.

Pilots who reached space

In 1984, Squadron Leader Rakesh Sharma, an Air Force pilot, flew on a Soviet spacecraft and became the first Indian in space. In June 2025, Group Captain Shubhanshu Shukla, another Air Force pilot, flew to the International Space Station. Several of India's astronauts for the Gaganyaan programme also come from the Air Force, because test pilots are trained to stay calm when things go wrong.

Women in the cockpit

Women have flown Air Force helicopters and transport planes since the 1990s. In 2016, Avani Chaturvedi, Bhawana Kanth and Mohana Singh were commissioned as India's first women fighter pilots. Today women fly fighter jets in front-line squadrons, and women officers serve across the force as engineers, air traffic controllers and meteorologists.

On Air Force Day, crowds gather to watch the Surya Kiran team fly red-and-white jets just a few metres apart, and the Sarang team swing their helicopters around each other like dancers. Behind the show are years of training.

Myth or fact?

Myth: An air force only fights wars.

Truth: Most of the time, the Indian Air Force is training, guarding the skies and helping in emergencies. It has carried food and medicine after cyclones and floods, rescued pilgrims in the mountains and flown Indians home from conflict zones abroad.

Try this

Fold three paper planes. Make one with wide wings, one with narrow pointed wings, and one with small flaps bent up at the back. Throw each gently from the same spot and see which flies furthest, which glides slowest and which loops. Real aircraft designers make the same kinds of choices for different jobs.
