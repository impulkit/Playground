What comes after a trillion?

After a trillion come quadrillion, quintillion and sextillion, and a nine-year-old boy named the most famous giant number of all, the googol.

The short answer

After a trillion comes a quadrillion, then a quintillion, sextillion, septillion, octillion, nonillion and decillion. Each one is a thousand times bigger than the one before.

A million has 6 zeros. A billion has 9, a trillion 12, a quadrillion 15 and a quintillion 18. The names follow Latin counting words: quadr- means four, quint- five, sext- six, dec- ten. So you can keep going with names like vigintillion, which has 63 zeros. In India, the same amounts can also be named in lakh and crore, which group zeros differently.

Surprising but true

A million seconds is about 11 and a half days. A billion seconds is nearly 32 years. A trillion seconds is about 31,700 years.

Counting by thousands

Most English speakers today use what mathematicians call the short scale. Every new name means "multiply by a thousand". It matches the way we put commas in numbers: 1,000,000,000 for a billion, with three zeros between each comma.

Mathematicians usually skip the names and use powers of ten. A quintillion is written 10¹⁸, which means 1 with 18 zeros after it. It's shorter, harder to misread and works the same in every language.

To feel the size of these jumps, think about time. A million seconds is about 11 and a half days. A billion seconds is nearly 32 years. A trillion seconds is about 31,700 years, which takes you back to the last Ice Age, long before anyone built a city.

When a billion was bigger

For centuries Britain and much of Europe used the long scale, in which each new name meant a million times bigger. A billion was a million million, which has 12 zeros. Britain officially switched to the American billion in 1974.

Many European languages still use the long scale. In French, a milliard has 9 zeros and a billion has 12. Translators and news reporters have to be careful, or a country's budget can come out a thousand times too big. Scientists avoid the problem by writing powers of ten, which mean the same thing in every country.

Indian newspapers face a similar puzzle every day. The same sum might be reported as 10 crore rupees in one story and 100 million rupees in another. Both are correct.

The Indian way

The Indian system groups numbers after the first thousand in steps of a hundred. A lakh has 5 zeros, a crore 7, an arab 9 and a kharab 11. Older lists go further, with words like neel, padma and shankh, though books don't all agree on exactly how big each one is.

Ancient Indian writers loved huge numbers. One Buddhist text, the Lalitavistara, tells of the young Buddha naming numbers in a contest, climbing all the way to one with 53 zeros.

The googol and its giant cousin

In 1920, the American mathematician Edward Kasner asked his nine-year-old nephew, Milton Sirotta, to make up a name for 1 followed by 100 zeros. Milton said "googol". He also suggested an even bigger number, the googolplex: 1 followed by a googol of zeros. Kasner put both in a book in 1940.

A googol is far more than the number of atoms scientists estimate are in the whole visible universe, which is around 10⁸⁰. A googolplex is so big you could never write it out, because there isn't enough space in the universe for all the zeros. Decades later, the founders of Google named their company after the googol, spelling it differently.

Names for real things

Scientists also need names for giant measured amounts. In 2022, the international body that runs the metric system added two new prefixes: ronna, for 27 zeros, and quetta, for 30. The mass of the Earth is about 6 ronnagrams. Even so, all these numbers are still finite. Keep adding zeros forever and you never reach infinity, which isn't really a number at all.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A billion means the same number in every country.

Truth: In the past, Britain and much of Europe used "billion" to mean a million million, which has 12 zeros. Britain switched to the American billion of 9 zeros in 1974, but in French, German and Spanish the word that sounds like "billion" still means 12 zeros.

Try this

Write the number one trillion on a long strip of paper, putting a comma after every three zeros from the right. Then write it again the Indian way, with commas after the first three zeros and then after every two. Count how many commas each way needs, and say each number out loud.
