What is Punjab known for?

Punjab's name means "five waters", and those rivers turned its plains into one of the great grain baskets of the world.

The short answer

Punjab is a state in north-west India, famous for its farms, its food, its music and as the home of Sikhism. Its name comes from Persian: panj means five and aab means water. The five rivers that gave it the name are the Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Sutlej.

Those rivers spread rich soil over a flat, wide plain. Today Punjab grows enormous amounts of wheat and rice. It is also known for the Golden Temple in Amritsar, for loud, joyful bhangra music, and for buttery parathas and lassi. And it is known for the many Punjabis who have moved abroad, from Canada and Britain to California.

Surprising but true

Punjab covers only about 1.5% of India's land, yet in many years it has supplied a large share of the wheat and rice the government buys to feed the country.

A land cut in two

For most of history, Punjab was one region that stretched across what is now India and Pakistan. In 1947, when British rule ended, it was divided. The western part, with the city of Lahore, went to Pakistan. The eastern part stayed in India.

Partition forced millions of people to leave their homes and cross the new border, and many lost their lives in the violence. Families still tell stories of the villages they left behind. Indian Punjab was later divided again, in 1966, when Haryana and parts of Himachal Pradesh became separate. Its capital is Chandigarh, a planned city it shares with Haryana. The city was designed in the 1950s by the Swiss-French architect Le Corbusier, with numbered sectors laid out like squares on a chessboard.

The grain basket

In the 1960s, India worried about famine. Scientists brought in new, high-yielding wheat and rice, along with fertilisers and wells. Punjab's farmers took them up quickly, and harvests jumped. This became known as the Green Revolution.

Punjab covers only about 1.5% of India's land, but for decades it has supplied a big share of the grain the government buys for ration shops. That success has a cost. Pumping so much groundwater for rice has made water tables fall in many districts. And when farmers burn leftover straw after the rice harvest, the smoke adds to the air pollution that hangs over northern India each autumn.

What is bhangra?Bhangra started as farmers celebrating the wheat harvest in Punjab. Today it fills dance floors in Birmingham, Toronto and New York.

The home of Sikhism

Guru Nanak, the founder of Sikhism, was born in 1469 in a village now called Nankana Sahib, in Pakistani Punjab. He taught that there is one God, that all people are equal, and that honest work and sharing matter. Nine Gurus followed him. The tenth, Guru Gobind Singh, founded the Khalsa, the community of initiated Sikhs, at Anandpur Sahib in Punjab in 1699. Their teachings are collected in the Guru Granth Sahib, the holy book of Sikhs.

Gurdwaras across Punjab, and across the world, run free community kitchens called langar. Anyone can sit on the floor and eat, whoever they are. The Punjabi language is written in Gurmukhi, a script shaped by the Sikh Gurus, and it is the main language of the state.

Songs, food and festivals

Bhangra began as a harvest dance, performed by farmers celebrating the wheat crop around Baisakhi in April. With its booming dhol drum, it now fills wedding halls from Ludhiana to London. Lohri, in January, is celebrated with bonfires, songs and peanuts.

Punjabi food is hearty: sarson da saag with makki di roti, chole, and thick glasses of lassi. Much of what restaurants abroad sell as "Indian food" is really Punjabi, carried there by the people who moved overseas. Punjab's farm kitchens have travelled further than most.

Myth or fact?

Myth: All Punjabis are Sikh.

Truth: Punjab is the home of Sikhism, and most people in Indian Punjab are Sikh. But many Punjabis are Hindu, Muslim or Christian. Across the border in Pakistani Punjab, most people are Muslim.
