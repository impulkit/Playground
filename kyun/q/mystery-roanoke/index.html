What happened to the lost colony of Roanoke?

In 1590, a governor came back to his colony in America to find it empty. The only clue was one word carved into a wooden post.

The short answer

In 1587, about 115 English settlers arrived on Roanoke Island, off the coast of what is now North Carolina, USA. Their governor, John White, sailed back to England for supplies. War with Spain delayed his return for three years.

When he finally came back in August 1590, the settlement was empty. The houses had been taken down. Carved into a post was the word CROATOAN, and the letters CRO were cut into a tree. There was no sign of a struggle and no bodies. White was never able to search further, and the colonists were never seen by English eyes again.

Surprising but true

The governor, John White, came back on his granddaughter's third birthday. She was Virginia Dare, the first child born to English parents in the Americas, and he never saw her again.

A colony at the edge

This was one of England's earliest attempts to settle in North America, more than 30 years before the Mayflower. An earlier group had tried in 1585 but gave up and sailed home the next year.

The 1587 colonists included families, not just soldiers. Among them was White's daughter, Eleanor Dare. On 18 August 1587, she gave birth to a girl named Virginia, the first child born to English parents in the Americas.

The settlers relied on trade and help from local Native nations. Relations with some were friendly, and with others tense. A man named Manteo, from the Croatoan people, had travelled to England and back and acted as a guide and translator.

The long wait

White left at the end of August 1587, expecting to return within months. But in 1588 Spain sent a huge war fleet, the Armada, against England. Every good ship was needed at home.

He returned on 18 August 1590, his granddaughter's third birthday. He found grass growing and his own chests dug up and ruined. Before leaving, he had agreed on a code. If the colonists moved, they would carve where they went. If they were in danger, they would add a cross. CROATOAN had no cross.

Idea one: they joined the Croatoan

Croatoan was the name of an island about 80 km south, now part of Hatteras Island, and of the people who lived there. Manteo came from there. Many historians think the colonists, short of food, went to live with the Croatoan and slowly became part of their community.

Archaeologists digging on Hatteras since the late 1990s have found English objects from the right period, such as a piece of a sword handle, a slate for writing and bits of metal. But such items could also have come through trade, so the finds don't settle the case.

Idea two: they moved inland

In 2012, researchers at the British Museum studied John White's own map of the region. Under a patch of paper, they spotted a hidden symbol that looked like a fort, about 80 km inland near the Albemarle Sound.

Was it a planned new home? A team began digging near the spot and found English pottery that might belong to the colonists. Others think the pieces come from later settlers. Old maps can hold secrets, but they don't always explain them.

Idea three: something went wrong

Some colonists may have died from disease, hunger or conflict. The years around 1587 had severe droughts, shown by tree rings from very old cypress trees in the area. A failed harvest would have been hard on everyone, settlers and Native people alike.

It's also possible the group split up, with some going south and some inland. That would explain why both theories turn up evidence.

Why the story lasts

The lost colony has become a legend in America, and every summer a play about it is performed on Roanoke Island. It also reminds us that the land was already home to many Native nations, whose own histories of this time were rarely written down by Europeans.

Archaeologists keep digging, using careful methods described in how archaeology works. One new find could change the answer. Until then, a single carved word is the last message from 115 people.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Roanoke colonists vanished without a single clue.

Truth: They left a clear clue, the word CROATOAN, the name of a nearby island and the Native people who lived there. The mystery is what happened after that, not whether they left a message.
