What is the story behind The Jungle Book?

The most famous story about an Indian jungle was written in a snowy farmhouse in Vermont, USA, by a man born in Bombay.

The short answer

The Jungle Book is a collection of stories by Rudyard Kipling, published in 1894. The most famous ones are about Mowgli, a boy raised by wolves in the forests of central India. He is taught the Law of the Jungle by Baloo the bear and Bagheera the black panther, and hunted by Shere Khan, the tiger.

Kipling was born in Bombay in 1865, but he wrote the book thousands of kilometres away. He was living in the US state of Vermont with his American wife. A second collection, The Second Jungle Book, followed in 1895. Together they have been retold in films, cartoons and plays ever since.

Surprising but true

Many names in The Jungle Book are simply Hindi words. Baloo comes from bhalu, bear, and Hathi is the Hindi word for elephant.

A jungle with rules

The Mowgli stories are set in the Seoni hills, in what is now Madhya Pradesh. Kipling probably never visited Seoni himself, but he read reports by British forest officers and drew on what he remembered of India.

In the stories, the jungle is not wild chaos. It has a Law. The wolf pack meets at Council Rock, led by the old wolf Akela. Animals must not kill humans, and they must share water during a drought. Shere Khan breaks the Law, which is why the others look down on him. The monkeys, the Bandar-log, are mocked for having no law at all.

Many names come from Hindi. Baloo is from bhalu (bear), Hathi means elephant, and Shere Khan combines sher (tiger) with a title, Khan. Mowgli, though, is Kipling's own invention.

More than Mowgli

Only some of the stories are about Mowgli. "Rikki-Tikki-Tavi" tells of a brave mongoose who protects an English family's garden from two cobras, Nag and Nagaina. "Toomai of the Elephants" follows a boy who sees elephants dance in the forest at night. "The White Seal" is set far away in the icy Bering Sea.

Each story ends with a poem or song. Kipling cared about rhythm and the sound of words, which is one reason his sentences are fun to read aloud.

From cubs to cartoons

The book quickly travelled beyond readers. In 1916, the founder of the Scout movement, Robert Baden-Powell, used it to shape his new programme for younger boys, the Wolf Cubs. Cub groups are still called packs, and the adult leader is still called Akela in many countries, part of Scouting today.

Disney's animated film of 1967 made the story famous worldwide, with Baloo singing "The Bare Necessities". It changed the story a lot, making it lighter and funnier. In 2016, Disney made a live-action version, with an Indian-American boy, Neel Sethi, as Mowgli and computer-made animals.

"Jungle jungle baat chali hai"

For many Indian children, the best-known version is a Japanese animated series that was dubbed into Hindi and shown on Doordarshan in the early 1990s. Its theme song, with lyrics by the poet Gulzar, began "Jungle jungle baat chali hai, pata chala hai", and millions of children could sing it. It became one of the most loved shows of the time on Indian television.

Readers today sometimes feel uneasy about Kipling, who supported the British Empire. Some see the jungle's strict Law and hierarchy as an echo of his ideas about rule and order. Even so, the forests he described, with their wolves, langurs and tigers, are real, and several national parks in central India still protect them. Pench Tiger Reserve, close to Seoni, is often described as the real Jungle Book country, and visitors there can still hear langurs bark their warning when a tiger is near.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Kipling wrote The Jungle Book while living in an Indian jungle.

Truth: He wrote it in Vermont, in the north-east of the USA, where he lived with his American wife in the 1890s. He drew on his childhood in India and on books and reports about Indian wildlife.
