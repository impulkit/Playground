What are India's Seven Sister states?

Seven states in India's northeast are joined to the rest of the country by a strip of land only about 22 km wide at its narrowest point.

The short answer

The Seven Sisters are the seven states of India's northeast: Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura. The nickname was coined in 1972 by a journalist, Jyoti Prasad Saikia, who wrote about how closely these neighbours depend on one another. Sikkim, which joined the region's official council in 2002, is often called their brother.

Together the states cover about 8% of India's land and hold about 4% of its people. They are linked to the rest of the country by the Siliguri Corridor, a narrow strip of land in West Bengal sometimes called the Chicken's Neck. Almost all of the region's border is with other countries.

Surprising but true

In Meghalaya, people grow bridges. They train the roots of rubber fig trees across streams until the roots are strong enough to walk on.

Mountains, rivers and rain

The Northeast runs from the snowy eastern Himalayas in Arunachal Pradesh to the green hills of Mizoram. Through the middle flows the mighty Brahmaputra. In Assam it spreads so wide that it holds Majuli, one of the largest river islands in the world.

Meghalaya means "abode of the clouds". Its villages of Mawsynram and Cherrapunji are often called the wettest places on Earth, with more than 11 m of rain in some years. That is about six times the height of a tall adult. All that rain makes the region one of the greenest and richest in plants and animals anywhere in India.

Bridges that grow

In the Khasi and Jaintia hills of Meghalaya, streams turn into torrents in the monsoon, and wooden bridges rot. So villagers grow bridges instead. They guide the roots of rubber fig trees across a stream, often along bamboo poles. Over 15 to 30 years, the roots thicken and knot together into a living bridge. Some are believed to be well over a century old, and one village has a double-decker bridge.

Tea, rhinos and ghost peppers

Assam's tea gardens produce about half of India's tea. Its strong, malty taste is used in breakfast teas around the world. Assam is also home to Kaziranga National Park, where about two-thirds of the world's one-horned rhinos live.

How did Kaziranga save the one-horned rhino?A hundred years ago there may have been only a few dozen rhinos in Kaziranga. Now about two out of every three on Earth live there.

The hills are full of bamboo, used for houses, baskets, bridges and even food, as bamboo shoots. Manipur's Loktak Lake has floating islands of plants called phumdis. One of them forms a national park, home to the rare sangai deer. In the eastern forests live red pandas, hornbills and clouded leopards. The bhut jolokia, or ghost pepper, from Assam and Nagaland, was once named the hottest chilli in the world.

Many peoples, many languages

The Northeast is one of the most varied regions in the world. It is home to more than 200 communities, including the Assamese, Bodo, Khasi, Garo, Mizo, Meitei, Adi, Apatani and many Naga groups. Many speak languages related to those of Tibet and Southeast Asia rather than other Indian languages. That adds dozens of languages to India's count.

Festivals are just as varied. Assam celebrates Bihu, which marks the farming year. Nagaland holds the Hornbill Festival every December, where tribes share dances, songs and food. Arunachal Pradesh's Dong village is among the first places in India to see the sunrise each morning.

Far, yet close

For a long time the region felt far away to many Indians, and people from the Northeast have sometimes faced unfair stereotypes in other cities. New railways, roads and airports are bringing it closer. Today its music, football players, boxers and food are known across the country.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The people of the Northeast all share one culture and language.

Truth: The region has more than 200 communities with their own languages, foods, clothes and festivals. Assamese, Bodo, Khasi, Mizo, Manipuri and dozens of Naga languages are only a few of them.
