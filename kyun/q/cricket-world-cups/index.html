What happened at India's World Cup wins?

In 1983 India had won just one World Cup match in eight years. Then a team of underdogs beat the mighty West Indies at Lord's.

The short answer

India's men have won five World Cups: the 50-over World Cup in 1983 and 2011, and the Twenty20 World Cup in 2007, 2024 and 2026. In 2025 India's women won their first 50-over World Cup.

Each win is remembered in a different way. 1983 was the shock that made India fall in love with one-day cricket. 2007 launched the T20 boom. 2011 was a home win with Sachin Tendulkar carried on his teammates' shoulders. 2024 ended a long wait with a breathtaking final over.

Surprising but true

Before the 1983 World Cup, India's only win in two earlier tournaments was against East Africa, a team of amateurs.

1983: the underdogs at Lord's

The West Indies had won the first two World Cups, in 1975 and 1979, with fearsome fast bowlers and great batters. India had won only one match in those tournaments.

In England in 1983, captain Kapil Dev's team began to surprise people. Against Zimbabwe at Tunbridge Wells, India slumped to 17 for 5. Kapil walked out and hit 175 not out, one of the greatest one-day innings ever. The match was not televised, because the BBC was on strike that day.

In the final at Lord's in London on 25 June, India made only 183. Then Kapil ran backwards to catch a top-edged shot from Viv Richards, and the West Indies collapsed to 140. Mohinder Amarnath was player of the match.

2007: a new kind of cricket

The first T20 World Cup was held in South Africa in 2007. Few expected India to take the short format seriously, and a young M. S. Dhoni was made captain.

Yuvraj Singh hit six sixes in one over against England. In the final in Johannesburg, India faced Pakistan. Pakistan needed six runs from four balls with one wicket left when Misbah-ul-Haq tried a scoop shot. Sreesanth caught it, and India won by five runs. Within months, the IPL was born.

2011: the night at the Wankhede

India co-hosted the 2011 World Cup. The final, against Sri Lanka, was played at the Wankhede Stadium in Mumbai on 2 April. Chasing 275, India lost Sachin and Virender Sehwag early.

Gautam Gambhir made 97, and Dhoni promoted himself up the order. He finished the match with a six over long-on. Yuvraj Singh was player of the tournament. The players lifted Sachin, playing his sixth World Cup, onto their shoulders and carried him round the ground.

2024: a catch on the boundary

India had lost several finals and semi-finals in the years after 2011. In the 2024 T20 World Cup final in Barbados, South Africa needed 30 runs from 30 balls with plenty of wickets left. India looked beaten.

The bowlers fought back, led by Jasprit Bumrah. In the last over, Suryakumar Yadav ran along the boundary and caught a huge hit from David Miller. He tossed the ball up as he stepped over the rope, came back in and caught it again. India won by seven runs. Virat Kohli and Rohit Sharma retired from T20 internationals straight afterwards. In March 2026, playing at home, India won the T20 World Cup again, beating New Zealand by 96 runs in the final.

2025: the women's turn

India's women had reached two World Cup finals before, in 2005 and 2017. In November 2025, playing at home, they beat South Africa in the final at Navi Mumbai. Harmanpreet Kaur's team became the first Indian women to lift the trophy, and you can read more of their story in women's cricket.

Each win happened in front of a bigger crowd of fans than the last, on televisions, then phones. In 1983 most Indians followed the final on radio.

Myth or fact?

Myth: India were one of the favourites to win the 1983 World Cup.

Truth: India were huge underdogs. They had won just one match in the two earlier World Cups, and the West Indies had won both. Bookmakers in England offered odds of 66 to 1 against them winning.
