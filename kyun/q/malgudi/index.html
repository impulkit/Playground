What is Malgudi Days?

Malgudi has a railway station, a river, a market road and a school full of mischief. It just doesn't exist on any map.

The short answer

Malgudi is an imaginary small town in South India, invented by the writer R. K. Narayan. Malgudi Days is one of his collections of short stories, first published in 1943. It is also the name of a much-loved television series based on his stories, first shown on Doordarshan in 1986.

The stories are about ordinary people: a schoolboy, an astrologer, a postman, a snake charmer, a stubborn old man. Nothing very dramatic happens in most of them. Their charm lies in small details, gentle humour and a sharp eye for how people really behave.

For many Indians, the hummed theme tune of the TV series, "ta na na, ta na na na", brings back childhood in a few seconds.

Surprising but true

The TV series of Malgudi Days was filmed mostly in Agumbe, a small, rainy village in the Western Ghats of Karnataka, not in a town like the one in the books.

A town that grew in books

Malgudi first appeared in Narayan's first novel, Swami and Friends, in 1935. It follows Swaminathan, a ten-year-old who hates arithmetic, dodges homework and gets into scrapes with his friends.

Narayan kept returning to Malgudi for the rest of his life, in more than a dozen novels and many short stories. Over the years the town grew. It got a new bank, a cinema, a printing press, a college. Readers watched it change from a sleepy place into a busier town, just as real Indian towns were changing around them.

Narayan lived most of his life in Mysuru, and many readers see that city in Malgudi's streets. The town has a river, the Sarayu, and a railway station, which Narayan said was the first thing he pictured.

How Narayan got published

Narayan wrote in English, which was unusual for an Indian storyteller in the 1930s. His first book was rejected by several publishers. A friend passed the manuscript to the English novelist Graham Greene, who liked it and helped get it published in London. Greene and Narayan stayed friends for decades.

Narayan's later novel The Guide, published in 1958, won the Sahitya Akademi Award and became a famous Hindi film in 1965. He was later given the Padma Vibhushan, one of India's highest honours.

Malgudi on television

In 1986, the actor and director Shankar Nag turned Narayan's stories into a series for Doordarshan. At a time when most Indian homes had a single channel, millions of families watched it together.

Nag filmed most of it in Agumbe, a village in the rainforests of the Western Ghats, with red-tiled houses, narrow streets and almost constant rain. Local people acted alongside professional actors. Narayan's younger brother, R. K. Laxman, India's best-known cartoonist, drew the sketches that appear in the opening and between scenes. The music, with its famous humming, was composed by L. Vaidyanathan.

The young actor Master Manjunath played Swami and became one of the most recognisable child stars of Indian television.

Why it still feels fresh

Narayan's sentences are short and plain. He rarely explains his characters' feelings. He shows what they do and lets you work out the rest, often with a quiet joke at the end.

Because his stories avoid big events, they have aged slowly. A boy who hates maths homework, a shopkeeper worried about money, a child who wants a dog: these could be anywhere. That is why Malgudi has readers in the USA and Britain as well as India, and why some families now watch the old series on their phones, decades after it first aired.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Malgudi is a real town in Karnataka or Tamil Nadu.

Truth: Malgudi is imaginary. R. K. Narayan drew on places he knew, especially Mysuru and the towns of South India, but he never put Malgudi on a real map.
