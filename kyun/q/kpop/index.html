What is K-pop?

Many K-pop stars train for years before fans ever hear them, practising singing, dancing and languages for hours every day.

The short answer

K-pop is popular music from South Korea. Most of it is made by big companies that pick young singers and dancers, train them for years and launch them in groups. Songs are catchy, mix several styles, and come with sharp, tightly rehearsed dance routines and expensive videos.

From the 2000s, K-pop spread far beyond Korea through YouTube and social media. Today groups like BTS and Blackpink fill stadiums in the USA, and K-pop fan clubs meet in cities from Mumbai to Mexico City.

Surprising but true

The song "Gangnam Style" by PSY became the first video on YouTube to pass 1 billion views, in December 2012.

How it began

Korean pop songs existed long before, but many people date modern K-pop to 1992. That year a group called Seo Taiji and Boys performed a song mixing rap, rock and dance on a TV talent show. The judges gave it low marks, but young viewers loved it.

Soon new companies, including SM Entertainment, YG and JYP, began building groups on purpose. They chose trainees, often in their early teens, through auditions. In the late 1990s, Chinese newspapers started calling the spread of Korean pop culture across Asia "Hallyu", the Korean Wave.

Years of training

A K-pop trainee might spend several years practising before debuting, if they debut at all. Days are packed with singing lessons, dance practice, language classes and fitness training. Many trainees never get picked for a group.

The system has critics. Former trainees and stars have spoken about long hours, strict diets and tough contracts. Some companies have changed their rules after public complaints, and the debate continues.

The training shows on stage. A K-pop group of seven or more members can move in perfect unison, swap positions in a blink and sing live while dancing.

Going global

In 2012, the singer PSY released "Gangnam Style", a joke song about a rich Seoul neighbourhood. Its silly horse-riding dance made it the first video on YouTube to pass 1 billion views.

How did online videos take over?The first video ever uploaded to YouTube is 19 seconds long and shows a young man at a zoo, talking about elephants' trunks.

BTS, a seven-member group, debuted in 2013. They spoke to fans directly through social media and live videos, and their fan club, called ARMY, grew into millions. In 2020 their song "Dynamite" became the first by a K-pop act to top the US Billboard Hot 100. In 2019, Blackpink became the first K-pop girl group to play at the Coachella festival in California.

K-pop in India

Korean music and TV dramas have been popular in Northeast India for more than twenty years, especially in Manipur, Nagaland and Mizoram. During the 2010s they spread across the rest of the country, helped by streaming and cheap mobile data.

Today Indian K-pop fans run dance cover groups, learn Korean words from songs, and hold contests. Several Indian universities, including Jawaharlal Nehru University in Delhi, teach the Korean language, and many fans start by singing along to lyrics written in Hangul, the Korean alphabet. The Korean Cultural Centre in New Delhi has run K-pop contests for Indian fans for years.

Fans as part of the music

K-pop fans don't only listen. They organise streaming parties to push songs up the charts, design fan chants for concerts, and raise money for charity in their favourite star's name. Each group has an official light stick, so a stadium crowd glows in the group's colour.

That closeness between stars and fans is a big part of why K-pop feels different. Many fans feel they are helping a group succeed, rather than simply watching from the crowd.

Myth or fact?

Myth: K-pop is one style of music.

Truth: K-pop mixes many styles, including pop, hip-hop, electronic dance music, R&B and rock, sometimes in one song. It is better thought of as a way of making and presenting music, with groups, big dance routines and close ties with fans.
