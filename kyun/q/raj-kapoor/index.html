Who was Raj Kapoor?

In the 1950s, people in Moscow sang a Hindi film song on the streets, thanks to a young actor from Bombay playing a lovable tramp.

The short answer

Raj Kapoor was one of the most loved actors and filmmakers in Indian cinema. Between the late 1940s and the 1980s, he acted in, directed and produced films that mixed comedy, romance, music and big questions about rich and poor.

His most famous character was a cheerful, penniless wanderer, a little like Charlie Chaplin's Tramp, with baggy trousers, a battered hat and a big heart. Films such as Awaara (1951) and Shree 420 (1955) made him a star in India and across the Soviet Union, the Middle East and parts of Asia and Africa. People called him "the showman" of Hindi cinema.

Surprising but true

Raj Kapoor was born in Peshawar, now in Pakistan, and later became one of the most famous Indian faces in the Soviet Union and China.

A family of actors

Raj Kapoor was born in 1924 in Peshawar, then part of British India, now in Pakistan. His father, Prithviraj Kapoor, was a famous stage and film actor who later moved the family to Bombay. Young Raj worked as a clapper boy and assistant on film sets, sweeping floors and holding the board that marks each shot.

He started acting as a child and got his first lead role in 1947. A year later, aged 24, he founded his own company, R. K. Films. The Kapoors went on to become one of the best-known families in Indian cinema, with his sons, grandchildren and great-grandchildren acting in films.

The tramp who won hearts

In Awaara, Raj Kapoor plays a boy pushed into a life of crime by poverty. The film asks whether people are born good or bad, or made so by their surroundings. Its title song, "Awaara Hoon" ("I am a vagabond"), became a hit far beyond India. The film was shown at the Cannes Film Festival in 1953.

In Shree 420, a poor young man arrives in Bombay and is tempted by easy money. The song "Mera Joota Hai Japani" has him singing that his shoes are Japanese, his trousers English and his hat Russian, but his heart is Indian. Newly independent India loved it.

A star in Moscow

During the Cold War, the Soviet government allowed few Western films. Indian films, with their stories about the struggles of ordinary people, were welcome. Awaara was released there in 1954 and drew huge audiences. Raj Kapoor was mobbed by fans on his visits.

For many Soviet citizens, India meant Raj Kapoor and his songs. Even today, older Russians, Uzbeks and others sometimes greet Indian visitors by humming "Awaara Hoon".

Music and teamwork

Raj Kapoor's films are remembered as much for their songs as their stories. He worked again and again with the same team: the composers Shankar and Jaikishan, the lyricists Shailendra and Hasrat Jaipuri, the playback singer Mukesh, who sang for him, and Lata Mangeshkar. Many of their film songs are still played at weddings and on radio.

He often acted with Nargis, and their pairing in films such as Barsaat and Shree 420 is one of the most famous in Indian cinema.

Ups and downs

Not every film succeeded. Mera Naam Joker (1970), about a circus clown, was long and expensive and flopped at first, leaving him in debt. He bounced back with Bobby (1973), a teenage love story that launched a new generation of young romantic heroes.

Critics have pointed out that some of his later films leaned on glamour more than storytelling. Still, he won many awards, including the Dadasaheb Phalke Award for 1987. He fell seriously ill at the ceremony in May 1988 and died a month later. In 2024, India marked his 100th birthday with festivals of his films. Mera Naam Joker is now seen as a classic, and its circus scenes are still shown to film students.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Raj Kapoor only acted in films.

Truth: He was also a director and producer. He founded his own studio, R. K. Films, in 1948, when he was just 24, and directed many of his best-known films himself.
