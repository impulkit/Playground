How do maps shrink the world?

In 1852, an Indian mathematician worked through years of survey numbers and found that one peak, then called Peak XV, was the highest in the world.

The short answer

A map is a drawing of a place made much smaller, so it fits on paper or a screen. The scale tells you how much smaller.

A scale might say 1:50,000. That means 1 centimetre on the map stands for 50,000 centimetres on the ground, which is 500 metres. Many maps also have a little bar with marks showing 1 km or 1 mile, so you can measure distances with a ruler or a piece of string.

The bigger the second number, the more the world has been shrunk. A street map of your town might be 1:10,000. A map of all of India in a school atlas might be 1:1,50,00,000 or smaller. Shrinking a round Earth onto flat paper also bends some shapes, which causes trouble on world maps.

Surprising but true

On many common world maps, Greenland looks about as big as Africa. In reality, Africa is about 14 times larger.

Choosing a scale

Mapmakers choose a scale depending on what the map is for. A hiking map needs every path and stream, so it uses a large scale, where a small area fills the page. A world map shows only big things like countries and oceans, so it uses a tiny scale.

A scale is really a fraction. A scale of 1:50,000 means the map is 1/50,000 the size of the land. Zooming in on a phone map is like switching between scales in an instant.

Measuring India with triangles

In 1802, a British army officer named William Lambton began one of the greatest measuring projects ever attempted: the Great Trigonometrical Survey of India. Starting near Madras, now Chennai, his teams measured a base line along the ground with great care. Then they measured the angles from hilltops and towers to distant points, building chains of triangles across the land.

After Lambton died in 1823, George Everest took over. The teams worked for decades, through jungles and heat and illness. The central line, called the Great Arc, ran about 2,500 km from near Kanyakumari in the far south up to the Himalayas.

The highest mountain

By the 1840s, surveyors had measured the angles to distant snow peaks in the Himalayas from the plains far below. In 1852, Radhanath Sikdar, a brilliant mathematician who was chief "computer" for the Survey, finished calculations showing that Peak XV was taller than any other mountain known.

It was later named Mount Everest, after the head of the survey, although Everest himself had objected that local people should have their own name used. Today the mountain is also known as Sagarmatha in Nepal and Chomolungma in Tibet.

Which is the tallest mountain on Earth?Everest is the highest. But measured from its base on the sea floor, a mountain in Hawaii is taller.

The trouble with world maps

The Earth is round, and paper is flat. Turning one into the other always stretches or squashes something. The different ways of doing it are called projections.

In 1569, the Flemish mapmaker Gerardus Mercator made a world map for sailors. On his map, a straight line is a constant compass direction, which made it very useful for navigation. But it stretches land near the poles. Greenland looks as big as Africa, when Africa is about 14 times bigger. Many people still picture the world this way.

Maps today

Modern maps are made from satellite photos and measurements. GPS satellites let surveyors fix positions to within centimetres. The Survey of India, founded in 1767, is one of the oldest scientific departments in the country and still makes official maps.

Maps can never show everything. The writer Lewis Carroll joked about a country that made a map at a scale of a mile to the mile, the same size as the land itself. Farmers complained it would cover the fields and block the sunlight, so they used the country itself as its own map instead.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Greenland is about the same size as Africa.

Truth: It only looks that way on maps using the Mercator projection, which stretches places near the poles. Africa is really about 14 times bigger than Greenland.

Try this

Measure your bedroom or classroom with a tape measure or by counting your footsteps. Draw a map of it on squared paper, using one square for every half metre. Add the bed, desk or door in the right places. Write your scale in the corner, just as real mapmakers do.
