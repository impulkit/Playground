What is a patent?

Abraham Lincoln is the only US president to hold a patent, for a way of lifting riverboats over sandbanks. It was never built.

The short answer

A patent is a legal right, given by a government, that lets an inventor stop anyone else from making, using or selling their invention for a set time, usually 20 years. In return, the inventor must publish a detailed description of how the invention works, so others can learn from it.

To get one, the invention must be new, it must not be obvious to an expert, and it must be useful or able to be made in industry. A patent covers a specific thing, like a new kind of battery or a medicine, not a general wish like "a car that flies". Each country has its own patent office. India's head office is in Kolkata.

Surprising but true

In 1995 an American patent was granted for using turmeric to heal wounds. India's scientists got it cancelled by showing that Indians had used turmeric this way for centuries.

A deal between inventors and everyone else

Inventing can take years and cost a lot. If anyone could copy a new machine the moment it appeared, inventors might keep their methods secret or not bother at all. A patent offers a reward: a period when only you can sell the invention.

But it comes with a price. You have to explain your invention so clearly that an expert could build it. When the patent runs out, the knowledge belongs to everyone. That's why cheap "generic" medicines appear once a drug's patent expires.

From Venice to Lincoln

One of the earliest patent laws was passed in Venice in 1474. It promised inventors 10 years of protection for new devices. Many other countries later followed. The writers of the US Constitution in 1787 gave Congress the power to grant patents. The first American patent was issued in 1790, for a new way of making potash, used in soap and fertiliser.

Abraham Lincoln grew up near rivers and had seen boats stuck on sandbanks. In 1849 he patented a system of air-filled floats to lift boats over shallow water. He remains the only US president with a patent.

Racing to the office

Sometimes two inventors work on the same thing at once. On 14 February 1876, Alexander Graham Bell's lawyer filed a patent for the telephone on the same day that another inventor, Elisha Gray, filed papers for a similar idea. Bell got the patent, and arguments about who really invented the telephone went on in the courts for years.

Thomas Edison held more than a thousand US patents. His long rivalry with Nikola Tesla and the Westinghouse company over electricity systems was partly a fight over patents and the money they brought.

India and the turmeric case

In 1995 the United States granted a patent to two researchers for using turmeric to help heal wounds. India's Council of Scientific and Industrial Research challenged it. It showed old Sanskrit texts and a 1953 article from an Indian medical journal proving the use was already known. In 1997 the patent was cancelled.

Why is turmeric yellow?Turmeric's yellow can turn bright red in a second, if you add a little soap.

A similar fight followed over a European patent on a fungicide made from the neem tree, which was later revoked too. To stop this happening again, India built a huge digital library of traditional medicine, translated into several languages, so patent examiners around the world can check it.

Arguments about patents

People argue about whether patents always help. Supporters say they reward invention and get new medicines made. Critics say some patents last too long, keep life-saving drugs expensive in poorer countries, or are granted for tiny tweaks to old products.

India's Patents Act of 1970, updated in 2005, includes a rule against patenting small changes to existing medicines unless they work better. This has helped India become one of the world's biggest makers of affordable generic medicines, though some drug companies have challenged it in court.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A patent lets you own an idea forever.

Truth: Patents last a limited time, usually 20 years, and then the invention is free for anyone. You also can't patent a mere idea, a law of nature or a discovery like a new plant found in a forest. You can only patent a specific new invention that works.

Try this

Invent something simple that solves a small problem at home, such as a better way to keep pencils from rolling off a desk. Write a "patent" for it, with a title, a drawing with labelled parts, and a paragraph explaining what makes it new. Then search online with an adult to see if someone has already had the same idea.
