What makes a great captain?

A captain cannot bat for everyone or bowl every ball. Yet one quiet decision, like who bowls the last over, can win or lose a World Cup.

The short answer

A great captain makes good decisions when everyone else is nervous. They read the game, notice what the other team is planning, and change their own plan fast.

They also get the best out of their players. They back people who are struggling, stay calm so the team stays calm, and take the blame when things go wrong. Skill with the bat or ball helps, but it is not the main thing.

Surprising but true

One of England's most admired Test captains, Mike Brearley, averaged under 23 runs with the bat in Test cricket, far less than most top batters. He kept his place because he was so good at leading.

How it works

Think of a captain like the driver of a crowded school bus on a busy road. The driver cannot control the traffic. But they choose the lane, the speed and when to brake, and the children feel safe if the driver is calm.

In cricket, a captain makes choices all match long:

  • 1. The toss. Bat or bowl first? The pitch, the weather and dew at night all matter.
  • 2. The batting order. Who goes in when? Sometimes a captain moves a batter up to take on a certain bowler.
  • 3. Bowling changes. Which bowler bowls which over, and against which batter? The last overs need a bowler with nerves of steel.
  • 4. Field placings. A captain places 9 fielders, since the bowler and wicketkeeper have fixed spots. A catcher close to the bat means attack; fielders near the boundary mean saving runs.
  • 5. Reviews. With the Decision Review System, a team can ask TV replays to check an umpire's decision. Each team gets only a few unsuccessful reviews, so a captain must judge when to use one.

A captain is like a chess player, guessing the opponent's next move. That idea has its own maths, called game theory.

Where it came from

Early cricket teams in England were often led by wealthy gentlemen, who were not always the best tacticians. Over time, teams learned that leading well was a skill of its own.

Some captains changed the game with bold, even risky, ideas. In 1932 and 1933, England's Douglas Jardine told his fast bowlers to aim at Australian batters' bodies, with fielders crowded on the leg side. This "Bodyline" plan won the series but caused so much anger that cricket's lawmakers later limited how many fielders could stand behind the batter on the leg side.

In 1985, England's Mike Brearley wrote The Art of Captaincy. He argued that a captain's main job is understanding people: knowing who needs a push and who needs a pat on the back.

India's story

India played its first Test at Lord's in London in 1932, led by C. K. Nayudu. In 1968, the Nawab of Pataudi led India to its first series win abroad, in New Zealand. In 1971, Ajit Wadekar's team won series in both the West Indies and England.

In 1983, Kapil Dev led India to its first World Cup. In the final at Lord's, India scored only 183. Kapil kept believing, kept attacking, and ran backwards to take a famous catch from the great Viv Richards. West Indies were all out for 140.

Around 2000, Sourav Ganguly built a fearless team by backing young players like Virender Sehwag, Yuvraj Singh, Harbhajan Singh and Zaheer Khan. Then came MS Dhoni, called Captain Cool. He won the T20 World Cup in 2007, the World Cup in 2011 and the Champions Trophy in 2013. In the IPL, he led Chennai Super Kings to five titles. Later, Virat Kohli brought fierce energy to Test cricket.

Women's captains wrote their own chapters. Mithali Raj led India to two World Cup finals, and Harmanpreet Kaur's team won the 2025 Women's World Cup.

Other sports have their leaders too. Dhyan Chand captained India's hockey team to Olympic gold at Berlin in 1936.

The numbers that matter

Some of cricket's most famous captaincy calls fit into a few small numbers.

  • - 13 runs off 6 balls: what Pakistan needed in the last over of the first T20 World Cup final, in 2007. Dhoni gave the ball to Joginder Sharma, a little-known bowler, instead of a big name. Misbah-ul-Haq tried a scoop shot and was caught. India won by 5 runs.
  • - 91 not out: Dhoni's score in the 2011 World Cup final in Mumbai. He moved himself up the order, ahead of the in-form Yuvraj Singh, to face Sri Lanka's spinners. He finished the match with a six.
  • - 2 wickets in 2 balls: what Ishant Sharma took in the 2013 Champions Trophy final against England. He had been expensive earlier, but Dhoni trusted him again late in a rain-shortened match. India won by 5 runs.
  • - 2 runs off 1 ball: what Bangladesh needed against India at Bengaluru in 2016. Dhoni took off one glove so he could run faster, then ran out the batter himself. India won by 1 run.
  • - 183: the small total Kapil Dev's India defended to win the 1983 World Cup.

What the science says

Scientists have studied pressure for more than 100 years. In 1908, Robert Yerkes and John Dodson ran experiments with mice. Their results led to a famous idea: a little pressure can help you perform better, but too much makes you worse. Picture a hill: performance rises with pressure, peaks, then falls.

When we feel danger, the body releases adrenaline. The heart races and breathing speeds up. That helps you run from a tiger but makes careful thinking harder, which is exactly what pressure does to your body in a tense final.

Psychologists have also found that experts can "choke" when they start thinking too hard about each tiny step of something they normally do without thinking. That is why many captains focus on the next ball, not the final score. Slow breathing and mindfulness can help calm the body.

Leadership research adds one more idea. In 1999, the researcher Amy Edmondson described "psychological safety": teams do better when people are not afraid to speak up or make mistakes. Later, a study inside Google found it was the most important thing its best teams shared. A captain who backs a player after a bad day is building exactly this.

What people argue about

Calm or fiery? Some people say the best captains stay calm, like Dhoni. Others point to fiery leaders like Ganguly and Kohli, whose passion lifted their teams. Most experts say both styles can work if the captain is true to themselves.

Gut or data? Teams now use analysts and numbers to plan every over. Some say the captain's job is to follow the statistics. Others say a captain's feel for the moment still beats any spreadsheet.

Win at any cost? Bodyline won a series but hurt the game's spirit. People still argue about where clever tactics end and unfair play begins.

Myths and mix-ups

"Captains are born, not made." Most great captains learned by watching, failing and trying again.

"Calm means not caring." A calm captain usually cares a lot. Staying calm is a skill they practise, so their team does not catch their nerves.

"The captain decides everything alone." Modern captains work with coaches, analysts and senior players. The captain makes the final call on the field, but the plan is built together.

What it means for you

You do not need a cricket team to be a captain. Leading a group project, a school house or a family trip uses the same skills: listen first, make a plan, stay calm when it goes wrong, and give credit to others.

When you watch a match, try watching the captain instead of the ball. Why did they move that fielder? Why that bowler now? You will start to see the game as a puzzle, the way great captains do. Keeping score with cricket statistics can help you spot patterns too.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The best player in the team always makes the best captain.

Truth: Leading is a different skill from batting or bowling. Some great players found captaincy hard, while some ordinary players, like England's Mike Brearley, became famous leaders because they read the game and the people so well.

Try this

Play any game with friends, like a relay race or a quiz, in two teams. Take turns being captain for one round each. Before the round, the captain must make one plan and explain why. Afterwards, each player says one thing the captain did that helped them. Which captains got the best from their team, and was it always the best player?
