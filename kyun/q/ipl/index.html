How did the IPL change cricket?

The IPL turned a three-hour game into one of the richest sports leagues on Earth, and changed how cricket is played everywhere.

The short answer

The Indian Premier League, or IPL, is a professional cricket competition that began in 2008. City-based teams play Twenty20 matches, with 20 overs a side, over about two months each spring. It mixes Indian players with stars from around the world.

The IPL changed cricket in three big ways. It brought huge amounts of money into the game. It made batting more daring, as players invented new shots. And it inspired copycat leagues from Australia to the United States.

Surprising but true

In the very first IPL match in 2008, Brendon McCullum hit 158 not out, a score that set the tone for the whole league.

How it started

In 2007 India won the first T20 World Cup in South Africa. Fans went wild for the short, fast format. The Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) quickly launched a league for it.

The first match was played on 18 April 2008 at the Chinnaswamy Stadium in Bengaluru. Brendon McCullum of New Zealand hit 158 not out from 73 balls for Kolkata Knight Riders. Crowds and television audiences were enormous from the start.

The league has had to move house twice. In 2009 it was played in South Africa because India was holding a general election. In 2020, during the COVID-19 pandemic, it moved to the United Arab Emirates and was played in mostly empty stadiums.

Auctions and owners

Each team is owned by a company or businesspeople and represents a city. Players are bought at an auction, where teams bid against each other with fixed budgets. Watching a young player from a small town go for crores of rupees has become a television event in itself.

An IPL team can field up to four overseas players at a time. Indian youngsters share a dressing room with world champions from other countries, which many say has made them better and braver.

Money and screens

The IPL's broadcast rights for 2023 to 2027 were sold for about ₹48,000 crore. That money pays players, builds facilities and funds Indian cricket. Most fans now watch on phones as well as television, through streaming apps.

Players from countries with smaller cricket budgets can earn more in two months of IPL than in years of playing at home. That has changed what young cricketers everywhere dream about.

A new way to bat

T20 cricket rewards risk. IPL batters began to play ramps over the wicketkeeper, reverse sweeps and switch hits, shots once seen as tricks. Team totals rose steadily. In 2024 Sunrisers Hyderabad scored 287 for 3 against Royal Challengers Bengaluru, the highest total in IPL history at the time.

Bowlers fought back with yorkers, slower balls and knuckle balls. Teams now use data analysts to plan which bowler should face which batter.

Praise and worries

Fans love the IPL's speed, music and drama. It has given hundreds of Indian players a professional living and found stars such as Jasprit Bumrah. Royal Challengers Bengaluru, one of the original teams, finally won its first title in 2025 after 17 years of trying, and won again in 2026.

It has also faced criticism. In 2013 a spot-fixing scandal led to two teams being suspended for two seasons. Some players and former cricketers worry that the money pulls talent away from Test cricket and crowds the calendar. Others argue that the IPL has brought millions of new fans to cricket in all its forms. Even kabaddi and kho-kho have copied its idea, with city teams and prime-time matches of their own.

Myth or fact?

Myth: In T20 cricket, bowlers do not matter; it is all about big hitting.

Truth: Bowlers win plenty of IPL matches. Spinners and fast bowlers with clever slower balls and yorkers are among the most valued players, and several of the league's Most Valuable Player awards have gone to bowlers or all-rounders.
