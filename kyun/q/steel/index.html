How is steel made?

Steel is mostly iron with a pinch of carbon, often less than one part in a hundred, and that pinch makes it far stronger than iron alone.

The short answer

Steel is made by turning iron ore, a reddish rock, into iron, then carefully controlling how much carbon is left in it. Pure iron is fairly soft. Iron with a lot of carbon is hard but snaps easily. Steel sits in between, with just a little carbon, usually less than 2%. That makes it strong and tough at the same time.

Most steel is made in one of two ways. Giant blast furnaces melt iron ore with coke, a form of coal, and the molten iron is then cleaned up into steel. Electric furnaces melt old scrap steel and make it new. Either way, the liquid steel is poured, cooled and rolled into sheets, bars, beams and wires. Most steel is magnetic, unlike aluminium or copper, so recycling plants can pull it out of mixed scrap with giant magnets.

Surprising but true

Steel is the most recycled material on Earth by weight. A tin can or old car may come back as part of a bridge.

From rock to iron

Iron ore is iron joined to oxygen, a lot like rust. To free the iron, steelworks pour ore, coke and limestone into the top of a blast furnace, a tower as tall as a 10-storey building. Hot air is blasted in at the bottom.

The coke burns, reaching about 1,500 °C, and takes the oxygen away from the ore. Liquid iron trickles to the bottom. The limestone grabs sand and other unwanted bits, forming a crust called slag that floats on top. Workers tap the furnace and the glowing iron flows out like orange syrup.

From iron to steel

Iron from a blast furnace has too much carbon. In a basic oxygen furnace, pure oxygen is blown into it at high speed. The oxygen burns off extra carbon in a shower of sparks, and the iron becomes steel in about 20 minutes.

Steelmakers then add small amounts of other metals for special jobs. Chromium makes stainless steel that resists rust, used for kitchen sinks and steel tiffins. Manganese makes steel tougher for rail tracks. The liquid steel is poured into moulds or cast in long glowing strips, then squeezed between rollers into shape.

A very old metal

People started using iron more than 3,000 years ago, at the start of the Iron Age. Smiths in South India made a famous high-carbon steel called wootz, which was traded far to the west and used for strong, patterned blades.

Ancient Indian metalworkers were skilled with iron too. In Delhi, an iron pillar about 7 metres tall has stood for around 1,600 years with very little rust. It is wrought iron, not steel. Scientists think a thin protective layer, helped by phosphorus in the iron and Delhi's fairly dry air, has kept it safe.

Cheap steel arrived in 1856, when Henry Bessemer in England found a way to blow air through molten iron to burn off carbon quickly. Suddenly steel could be made by the tonne, and it powered the later Industrial Revolution: railways, ships and bridges. In 1907 Jamsetji Tata's dream became Tata Steel at Jamshedpur, India's first large steel works, which produced its first steel in 1912.

Steel today

The world makes about 1.85 billion tonnes of steel a year. China makes about half. India comes second and is growing fast. The frames of skyscrapers, the bars inside concrete, cars, ships, washing machines and nails are all steel.

Making steel from ore gives off a lot of carbon dioxide, around 7 to 9% of the world's total by many estimates. Recycling scrap in electric furnaces produces far less, and steel can be recycled again and again without losing strength. Some companies are now testing steel made with hydrogen instead of coal, which gives off water vapour instead of carbon dioxide.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Stainless steel can never rust.

Truth: Stainless steel resists rust because chromium forms an invisible protective skin. Salty water, scratches or harsh cleaners can break that skin, and then even stainless steel can get rust spots.

Try this

Use a fridge magnet to test metal things around your home, such as spoons, keys, pans, coins and a tiffin box. Make two lists, one for things the magnet sticks to and one for things it doesn't. Most steel is magnetic, but some stainless steel isn't, so you may get surprises in the kitchen.
