Do whales visit India's seas?

Off India's west coast lives a small group of humpback whales that has stayed in the Arabian Sea for tens of thousands of years, cut off from all other humpbacks.

The short answer

Yes. Whales live in and pass through the seas around India, though they are rarely seen from shore. Researchers have recorded around 30 kinds of whales and dolphins in Indian waters. Large whales seen here include the blue whale, the humpback, Bryde's whale and the sperm whale.

Blue whales, the largest animals that have ever lived, are seen off the coasts of Kerala, Maharashtra and Lakshadweep, and in big numbers off southern Sri Lanka. A rare group of humpback whales lives in the Arabian Sea all year round. Whales are protected by Indian law, and it is illegal to hunt them or trade in their parts.

Surprising but true

Arabian Sea humpback whales sing and breed in the same warm waters where they feed, which makes them unusual among humpbacks, most of which travel thousands of kilometres between the two.

A whale cut off from the world

Most humpback whales spend summer feeding in cold polar waters, then swim thousands of kilometres to warm seas to breed. The Arabian Sea humpbacks don't. They stay in the warm waters off Oman, Pakistan and western India all year.

In 2014, scientists studying their DNA found that this group had been separated from other humpbacks for about 70,000 years. That may make it the most isolated whale population on Earth. Only a small number survive, probably fewer than 100. Fishermen and researchers have reported them off Gujarat, Maharashtra and Goa, and some have been heard singing on underwater microphones.

Blue giants off the coast

The blue whale can grow to about 30 metres long, a little longer than a basketball court, or about one and a half Mumbai local train coaches. It eats tiny shrimp-like animals called krill and small fish, gulping huge mouthfuls of water and pushing it out through brush-like plates.

In 2015, people in boats off Sindhudurg in Maharashtra filmed blue whales close to the coast, which surprised many. Whale watchers often see them near Mirissa and Trincomalee in Sri Lanka, not far from India's southern tip. Scientists think some of these whales may live in the northern Indian Ocean all year, instead of travelling to Antarctica.

Other whales in Indian waters

Bryde's whales, pronounced "broo-dus", are among the most common large whales near India. They prefer warm water and eat small fish like sardines. Sperm whales, with their huge square heads, dive deep around the Andaman and Nicobar Islands and off Sri Lanka, hunting squid in the dark.

Smaller toothed whales, such as pilot whales and false killer whales, also visit. Together with the river and coastal dolphins, they make India an important place for whale research, even though many species are seldom seen.

Strandings and dangers

Sometimes whales wash up on beaches, a stranding. In India, blue whales, Bryde's whales and pilot whales have stranded on beaches from Gujarat to Tamil Nadu. Scientists study each one to learn about the animal's life and what went wrong.

Whales face many dangers: getting tangled in fishing nets, being hit by ships, and noise from ships and seismic surveys that drowns out their calls. Plastic and chemical pollution add to the stress. In the past, whalers also prized ambergris, a waxy lump formed in sperm whales' guts and once prized for perfume. Selling it is now illegal in India, and police still seize smuggled lumps from time to time.

Watching and listening

Spotting whales from India is still rare, but it is getting easier. Researchers place underwater microphones that record whale calls day and night, and fishermen share photos taken on phones. Some Indian coastal states have started tracking sightings so that scientists can map where whales go.

If you ever see a whale on a boat trip, stay calm and keep your distance. A blow of spray rising from the waves may be your only clue that a giant is passing just below.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Whales are big fish.

Truth: Whales are mammals, like us. They breathe air through blowholes, give birth to live babies and feed them milk. Their ancestors were land animals that went back to the sea about 50 million years ago.

Try this

Use a measuring tape or count big steps to mark out 30 metres in a playground, park or long corridor. That is about the length of the biggest blue whales. Lie down at one end and ask friends to stand along the line, so you can see how many children fit along one whale.
