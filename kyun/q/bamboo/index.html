How does bamboo grow so fast?

Some bamboo can grow almost a metre in a single day. You could nearly watch it happen.

The short answer

Bamboo grows fast because it doesn't have to build much that is new. When a bamboo shoot pushes out of the ground, it is already as wide as it will ever be. Packed inside are all its sections, separated by rings called nodes, like the parts of a folded telescope.

Instead of making lots of new cells, the shoot stretches the ones it already has. Every section grows longer at the same time, so the whole stem shoots upwards. Some kinds of bamboo can grow close to a metre in a single day.

The energy for this sprint comes from food stored in a huge underground network of stems, called rhizomes, which link many stems together.

Surprising but true

Until 2017, Indian law counted bamboo as a tree. Botanically it is a grass, a giant cousin of rice and wheat.

A season to grow, years to harden

A bamboo stem reaches its full height in just a few months. After that it never grows taller. Over the next few years, its walls fill with tough fibres and silica, the same stuff as sand, and it becomes hard and strong.

Each year, new shoots come up from the rhizomes, so a bamboo clump keeps getting bigger. People in many parts of India and East Asia harvest the young shoots before they toughen and cook them as vegetables or pickles.

A grass, not a tree

Bamboo looks like a tree, but it is a grass, a giant cousin of rice, wheat and sugarcane. Like other grasses, it has hollow, jointed stems and long, narrow leaves. It never forms wood with yearly rings.

For a long time, Indian law treated bamboo as a tree, so farmers needed permits to cut and move bamboo even from their own land. In 2017, the law was changed so that bamboo grown outside forests is no longer counted as a tree. India has one of the largest areas of bamboo in the world, rivalled only by China.

Flowering every few decades

Most plants flower every year. Some bamboos wait for decades. One kind that grows across Mizoram and other parts of the northeast flowers roughly every 48 years. When it does, every plant of that kind flowers at almost the same time, across huge areas, and then dies.

The bamboo seeds are a feast for rats. Rat numbers explode, and when the seeds run out, the rats move into fields and eat the crops. In Mizoram this is called mautam. It brought terrible famine in 1959. When it came again around 2006 to 2008, the government and villagers were better prepared.

How the plants keep time over so many years is still a puzzle.

Building with bamboo

Bamboo is light, springy and strong for its weight, which makes it a superb building material. In the northeast of India, many homes are built on bamboo frames. In Hong Kong, workers still put up scaffolding of bamboo poles around skyscrapers. You can see how people build with local materials in homes around the world.

It makes flutes, baskets, mats, bridges and even bicycles. In the 1880s, Thomas Edison's team tested thousands of materials for light bulb filaments. A thin strip of baked bamboo from Japan glowed for over a thousand hours, and it was used in Edison's bulbs for years.

Food for pandas, homes for many

Giant pandas in China eat almost nothing but bamboo, and the little red panda of the Himalayas loves it too. In Indian forests, elephants tear down bamboo to munch the leaves, and many birds nest in the thickets.

Because it grows back quickly after cutting, bamboo can replace wood and plastic in many everyday things. Planted on bare slopes, its tangled roots also help hold soil together during heavy monsoon rain.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Bamboo is a kind of tree.

Truth: Bamboo is a grass. Its stems are hollow and jointed like other grasses, and it never grows a trunk with yearly growth rings the way trees do.

Try this

Find a bamboo stick, a broom handle made of bamboo or a picture of a bamboo stem. Count the rings, called nodes, and measure the gap between them. Then look at a stalk of grass or a piece of sugarcane. They have the same jointed design, because they belong to the same big family.
