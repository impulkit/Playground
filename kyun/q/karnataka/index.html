What is Karnataka known for?

A thousand-year-old stone statue taller than a five-storey building, India's tech capital and most of the country's coffee all share one state.

The short answer

Karnataka is a large state in southern India, stretching from the Arabian Sea coast across the forested Western Ghats to a dry, rocky plateau. Its capital, Bengaluru, is famous as India's technology hub. But the state is also known for its history, its coffee, its silk and sandalwood, and the Kannada language.

It holds the ruins of Hampi, once the capital of a great empire, the palaces of Mysuru, and ancient Jain and Hindu temples. Its hills grow most of India's coffee, and its forests shelter elephants and tigers. People speak Kannada across the state, and Tulu, Konkani and Kodava are spoken in some regions too.

Surprising but true

Karnataka grows about 70% of India's coffee, and a popular legend says it all began with seven beans smuggled in from Yemen.

A giant carved from one rock

At Shravanabelagola, a small town in Karnataka, a statue of Gommateshwara, also called Bahubali, stands on top of a hill. It is carved from a single block of granite and is about 17 m tall, taller than a five-storey building. It was made around 983 CE and is one of the most important places for Jains.

According to Jain tradition, Bahubali stood in meditation so long that vines grew up his legs, and the statue shows them climbing around him. Every 12 years or so, a huge ceremony called the Mahamastakabhisheka takes place. Priests on scaffolding pour milk, turmeric and flower petals over the statue's head.

Capital of an empire

In the 1300s to 1500s, Hampi was the capital of the Vijayanagara empire, one of the richest kingdoms in India. Travellers from Portugal and Persia described huge markets selling gems, horses and spices. Today, its ruins are spread among enormous boulders beside the Tungabhadra river.

Visitors can see a stone chariot, temples with pillars that ring like bells when tapped, and the remains of royal baths. The empire fell after a battle in 1565, and the city was looted and left to crumble. In 1986, UNESCO listed Hampi as a World Heritage Site. Nearby, at Pattadakal and Badami, even older temples and cave shrines were carved by the Chalukya kings.

Coffee and the Western Ghats

The legend goes that in the 1600s, a Sufi saint named Baba Budan smuggled seven coffee beans from Yemen and planted them in the hills near Chikkamagaluru. Whatever the true details, Karnataka now grows about 70% of India's coffee. Plantations in Kodagu (also called Coorg) and Chikkamagaluru grow coffee in the shade of taller trees, alongside pepper vines and cardamom.

These hills are part of the Western Ghats, a mountain range that catches heavy monsoon rain. Jog Falls, where the Sharavati river plunges about 253 m, roars in the rainy season. The forests are home to many species found nowhere else.

Silk, sandalwood and stories

Mysore silk, woven in shining colours with gold borders, is famous across India. So is Karnataka's sandalwood, whose fragrant oil goes into soaps and incense. Mysuru's palace and its Dasara festival draw huge crowds each year. Wooden toys from the town of Channapatna, painted with bright natural colours, are another Karnataka speciality.

On the coast, people perform Yakshagana, an all-night theatre of dance, drumming and song, with huge headdresses and bright face paint. The Kannada language has a written history stretching back well over a thousand years, and Kannada writers have won the Jnanpith, India's top literary prize, eight times. That's more than writers in most other Indian languages, a fact many in Karnataka are proud of.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Bengaluru is the only important place in Karnataka.

Truth: Bengaluru is the capital and the largest city, but Karnataka also has Mysuru's palaces, the ruins of Hampi, coffee estates in Kodagu, beaches along the coast, and the Western Ghats' forests full of elephants and tigers.
