How fast is Usain Bolt?

For a few strides in 2009, Usain Bolt ran at about 44 km/h, as fast as a car on a busy city road.

The short answer

Usain Bolt is the fastest person ever timed. In 2009, at the World Championships in Berlin, he ran 100 metres in 9.58 seconds and 200 metres in 19.19 seconds. Both are still world records.

His average speed in that 100 metres was about 37.6 km/h. Between the 60-metre and 80-metre marks, he reached about 44 km/h. That is roughly the speed of a car on a city road. A cheetah is much faster, but no human has run as fast as Bolt over that stretch.

It is worth remembering how short a 100-metre race is. It is a little shorter than a football field, and about five times the length of a cricket pitch. Bolt covered it in less time than it takes most people to tie a shoelace.

Surprising but true

As a boy in Jamaica, Usain Bolt's first love was cricket, and he wanted to be a fast bowler.

A cricket-loving boy

Bolt was born in 1986 in Sherwood Content, a small village in Trelawny, Jamaica. As a boy he loved cricket and wanted to be a fast bowler. A cricket coach at school noticed how fast he ran and suggested he try athletics.

By 15 he was winning the 200 metres at the World Junior Championships in Kingston, in front of a home crowd. He was so nervous before the race that he put his shoes on the wrong feet, according to his own account. He won anyway.

How he did it

Bolt is about 1.95 metres tall, much taller than most sprinters. Tall people usually take longer to get going, and Bolt was often slow out of the blocks. But once he got up to speed, his long legs covered about 2.4 metres with each stride.

In the 100 metres he took about 41 strides, while most rivals took 44 or 45. He also moved his legs very quickly for someone so tall. Sports scientists filmed his races to study his speed, his stride and how long his feet stayed on the ground.

Beijing, London, Rio

At the 2008 Beijing Olympics, Bolt won the 100 metres in a world record of 9.69 seconds, even though he slowed down to celebrate before the line. He also won the 200 metres in a world record.

He won both races again in London in 2012 and in Rio in 2016. No one else has won the Olympic 100 and 200 metres at three Games in a row. Fans copied his famous pose, an arm pointing up at the sky like a bolt of lightning.

Eight golds, not nine

Bolt also won relay golds with the Jamaican team. In 2017, one of his 2008 relay teammates was found to have used a banned substance, and the team lost that gold medal. Bolt himself was never found to have cheated, but he had to give back the medal. That left him with eight Olympic golds.

He also had scoliosis, a curve in the spine. He worked on strengthening the muscles around his back and hips to protect it.

The last race

Bolt retired after the 2017 World Championships in London. In his last race, a relay, he pulled up with a hamstring injury and fell to the track. It was a sad finish, but the crowd cheered him anyway.

After athletics he trained briefly with a professional football club in Australia. His records still stand more than fifteen years later. Some scientists think humans can go a little faster, perhaps under 9.5 seconds one day, but nobody knows for sure.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Tall people can't be great sprinters because they're slow to start.

Truth: Bolt was often slow out of the blocks, but his long stride made up for it later in the race. He showed that being tall can be an advantage once a sprinter gets moving.

Try this

Measure 20 metres with a tape and time a friend running it from a standing start, then from a short run-up. Divide 20 by the time in seconds to get metres per second, and multiply by 3.6 to get km/h. Compare with Bolt's top speed of about 44 km/h.
