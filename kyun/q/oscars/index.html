What are the Oscars?

The first Oscars ceremony, in 1929, lasted about 15 minutes. Today the show runs for more than three hours and is watched around the world.

The short answer

The Oscars, officially the Academy Awards, are prizes for films. They are given each year by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, a group of more than 10,000 film professionals, such as actors, directors, editors and sound designers.

Members vote for the best work of the previous year in more than 20 categories, from Best Picture to Best Visual Effects. Winners receive a golden statuette of a knight holding a sword and standing on a reel of film. The ceremony is held in Los Angeles, usually in March, and the 98th took place in 2026.

Surprising but true

Oscar winners are not allowed to sell their statuettes. Since the early 1950s, winners have had to agree to offer them back to the Academy for one dollar first.

How it began

The Academy was founded in 1927 by people from the Hollywood film industry. Two years later, on 16 May 1929, it held its first awards dinner at a hotel in Hollywood. About 270 guests attended. The winners had been announced months earlier, and the handing out took around 15 minutes.

By the 1950s the ceremony was on television. Today it is a huge live show with songs, jokes and a red carpet where photographers crowd around famous guests.

Who votes, and how

Each branch of the Academy nominates in its own area. Editors pick the editing nominees, and cinematographers choose the cinematography ones. Then all voting members can choose winners in almost every category.

Best Picture uses a special system called a preferential ballot. Voters rank the films from favourite to least favourite. If no film gets more than half the first choices, the film with the fewest is knocked out and its votes go to each voter's next choice. This repeats until one film has a majority.

The little gold man

The statuette is about 34 centimetres tall and weighs about 3.9 kilograms, roughly as heavy as a newborn baby. It is made of metal and coated in 24-carat gold. Nobody is completely sure why it is called Oscar. One popular story says an Academy librarian thought it looked like her uncle Oscar, but this has never been proved.

During the Second World War, metal was scarce, so for a few years winners got statuettes made of painted plaster. They could swap them for metal ones after the war.

India at the Oscars

In 1983 costume designer Bhanu Athaiya became the first Indian to win an Oscar, for her work on the film Gandhi. In 1992 Satyajit Ray received an honorary Oscar for his lifetime of films. He was very ill in a Kolkata hospital, so he accepted it by video.

In 2009 the British film Slumdog Millionaire, set in Mumbai, won eight Oscars. A.R. Rahman won two, for the music and for the song "Jai Ho", shared with lyricist Gulzar. Resul Pookutty won for sound mixing. In 2023 "Naatu Naatu" from the Telugu film RRR won Best Original Song, for composer M.M. Keeravani and lyricist Chandrabose. The same night, The Elephant Whisperers, about a couple in Tamil Nadu caring for an orphaned baby elephant, won Best Documentary Short.

Wins, records and arguments

Three films share the record of 11 Oscars: Ben-Hur (1959), Titanic (1997) and The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King (2003). Walt Disney won more Oscars than any other person, mostly for cartoons.

The Oscars are often criticised too. For decades, few women and people of colour were nominated, and in 2015 and 2016 the online campaign #OscarsSoWhite drew attention to this. The Academy has since added thousands of new members from more countries. Many people also point out that the Oscars mostly reward English-language films, though the Korean film Parasite became the first non-English film to win Best Picture, in 2020.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Oscar winners take home a big cash prize.

Truth: There is no prize money. Winners get the statuette, and they agree that if they ever want to sell it, they must first offer it back to the Academy for one dollar.
