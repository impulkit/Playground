What is the Statue of Unity?

At 182 m, this statue of Sardar Patel in Gujarat is about twice as tall as the Statue of Liberty, pedestal and all.

The short answer

The Statue of Unity is a giant statue of Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, the leader who helped bring more than 560 princely states together to form India in 1947. It stands 182 m tall on a small river island near Kevadia in Gujarat, now also called Ekta Nagar.

It is the tallest statue in the world. That is about as tall as a 60-storey building, and around 18 times the height of a three-storey school. It shows Patel walking, wearing a simple shawl over his kurta, and looking towards the Sardar Sarovar Dam on the Narmada river. It opened on his birthday, 31 October 2018.

Surprising but true

The statue's height, 182 m, was reportedly chosen to match the number of seats in Gujarat's state assembly.

Why Patel, and why so big?

Sardar Patel is remembered as the Iron Man of India. As the country's first Home Minister, he persuaded rulers of princely states to join India, which gave the country much of its present shape. The statue's name honours that work.

The idea was announced in 2010 by the Gujarat government. Planners wanted something people would travel to see, like the Statue of Liberty in New York. The location had a link too. Patel is often credited with first backing the idea of a big dam on the Narmada, and the statue now overlooks it.

How it was built

The design came from Ram V. Sutar, a sculptor then in his late eighties who had made hundreds of statues of Indian leaders. He started with small models, studied old photographs of Patel, and scaled them up.

Building a statue this size is closer to building a skyscraper than carving a sculpture. The core is two tall towers of reinforced concrete, inside a steel frame. Around this frame is a skin of bronze panels, each one cast separately and fitted together. The panels were cast in China and shipped to India, which caused some argument at the time.

How did Chola artists cast bronze gods?Every Chola bronze began as a figure of beeswax, and every mould was smashed to get the statue out, so no two are exactly alike.

Engineers designed it to survive powerful winds and earthquakes. A statue is harder to build than a tower because it is not a neat shape. The legs, for example, had to be made wide apart and strong enough to hold everything above them. Around 3,000 workers and engineers took about four years to finish it.

Iron from farms

Before construction began, organisers ran a campaign asking farmers across India to donate old iron farm tools, such as worn-out ploughs. The idea was that every village could add a little to the statue of the man who united the country. Many thousands of villages took part.

How it compares

The Statue of Liberty is 93 m tall including its stone pedestal, so the Statue of Unity is about twice its height. Before 2018, the tallest statue was the Spring Temple Buddha in Henan, China, at about 128 m.

At 182 m, the Statue of Unity is taller than the Qutub Minar in Delhi, about 73 m, stacked twice.

Praise and questions

Supporters say the statue honours a national hero and has brought jobs and tourists to a quiet tribal region. Several million people visit every year. Around it have grown gardens, a safari park and other attractions.

Critics have raised other points. The project cost about ₹3,000 crore, and some argued that money could have gone to schools or farms. Some Adivasi (tribal) families living nearby said they lost land or were not consulted fairly. These debates happen around many giant projects, from dams to highways.

At night, the statue is lit up, and a laser and sound show tells Patel's story on the side of the dam. It ends, as many visits do, with people craning their necks to see the top.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Statue of Unity is made of solid metal.

Truth: It is mostly hollow. Inside is a frame of steel and reinforced concrete, with lifts and a viewing gallery. The outside is covered with bronze panels.
