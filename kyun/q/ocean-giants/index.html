What are the biggest creatures in the sea?

The biggest animal that has probably ever lived isn't a dinosaur. It's swimming in the ocean right now, eating creatures smaller than your thumb.

The short answer

The biggest creature in the sea, and probably the biggest animal ever to live, is the blue whale. It can grow to about 30 metres long, as long as three school buses, and weigh more than 150 tonnes. Its heart alone weighs about as much as two grown men.

The biggest fish is the whale shark, which usually reaches about 12 metres. The heaviest animal without a backbone is the colossal squid. And if you count length, some strange, stringy animals are even longer than a blue whale.

Surprising but true

A lion's mane jellyfish found in 1870 had tentacles about 37 metres long, longer than a blue whale.

Bigger than any dinosaur

The heaviest dinosaurs, like Argentinosaurus, may have weighed somewhere around 70 tonnes. A big blue whale is about twice that. Land animals can't grow this large because their legs would have to carry all their weight. In the sea, water holds the body up.

Blue whales eat krill, shrimp-like creatures a few centimetres long. They gulp a mouthful of water bigger than their own body, then push it out through comb-like plates called baleen, trapping the krill. Studies suggest one whale can eat several tonnes in a single day during feeding season.

The gentle giant fish

Whale sharks are spotted like a starry night sky. Each pattern is unique, so scientists identify individuals from photos, a bit like fingerprints. They are filter feeders and harmless to people.

Every year, many whale sharks visit the coast of Gujarat. Fishermen once caught them in large numbers. In 2001 India made the whale shark the first fish to get the strongest legal protection, and Gujarat's fishing villages now help rescue sharks tangled in nets.

Giants of the deep

The colossal squid lives in icy waters near Antarctica. The heaviest one ever caught, in 2007, weighed about 495 kilograms. It has the biggest eyes of any known animal, each about the size of a football. In 2025 scientists filmed a young colossal squid alive in the deep sea for the first time.

The giant squid is longer and slimmer, reaching perhaps 12 metres including its long feeding tentacles. Sperm whales, the biggest animals with teeth, plunge into the dark to hunt them, and some carry round scars from squid suckers.

Why the sea grows giants

Water holds up a heavy body, so bones don't have to carry all the weight. That's why a whale stranded on a beach is in danger even while it can still breathe: out of water, its own weight presses on its lungs. Cold seas also suit big bodies, because a large animal loses heat more slowly than a small one.

Food matters too. Fossils suggest the biggest whales grew so huge only in the last few million years, as ocean currents began piling krill into rich patches. A giant mouth can scoop up a whole swarm at once, and a giant body can store fat for long trips between feeding grounds.

Giants have one weakness. They breed slowly, and a blue whale mother usually has a single calf every two or three years. That's why whaling in the 1900s brought them close to disappearing.

Long, longer, longest

Length is a stranger contest. A lion's mane jellyfish washed up in Massachusetts in 1870 had tentacles about 37 metres long. In 2020 a research ship filmed a deep-sea siphonophore off Western Australia coiled in a spiral. Scientists estimated its outer ring alone was about 47 metres long. A siphonophore isn't one animal but a colony of many tiny linked ones working as a team.

For sheer weight among bony fish, the record goes to a giant sunfish found dead near the Azores in 2021, which weighed 2,744 kilograms. And the strangest part? Millions of years ago, the seas held a shark so big that its teeth were as large as your hand, the mighty megalodon.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Whales are big fish.

Truth: Whales are mammals, like dogs and people. They breathe air through blowholes, are warm-blooded and feed their babies milk. The biggest fish is the whale shark, which is a true shark.

Try this

Measure out a blue whale. With chalk or string, mark 30 metres along a school playground or a quiet lane, about three school buses end to end. Then mark 12 metres for a whale shark and about 1.5 metres for yourself. Walk the length of each, counting your steps.
