How does the world's largest election work?

For years, election officials trekked into Gujarat's Gir forest, home of Asia's last wild lions, to set up a polling booth for a single voter.

The short answer

Every five years, India elects the 543 members of its Lok Sabha, the house of Parliament that chooses the government. In 2024, about 97 crore people were registered to vote, more than the populations of the USA and the European Union put together. About 64 crore of them actually voted.

Running it is a huge job. The Election Commission sets up more than ten lakh polling stations, trains crores of officials, and moves electronic voting machines across mountains, rivers and deserts. Voting is spread over several weeks and phases. Then, on one day, all the votes are counted, and the results come in within hours.

Surprising but true

Election rules say no voter should have to travel more than about 2 km to vote, so polling stations are set up in forests, deserts, islands and high Himalayan villages.

A booth for one voter

The rule is that no voter should have to travel more than about 2 km to reach a polling station. That leads to some unusual booths.

For many years, a polling station was set up at Banej, deep in Gujarat's Gir forest, for one voter: a priest named Bharatdas Darshandas, who looked after a temple there. A team of officials travelled through lion country to set up the booth just for him. Elsewhere, staff have hiked for days to reach Himalayan villages, crossed rivers on boats in Assam and ridden camels in the Thar Desert. One polling station at Tashigang in Himachal Pradesh, at about 4,650 metres, is among the highest in the world.

Symbols for everyone

In 1951, most Indians could not read or write. Experts around the world wondered whether a fair election was even possible. The answer was symbols. Every party got a simple picture, such as a pair of bullocks, a lamp or a hand, so voters could pick one without reading a name.

The first general election lasted from October 1951 to February 1952. About 17 crore people were eligible, and voting went smoothly enough to impress observers abroad. Symbols are still used today, on machines instead of paper.

On voting day

Voters line up, show an identity card and have their name checked against the voters' list. An official paints a line of purple indelible ink on the left index finger, which lasts for days and stops anyone voting twice. Inside the booth, the voter presses a button next to a candidate's name and symbol on the electronic voting machine.

A small printer called a VVPAT shows a paper slip for about seven seconds, so voters can check that their choice was recorded. Since 2013, voters can also choose NOTA, "None of the Above", if they don't want any candidate.

Counting and results

In 2024, voting took place in seven phases, from 19 April to 1 June. On 4 June, the machines were opened and counted across the country. Because the machines store the votes electronically, most results were known by that evening.

The party or group of parties that wins a majority of Lok Sabha seats, at least 272, forms the government, and its leader becomes Prime Minister. Turnout in 2024 was about 66 per cent, and, for only the second time, women voted at a slightly higher rate than men. Every citizen aged 18 or over gets the same single vote, rich or poor, in a city flat or a forest temple. That simple rule is what voting in any democracy rests on, and India applies it on a scale nobody else has tried.

Myth or fact?

Myth: In a country as big and poor as India was in 1951, a fair national election was impossible.

Truth: Many experts doubted it at the time, because most voters could not read. India used party symbols on ballots so anyone could vote, and the first general election in 1951–52 was widely seen as a success.

Try this

Run an election at home or in class for something small, like a class pet's name. Make a list of voters, set up a hidden corner for voting, mark each voter's finger with a washable pen so nobody votes twice, and count the votes in front of everyone. Notice how each rule makes cheating harder.
