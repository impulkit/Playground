Who was Abraham Lincoln?

He had about a year of schooling in total, taught himself law from borrowed books, and led the USA through its bloodiest war.

The short answer

Abraham Lincoln was the 16th President of the United States. He led the country through the Civil War of 1861–1865, when southern states tried to break away to protect slavery.

Lincoln held the country together, and the war ended with slavery abolished across the USA. He was killed just days after the main fighting stopped. He is remembered for his honesty and his gift with words. He believed a nation built on the idea that "all men are created equal" could not stay half slave and half free. People who met him often mentioned his jokes and stories, which he used to make a point or calm a tense room.

Surprising but true

Lincoln is the only US president to hold a patent, for a device to lift riverboats over sandbanks.

The boy in the log cabin

Lincoln was born in 1809 in a one-room log cabin in Kentucky. His family moved to Indiana and later to Illinois, clearing forest to farm. He split logs for fences, which later earned him the nickname "the Rail-Splitter".

His schooling added up to about a year, in short stretches. Books were rare on the frontier, so he borrowed whatever he could find, including the Bible and a history of George Washington. He read while walking behind the plough.

As a young man he ran a village shop, worked as a postmaster and steered a flatboat of goods down the Mississippi River to New Orleans. He taught himself law and became a lawyer in Springfield, Illinois.

Rising in politics

Lincoln served in the Illinois legislature and one term in the US Congress. In 1858 he ran for the Senate against Stephen Douglas. Their debates about whether slavery should spread to new western lands drew huge crowds. Lincoln lost that race, but people across the country now knew his name.

In 1860 he was elected president. He opposed spreading slavery, and southern states feared he would end it altogether. Before he even took office, seven states left the Union. In April 1861, fighting began.

The Civil War

The Civil War was the deadliest war in American history. Around 6 to 7 lakh soldiers died, more than in any other war the country has fought.

At first, Lincoln said his main aim was to keep the country united. As the war went on, he decided that ending slavery was both right and necessary to win. On 1 January 1863, he issued the Emancipation Proclamation, declaring enslaved people in the rebel states free. Black soldiers could now join the Union army, and almost 2 lakh did.

Two minutes at Gettysburg

In July 1863, a huge battle was fought at Gettysburg, Pennsylvania. In November, Lincoln spoke at the opening of a cemetery there. His speech lasted about two minutes. He said the nation must make sure that "government of the people, by the people, for the people, shall not perish from the earth."

The end

Lincoln was re-elected in 1864. He pushed Congress to pass the 13th Amendment to the Constitution, banning slavery for good. The main southern army surrendered on 9 April 1865.

Five days later, on 14 April, Lincoln was shot while watching a play at Ford's Theatre in Washington. He died the next morning. The 13th Amendment became law that December, ending slavery in the USA.

Lincoln's face is on the US five-dollar note, and on Mount Rushmore. In Washington, D.C., a giant statue of him sits inside the Lincoln Memorial, completed in 1922.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Lincoln's Emancipation Proclamation freed every enslaved person in the USA in 1863.

Truth: It applied only to states fighting against the Union, where Lincoln had no power to enforce it at first. Slavery ended everywhere in the country only with the 13th Amendment to the Constitution in December 1865.
