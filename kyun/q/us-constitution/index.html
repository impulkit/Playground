How does the US Constitution work?

Written in 1787, it's the oldest written national constitution still in use.

The short answer

The US Constitution is the rulebook for how the United States is governed. It was written in 1787 in Philadelphia and starts with the words "We the People".

It splits power into three branches:

  • - Congress makes the laws.
  • - The President carries out the laws.
  • - The Supreme Court decides what the laws mean.

Each branch can check the others, so no one gets too much power. This idea is called checks and balances. The President can veto, or block, a law. Congress can overturn a veto with a two-thirds vote. The courts can strike down laws that break the Constitution.

Surprising but true

India's Constitution is the longest of any country's. The US Constitution is one of the shortest, only about 4,400 words in its original form.

A hot summer in Philadelphia

After the American Revolution, the 13 former colonies first worked together under a weak agreement called the Articles of Confederation. The central government couldn't collect taxes or settle quarrels between states.

In the summer of 1787, 55 delegates met in Philadelphia, in the building now called Independence Hall. They kept the windows shut so nobody could overhear, despite the heat. George Washington presided. James Madison, who took careful notes, is often called the Father of the Constitution. On 17 September, 39 delegates signed it.

Big states and small states

The hardest argument was about power. Big states wanted seats in Congress based on population. Small states wanted every state to count equally.

The answer, called the Great Compromise, gave Congress two parts. In the House of Representatives, bigger states get more seats. In the Senate, every state gets two senators. That balance still shapes how the president is chosen, through the Electoral College.

Why did America fight for independence?The fight that created the United States began with a tax on tea, and that tea was shipped by the same company that ruled much of India.

A painful silence

The Constitution allowed slavery to continue. For counting population, it said each enslaved person would count as three-fifths of a person. Many of the men who signed it owned enslaved people.

It took a civil war to change this. The 13th Amendment ended slavery in 1865. The 14th promised equal protection of the laws, and the 15th said men could not be denied the vote because of race.

Changing the rules

The Constitution can be changed by amendments, but it's hard on purpose. An amendment needs two-thirds of both houses of Congress and the approval of three-quarters of the states. There have been only 27.

The first ten, added in 1791, are the Bill of Rights: freedoms like free speech and religion. The 19th Amendment gave women the vote in 1920. One amendment, banning alcohol in 1919, was cancelled by another in 1933.

The student who finished an amendment

In 1982, a university student in Texas named Gregory Watson wrote a paper about an amendment on how members of Congress are paid. It had been proposed in 1789 but never approved by enough states. He got a C grade.

Watson then wrote letters to state lawmakers for ten years. In 1992, enough states finally approved it, more than 200 years after it was written. It became the 27th Amendment. In 2017, his old university changed his grade to an A.

Short and long

The US Constitution is short: about 4,400 words originally. India's Constitution, which came into force in 1950, is the longest in the world. India's writers studied many countries' constitutions, including this one, and borrowed ideas such as judicial review and a bill of fundamental rights. The original American parchment is on display at the National Archives in Washington, D.C.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Declaration of Independence and the Constitution are the same document.

Truth: The Declaration of Independence, from 1776, announced that the colonies were breaking away from Britain. The Constitution, written 11 years later, set up how the new country would be governed.
