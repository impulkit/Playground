What are Feluda and Professor Shonku?

One of India's greatest film-makers also wrote dozens of stories about a sharp-eyed detective and a scientist who invented a pill that cures almost everything.

The short answer

Feluda and Professor Shonku are two characters created by the Bengali film-maker and writer Satyajit Ray. Both first appeared in Sandesh, the children's magazine his grandfather had started and his father had run.

Feluda is a private detective from Kolkata, clever, calm and very observant, who solves mysteries across India and beyond. Professor Shonku is an eccentric scientist and inventor whose adventures are told through entries in his diary. Ray wrote more than thirty stories about each, illustrated many himself, and turned two Feluda stories into films. They remain among the best-loved books in Bengali, and English translations have found readers across India.

Surprising but true

Satyajit Ray filmed the Feluda story Sonar Kella at the real golden sandstone fort of Jaisalmer, and the film helped make the town a tourist destination.

Feluda, the Bengali Sherlock

Feluda's full name is Prodosh Chandra Mitter. He first appeared in 1965 in "Feludar Goendagiri", "Feluda's Detective Work". He is a big admirer of Sherlock Holmes, does yoga, has a sharp memory and is calm in a crisis.

His stories are told by his young cousin, Tapesh, nicknamed Topshe, who follows him everywhere. From 1971, they are joined by Lalmohan Ganguly, a writer of silly thrillers who uses the pen name Jatayu. His car, his mistakes and his nervous courage give the stories much of their humour.

Feluda's cases take him to Varanasi, Darjeeling, Lucknow, Kathmandu and London. Readers learn a lot about history, art and geography along the way. Ray kept the stories free of gore, so that children could enjoy them.

Sonar Kella: the golden fortress

In Sonar Kella, a boy in Kolkata claims to remember a past life in a "golden fort" with peacocks and buried jewels. Feluda's team travels to Rajasthan, chased by criminals who want the treasure, to find the fort.

Ray filmed the story in 1974 at Jaisalmer, whose fort of yellow sandstone glows gold in the sun, one of the great Rajasthan forts. The film was a huge hit with Bengali audiences, and many began visiting Jaisalmer because of it.

Ray's second Feluda film, Joi Baba Felunath (1979), was set in Varanasi.

Professor Shonku, inventor

Trilokeshwar Shonku first appeared in 1961 in "Byomjatrir Diary", "The Diary of a Space Traveller". He lives in Giridih, a small town now in Jharkhand, with his cat, Newton, and his servant, Prahlad.

Shonku's inventions are wonderfully strange. Miracurall is a pill that cures almost any illness. The Annihilin pistol can make an attacker vanish into thin air. He builds a robot named Bidhushekhar, who develops a mind of its own. He travels to Mars in his very first story, later to remote corners of the Earth, and meets scientists from around the world.

The stories mix real science with fantasy, and Shonku is honest about failures. Like many real experiments, his inventions sometimes work in ways he never expected.

A writer who drew his own world

Ray came from a family of storytellers. His father, Sukumar Ray, wrote famous nonsense verse, and his grandfather, Upendrakishore, wrote children's stories. When Satyajit revived Sandesh in 1961, he needed stories to fill it, so he wrote them.

He was already world famous for films like Pather Panchali (1955), and in 1992 he received an honorary Oscar for his life's work. Yet many Bengali readers know him first as the man who gave them Feluda. Ray designed the covers of his books, drew the illustrations, and even created typefaces. Few writers have controlled every detail of how their stories looked.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Satyajit Ray only made films.

Truth: Ray also wrote and illustrated dozens of stories and novels, designed book covers and typefaces, composed music for his later films and edited the children's magazine Sandesh.
