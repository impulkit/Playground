What are Onam and Pongal?

On Pongal morning in Tamil Nadu, families watch a pot of rice and milk on the fire, waiting for it to boil over. When it does, everyone shouts "Pongalo Pongal!"

The short answer

Onam and Pongal are harvest festivals from South India. Onam is celebrated in Kerala in August or September, at the end of the monsoon rains. Pongal is celebrated in Tamil Nadu in mid-January, after the rice harvest. Both are times for new clothes, family feasts and giving thanks for good crops.

Each has its own customs. Onam brings flower carpets, a giant vegetarian feast and snake boat races. Pongal is named after a sweet rice dish boiled until it overflows, and one of its days honours the cattle that help on farms.

Surprising but true

In Kerala's snake boat races during the Onam season, a single boat can be more than 30 metres long, with around a hundred rowers who pull in time to songs.

Onam and King Mahabali

The best-known Onam story is about Mahabali, a generous asura king who ruled Kerala in a golden age when everyone was equal and no one went hungry. The gods grew worried about his power. Vishnu came as Vamana, a small boy-priest, and asked the king for as much land as he could cover in three steps.

Mahabali agreed. Vamana grew enormous. His first two steps covered the earth and the sky, and for the third, Mahabali offered his own head and was pushed down to the underworld. Because he was so loved, he was allowed to visit his people once a year. Onam is his homecoming.

Flowers, feasts and boats

During the ten days of Onam, families make a pookalam, a carpet of flower petals, at their doorstep. It grows larger each day. On Thiruvonam, the main day, they serve the Onasadya, a feast of often more than 20 dishes on a banana leaf, from banana chips to several kinds of payasam, a sweet pudding. It's a bit like a thali laid out on a leaf.

In the backwaters of Kerala, snake boat races, called vallam kali, are held around Onam. Each long, narrow boat carries dozens of rowers pulling in rhythm. The Nehru Trophy Boat Race on Punnamada Lake, near Alappuzha, began after Jawaharlal Nehru's visit in 1952.

Pongal, four days long

Pongal falls in the Tamil month of Thai, on the same day as Makar Sankranti in much of India, when people elsewhere fly kites. It lasts four days.

On Bhogi, the first day, families clear out old things and clean their homes. Thai Pongal, the main day, is when the new rice is cooked with milk and jaggery in a clay or brass pot, outside in the sun, until it bubbles over. The overflow is a sign of plenty. The word "pongal" itself means "boiling over".

A day for the cattle

The third day, Mattu Pongal, honours cattle. Cows and bulls are washed, their horns are painted and decorated with bells and flowers, and they are fed sweet pongal. Farmers thank them for ploughing fields and giving milk.

In some villages, this is also when jallikattu is held, a sport in which young men try to hold on to a running bull. It has been the subject of long court cases over animal welfare. The Supreme Court allowed it to continue in 2023 under rules set by the state. The fourth day, Kaanum Pongal, is for visiting relatives and going on outings.

Patterns at the doorway

In Tamil Nadu, women draw kolam at the doorstep every morning with rice flour, and the designs grow bigger and more colourful at Pongal. These patterns of dots and loops follow careful rules of symmetry, as rangoli and symmetry shows.

Because they are made of rice flour, kolams also feed ants and small birds during the day. By evening they are often half eaten, and the next morning a new one begins.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Onam and Pongal are religious festivals only for Hindus.

Truth: Both have Hindu roots, but in Kerala and Tamil Nadu they are widely celebrated as harvest and cultural festivals. Many Christians, Muslims and others join in the feasts, races and decorations.

Try this

Make a mini pookalam, a flower carpet, on a tray or a sheet of paper. Draw a circle, divide it into rings and segments, then fill each part with fallen petals or leaves of different colours. Keep the pattern symmetrical, as Onam flower carpets usually are.
