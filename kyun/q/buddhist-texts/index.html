What are the Buddhist scriptures?

For about four hundred years, the Buddha's teachings were not written down at all. Monks carried them in their memories and chanted them aloud together.

The short answer

There isn't one single Buddhist book. Instead there are several large collections, each treasured by a different branch of Buddhism. All of them hold teachings said to come from the Buddha, the Indian teacher who lived about 2,500 years ago. They also contain rules for monks and nuns, poems, stories and explanations by later scholars.

The oldest complete collection is the Pali Canon, used in Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, Cambodia and Laos. Other traditions, especially in China, Japan, Korea and Tibet, use bigger collections that include many texts first written in Sanskrit.

Surprising but true

The oldest printed book with a date on it is a Buddhist text, the Diamond Sutra, printed in China in 868 CE.

Libraries in people's heads

According to Buddhist tradition, soon after the Buddha died, about 500 monks met at Rajagriha, today's Rajgir in Bihar. His close companion Ananda recited the teachings he had heard, and another monk, Upali, recited the rules for monastic life. The others listened, checked and agreed.

For centuries after that, nobody wrote these teachings down. Groups of monks memorised them and chanted them together, often in patterns of repeated lines that made them easier to remember. Many monks today can still recite hours of text from memory.

The three baskets

The Pali Canon is also called the Tipitaka, meaning "three baskets". The first basket, the Vinaya, holds rules for monks and nuns, down to how to wear robes and share food. The second, the Sutta, holds the Buddha's talks and conversations. The third, the Abhidhamma, carefully analyses the mind.

Tradition says the canon was first written down in Sri Lanka in the 1st century BCE, on dried palm leaves. Inside the Sutta basket are some of the most loved Buddhist writings. The Dhammapada is a collection of 423 short verses about living wisely. The Jataka tales are stories of the Buddha's past lives, often as a monkey, an elephant or a deer.

Sutras that travelled

As Buddhism spread, new texts called sutras appeared, many in Sanskrit. They include the Lotus Sutra, the Heart Sutra and the Diamond Sutra, which became central in East Asia. Scholars from China walked to India to collect them. In the 600s CE, the monk Xuanzang studied at Nalanda and returned home with hundreds of texts, then spent years translating them.

Tibetan Buddhists collected their scriptures into two huge sets: the Kangyur, believed to be the Buddha's words, and the Tengyur, made up of commentaries. Together they fill hundreds of volumes.

How did the printing press change the world?Before the printing press, a single book could take a scribe months to copy by hand. Within fifty years of Gutenberg, Europe had printed millions.

Bark, paper and marble

Some of the oldest Buddhist manuscripts known are fragile scrolls of birch bark from Gandhara, in today's Pakistan and Afghanistan. They are about 2,000 years old and are now kept at the British Library in London.

The British Library also holds a copy of the Diamond Sutra found in a cave at Dunhuang in western China. It was printed from carved wooden blocks in 868 CE, and it carries the date, which makes it the oldest dated printed book known. In Mandalay, Myanmar, the whole Pali Canon was carved onto 729 marble slabs in the 1860s, each in its own small shrine. People call it the world's largest book.

Reading them today

In 1881 the Pali Text Society was founded in London to print the Pali Canon in the Roman alphabet, along with English translations. Today the texts are printed in Sinhala, Burmese, Thai and Devanagari scripts too, and much of the canon can be read free online.

In monasteries from Sri Lanka to Japan, monks still chant passages every morning and evening, and lay Buddhists listen at festivals and funerals. Many children in Theravada countries learn short Pali verses by heart, just as young monks did more than 2,000 years ago.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Buddhism has one holy book, the way many people think of the Bible or the Quran.

Truth: Different Buddhist traditions use different collections. Theravada Buddhists follow the Pali Canon, while Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Tibetan Buddhists use much larger collections with many more sutras.

Try this

Choose a short poem of four to six lines. Learn it by heart with two friends, then recite it together slowly. Notice how hearing the others keeps you on track. That is how monks kept long teachings word-perfect before they were written down.
