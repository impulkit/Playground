What does an architect do?

An architect in Kerala, Laurie Baker, left holes in his brick walls on purpose, so breezes could cool the rooms inside.

The short answer

An architect designs buildings and the spaces around them. Before any brick is laid, the architect works out how a home, school, hospital or airport will look. They plan how people will move through it, and how it will stay comfortable through hot summers and wet monsoons.

They turn a client's wishes into drawings and models, then into detailed plans builders can follow. Along the way they juggle money, safety laws, the weather and the land itself.

Surprising but true

Frank Lloyd Wright's famous house Fallingwater, finished in the late 1930s, is built right over a waterfall, so the sound of rushing water fills the house.

From a sketch to a plan

Most projects start with talking. A family wants a house with a big kitchen. A city wants a library that children will love. The architect asks questions, visits the site and studies the sunlight, slope and neighbours.

Next come quick sketches, then neater drawings to scale. A plan shows a building from above, like a map. If one centimetre on paper stands for one metre in real life, the drawing is at a scale of 1 to 100, the same idea used in maps. Many architects also build small models from card or foam, and today design software can show a 3D building that clients can explore on a screen.

Working with a team

An architect never works alone. Structural engineers check that floors, beams and foundations can carry the weight. Other specialists plan the wiring, water pipes and air conditioning. Builders then turn the drawings into concrete and steel.

During construction, the architect visits the site to answer questions and spot mistakes. A wall in the wrong place can be fixed easily on paper. Fixing it after it is built costs far more.

Architects also have to follow building codes. These are rules about how wide stairs must be, how many fire exits a cinema needs and how strong a building must be in an earthquake zone. In cities like Delhi or San Francisco, where the ground can shake, those rules shape the whole design from the first sketch.

How do skyscrapers stay standing?For a very tall building, the biggest enemy isn't its own weight. It's the wind.

Buildings that suit their place

Good architects design for the climate. Laurie Baker, who was born in England and became an Indian citizen, spent decades building in Kerala. He used local brick in clever patterns, leaving gaps called jaalis so air could flow through. His buildings stayed cool and cost far less than usual.

Balkrishna Doshi of Ahmedabad worked with the Swiss-French architect Le Corbusier on Chandigarh and later designed low-cost housing in Indore where families could add rooms as they grew. In 2018 he became the first Indian to win the Pritzker Prize, often called architecture's Nobel.

Famous buildings, famous names

In the United States, Frank Lloyd Wright designed Fallingwater, a house perched over a waterfall in Pennsylvania. Zaha Hadid, who grew up in Iraq and worked in London, designed buildings full of sweeping curves. In 2004 she became the first woman to win the Pritzker Prize.

Not all architecture is grand. Someone designed your school's corridors, the bus stop at the corner and the steps outside the post office. Small choices, like a wider doorway or a ramp, decide whether a wheelchair user can get in at all.

How to become one

In India, students usually take an entrance test such as NATA after school and then study for five years for a B.Arch degree. To call yourself an architect, you must register with the Council of Architecture, under a law passed in 1972.

In the United States, students earn an architecture degree, work for a few years under licensed architects and pass a set of exams. Drawing helps, but so does maths, curiosity and listening. The best architects spend a lot of time asking people how they live.

Myth or fact?

Myth: An architect and a civil engineer do the same job.

Truth: They work closely, but their jobs differ. The architect designs how a building looks, feels and works for people. The engineer makes sure its frame, foundations and systems are strong and safe.

Try this

Measure your bedroom with a tape or by counting your footsteps. Draw it on squared paper, letting one square stand for half a metre, and add the bed, door and window. Then move the furniture around on paper and see if you can find a better layout.
