What is Odisha famous for?

On some nights, hundreds of thousands of sea turtles crawl onto Odisha's beaches together to lay their eggs, one of the largest nesting gatherings on Earth.

The short answer

Odisha is a state on India's east coast, facing the Bay of Bengal. It's famous for the Konark Sun Temple, the Jagannath temple and Rath Yatra at Puri, and the classical dance called Odissi. Its coastline is where huge numbers of olive ridley sea turtles come to nest.

It also has one of India's richest histories. The ancient kingdom of Kalinga was here. So is Chilika Lake, where dolphins swim in brackish water, and hundreds of thousands of birds arrive each winter. And in recent years, Odisha has become known around the world for how well it protects people from cyclones.

Surprising but true

The Konark Sun Temple is built as a giant stone chariot, with 24 carved wheels that can work as sundials.

The chariot of the Sun

The Konark Sun Temple was built in the 13th century, under King Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga dynasty. It was designed as a giant chariot for Surya, the Sun god, pulled by seven stone horses on 24 huge carved wheels. Each wheel is taller than a grown adult.

Some of the wheels work as sundials: the shadows of their spokes tell the time. The temple walls are covered with carvings of dancers, musicians, animals and daily life. The main tower has collapsed, but what remains is still enormous. In 1984, UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage Site.

Rath Yatra at Puri

At Puri, on the coast, stands the temple of Jagannath, a form of Krishna worshipped with his brother Balabhadra and sister Subhadra. Every year, usually in June or July, the three deities are brought out and placed on enormous wooden chariots, rebuilt from fresh timber each year.

Hundreds of thousands of people pull the chariots by thick ropes through the streets to another temple a few kilometres away. The English word "juggernaut", meaning a huge, unstoppable force, comes from Jagannath and these giant chariots.

Turtles, dolphins and a giant lagoon

Olive ridleys are small sea turtles. On some nights, they come ashore together in vast numbers to nest, an event called an arribada, a Spanish word for "arrival". Gahirmatha and the Rushikulya river mouth in Odisha are among the most important places in the world for this. Weeks later, the hatchlings dig out and race for the waves.

Chilika Lake, south of Puri, is India's largest coastal lagoon, where seawater and river water mix. It is home to rare Irrawaddy dolphins, and to flamingos, ducks and other birds flying in from as far away as Siberia each winter.

War, peace and art

Around 261 BCE, the emperor Ashoka conquered Kalinga, in what is now Odisha. The war was so bloody that, according to his own rock inscriptions, he was filled with regret. He turned towards Buddhism and the idea of ruling through kindness. One of his edicts is carved on a rock at Dhauli, near Bhubaneswar.

Odisha's art traditions run deep. Odissi dancers use curved poses inspired by temple carvings. Painters in the village of Raghurajpur make pattachitra, detailed paintings on cloth, often showing Jagannath.

Ready for the storm

Odisha's long, flat coast is often hit by cyclones. In 1999, a super cyclone killed around 10,000 people. Afterwards, the state built hundreds of shelters, trained volunteers and planned how to move people fast.

When Cyclone Phailin struck in 2013, around a million people were evacuated, and the number of deaths stayed very low. Other states and countries have since studied what Odisha did. Each new storm still tests the plan, and each time the shelters along the shore fill with families waiting for the wind to pass.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The wheels of the Konark temple are only decoration.

Truth: They are beautifully carved, but some of them also work as sundials. The shadow cast by the spokes on the wheel's rim can be used to tell the time of day.

Try this

Fill a shallow tray with sand and bury a few marbles in a small hollow, the way a turtle buries its eggs. Smooth the sand over. Ask a friend to find them using only their fingers. Then think about how hard it is for predators, and for people counting nests.
