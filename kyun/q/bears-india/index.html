Which bears live in India?

India is home to four kinds of bear, including one that sucks up termites like a vacuum cleaner and carries its cubs on its back.

The short answer

Four kinds of bear live in India: the sloth bear, the Asiatic black bear, the Himalayan brown bear and the sun bear. Each lives in a different part of the country, from hot central forests to snowy mountain meadows.

The sloth bear is the one most Indians are likely to come across in a forest, and it is found across much of the country. The black bear and brown bear live in the Himalaya, and the small sun bear lives only in the forests of the Northeast. India has no polar bears, and no giant pandas.

Surprising but true

Baloo, the bear in Rudyard Kipling's The Jungle Book, is generally thought to be a sloth bear, the shaggy termite-eater of Indian forests.

The shaggy termite-eater

Sloth bears have long, scruffy black coats, pale snouts and a cream-coloured V or U on the chest. Their curved claws, up to about 8 centimetres long, are made for digging into hard termite mounds. Early European naturalists saw them hanging from branches and thought they were related to sloths, which is how they got the name.

Their trick is suction. A sloth bear is missing its two middle upper front teeth, so its lips and mouth work like a vacuum tube. The slurping can be heard from far away. They also love fruit and honey, and they climb trees to raid beehives.

Sloth bear mothers do something few other bears do: they carry their cubs on their backs, sometimes for months. The cubs cling to the fur as the mother walks.

Black bears and brown bears

The Asiatic black bear has a big white V on its chest, which gives it the name "moon bear". It lives in forests from Kashmir across the Himalaya to the Northeast. It eats acorns, fruit, insects and sometimes crops, which can lead to conflict with farmers.

The Himalayan brown bear is larger and lives higher up, in meadows and rocky slopes of Ladakh, Kashmir and Himachal Pradesh. In winter, food runs out. So brown bears and some black bears sleep through the coldest months in dens, a long rest called hibernation. Sloth bears and sun bears, which live in warmer places, don't need to.

The smallest bear

The sun bear is the smallest bear in the world, roughly the size of a large dog. In India it is found only in a few northeastern states, such as Mizoram and Arunachal Pradesh, and it is rarely seen. It has a golden patch on its chest that looks like a rising sun.

Sun bears have very long tongues, which they poke into holes in trees to lick out insects and honey. They spend lots of time climbing and even build nests of branches high in trees.

Bears and people

Bears and people often share the same forests, and that can be risky for both. Sloth bears are not usually hunters of people, but when startled at close range they may attack to defend themselves or their cubs. In parts of central India, many villagers have been injured this way while collecting firewood or flowers in the forest.

Forest departments teach villagers to walk in groups, carry a torch at night and make noise along forest paths. Black bears in the Himalaya sometimes raid maize fields and apple orchards, and some states pay farmers for damaged crops. Protecting bears means keeping forests healthy, with enough fruit, insects and honey that the bears have no reason to wander into villages.

From dancing bears to rescue

For centuries, some travelling performers in India caught sloth bear cubs, pierced their snouts and trained them to "dance" on ropes in the street. It was cruel, and it was banned under India's wildlife laws. Groups such as Wildlife SOS worked with the government and the performers' families to rescue the bears and help the families find other jobs. India's last known dancing bear was rescued in 2009.

Many rescued bears live at sanctuaries near Agra and Bengaluru. Wild sloth bears are also protected in reserves such as Daroji in Karnataka, near Hampi, where they roam among giant boulders. Sloth bears can be grumpy when surprised, so forest villagers often talk or sing loudly while walking to warn them.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The red panda is a kind of bear that lives in the Himalaya.

Truth: Despite its name, the red panda is not a bear. It belongs to its own family, and its closest relatives include raccoons, skunks and weasels. The giant panda of China is a bear, but it does not live in India.

Try this

Make a sloth bear straw test. Put some puffed rice in a bowl and try to lift the pieces using only a drinking straw and suction. Then try a wider straw, such as a bubble-tea straw, and see which one works better.
