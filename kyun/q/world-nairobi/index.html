What is Nairobi like?

In Nairobi, giraffes and lions roam a national park so close to the city that you can photograph a rhino with skyscrapers behind it.

The short answer

Nairobi is the capital of Kenya, in East Africa, and one of the strangest mixes of city and wild anywhere. On one side of a fence there are glass office towers, traffic jams and phone shops. On the other side, lions hunt zebras and buffaloes wallow in mud.

About 44 lakh people lived in the city at the 2019 census, and more arrive every year. It sits about 1,795 m above sea level, higher than most hill stations in India, so it rarely gets very hot even though it is close to the equator. It is a centre for business, technology, wildlife protection and the United Nations.

Surprising but true

Nairobi began in 1899 as a muddy railway camp, halfway along a line being built from the coast to Lake Victoria.

A park inside the city

Nairobi National Park opened in 1946, the first national park in Kenya. It covers about 117 square kilometres, a little over half the area of Kolkata city, and its main gate is only about 7 km from the city centre.

Giraffes, zebras, lions, cheetahs, hippos and over 400 kinds of birds live there. It is also a sanctuary for black rhinos, which are critically endangered. Three sides of the park are fenced. The southern side stays open, so animals can wander out to the wider savanna and back during their yearly migrations.

From railway camp to capital

In 1899, British engineers building a railway from Mombasa on the coast to Lake Victoria needed a base before the line climbed into steep hills. They chose a swampy spot by a cool stream. Workers from India made up much of the labour force, and many stayed. That is one reason Nairobi still has old Hindu temples, Sikh gurdwaras and families of Indian origin.

The camp grew fast. By 1907 it was the capital of British East Africa. When Kenya became independent in 1963, Nairobi stayed the capital.

The city grew in rings. The old centre has colonial-era buildings and the tall, round Kenyatta International Convention Centre. Around it spread neighbourhoods rich and poor. One of them is Kibera, among the largest informal settlements in Africa, where hundreds of thousands of people live in small homes of tin and mud brick.

A green fight

In 1989, the government planned a 60-storey tower in Uhuru Park, one of Nairobi's few big green spaces. Wangari Maathai, a scientist who had started a tree-planting movement, protested loudly. She was mocked and even arrested at times, but the tower was never built.

Later she helped save Karura Forest on the city's northern edge from being cut up for housing. Today you can walk or cycle under its trees and find a waterfall. In 2004, Maathai became the first African woman to win the Nobel Peace Prize.

The city that sends money by text

In 2007, a Kenyan phone company launched M-Pesa, a way to send money with a simple text message, even on a basic phone with no internet. A farmer could pay school fees, and a worker in the city could send cash to a grandmother in a village hundreds of kilometres away, in seconds.

It spread so fast that most Kenyan adults now use it. Tech companies opened offices in the city, and people started calling Nairobi the "Silicon Savannah". India's UPI works differently, but it chases the same idea of money that moves as easily as a message.

Orphan elephants and a UN headquarters

On the edge of the park, a charity raises baby elephants whose mothers died. Each morning visitors watch keepers feed them milk from giant bottles. Nearby, at the Giraffe Centre, you can feed rare Rothschild's giraffes from a raised platform, and they lick the food off your hand with tongues about 45 cm long.

Nairobi is also home to the headquarters of the UN Environment Programme. And far from the city, out in dry country to the north-east, Kenya runs a very different service: a library carried by camels.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Africa is one hot country full of jungle, and its cities are small.

Truth: Africa has 54 countries. Nairobi is a large, modern city high on a plateau, where nights can be cool enough for a jumper and the land around it is mostly grassland, not jungle.
