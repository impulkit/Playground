How did A.R. Rahman change film music?

He began by recording advertising jingles in a small studio at his home in Chennai. Years later, he won two Oscars in one night.

The short answer

A.R. Rahman changed Indian film music by making it sound new. In the early 1990s, he used digital recording, electronic keyboards and careful layers of sound in a way few Indian composers had tried. He mixed Carnatic music, Sufi songs, Western pop, reggae and much more.

His first film soundtrack, for the Tamil film Roja in 1992, was a huge hit and won a national award. Soon he was writing music for Hindi films too. Over the next twenty years, his songs became some of the best-loved in India. In 2009, he won two Oscars for the film Slumdog Millionaire.

Surprising but true

Rahman's father, a film music arranger, died when Rahman was nine, and the family rented out his musical instruments to get by.

A childhood surrounded by instruments

Rahman was born in 1967 in Madras, now Chennai, as A.S. Dileep Kumar. His father, R.K. Shekhar, arranged and conducted music for Malayalam and Tamil films. Young Dileep sat beside him at the keyboard.

When he was nine, his father died. To earn money, the family rented out his father's instruments. As a teenager, Dileep started working as a keyboard player for film composers, including the famous Ilaiyaraaja, and studied Western classical music. In his early twenties, he and his family embraced Sufi Islam, and he took the name Allah Rakha Rahman.

The studio at home

Rahman built a recording studio next to his home and called it Panchathan Record Inn. There he composed hundreds of advertising jingles, learning to say something catchy in thirty seconds.

He worked mostly at night and treated the studio like an instrument. He recorded parts one at a time and layered them, the way a painter adds coats of colour. His method meant he could spend weeks on a single song, adjusting tiny details until it felt right.

A new sound for Indian cinema

In 1992, the director Mani Ratnam asked him to compose music for Roja. The songs sounded fresh and clean, with crisp drums and unusual sounds. They were a huge success, first in Tamil and then dubbed into Hindi.

Hindi films followed: Rangeela, Dil Se, whose song Chaiyya Chaiyya was filmed on top of a moving train, Taal and Lagaan. His music drew on Carnatic music, Sufi singing and folk songs, along with hip-hop and electronic beats. In 1997, for fifty years of India's independence, he released the album Vande Mataram, with the song Maa Tujhe Salaam.

Two Oscars in one night

In 2008, the British director Danny Boyle made Slumdog Millionaire, set in Mumbai, and asked Rahman to compose the music. At the Academy Awards in 2009, he won Best Original Score and, with the lyricist Gulzar, Best Original Song for Jai Ho.

He went on to write music for films and stage shows in Britain and Hollywood. Earlier, he had written the music for Bombay Dreams, a musical produced in London by Andrew Lloyd Webber.

Teaching the next generation

People sometimes call Rahman "the Mozart of Madras", comparing him to Mozart for his gift with melody. He is known for being quiet and shy in interviews, and for thanking God when he wins awards.

In 2008, he founded the KM Music Conservatory in Chennai to train young musicians in both Indian and Western music. Many composers who grew up listening to his songs say he showed them that Indian film music could take ideas from anywhere and still sound Indian. His work is a big part of the story of Bollywood songs.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Slumdog Millionaire was A.R. Rahman's first big success.

Truth: Rahman had been one of India's most famous composers since his first film, Roja, in 1992. Slumdog Millionaire made him famous with many more people around the world.
