What was the real Velociraptor like?

The film version is taller than a person and covered in scales. The real one was the size of a turkey, and wore feathers.

The short answer

The real Velociraptor was a small, fast, feathered hunter from the Gobi Desert in Mongolia. It lived about 75 million years ago. A grown one was about 2 m long, but most of that was tail. It weighed about as much as a large dog.

It had a curved killing claw on each foot, sharp teeth and long arms with feathers. If you saw one today, you might first think it was a strange, toothy bird. That would not be a silly mistake, because Velociraptor was a close cousin of birds.

Surprising but true

Bumps on a Velociraptor arm bone show where big feathers once grew, the same bumps you can find on a crow's wing bone.

Found in the desert

The first Velociraptor skull was dug up in 1923 at a place called the Flaming Cliffs, in Mongolia. The team came from the American Museum of Natural History in New York. They had gone to the Gobi hunting for early humans, and found dinosaurs and nests of eggs instead.

The name, given in 1924, means "swift thief". Back then, the Gobi was a dry land of sand dunes and short-lived streams. Sudden sandstorms and collapsing dunes buried animals quickly, which is why its fossils are so well kept.

The fighting dinosaurs

In 1971, a Polish and Mongolian team found one of the most famous fossils in the world. A Velociraptor is locked in battle with a Protoceratops, a plant-eater about the size of a sheep. The raptor's foot claw is in the Protoceratops's neck. The Protoceratops has the raptor's arm in its beak.

Both died together, probably when a sand dune collapsed on them. It is a frozen moment from 70-odd million years ago, and it shows that Velociraptor really did attack animals bigger than itself. Protoceratops was an early relative of Triceratops.

A body built for grabbing

The most famous part of Velociraptor is the big curved claw on the second toe of each foot. Raptor footprints show only two toes touching the ground, so the claw was held up while walking, like a folded penknife. That kept its tip sharp.

Its skull was long and low, about as long as your forearm, with dozens of small teeth edged like a steak knife. The tail was stiffened by thin bony rods, so it worked like a balancing pole when the animal turned fast.

Some scientists think raptors used the foot claws to pin small prey down while they ate, the way a hawk or eagle does today. Velociraptor also took easy meals. One skeleton found in Mongolia has a piece of flying reptile bone in its belly. That reptile was probably too big for a Velociraptor to catch, so it may have been feeding on a body it found.

Feathers on the arms

For years, artists drew Velociraptor with scales. Then, in 2007, scientists looked closely at a Velociraptor forearm bone. They found a row of small bumps called quill knobs. In living birds, these are where strong wing feathers attach to the bone.

So Velociraptor had real, long feathers on its arms. It could not fly. It was far too heavy for its arm size. The feathers may have helped it show off, keep eggs warm, or balance while it pinned down prey.

Why films got it wrong

The raptors in the 1993 film Jurassic Park were about as tall as the actors. The film makers chose the name Velociraptor because it sounded better. Their animals were really closer in size to Deinonychus, a larger relative from North America. When the film was made, scientists had not yet found the quill knobs either.

Films about pack-hunting raptors also go further than the evidence. Some raptor footprints in China show several animals walking side by side. Nobody has proof that Velociraptor itself hunted in teams, so that part remains an open question.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Velociraptor was a tall, scaly hunter that could open doors.

Truth: The real animal was about the size of a turkey and was covered in feathers. The film monsters were made bigger on purpose, and there is no sign that any dinosaur opened doors.
