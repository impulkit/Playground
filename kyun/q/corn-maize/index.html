How did maize become a world crop?

The bhutta you buy roasted on a monsoon evening began as a scrawny wild grass in Mexico, with a cob smaller than your little finger.

The short answer

Maize, also called corn, was first farmed in southern Mexico about 9,000 years ago. Its wild ancestor is a grass called teosinte, which has tiny ears with just a few hard seeds. Over thousands of years, farmers kept planting seeds from the plants they liked best: bigger ears, softer kernels, more rows. Slowly, teosinte became maize.

For most of history, maize grew only in the Americas. After 1492, European sailors carried it across the oceans. It spread fast, because it grows quickly, gives a big harvest from a small field and copes with many climates.

Today it is grown on every continent except Antarctica, and it is the world's biggest grain crop by weight.

Surprising but true

By weight, maize is now the biggest grain crop on Earth, ahead of both rice and wheat.

A grass that needed people

Teosinte looks so different from maize that scientists argued for decades about whether they were related. DNA studies settled it. Most experts now think maize came from teosinte growing in the Balsas River valley of Mexico.

The change was so complete that maize can no longer survive alone. Its seeds are wrapped tightly in husks and stuck to the cob, so they can't scatter. Without farmers to peel and plant them, maize would die out in a few years.

People in the Americas bred thousands of local kinds: red, blue, black, speckled, popping and sweet. The Maya creation story tells that the gods made the first humans out of maize dough.

How did farming begin?The ancestor of corn was a skinny wild grass with a few rock-hard seeds. It took thousands of years of farmers' choices to turn it into a cob.

Cooking it the clever way

Eaten plain, maize is missing a B vitamin called niacin in a form the body can easily use. Farmers in Mexico and Central America solved this long ago. They soaked and cooked dried kernels with lime or wood ash. This process, called nixtamalisation, freed the niacin and made the dough easier to shape, which is why tortillas hold together.

When maize spread to Europe and parts of Africa, people often skipped this step. Where families ate little else, a disease called pellagra, which causes skin rashes and tiredness, became common. It took until the early 1900s to work out why.

Across the oceans

Columbus took maize back to Spain in 1493. Within a few decades it was growing in Italy, Turkey and Africa. Portuguese traders carried it along their sea routes, and it probably reached India in the 1500s.

It found a home here. In Punjab, winter means makki di roti with sarson ka saag. In the monsoon, street sellers roast bhutta over coals, rubbing them with lime and chilli salt. In the hills of Himachal and the north-east, maize is an everyday staple.

Corn, the giant crop

Today the world harvests well over a billion tonnes of maize a year. The USA grows the most, mainly across the Midwest "Corn Belt", followed by China. American farmers measure their harvest in bushels, and a big year's crop runs to well over 10 billion of them. India is among the top ten growers.

Most of it isn't eaten as corn on the cob. Large amounts feed chickens, pigs and cattle. In the USA, a big share is turned into ethanol, a fuel mixed into petrol. Maize also becomes corn starch, corn syrup and oil, used in thousands of foods, from ketchup to biscuits.

Small kernels, big pops

Some kinds of maize do something special. Popcorn kernels have a hard, waterproof shell. When heated, the water inside turns to steam and the kernel bursts, turning itself inside out.

People in the Americas were popping corn thousands of years ago. Archaeologists in Peru have found popped kernels more than 6,000 years old. The cinema snack in your hand comes from one of the oldest crops on the planet.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Maize has always been grown in India.

Truth: Maize comes from the Americas. It reached India only about 500 years ago, probably with Portuguese traders, and spread so well that many people now think of makki and bhutta as completely Indian.

Try this

Look at the ingredients on five packaged foods at home, such as biscuits, ketchup, breakfast cereal or soup. Count how many list maize, corn, corn starch, corn syrup or cornflour. Many kitchens hold more maize than people think.
