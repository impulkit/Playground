Why is Hanuman so loved?

As a baby, Hanuman is said to have mistaken the rising Sun for a ripe mango and leapt into the sky to eat it.

The short answer

Hanuman is the mighty monkey hero of the Ramayana, the ancient epic about Prince Rama. He is strong enough to lift a mountain and fast enough to leap across the sea. But what people love most is his loyalty. He never asks for anything for himself.

For Hindus, he is a god who protects, gives courage and removes fear. Truck drivers paint his face on their dashboards, wrestlers pray to him before a bout, and students recite his prayer before exams. Temples to him stand on roadsides, hilltops and street corners across India, and Tuesdays and Saturdays are special days for his worship.

Surprising but true

The 40-verse Hanuman Chalisa, written in the 1500s, is one of the most recited prayers in India, and millions of people know it by heart.

A baby who tried to eat the Sun

The stories start early. Hanuman's mother was Anjana and his father Kesari, and the wind god Vayu is said to be his divine father. As a hungry baby, Hanuman saw the rising Sun, thought it was a glowing fruit and jumped into the sky to grab it.

The god Indra, alarmed, struck him with a thunderbolt. Hanuman fell, hurting his jaw. In Sanskrit, hanu means jaw, which gives one explanation for his name. Vayu was so upset that he stopped the wind, and the world began to choke. To calm him, the gods gave the baby gifts: strength, speed and the power to change his size.

The great leap

In the Ramayana, the demon king Ravana kidnaps Sita, Rama's wife, and carries her to Lanka. Hanuman makes a giant leap across the ocean to find her. He gives Sita Rama's ring as proof that help is coming.

Ravana's guards catch him and set his tail on fire. Hanuman simply grows bigger, breaks free and leaps from roof to roof, setting Lanka ablaze with his burning tail. Children have loved that part of the story for centuries.

Later, when Rama's brother Lakshmana is badly wounded, Hanuman flies to the Himalaya to fetch a healing herb called sanjeevani. Unable to tell which plant it is, he lifts the whole mountain and carries it back.

What is the Ramayana?A prince, a princess, a demon king and an army of monkeys. The Ramayana has been retold for more than 2,000 years, from Indian villages to Thai palaces.

Strength with humility

What makes Hanuman different from many strong heroes is that he is humble. One famous picture shows him tearing open his chest to reveal Rama and Sita sitting in his heart. His whole strength is used in the service of others.

In the 1500s, the poet Tulsidas wrote the Hanuman Chalisa, forty verses in Awadhi, a form of Hindi. It is short enough to learn by heart, and millions of people recite it daily. It turns up in devotional music, films and even ringtones.

A hero across Asia

Hanuman's stories travelled with the Ramayana. In Thailand, he appears in the Ramakien as a clever, sometimes cheeky hero, and his figure decorates temple walls in Bangkok. In Indonesia, Hanuman dances in the famous kecak performance of Bali, while a circle of men chant "cak-cak-cak".

The grey langur monkeys of India are often called Hanuman langurs, and many people treat them kindly because of the stories. In many akharas, the traditional wrestling schools of north India, a Hanuman shrine stands beside the mud pit.

Hanuman is said to be a chiranjivi, one who lives forever, so many believe he still listens wherever the Ramayana is told. His friend in the temple next door is often Ganesha, the remover of obstacles, whose favourite stories include a race around the whole world.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Hanuman Jayanti, Hanuman's birthday festival, falls on the same day everywhere in India.

Truth: In much of north India it is celebrated on the full moon of Chaitra, in spring. In Tamil Nadu it is usually marked in the month of Margazhi, in December or January, and other regions have their own dates.
