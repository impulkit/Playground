What makes London famous?

London has the world's oldest underground railway, a castle where ravens are kept by royal tradition, and a clock bell called Big Ben that most people mistake for a tower.

The short answer

London is the capital of the United Kingdom and one of the biggest and oldest cities in Europe. It is famous for its history, from Roman walls to royal palaces. It is also famous for its red buses, black cabs and the Underground, nicknamed the Tube.

For centuries London was the centre of a huge empire, which is why it is linked with places all over the world, including India. Today about 9 million people live there, and hundreds of languages are spoken in its schools.

Surprising but true

Britain's first Indian restaurant, the Hindoostane Coffee House, opened in London in 1810.

Roman beginnings

Around 47 CE, the Romans built a bridge over the River Thames and a town beside it called Londinium. In about 60 CE, Queen Boudica of the Iceni tribe led a revolt and burned it. The Romans rebuilt it with a stone wall, parts of which still stand.

In 1066, William the Conqueror took England. He began the White Tower, the heart of the Tower of London, a few years later. The Tower has been a royal palace, a prison and a treasury. The Crown Jewels are kept there. Tradition says that if its ravens ever leave, the kingdom will fall, so at least six are always kept.

The Great Fire

In September 1666, a fire started in a baker's shop on Pudding Lane. Most houses were made of wood, packed close together, and a strong wind spread the flames. The fire burned for four days and destroyed about 13,000 houses and the old St Paul's Cathedral.

The architect Christopher Wren rebuilt St Paul's with a great dome, finished in 1710. He also designed dozens of new churches. A tall column called the Monument marks the fire. It is 61 m high, the same as its distance from where the fire began.

Under the streets

By the 1850s, London's streets were jammed with horses and carts. So engineers dug a railway underground. On 10 January 1863, the Metropolitan Railway opened, the first underground railway in the world. Later, deeper tunnels were dug, round like tubes, which gave the Tube its name.

Time, theatre and a famous bell

In 1884, countries agreed that the line of longitude through the Royal Observatory at Greenwich, in London, would be 0°. World time zones are still measured from it, so Indian time is "GMT plus five and a half hours".

Shakespeare wrote and acted in London around 1600. A copy of his open-air theatre, the Globe, was rebuilt near the old site and opened in 1997.

Why is Shakespeare still famous?About half of Shakespeare's plays might have vanished forever if two actor friends hadn't collected them into one big book after he died.

At the Houses of Parliament stands the famous clock tower. The giant bell inside, cast in 1858 and weighing more than 13 tonnes, is nicknamed Big Ben.

London and India

The East India Company had its headquarters in London, and wealth from India flowed into the city. Many Indians came to study. Gandhi trained as a lawyer in London from 1888 to 1891, and B. R. Ambedkar studied at the London School of Economics.

In 1810, Sake Dean Mahomed, from Patna, opened the Hindoostane Coffee House, Britain's first Indian restaurant. Today London has thousands of Indian, Pakistani and Bangladeshi restaurants. Indian festivals are part of city life too. Every year, Diwali on Trafalgar Square brings music, dance and food stalls to the centre of London. The city's Neasden Temple, opened in 1995, was built with stone carved in India and shipped over.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Big Ben is the name of the clock tower at the Houses of Parliament.

Truth: Big Ben is the nickname of the great bell inside the tower. The tower was called the Clock Tower and, since 2012, the Elizabeth Tower.
