Why are hornbills special?

A mother hornbill walls herself up inside a tree trunk for months, and her mate feeds her through a slit no wider than a finger.

The short answer

Hornbills are big forest birds with huge curved beaks, many topped by a hollow helmet called a casque. About 60 kinds live in Africa and Asia, and 9 in India. The largest Indian one, the great hornbill, can have wings 1.5 metres across. When it flies overhead, the air rushing through its feathers makes a loud whooshing sound, like a steam engine puffing.

They are special for three big reasons. They have one of the strangest nesting habits of any bird. They plant forests by spreading seeds. And for many communities in northeast India and the Western Ghats, they are deeply woven into culture.

Surprising but true

Zazu, the bossy bird adviser in The Lion King, is a red-billed hornbill, a smaller African cousin of India's hornbills.

A mother behind a wall

When it's time to nest, a pair of great hornbills finds a hole high up in an old tree. The female climbs inside. Then, using mud, food and her own droppings, she seals the entrance, leaving only a narrow slit. The male passes her food through this gap, sometimes coughing up dozens of figs one after another.

She stays inside for three months or more, lays her eggs and raises her chick. While she's there, she sheds her flight feathers and grows new ones. The wall keeps out snakes, monkeys and other egg thieves. If anything happens to the male, the family is in danger, which is why every nesting male matters.

Great hornbill pairs often stay together for years, and many return to the same nest tree season after season. Big old trees with holes wide enough are rare, so losing one can leave a pair with nowhere to breed.

Farmers of the forest

Hornbills eat a lot of fruit, especially figs from trees such as the banyan. They swallow fruit whole and later drop the seeds, often far from the parent tree. Some large forest trees depend on big birds like hornbills to carry their heavy seeds away.

Scientists studying hornbills in Arunachal Pradesh found that they move huge numbers of seeds every day. Because of this, people call them "farmers of the forest". Where hornbills disappear, those trees struggle to spread, and the whole forest slowly changes.

Hornbills and people

For many communities in India's northeast, the hornbill is a symbol of courage and beauty. Naga dancers wear its feathers, and Nagaland names its biggest yearly celebration the Hornbill Festival, held from 1 to 10 December. It began in 2000 and is now held at Kisama, a heritage village near Kohima, where dancers, cooks and craftspeople from all the major Naga tribes gather. Hunting for beaks and feathers once took a heavy toll. In Arunachal Pradesh, the Nyishi people now often wear headgear with beaks made of fibreglass instead of real ones.

In 2011, the Nature Conservation Foundation and the forest department began a nest adoption programme around Pakke Tiger Reserve. Members of the Nyishi community, some of them former hunters, now guard nest trees. People anywhere can pay to "adopt" a nest, and the money supports the guardians. The number of known nests protected has grown year by year.

Where to see them

Great hornbills live in the forests of the Western Ghats, the Himalayan foothills and the northeast. The great hornbill is the state bird of both Kerala and Arunachal Pradesh. In Kerala, it can be spotted in places like the Athirappilly forests. The smaller Indian grey hornbill is much easier to find. It even lives in parks in cities such as Delhi, where you may hear its squeaky call or see it flapping and gliding between trees.

Across the sea in Africa, ground hornbills walk on the savanna hunting insects and snakes, and red-billed hornbills hop around camps looking for scraps. Zazu, the fussy adviser in Disney's The Lion King, is one of these.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A hornbill's huge beak and the "helmet" on top must be very heavy.

Truth: The casque, the helmet-like part on top, is mostly hollow inside, with thin walls and a light honeycomb of bone. It may help the call sound louder. Only the helmeted hornbill of South-East Asia has a solid casque.

Try this

Hornbills spread seeds by eating fruit and dropping the seeds far away. Collect a few seeds from fruit you eat, such as papaya or tomato, wash them, and plant them in a pot of soil. Keep it moist and see how many sprout in two weeks. In a forest, the seeds a hornbill drops get the same chance to grow.
