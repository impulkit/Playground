Where do otters live in India?

India has three kinds of otter, including the smallest otter on Earth, and most people living near them have never seen one.

The short answer

Otters live along many of India's rivers, lakes, mangroves and even paddy-field canals. There are three kinds. The smooth-coated otter is the biggest and the most common, found from the Chambal river to the Kaveri and the mangrove creeks of the Sundarbans. The Eurasian otter lives in cold Himalayan streams and in the hills of the south and Northeast.

The third is the Asian small-clawed otter, the smallest otter in the world. A grown one usually weighs less than a bag of rice. It lives in forest streams of the Western Ghats, the Northeast and the eastern coast.

Most otters are shy and busiest at dawn and dusk, so even people who fish the same river for years may only spot their tracks.

Surprising but true

In parts of the Sundarbans in Bangladesh, a few fishing families still work with trained otters that chase fish into their nets.

Built for the water

An otter's body is shaped like a torpedo with legs. Its feet are webbed, and its thick tail works as a rudder. When it dives, it can close its ears and nostrils tight. Two layers of fur trap a blanket of air next to the skin, so the otter stays dry underneath even after an hour of swimming.

The best tool is on its face. Stiff whiskers pick up tiny ripples made by a moving fish, which helps in brown, muddy rivers where eyes are almost useless. Small-clawed otters hunt differently. Their claws are tiny and their fingers are nimble, so they feel under stones for crabs and snails, almost like you searching for a lost coin under a sofa.

Family life on the riverbank

Smooth-coated otters live in families: a mother, a father and their pups from one or two years. A family can be four to a dozen strong. They sleep in a den dug into the bank, called a holt, often among tree roots.

They talk all the time. Researchers have recorded chirps, whistles, squeaks and growls, each with a different job, from "stay close" to "back off". Otters also leave messages with their droppings, called spraint, on rocks along the river. Many otter watchers in Europe say fresh Eurasian otter spraint smells oddly sweet, a bit like jasmine tea.

Small-clawed otters play a strange game. In zoos and in the wild, they juggle pebbles between their paws and mouth for no clear reason. Scientists still argue about whether it is practice, boredom or simply fun.

Fishing partners in the Sundarbans

The Sundarbans delta, shared by India and Bangladesh, is one of the best otter homes in Asia. Its mangroves are full of creeks where fish hide among the roots.

On the Bangladesh side, a few fishing families still keep tame smooth-coated otters on long cords. At night the otters swim under the boat and chase fish towards a net. The method is centuries old. Only a handful of families still use it, and the number keeps shrinking as fish catches fall.

Why otters are in trouble

All three Indian otters are now listed as threatened or near threatened worldwide. Their soft fur has been smuggled for coats for decades. Sand mining tears up the riverbanks where they dig holts. Dams cut rivers into pieces, and factory waste poisons the fish they eat.

In 2019, an international wildlife treaty called CITES gave the smooth-coated and small-clawed otters its highest protection, banning nearly all trade in them. Otters are also protected under India's wildlife law.

There is good news too. Otters need clean water with lots of fish, so seeing them means a river is fairly healthy. People have started to film otter families in city lakes and canals in several Asian cities, and in India they turn up in reservoirs and wetlands where few expected them. Their cousin on the Pacific coast of North America faced near wipe-out from hunters a century ago and came back. And that sea-going cousin has a trick no Indian otter needs: it holds paws with its family while it sleeps, so nobody drifts away on the waves.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Otters are a kind of rodent, like beavers.

Truth: Otters belong to the weasel family, along with badgers, martens and the honey badger. Beavers are rodents. The two simply happen to share the same watery lifestyle.
