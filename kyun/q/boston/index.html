What is Boston known for?

In 1773, angry colonists in Boston dumped 342 chests of tea into the harbour, and the splash helped start a revolution.

The short answer

Boston, in the north-eastern US state of Massachusetts, is one of America's oldest cities. It is famous as a birthplace of the American Revolution, for its great universities, and for its long love of sport. Its old brick streets, harbour and historic buildings make American history feel close.

Nearby Cambridge is home to Harvard, the oldest university in the US, and MIT, one of the world's leading science and engineering universities. Boston also runs the world's oldest annual marathon, has the oldest major-league baseball park still in use, and opened the country's first underground railway. Many Americans see it as the city where their country's story began.

Surprising but true

Boston has the oldest public school in the United States, Boston Latin School, founded in 1635, a year before nearby Harvard.

A Puritan town on a harbour

In 1630, English Puritans, a strict Protestant group seeking a place to practise their faith, founded Boston on a hilly peninsula. They named it after Boston, a town in Lincolnshire in England, where some of them came from. The town grew fast thanks to its sheltered harbour, fishing and shipbuilding.

The Puritans cared about education, so that people could read the Bible. In 1635 they founded Boston Latin School, the oldest public school in the US that is still running. A year later, in 1636, a college was founded across the river. It was later named Harvard.

Tea in the harbour

By the 1770s, many colonists were angry at British taxes passed by a Parliament in London where they had no representatives. Their slogan was "no taxation without representation". On the night of 16 December 1773, a group of protesters boarded three ships and dumped 342 chests of tea into Boston Harbour.

Britain responded by closing the port. Tensions grew, and in April 1775 the first fighting of the Revolutionary War broke out near Boston, at Lexington and Concord. The night before, Paul Revere rode out to warn that British troops were coming. Today, the Freedom Trail, a red line painted and set in brick along the pavement, leads visitors about 4 km (2.5 miles) past 16 historic sites.

Why did America fight for independence?The fight that created the United States began with a tax on tea, and that tea was shipped by the same company that ruled much of India.

Inventors and firsts

Boston has been home to many inventions. In 1876, Alexander Graham Bell, working in Boston, made the first successful telephone call to his assistant. In 1897, the city opened the first underground railway in the United States, the Tremont Street Subway, still in use today.

Hospitals and universities in the Boston area have made big medical breakthroughs, including one of the first public demonstrations of anaesthesia during surgery, in 1846. Boston Common, which opened in 1634, is often called the oldest public park in the US.

Runners and Red Sox

The Boston Marathon was first run in 1897, a year after the first modern Olympic marathon. It is the world's oldest marathon still run every year, held each April on Patriots' Day. In 1967, Kathrine Switzer became the first woman to run it as an officially numbered entrant, even though an official tried to push her off the course.

Boston's baseball team, the Red Sox, plays at Fenway Park, which opened in 1912. Its tall left-field wall, painted green, is nicknamed the Green Monster. The Red Sox went 86 years without winning the World Series, from 1918 to 2004, and when they finally won, the whole city celebrated. Fans still talk about that year the way their great-grandparents talked about the tea in the harbour.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Boston Tea Party was a protest against high tea prices.

Truth: The Tea Act of 1773 actually made legal tea cheaper. Colonists objected because Britain was taxing them without their consent, and because one company was being given control of the tea trade.
