Why is Little Women still read?

Louisa May Alcott wrote Little Women after her publisher asked for a book for girls. She found it dull to write, and it made her famous.

The short answer

Little Women is a novel by the American writer Louisa May Alcott, published in two parts in 1868 and 1869. It follows four sisters, Meg, Jo, Beth and Amy March, as they grow up in a small Massachusetts town while their father is away serving in the American Civil War.

It is still read because the sisters feel real. They quarrel, make mistakes, worry about money, fall in love and chase dreams. Jo, who wants to be a writer and hates being told how girls should behave, has been a hero to readers for more than 150 years. The book has never gone out of print and has been made into films, plays, TV series and an opera.

Surprising but true

Readers begged Alcott to marry Jo to her rich neighbour Laurie. She refused and gave Jo a poor, older professor instead, partly out of stubbornness.

A book written to order

Alcott was not a rich, comfortable author. Her family was poor, and she had been writing to support them since she was young, including sensational thrillers under a false name.

In 1867, a publisher named Thomas Niles asked her to write a book for girls. She wasn't keen. In her journal she wrote that she was plodding on, didn't enjoy it and had never known many girls except her sisters. So she wrote about them.

She wrote at Orchard House, the family home in Concord, Massachusetts, near Boston. Part one was a success, and readers demanded more. She wrote part two in a few months.

What was the American Civil War?From 1861 to 1865, Americans fought Americans over whether the country would stay together and whether slavery would survive. More soldiers died than in any other American war.

Four sisters, four dreams

Each March sister wants something different. Meg, the eldest, longs for pretty things and a family of her own. Jo wants to write and to be free. Beth, shy and gentle, loves music and her home. Amy, the youngest, wants to be an artist and to be admired.

The book doesn't say one dream is right. It lets each sister grow in her own way. Many readers argue for years about which sister they are most like. The sisters also make their own fun. They stage dramatic home-made plays, with Jo happily playing the villains, a small slice of theatre in a cold parlour. They even run a family newspaper.

The family also struggles. Their father is away at war, and money is short. When Beth falls seriously ill, the story becomes heartbreaking. Alcott's own sister Lizzie had died young, and the sadness in those chapters is real.

Alcott's own fight

Alcott's family believed strongly in fairness. They were opponents of slavery, and their homes sheltered at least one person escaping slavery. During the Civil War, Louisa worked as a nurse in a Union army hospital in Washington, until she caught typhoid fever and was sent home. Her letters became a book, Hospital Sketches.

She also supported women's right to vote. In 1879, when women in Massachusetts were allowed to vote in elections for school boards, she was the first woman to register in Concord. Women across the USA would not get the full vote until 1920, more than 30 years after she died.

Why it still matters

Readers keep finding new things in Little Women. Some love it for its warmth and family life. Others read it for Jo's ambition and her refusal to be squeezed into a small life.

In 2019, the director Greta Gerwig made a film that made Alcott's own struggles part of the story. It brought a new wave of readers. Like Jo writing plays in the attic, Alcott took the people around her and turned them into a story that has outlived them all.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Louisa May Alcott always dreamed of writing a story for girls.

Truth: She wrote it because her publisher asked. In her journal she said she found it dull work and had never known many girls except her sisters. She drew on her own family to make it real.

Try this

Write a short "family newspaper", as the March sisters did with their Pickwick Portfolio. Include a news report about something that happened at home this week, a poem, a joke and an advertisement for a made-up product. Read it aloud at dinner.
