What are freckles and moles?

Freckles come out in summer and fade in winter, like a sunlight diary written on your skin.

The short answer

Freckles and moles are both small coloured spots on the skin, and both get their colour from melanin, the pigment behind skin colour. But they're quite different.

A freckle is a small, flat spot where the skin's pigment cells make extra melanin when sunlight hits them. Freckles often appear on the nose, cheeks and shoulders, darken in summer and fade in winter. A mole is a small cluster of pigment cells themselves. Moles can be flat or raised, brown, black or skin-coloured, and they usually stay all year round. Almost everyone has some moles. Freckles are much more common in people with fair skin, especially redheads.

Surprising but true

A freckle doesn't have extra colour-making cells. It has the same cells as the skin around it, just working much harder. A mole, by contrast, really is a little crowd of those cells.

How freckles form

Melanin is made by cells called melanocytes, which sit at the base of the outer skin. When ultraviolet light from the sun reaches them, they make more melanin to protect the skin. That's how a tan forms, usually over a few days.

In people with freckles, the melanocytes don't respond evenly. Some patches go into overdrive and make lots of pigment, while the skin between them stays pale. The result is a scatter of dots. Freckles usually first appear in early childhood and often become fewer and paler with age.

Whether you freckle depends a lot on your genes. A gene called MC1R is especially important. Certain versions of it make the body produce more of the reddish kind of melanin and less of the dark kind. The same versions are linked to red hair and skin that burns easily.

How moles form

A mole, called a naevus by doctors, forms when melanocytes gather together in a clump instead of spreading out evenly. Most moles appear during childhood and the teenage years, and some keep appearing until about age 40. A small number of babies are born with one.

A typical adult has somewhere between 10 and 40 moles. Moles can slowly change over a lifetime. Some get lighter or raised, and many fade away in old age. Having lots of sun as a child seems to increase how many moles a person develops.

Beauty spots and the evil eye

People have long given meaning to these marks. In 18th-century Europe, fashionable men and women stuck small black patches of fabric on their faces to look like beauty spots. In many Indian families, a small black dot of kajal is placed on a baby's cheek or behind the ear. It is meant to ward off the evil eye. A mole on the face is often called a til, after the small dark sesame seed. Old books on face-reading in both India and China claimed that a mole's position could predict a person's luck, though there is no scientific evidence for this.

When to show a doctor

Nearly all freckles and moles are harmless. Very rarely, a mole can turn into a type of skin cancer called melanoma. It's much more common in adults than children, and in people with fair skin who've had a lot of sun.

Doctors suggest watching moles for changes using ABCDE. A is for asymmetry, when the two halves look different. B is for a ragged or blurred border. C is for several colours or a change in colour. D is for diameter, larger than about 6 mm, the width of a pencil. E is for evolving, meaning any change in size, shape or feel.

If a mole itches, bleeds or changes, tell a trusted adult so a doctor can check it. Wearing a hat, finding shade and using sunscreen on sunny days are simple ways to keep skin healthy, freckles and all.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Freckles are just tiny moles.

Truth: They're different. Freckles are flat patches of extra pigment made by normal skin cells, and they fade without sun. Moles are clusters of pigment cells, can be flat or raised, and usually stay all year.

Try this

With a parent's permission, draw a simple outline of your arm on paper and mark where your moles or freckles are. Date it and keep it somewhere safe. In six months, compare. Freckles may have changed with the season. Moles usually stay the same, and noticing one that changes is a good reason to show an adult.
