What does a vet do?

A vet might treat a hamster in the morning, a cow in the afternoon and, on a busy day at a tiger reserve, a sleeping tiger.

The short answer

A vet, short for veterinarian, is a doctor for animals. Vets check animals for illness, give vaccines, set broken bones, do operations and advise owners on food and care. Some look after pets like dogs, cats and rabbits. Others care for cows, goats, horses and chickens on farms, or for wild animals in zoos and forests.

Vets also protect people. Many diseases, such as rabies and bird flu, can pass from animals to humans, so vets help stop outbreaks before they spread.

Surprising but true

Your doctor learns about one species. A vet has to learn how dozens of very different animals work, from parrots to buffaloes, and none of the patients can say where it hurts.

Patients who can't talk

A human doctor can ask, "Where does it hurt?" A vet can't. Instead, vets watch how an animal stands, breathes, eats and moves. They feel its belly, listen to its heart with a stethoscope and check its eyes, ears and teeth.

They also listen carefully to owners, who know what is normal for their animal. When they need more clues, vets take blood tests or X-rays, just as hospitals do for people. A limping dog might have a thorn in its paw, a sprain or a broken bone, and only careful checks will tell. Size adds to the puzzle. A dose of medicine that helps a horse could harm a kitten, so vets work out doses carefully for each animal's weight and species.

Many kinds of vets

In cities, many vets work in pet clinics, looking after dogs, cats, birds and fish. On farms, vets travel to treat cattle, buffaloes, sheep and poultry. India had about 53 crore farm animals in its 2019 livestock census, and many families depend on them for milk and income. Government vets run village clinics and vaccination drives to keep these herds healthy.

Other vets work with wildlife. In tiger reserves like Ranthambore or Kaziranga, wildlife vets treat injured animals and help move them safely. Some vets work in labs, studying diseases or checking that meat and milk are safe to eat.

How did wolves become dogs?Every dog on Earth, from a Chihuahua to a Rajapalayam hound, comes from wolves that lived alongside Ice Age hunters.

A day at a clinic

A pet clinic day often starts with check-ups and vaccines. Puppies and kittens need several jabs in their first months to protect them from diseases like rabies. Then come appointments: a cat that has stopped eating, a parrot plucking its feathers, a rabbit with overgrown teeth.

In the afternoon there may be operations, often neutering, which stops animals having unplanned babies. Emergencies can come at any time. A dog hit by a car or a cow struggling to give birth won't wait for office hours.

How vets train

The world's first veterinary school opened in Lyon, France, in 1761, partly to fight cattle diseases. Today, in India, students usually study for about five and a half years, including an internship, for a degree called BVSc and AH. They must register with the Veterinary Council of India or a state council to practise.

In the United States, students first go to college and then spend four more years at vet school to earn a DVM degree, followed by a licensing exam. Getting in is competitive. Students study anatomy, medicine and surgery for many species, much as doctors do for one.

The hard parts

Being a vet isn't only cuddling puppies. Vets see animals in pain, deal with worried or upset owners and sometimes have to put a very sick animal to sleep. They also risk bites, scratches and kicks.

Many vets say the best part is seeing an animal recover and go home. If you want to try it, helping at an animal shelter with an adult, or learning about pet care, is a good start. In villages across India, a vet's visit to a sick cow can mean a family's milk and money for the year are safe.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A dog with a warm, dry nose must be ill.

Truth: A dog's nose can be warm or dry for many harmless reasons, such as sleeping or sitting in the sun. Vets look at other signs, like eating, energy and behaviour, to tell if a dog is unwell.

Try this

Watch a pet or a street animal you know, from a safe distance, for ten minutes. Note how it breathes, walks, eats and rests. Vets learn to spot illness by knowing what normal looks like, so write down three things that seem normal for that animal.
