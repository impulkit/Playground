Is breakfast really the most important meal?

Breakfast is famous as the most important meal of the day. Part of that fame comes from cereal advertisements.

The short answer

It depends on who you are. For growing children, especially those who might not get much food otherwise, breakfast clearly matters. It gives the body and brain fuel after a long night without food and helps children concentrate in class.

For adults, the picture is less clear. Scientists who have run careful experiments find that skipping breakfast doesn't make most healthy adults heavier or less healthy, as long as they eat well later.

So breakfast is important for many people, especially kids. Whether it's the most important meal is more slogan than science.

Surprising but true

Your brain is only about 2 percent of your body's weight, but it uses about 20 percent of your energy, even while you sleep.

A hungry brain

Your brain is a heavy user of energy. It makes up about 2 percent of your weight but burns about 20 percent of your energy. Its main fuel is glucose, a sugar your body makes from food.

Overnight, your body keeps using energy for breathing, repairs and dreams while you sleep. By morning, it may have gone ten or twelve hours without food. Your liver releases stored glucose to keep the brain supplied, but those stores are limited. A meal tops them up.

Studies suggest that children who eat breakfast often find it easier to pay attention and remember things during morning lessons.

Where the slogan came from

In the late 1800s and early 1900s, American companies began selling ready-to-eat breakfast cereals. Firms such as Kellogg's promoted breakfast heavily, and advertisements told families it was the most important meal for good health.

Breakfast was already important to many people, but advertising helped turn it into a rule. Some of the early research on breakfast was even paid for by cereal makers. That doesn't make it wrong, but it is a reason to read it carefully. Our article on how numbers can mislead explains why.

What the research says

Hundreds of studies have compared people who eat breakfast with people who don't. Many find that breakfast-eaters are healthier and do better at school. But these studies have a problem: breakfast-eaters may also sleep more, exercise more or have more regular routines. Any of those could explain the difference.

Stronger evidence comes from trials, where researchers decide at random who eats breakfast. In 2019, a review of such trials in adults found that eating breakfast didn't help people lose weight. For children, trials of school breakfast programmes show benefits for attendance and attention, especially among children from poorer homes.

Breakfast for every child

Because of this, many governments give children breakfast at school. The USA made its School Breakfast Program permanent in 1975, and millions of children now eat breakfast at school every day.

India's mid-day meal scheme feeds about 11 crore children lunch at school (2025 figure). In 2022, Tamil Nadu began giving free breakfast to young children in government schools, and some other states have tried similar ideas.

What makes a good breakfast

Nutrition experts say a good breakfast mixes different nutrients. Protein and fibre help you feel full for longer. Whole grains release energy slowly. Very sugary breakfasts can leave you hungry again soon.

Many traditional breakfasts already do well. A plate of idli and sambar combines fermented rice and lentils. Poha with peanuts and vegetables, eggs with toast, or oats with fruit all work. In the end, a breakfast you enjoy and actually eat beats a perfect one you skip.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Skipping breakfast always makes people put on weight.

Truth: Careful trials in adults have found that skipping breakfast doesn't usually cause weight gain. What matters more is what and how much people eat over the whole day.

Try this

For one week, write down what you eat for breakfast and, at 11 a.m., rate your energy and hunger from 1 to 5. Look for patterns at the end. Remember it isn't a fair experiment, since many other things change from day to day, but it may tell you what suits you.
