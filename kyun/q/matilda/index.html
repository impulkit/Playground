Why do readers love Matilda?

By the age of four, Matilda had read every children's book in her village library and moved on to Dickens. Her parents hadn't noticed.

The short answer

Matilda is a children's novel by the British writer Roald Dahl, published in 1988 with drawings by Quentin Blake. It tells the story of Matilda Wormwood, a brilliant little girl whose parents ignore her, and who finds friendship and safety through books and a kind teacher, Miss Honey.

Readers love it because it is on the side of children. Matilda is small, clever and treated unfairly by grown-ups who should know better. She fights back with brains, pranks and, eventually, a magic power. The villains, her rude parents and the terrifying headmistress Miss Trunchbull, are so over the top that they are as funny as they are frightening.

Surprising but true

In Roald Dahl's first draft, Matilda was a naughty child who played nasty tricks. He rewrote the whole book and turned her into a gentle, book-loving genius.

A girl and a library

Matilda's parents prefer television and dodgy car deals. When she asks for a book, her father tells her to watch TV instead. So, at the age of four, she walks alone to the village library.

The librarian, Mrs Phelps, helps her choose. After the children's shelves, Matilda moves on to grown-up books, starting with Great Expectations by Charles Dickens. Her list includes Jane Eyre, Oliver Twist, Animal Farm and Kim by Rudyard Kipling, one of many British writers who wrote about India.

Books give Matilda a world much bigger than her house. As Dahl puts it, they carry her across the world while she sits in her little room.

The Trunchbull

At school, Matilda meets Miss Honey, a gentle teacher who sees how clever she is, and Miss Agatha Trunchbull, the headmistress. The Trunchbull is a former hammer-throwing champion who hates children. She locks pupils in the Chokey, a tall, narrow cupboard lined with spikes. When a boy named Bruce Bogtrotter is caught eating her cake, she forces him to eat an entire giant chocolate cake in front of the whole school, and he triumphantly finishes it. In one famous scene she swings a girl round by her pigtails and throws her over a fence.

Dahl's stories often have cruel adults and brave children. Some readers find them too nasty. But many children love seeing bullies face consequences. In Matilda, the meek and the clever win, and the bullies end up running away.

From naughty to kind

Dahl's first version of Matilda was very different. In the early draft, she was a wicked child who played tricks on her parents just to be mean. Dahl's editor and Dahl himself felt the story wasn't working, and he rewrote it almost completely. The new Matilda is good-hearted and loves reading. Her pranks, like putting superglue in her father's hat, only come after he has been unfair to her.

Dahl himself was a complicated figure. He wrote much-loved books, but he also made antisemitic remarks during his life. In 2020, his family publicly apologised for them.

On stage and screen

In 1996, the actor Danny DeVito directed a film of Matilda, set in the USA. In 2010, the Royal Shakespeare Company opened a musical version, with songs by the Australian comedian and composer Tim Minchin. It moved to London's West End and then to Broadway in New York, winning many awards.

In 2022, a film of the musical brought the story back to England. Its songs, like "Revolting Children" and "When I Grow Up", became favourites in school assemblies. The story's message has stayed simple: even if you are small, you can stand up for what is right.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Matilda is set in the USA.

Truth: The book takes place in an English village. The 1996 film moved the story to America, which is why many people remember it that way. The 2022 musical film went back to England.
