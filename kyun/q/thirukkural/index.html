What is the Thirukkural?

An ancient Tamil book packs advice on almost everything in life into 1,330 tiny poems of just seven words each.

The short answer

The Thirukkural, often just called the Kural, is a book of 1,330 short poems written in Tamil by a poet called Thiruvalluvar. Each poem is a two-line couplet of only seven words. Together they give advice on how to live well: how to be good, how to run a home or a country, and how to love.

It is one of the most loved books in Tamil and has been read for more than 1,500 years. Children in Tamil Nadu learn kurals by heart at school.

Surprising but true

The giant statue of Thiruvalluvar at Kanyakumari is 133 feet tall, one foot for each chapter of his book.

Tiny poems, big ideas

Every kural follows the same strict pattern, called a metre: four word-units in the first line and three in the second. That makes them easy to remember and hard to forget.

The book is split into 133 chapters, each with exactly ten kurals on one subject, such as patience, gratitude, friendship, learning or not gossiping. The first and largest groups are about virtue (Aram) and wealth (Porul). The last, about love, is called Inbam.

The very first kural says that just as the letter A comes first among letters, God comes first in the world. Kural 391 gives advice students still quote: learn thoroughly what you should learn, then live by it. Another says that friendship is not for laughing together, but for stepping in to scold a friend who is going wrong. A whole chapter praises farmers, saying that wherever the world wanders, it follows the plough.

Who was Thiruvalluvar?

Nobody knows for sure. Stories say he was a weaver from Mylapore, now part of Chennai, and that his wife Vasuki was famous for her patience. These are traditions, not records.

Even his date is argued over. Some scholars place him around 300 BCE, while many put the Kural somewhere around the 5th century CE. The book itself names no king, no city and no particular religion. Because of this, people of many faiths, and none, have claimed it as their own.

Tamil Nadu celebrates Thiruvalluvar Day in January, during the Pongal holidays. The state government even uses a Thiruvalluvar calendar, which counts its years from a traditional date for his birth, 31 BCE.

What are Onam and Pongal?On Pongal morning in Tamil Nadu, families watch a pot of rice and milk on the fire, waiting for it to boil over. When it does, everyone shouts "Pongalo Pongal!"

A book that travelled

Christian missionaries translated parts of the Kural into Latin in the 1700s. G. U. Pope, a missionary who loved Tamil, published a full English version in 1886. Today there are translations in dozens of languages, from Hindi to French and Chinese.

The Russian writer Leo Tolstoy read the Kural in German. In 1908 he quoted it in a long letter to an Indian reader, arguing for answering violence with love. A lawyer working in South Africa read that letter and printed it. His name was Mohandas Gandhi.

The Kural all around Tamil Nadu

Look up inside a Tamil Nadu government bus and you may see a kural painted above the windows. They appear on walls, in court judgements and in speeches in Parliament.

On 1 January 2000, a stone statue of Thiruvalluvar was unveiled on a rock off Kanyakumari, at India's southern tip. It is 133 feet (about 40 metres) tall, matching the 133 chapters. Its pedestal is 38 feet, the number of chapters on virtue. Few books have ever been measured in stone. The Kural is one of the oldest examples of poetry as practical advice, and much of it still fits a modern school day.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Thirukkural is only a book of moral rules.

Truth: The first two parts give advice on right living, friendship, money and leadership. The whole third part is love poetry about two people falling in love and missing each other.
