Who was Maya Angelou?

As a girl she stopped speaking for about five years. She grew up to read a poem to millions at a US president's swearing-in.

The short answer

Maya Angelou was an American writer, poet and civil rights worker. She is best known for I Know Why the Caged Bird Sings (1969), the story of her childhood. It was one of the first books by a Black American woman about her own life to become a bestseller.

She lived many lives. She was a dancer, a singer, a cook, a journalist in Africa, a teacher and a film director. In 1993 she read her poem "On the Pulse of Morning" at the inauguration of President Bill Clinton. In 2022 she became the first Black woman to appear on a US quarter-dollar coin.

Surprising but true

As a teenager in San Francisco, Maya Angelou became the city's first Black woman streetcar conductor.

A silent childhood

She was born Marguerite Annie Johnson in 1928 in St. Louis, Missouri. "Maya" came from her brother Bailey, who called her "My" for "my sister". When their parents' marriage ended, the two small children were sent to live with their grandmother in Stamps, Arkansas, a small town where Black and white people lived separate lives by law.

When she was about eight, something frightening happened to her, and afterwards she stopped speaking almost entirely for about five years. A neighbour, Bertha Flowers, gave her books and asked her to read poems aloud. Slowly, through Shakespeare and Black American poets, she found her voice again. She later said that this long silence taught her to listen, and to love the sound of words read aloud.

Streetcars, dancing and Africa

As a teenager in San Francisco she wanted to work on the city's streetcars. She kept going back to the office until they hired her, and she became the first Black woman to do the job there. She was about 16. Soon after, at 17, she became a mother, and she raised her son, Guy, largely on her own while working many different jobs. Later she toured Europe as a dancer and singer in the opera Porgy and Bess.

In the 1960s she lived in Egypt and then Ghana, writing for newspapers. Back in the USA she worked with Martin Luther King Jr. and Malcolm X in the civil rights movement. Both men were killed in the 1960s, and King died on her birthday in 1968.

What was the civil rights movement?Students, preachers, maids and six-year-olds took on unfair laws in America, and won with marches, boycotts and sitting still.

A book that broke the silence

Her friend, the writer James Baldwin, encouraged her to write about her early life. I Know Why the Caged Bird Sings told the truth about racism and about the harm done to her as a child. It was honest about painful things, which was rare at the time.

Because of that honesty, it has often been challenged in American schools and libraries. Many teachers argue the opposite: that it helps readers who have suffered feel less alone. She went on to write six more books about her life, along with many books of poetry.

Words for everyone

Her poem "Still I Rise" (1978) is about refusing to be kept down. It is read at school assemblies, protests and graduations around the world. She taught for many years at Wake Forest University in North Carolina, and received the Presidential Medal of Freedom, America's highest award for a civilian, in 2011.

She died in 2014 at the age of 86. Her readings of her poems are still easy to find, and her deep, slow voice is part of why people remember them.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Maya Angelou was mainly a poet.

Truth: She wrote poems, but she was also a dancer, singer, actor, journalist, teacher and film director. Her seven books about her own life are probably her most important work.

Try this

Pick a short poem you like and read it aloud three ways, first very fast, then very slowly, then in a whisper. Notice how the meaning seems to change. Angelou first found her voice again by reading poems aloud.
