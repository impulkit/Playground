Why is Cape Town so beautiful?

Cape Town has a flat-topped mountain in the middle of the city, and on some days a cloud pours over it like a tablecloth.

The short answer

Cape Town is squeezed between a mountain and the sea. In the middle of the city stands Table Mountain, about 1,085 m tall and almost perfectly flat on top. Around it lie white beaches, green vineyards, colourful houses and two oceans close by.

The view is only the start. The mountain is covered in plants found nowhere else, penguins waddle on a town beach, and whales swim in the bay. All of this sits in a city of about 48 lakh people (2022 census) at the south-western tip of Africa.

Surprising but true

The small Cape region has around 9,000 kinds of plants, and about 7 in every 10 grow nowhere else on Earth.

A mountain older than the Himalayas

Table Mountain's flat top is made of tough sandstone laid down at the bottom of an ancient sea, hundreds of millions of years ago. Wind and rain wore away the softer rock around it. The Himalayas, by comparison, began rising only about 50 million years ago, so Table Mountain is several times older.

When a strong south-east wind blows, moist air is pushed up over the mountain and cools into cloud. The cloud spills over the edge and melts away as it sinks. Locals call it the tablecloth. A cable car has carried visitors to the top since 1929, and its modern cars spin slowly as they rise, so every passenger gets the full view.

A whole plant kingdom in one corner

Scientists sort the world's plants into six "floral kingdoms". Most are enormous, covering whole continents. One of them, the Cape Floral Kingdom, fits into a small corner of South Africa.

It holds around 9,000 kinds of plants, and about 70 percent are endemic, found nowhere else. Table Mountain alone has more plant species than the whole of Britain. The most famous is the king protea, a flower as big as a dinner plate. Many of these plants need fire to spread, and their seeds sprout only after a wild blaze has swept through.

What are biomes?Climb a tall mountain near the equator and you pass through rainforest, cool forest, grassland and icy tundra in a single day, like walking from the tropics to the Arctic.

Penguins on the beach

At Boulders Beach, a short drive from the city, African penguins nest among big granite rocks. Swimmers sometimes share the water with them. Only two pairs settled there in 1982. The colony grew to thousands, but African penguins have declined so sharply across their range that they were listed as critically endangered in 2024.

Between about June and November, southern right whales come close to shore along the coast to have their calves.

A harder history

Khoikhoi and San people lived here for thousands of years before Europeans came. In 1488, the Portuguese sailor Bartolomeu Dias rounded the Cape. In 1652, the Dutch built a supply station so their ships sailing to India and Indonesia could pick up fresh food. It grew into a city.

Later, under the system called apartheid, people were split by skin colour and many families were forced out of their homes. On Robben Island, in the bay, Nelson Mandela spent 18 of his 27 years in prison. Today the island is a museum, and some guides are former prisoners.

The city that nearly ran dry

After three very dry years, Cape Town warned in 2018 that its taps might be switched off on "Day Zero". People were limited to 50 litres of water a day each, about what one short shower uses. Families reused bath water to flush toilets. The rains came back, and Day Zero never arrived.

The city also learned some lasting habits. Many homes now collect rainwater in tanks, gardens are planted with plants that need little watering, and hotels ask guests to take two-minute showers. Some families still reuse their bath water. Cape Town is one of several big cities, along with Chennai and São Paulo, that have faced a real risk of their taps running dry in recent years. It showed the world how quickly droughts can threaten even a rich, modern city.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Cape of Good Hope is the southernmost point of Africa.

Truth: The southernmost point is Cape Agulhas, further east. The Cape of Good Hope got famous because sailors heading round Africa to India saw it as the turning point.

Try this

Make your own tablecloth cloud. Put a book on a table to be the mountain, then gently blow steam from a cup of warm water (with an adult's help) or mist from a spray bottle over the top. Watch how the "cloud" spills down the far side.
