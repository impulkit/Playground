How do startups grow into big companies?

Flipkart began in 2007 as two young engineers in a Bengaluru flat selling books online. Eleven years later, Walmart paid about $16 billion for most of it.

The short answer

A startup is a young company trying to grow very fast, usually by finding a new way to solve a problem. Most begin with a few founders and very little money. They build a simple version of their product, see if people want it, and change it again and again.

To grow faster than their earnings allow, many startups sell small pieces of the company to investors in return for money. With that cash they hire people, build more and reach more customers. If it works, they may become giants. Most don't make it: the majority close within a few years. The few that succeed can become some of the biggest companies in the world.

Surprising but true

YouTube started in 2005 as a video dating website. When hardly anyone used it that way, its founders let people upload any video instead.

From a flat to a giant

In 2007 two former Amazon engineers, Sachin Bansal and Binny Bansal (who aren't related), started Flipkart in Bengaluru. At first they sold only books, and they sometimes delivered parcels themselves. Many Indians didn't trust paying online, so Flipkart offered cash on delivery, which became one of its biggest strengths.

Flipkart grew into one of India's largest online shops. In 2018, the American retailer Walmart bought a majority stake for about $16 billion, one of the biggest company deals in India's history.

Money for a slice

Startups raise money in rounds. Early on, friends, family or "angel investors" put in small amounts. Later come venture capital firms, which invest other people's money in many risky startups, knowing most will fail but hoping one will grow huge.

In return, investors get shares, a percentage of the company. If an investor pays ₹1 crore for 10%, the company is valued at ₹10 crore. That number is a bet on the future, not money in the bank. It's simple percentage maths, but it can make a company that has never made a profit look very rich on paper.

Unicorns and the stock market

In 2013 an American investor, Aileen Lee, used the word "unicorn" for startups worth $1 billion or more, because they seemed so rare. They aren't rare any more. India passed 100 unicorns in 2022, with names like Zerodha, Swiggy, Zomato, Byju's and Paytm among them.

Many startups eventually sell shares to the public on the stock market, an event called an IPO. Others are bought by bigger companies. Some fall back to earth. Byju's, an education company once valued at more than $20 billion, ran into deep money and legal trouble a few years later.

Changing the plan

Many successful startups ended up doing something different from what they first planned. YouTube started in 2005 as a site where people could upload dating videos. Instagram began as a check-in app called Burbn. Changing direction like this is called a pivot.

Not every startup needs investors. Zerodha, an Indian share-trading company, grew big without raising outside money, by keeping costs low and charging small fees. This is called bootstrapping.

Why some cities grow startups

Startups gather in places with universities, skilled engineers, investors and other founders to learn from. That's why Bengaluru, Delhi's surrounding region and Mumbai lead in India, and why Silicon Valley leads in the United States. In January 2016 the Indian government launched Startup India, offering tax breaks and easier rules. Today India has one of the largest numbers of startups of any country, many now in smaller cities such as Jaipur, Indore and Kochi.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Startups succeed because the founders had one brilliant idea from the start.

Truth: Many famous startups changed their idea completely, which is called a pivot. YouTube began as a dating site and Instagram began as a check-in app. Success usually comes from testing, listening to users and changing course.

Try this

Think of a small problem at your school or in your home, such as lost water bottles or messy shoe racks. Write a one-page plan for a business that could fix it. Who would pay, how much, and what would you need to start? Show it to a friend and ask for one honest criticism.
