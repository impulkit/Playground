How does India's Parliament work?

Hundreds of elected people from every corner of India meet in one building in Delhi to argue, question and make the country's laws.

The short answer

India's Parliament is the group of people who make the country's laws, decide how the government spends money, and keep a close eye on the government. It has two houses. The Lok Sabha, or House of the People, has 543 members elected directly by voters. The Rajya Sabha, or Council of States, speaks for the states and has up to 250 members.

Strictly speaking, the President of India is also part of Parliament, because no bill becomes a law without the President's signature.

Surprising but true

Rajya Sabha members are not chosen by ordinary voters. They are elected by the members of each state's assembly.

Two houses, two jobs

The Lok Sabha is the more powerful house. Every five years, India holds a general election, and whichever party or group of parties wins a majority of Lok Sabha seats forms the government. Its leader becomes Prime Minister. If the government ever loses the support of a majority in the Lok Sabha, it must resign.

The Rajya Sabha never dissolves. Its members serve six-year terms, and about a third of them retire every two years, so the house changes a little at a time. It works like a second pair of eyes, reading bills slowly and suggesting changes. For money bills, such as taxes, it can only make suggestions, and the Lok Sabha has the final word.

From bill to law

A proposed law is called a bill. A minister or member introduces it in one house. Members debate it, sometimes send it to a small committee for a closer look, and then vote. The same thing happens in the other house. When both houses agree, the bill goes to the President for assent, and it becomes an Act.

The rules for all of this are set out in the Constitution, which Parliament can change only with special, larger majorities. B. R. Ambedkar, who chaired the committee that drafted it, wanted the rules hard to bend.

Who was B.R. Ambedkar?As a boy he wasn't allowed to drink from the school tap. As a man he led the team that wrote India's Constitution.

Question Hour

A sitting of the Lok Sabha usually begins with Question Hour. Members ask ministers about anything from train accidents to vaccine supplies, and the minister must reply on the record. It is one of the main ways Parliament holds the government to account. The Speaker, an elected member, chairs the Lok Sabha and keeps order. The Vice-President of India chairs the Rajya Sabha.

Parliament usually meets in three sessions a year: the Budget session early in the year, the Monsoon session in July and August, and the Winter session near the end of the year.

A new home

For more than 90 years, Parliament met in a round building in Delhi designed by the British architects Edwin Lutyens and Herbert Baker and finished in 1927. Its outer ring of pillars made it look a bit like a giant wheel. In 2023, Parliament moved into a new triangular building next door. The new Lok Sabha chamber has a peacock theme, India's national bird, and the Rajya Sabha chamber is decorated with lotus designs.

Many voices in one room

Each state sends Lok Sabha members according to its population, so a large state like Uttar Pradesh sends 80 while Sikkim sends 1. That is why India's states matter so much at election time. Members speak in many languages, and interpreters translate debates as they happen, so an MP from Kerala can follow a speech made in Hindi or Bengali. Myth or fact?

Myth: Members of the Rajya Sabha are elected directly by the people, just like the Lok Sabha.

Truth: Most Rajya Sabha members are elected by the MLAs of each state's legislative assembly. The President also nominates 12 members known for work in fields like art, science, literature or social service.
