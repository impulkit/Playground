What is the statue above Rio de Janeiro?

High above Rio de Janeiro in Brazil, a 30 m statue spreads its arms over the city, and lightning hits it several times a year.

The short answer

The statue above Rio de Janeiro is Christ the Redeemer, called Cristo Redentor in Portuguese. It shows Jesus with his arms stretched wide, standing on top of Corcovado, a steep granite peak in the middle of the city. The statue is 30 m tall, about as high as a ten-storey building, and stands on an 8 m pedestal.

It was finished in 1931. Brazil is home to more Catholics than any other country, and for many people the statue is a religious symbol. For others it is simply the sign of Rio, seen from beaches, streets and aeroplane windows.

Surprising but true

The statue's outer skin is made of millions of small triangles of soapstone, and volunteers helped glue many of them onto cloth backing.

A statue for a birthday

In 1922 Brazil marked 100 years of independence from Portugal. Catholic groups in Rio proposed a giant statue on Corcovado and collected donations from people across the country. The idea was older. In the 1850s a priest had suggested a religious monument on the mountain, but nothing came of it for decades.

A Brazilian engineer, Heitor da Silva Costa, designed the statue and worked out how to build it. A French sculptor, Paul Landowski, made the models, and a Romanian sculptor, Gheorghe Leonida, shaped the face. The style is Art Deco, popular in the 1920s, with simple straight lines and a calm, still pose. From far away, the shape is a cross.

Built on a mountain top

Carrying heavy stone up Corcovado was the hard part. Luckily a small rack railway, with a toothed rail that lets a train climb steep slopes, had run up the mountain since 1884. It carried workers, cement and supplies to the top.

The statue was built around a frame of concrete reinforced with steel, which was still a new way to make sculpture. Workers then covered it with millions of thin, triangular pieces of soapstone. Soapstone is soft enough to cut easily but stands up well to weather. Many of the tiles were glued onto cloth before being fixed in place, and some volunteers wrote names or messages on the backs. The statue was made in sections, with the head and hands shaped separately. Its arms stretch 28 m from fingertip to fingertip, almost as wide as the statue is tall.

Struck from the sky

Standing alone on a high peak makes the statue a target for lightning. It is hit several times a year, especially in Rio's stormy summer. In 2014, one strike chipped a thumb and a finger.

The statue has lightning rods on its head and arms, with cables that lead the electricity safely down to the ground. Repair teams sometimes abseil down the arms on ropes to replace damaged soapstone.

At night, floodlights make the statue glow above the dark mountain. On special days it is lit in colours, or with images projected onto it, to mark festivals, football matches or causes the city wants to support.

A place for visitors and pilgrims

Today visitors reach the top by the same railway, by van or on foot through Tijuca National Park, one of the largest forests inside a city anywhere in the world. A small chapel inside the pedestal holds weddings and baptisms. Escalators and lifts now carry visitors up the last part of the climb.

In 2007, Christ the Redeemer was voted one of the New Seven Wonders of the World in a worldwide public poll. It shares that list with the Taj Mahal and the Great Wall of China. Other huge statues have been built since, including the Statue of Unity in Gujarat, which is about six times taller. But few have a setting like this one, with the ocean, beaches and green hills spread out far below.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Christ the Redeemer is the tallest statue of Jesus in the world.

Truth: It is the most famous, but not the tallest. Several statues are bigger, including one in Świebodzin, Poland, that is about 33 m tall without its base. Rio's statue stands out because of where it is, on a peak 700 m above the city.
