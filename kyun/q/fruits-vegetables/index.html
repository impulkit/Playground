What is the difference between a fruit and a vegetable?

To a botanist, bhindi, brinjal and cucumbers are all fruits, and a strawberry isn't even a berry.

The short answer

It depends on who you ask. To a botanist, a scientist who studies plants, a fruit is the part of a plant that grows from a flower and holds the seeds. Its job is to help those seeds spread.

"Vegetable" isn't a scientific word at all. It's a cook's word for the other parts of plants we eat: roots, stems, leaves, flowers and bulbs. So in the kitchen, fruits are usually the sweet parts we eat as snacks or dessert, and vegetables are savoury parts we cook into meals. The two lists overlap, which is why tomatoes and brinjals cause so many arguments.

Surprising but true

Bananas, grapes and tomatoes are all berries to a botanist, but strawberries and raspberries are not.

How a fruit forms

A flower has a small swollen part at its centre called the ovary, which holds tiny egg cells. When pollen from another flower reaches it, the eggs become seeds and the ovary swells into a fruit.

Some fruits are soft and juicy, like mangoes, which you can read about in the mango tree. Others are dry, like a pea pod or a coconut. Each is a different way of moving seeds away from the parent plant. Juicy fruits get eaten by animals, which drop the seeds somewhere else. Dry ones may split, float or catch the wind.

How do seeds fly?Plants can't walk, so many of them build their seeds parachutes, propellers and gliders, and let the wind do the rest.

Secret fruits in your sabzi

By the botanist's rule, many things we call vegetables are really fruits. Tomatoes, brinjals, bhindi, cucumbers, pumpkins, bottle gourds, bitter gourds, capsicums and chillies all grow from flowers and hold seeds.

The same rule makes some surprising berries. A berry, to a scientist, is a fleshy fruit grown from a single ovary with seeds inside. Grapes, guavas and bananas qualify. Strawberries and raspberries don't. Even the whole pumpkin is a kind of giant berry, botanists say.

Roots, stems, leaves and flowers

Real vegetables come from every other part of the plant. Carrots, radishes and beetroot are roots, which store food underground. Spinach, methi and cabbage are leaves. Onions and garlic are bulbs, made of thick, layered leaves.

Some are trickier. A potato looks like a root but is really a swollen stem, which is why it has "eyes" that sprout shoots, as you can read in the potato's story. Ginger and turmeric are underground stems too. Cauliflower and broccoli are tightly packed flower buds. Sugarcane is a stem full of sweet juice.

When a court decided

In 1893, the US Supreme Court was asked whether a tomato was a fruit or a vegetable. It mattered because the USA taxed imported vegetables but not fruit. The judges agreed that botanists call it a fruit, but said that ordinary people cook and serve it as a vegetable, so it should be taxed as one. The full story is in the tomato's tale.

The ruling shows that both answers can be right. Science sorts plants by how they grow. Kitchens sort them by how they taste and how they are eaten.

Why both matter for your plate

Whatever you call them, fruits and vegetables give you vitamins, minerals and fibre, which helps food move through your gut. Different colours often mean different nutrients, which is why doctors suggest eating a mix.

India grows more fruit and vegetables than almost any country except China. India's biggest fruit crop is the banana, and its biggest vegetable crop is the potato.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Strawberries are berries.

Truth: A botanist's berry grows from one flower part and has seeds inside, like a grape or banana. A strawberry's fleshy part grows from the base of the flower, and its "seeds" on the outside are actually tiny fruits of their own.

Try this

Collect a few vegetables from your kitchen with an adult's permission. Sort them into piles of roots, stems, leaves, flowers, and fruits with seeds inside. Shell a pea pod with your fingers and count the seeds. Peas are seeds, and the pod is the fruit.
