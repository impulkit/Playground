What do engineers do?

The world's highest railway arch bridge, over the Chenab river in Jammu and Kashmir, stands higher above the water than the Eiffel Tower is tall.

The short answer

Engineers solve practical problems using science and maths. A scientist might ask how something works. An engineer asks how to build something that works, safely, at a sensible cost and on time.

Civil engineers design roads, bridges, dams and buildings. Mechanical engineers design machines and engines. Electrical engineers work on power grids and circuits. Chemical engineers turn raw materials into fuels, medicines and plastics. Computer engineers design chips and software. And there are many more kinds, from engineers who design spacecraft to those who make sure clean water reaches every tap in a city.

Surprising but true

When the chief engineer of New York's Brooklyn Bridge fell ill in the 1870s, his wife Emily Roebling learned the engineering herself and passed his instructions to the builders for over a decade.

Design, test, improve

Most engineering follows a loop. First, understand the problem: who needs what, where and with what limits? Then sketch ideas and pick the best ones. Next, calculate. How heavy will it be? How strong must it be? What happens in an earthquake or a storm?

Then test. Engineers build models, run computer simulations and break things on purpose in labs. Each test reveals weaknesses. They redesign and test again. Only after many rounds does the real thing get built. Even then, engineers keep inspecting it for years to check it is behaving as expected.

The bridge above the clouds

The Chenab Rail Bridge in Jammu and Kashmir carries trains 359 metres above the river, higher above the water than the Eiffel Tower is tall. Opened in June 2025, it is the highest railway arch bridge in the world, part of a line linking the Kashmir valley to the rest of India's railways.

Its engineers had to plan for winds of over 200 km/h, earthquakes and freezing temperatures. They studied the rocks on both slopes for years. The steel arch was built out from both sides at once, using cables strung high across the gorge, until the two halves met in the middle in 2021.

Visvesvaraya and the dam

India celebrates Engineers' Day on 15 September, the birthday of M. Visvesvaraya, born in 1861 in a village in today's Karnataka. He designed automatic sluice gates to control water in reservoirs and built flood protection for Hyderabad after a disastrous flood in 1908.

As chief engineer in the kingdom of Mysore, he led the building of the Krishna Raja Sagara dam near Mysuru. Its water irrigated huge areas of farmland and supplied the city. He was awarded the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour, in 1955, and lived to the age of 100.

Emily Roebling and the Brooklyn Bridge

In New York, John Roebling designed the Brooklyn Bridge but died after an accident in 1869, before work began in earnest. His son Washington took over. In 1872 Washington became seriously ill after working in the pressurised chambers under the river, and could barely leave his room.

His wife, Emily Warren Roebling, stepped in. She studied mathematics, the strength of materials and cable construction, carried his instructions to the site and dealt with officials and engineers. When the bridge opened in 1883, she was the first person to cross it by carriage. A plaque on the bridge now honours her.

Becoming an engineer

In India, engineering is one of the most popular university subjects, and the IITs and many state colleges train hundreds of thousands of engineers a year. In the USA, engineers usually study for four years and some take exams to become licensed professionals.

Good engineers are curious about how things work and stubborn about getting them right. They also have to think about people. A brilliant design for a metro station is useless if passengers in wheelchairs cannot use it, or if it costs too much to build. The best engineering often disappears from view: you only notice the water, the power or the bridge when it fails.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Engineers just drive trains or fix engines.

Truth: In English the word "engineer" is used for many jobs, but professional engineers mainly design things, from bridges and phones to medicines and water systems, and work out how to build them safely.

Try this

Build a bridge from a sheet of paper between two books placed 20 cm apart. See how many coins it holds flat. Now fold the paper into a zigzag like a fan and try again. Engineers use shape, not just material, to make structures strong.
