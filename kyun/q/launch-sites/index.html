Where do rockets launch from?

India's first rocket was put together in a church in a Kerala fishing village and carried to the launch pad on a bicycle.

The short answer

Rockets launch from spaceports, special sites with launch pads, assembly buildings and control rooms. India's main spaceport is the Satish Dhawan Space Centre at Sriharikota, an island on the coast of Andhra Pradesh. The United States launches from Florida, California and Texas. Russia uses Baikonur in Kazakhstan, and Europe uses Kourou in French Guiana.

Engineers pick these places carefully. Most are on coasts, so that empty rocket stages fall into the sea, not on towns. Many are close to the equator, where Earth spins fastest, and most rockets head east to borrow that spin.

Surprising but true

Europe launches its big rockets from South America, at Kourou in French Guiana, because it is close to the equator.

Why the equator helps

Earth spins once a day. At the equator, the ground moves at about 1,670 km/h. Farther north or south it moves more slowly.

A rocket sitting on a pad near the equator is already zooming east at that speed. If it launches eastwards, it keeps that speed as a gift, so it needs less fuel. That's why Europe launches from Kourou, only about 5 degrees north of the equator. The Webb telescope left from there in 2021.

Sriharikota, at about 13 degrees north, has the sea on its east. Rockets fly out over the Bay of Bengal, away from people.

A church in Thumba

India's space story began in 1963 at Thumba, a fishing village near Thiruvananthapuram in Kerala. Thumba lies near the magnetic equator, a good place to study the upper atmosphere. The scientist Vikram Sarabhai chose it for a rocket station.

The local bishop and villagers agreed to give up their church, St Mary Magdalene's, for the project. The bishop's house became an office, and the church became a workshop. On 21 November 1963, a small American-made rocket called a Nike-Apache rose from Thumba. Parts were carried to the pad on bicycles and a bullock cart. The church is now a space museum.

Sriharikota

As ISRO's rockets grew bigger, it needed a larger site. In 1971 it opened a launch range on Sriharikota, a thin island between Pulicat Lake and the sea. It was later named after Satish Dhawan, a former ISRO chairman.

Every Indian rocket that has reached orbit has launched from here, including Chandrayaan-3 in 2023 and Aditya-L1 later that year. It has two launch pads, and a third is being built for heavier rockets.

How does ISRO launch a rocket?India's first rocket parts were carried to the launch pad on a bicycle. Today ISRO launches from an island and has sent 104 satellites up on a single rocket.

India's second spaceport

ISRO is building a second spaceport at Kulasekarapattinam, on the coast of Tamil Nadu. It will launch the Small Satellite Launch Vehicle, a little rocket for small satellites. The foundation stone was laid in 2024, and the site is expected to open around 2026 to 2027.

Its location solves a problem. Rockets from Sriharikota heading south to polar orbits have to curve around Sri Lanka, which wastes fuel. From Kulasekarapattinam, they can fly straight south over the sea.

Famous launch pads around the world

Kennedy Space Center in Florida has seen some of the most famous launches. Apollo 11 left from Launch Complex 39A in 1969, carrying the first people to walk on the Moon. SpaceX now launches from the same pad.

Baikonur, on the dry steppes of Kazakhstan, sent Sputnik into orbit in 1957 and Yuri Gagarin into space in 1961. Russia still rents it from Kazakhstan.

Some launches need to go north or south over the poles, for satellites that photograph the whole Earth. In the US, those often fly from Vandenberg in California, out over the Pacific.

Watching a launch

At Sriharikota, ISRO has a viewing gallery where members of the public can register to watch launches. Thousands come for big missions. From several kilometres away, you see the flash first and feel the rumble a few seconds later, because light is much faster than sound.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Rockets fly straight up into space and stay above the spot they launched from.

Truth: Rockets go up first, then tilt over and fly sideways. To stay in orbit, they must reach about 28,000 km/h sideways. A satellite in orbit is really falling around the Earth all the time.

Try this

Stick one small sticker on a ball's middle, like the equator, and one near the top. Spin the ball slowly once. Which sticker travelled farther in one turn? The middle one, which is why launching near the equator gives a rocket a bigger push.
