What makes Simone Biles special?

She is about as tall as a ten-year-old, and she does flips so hard that the rulebook has had to give them special scores.

The short answer

Simone Biles is an American gymnast and the most decorated gymnast in history. By 2024 she had won 11 Olympic medals, seven of them gold, and 30 World Championship medals. She does skills so difficult that few other women even try them, and several are named after her.

She is also famous for something braver than any flip. In 2021 she stepped back from most events at the Tokyo Olympics to protect her safety and mental health. Three years later she came back and won three gold medals in Paris.

Surprising but true

A hard start and a new home

Biles was born in Columbus, Ohio, in 1997. As a small child she spent time in foster care. Her grandparents, Ron and Nellie Biles, later adopted her and her younger sister, and raised them near Houston in Texas. She calls them Mum and Dad.

At six, on a day-care trip to a gym, she copied the older gymnasts. A coach sent a letter home suggesting she join a class. She trained with one coach, Aimee Boorman, for many years.

Power in a small body

Biles is about 1.42 metres tall, roughly the height of an average ten-year-old. A small body is easier to spin quickly, a bit like a skater who pulls in her arms to twirl faster. But her real secret is power. She jumps higher than most gymnasts, which gives her more time in the air to twist.

In gymnastics, each skill has a difficulty value. When a gymnast is the first to perform a new skill at a major competition, it can be named after her. Biles has skills named after her on the floor, the vault and the beam, including a triple-twisting double backflip.

Rio 2016

At her first Olympics, in Rio de Janeiro in 2016, Biles won four golds: team, all-around, vault and floor. She also won bronze on the beam. She was 19 and became famous around the world almost overnight.

Her floor routines were full of energy and jokes. Judges and fans could see she loved performing. She was chosen to carry the US flag at the closing ceremony.

Gymnastics scoring changed in 2006. Instead of a perfect 10, each routine now gets a difficulty score plus an execution score. That lets a gymnast like Biles gain extra points by trying harder skills, if she can land them cleanly.

The twisties

At the Tokyo Olympics, held in 2021, Biles was expected to win many golds. During the team final she lost her sense of where she was in the air, a problem gymnasts call "the twisties". In a sport where a bad landing can break a neck, that is dangerous.

She withdrew from several events. Some people criticised her, but many athletes and doctors praised her for putting safety first. She returned to win bronze on the beam. Her decision started a worldwide conversation about athletes' mental health.

Paris 2024

In Paris in 2024 Biles won gold in the team event, the all-around and the vault, and silver on the floor. She was 27, old for a top gymnast. She has said therapy helped her return.

Biles has also spoken out to protect young athletes, after the team doctor of USA Gymnastics was jailed for abusing many gymnasts, including her. She wants children in sport to feel safe and to be heard. In 2023 she married the American football player Jonathan Owens, and she still trains at a gym her family owns near Houston.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Being small makes gymnastics easy.

Truth: A short body spins faster, which helps with flips, but it still needs huge strength. Biles can lift and launch her own body weight with power that many much bigger athletes cannot match.

Try this

Stand on one foot with your arms out and count how long you can balance. Then try it with your eyes closed. Most people wobble much sooner. Gymnasts train their eyes, inner ears and muscles together so they can balance even while spinning.
