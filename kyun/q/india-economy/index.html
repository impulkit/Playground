How big is India's economy?

India's economy is one of the six biggest in the world, yet if you share it out among all its people, each gets far less than in most countries.

The short answer

Economists usually measure an economy with GDP, short for gross domestic product. It is the total value of everything a country produces in a year, from rice and cars to haircuts and software. In 2025, India's GDP was about 3.9 trillion US dollars, according to IMF estimates published in April 2026. In Indian numbers, that is roughly 345 lakh crore rupees.

That places India among the six largest economies in the world, after the USA, China, Germany, Japan and the UK. The order shifts from year to year, partly because of exchange rates. If you adjust for the fact that things cost less in India, its economy is the third largest. But India also has about 1.4 billion people, so the average income per person is still modest.

Surprising but true

In 2025 the rupee fell against the dollar, so India's economy looked smaller in dollars even though it grew by roughly 8 to 9% in rupees that year.

Big but uneven

Divide India's GDP by its population and each person gets less than 3,000 dollars a year. In the USA, the figure is well over 80,000 dollars. Averages also hide differences. Some Indians are among the richest people on Earth, while millions of families live on very little.

There are big gaps between states too. Goa, Delhi and Karnataka produce far more per person than Bihar or Uttar Pradesh. Cities like Bengaluru, Mumbai and Hyderabad pull in workers from across the country, a bit like Silicon Valley or New York in the US economy.

What India makes

About half of India's GDP comes from services: banking, shops, transport, phone companies, hospitals and software. Companies in Bengaluru and Pune write code and run computer systems for customers around the world.

Factories make cars, medicines, steel, clothes and, more and more, smartphones. India is one of the world's biggest makers of generic medicines and vaccines. It is also the world's largest market for motorcycles and scooters, and its factories turn out millions every year. Farming produces less than a fifth of GDP, but it still employed about 43% of workers in 2025. That gap is one reason many farm families earn little.

Money also flows in from Indians abroad. Millions of Indians work in the Gulf, the USA, the UK and elsewhere, and many send money home to their families. India receives more of these payments, called remittances, than any other country: about 129 billion dollars in 2024, by World Bank estimates. For some families in Kerala or Punjab, that money pays for school fees and new homes.

How it got here

At independence in 1947, India was poor and mostly rural. For decades the government controlled much of the economy, with licences needed to open or expand a factory. Growth was slow. The Green Revolution of the 1960s and 1970s helped India grow enough grain to feed itself.

In 1991 India faced a crisis and nearly ran out of money to pay for imports. The government cut red tape, lowered import taxes and welcomed foreign companies. Those 1991 reforms set off decades of faster growth. More recently, cheap mobile data and instant payments through apps like UPI have brought millions of people into the digital economy.

Ranks and rupees

In 2025, many reports said India had passed Japan to become the fourth-largest economy. Then, in April 2026, the IMF's new figures put it sixth. Nothing had shrunk. The rupee had weakened against the dollar, so the same economy looked smaller when converted. India had also updated how it measures GDP, which lowered some past figures.

The IMF expects India to climb back up in the next few years, since it is growing faster than most large economies. Rankings make good headlines. For most families, though, what matters more is whether jobs, wages and prices are moving the right way.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A big economy means most people in that country are rich.

Truth: Size and wealth per person are different. India's economy is huge because it has so many people, but average income per person is still much lower than in countries such as the USA, Japan or China.
