What are the Commonwealth Games?

Every four years, athletes from about 70 countries and territories, most of them once ruled by Britain, meet for a multi-sport games. In 2030 it comes to Ahmedabad.

The short answer

The Commonwealth Games are a big multi-sport event, a bit like a smaller Olympics. They are held every four years. The athletes come from the nations of the Commonwealth, a group of mostly English-speaking countries that were once part of the British Empire.

There are swimmers and sprinters, boxers and weightlifters, cyclists and netball players. Para-athletes compete at the same Games, and their medals count in the same table.

India has taken part since 1934. It hosted the Games in Delhi in 2010, and Ahmedabad was chosen in November 2025 to host the 2030 Games, exactly a hundred years after the first.

Surprising but true

Small places such as the Isle of Man, Jersey and Norfolk Island send their own teams, so the Games can have more teams than there are countries in the Commonwealth.

From empire to Commonwealth

The first Games were held in Hamilton, Canada, in 1930. They were called the British Empire Games, and about 400 athletes from 11 teams took part.

At that time Britain ruled a huge empire, including India. After the Second World War, country after country won its freedom, and India became independent in 1947. Many of these new nations chose to stay linked in a friendly club, the Commonwealth.

The Games changed name to match. Since 1978 they have simply been the Commonwealth Games. Today about 70 teams take part, because places like Scotland, Wales, Jersey and the Isle of Man each enter their own.

The friendly games

People often call them "the friendly games". Athletes from tiny island nations share the track with world champions. Rugby sevens, netball and lawn bowls get a big stage here, though they are rare or absent at the Olympics.

Since 2002 the Games have mixed para-sport into the main programme, with events for athletes with disabilities held alongside the others. Birmingham 2022 also gave out more medals to women than to men, a first for any big multi-sport games.

Before each Games, a baton carrying a message from the head of the Commonwealth travels through the member nations, a bit like the Olympic torch. The relay began in 1958 and now visits dozens of countries on its way to the host city.

What are the Paralympics?The Paralympics began in 1948 as an archery contest for 16 injured ex-servicemen and women on a hospital lawn in England.

India at the Games

For India, the Games have long been a medal feast. Indian shooters, wrestlers, weightlifters, boxers and badminton players often finish on the podium. At Delhi 2010 India won 101 medals, its best ever, and came second in the table behind Australia.

In 1958, in Cardiff, the sprinter Milkha Singh won the 440 yards race, giving independent India its first Commonwealth gold in athletics. Many Indian sporting heroes since have first become famous here. A Commonwealth medal often leads to an Olympic one a few years later.

A smaller Games in 2026

The 2026 Games were meant to be held in Victoria, Australia, but the state pulled out in 2023 because of rising costs. Glasgow, which had hosted the Games in 2014, stepped in at short notice. To keep things cheap it held just 10 sports in four venues, from 23 July to 2 August 2026.

Many of India's best events, including shooting, wrestling, badminton, hockey and table tennis, were left out. Even so, India won 39 medals, 13 of them gold, and finished fourth.

Should the Games carry on?

Not everyone loves the Games. Some people say they are too expensive for host cities. Others feel uneasy that an event born from the British Empire still celebrates those old links.

Supporters answer that the Games give smaller nations a rare chance to shine, and that the Commonwealth today is a club of equals. Ahmedabad's centenary Games in 2030 will test which side is right. The city already has the Narendra Modi Stadium, the largest cricket ground in the world.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Commonwealth Games are only for countries that Britain still rules.

Truth: Almost all the teams come from independent countries. The Commonwealth is a voluntary club of nations, most of which used to be part of the British Empire, and a few, such as Rwanda and Mozambique, were never British colonies at all. Only a handful of small teams, such as Gibraltar and Bermuda, still come from British territories.
