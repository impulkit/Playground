How did people live in the Stone Age?

For more than 99% of the time humans and their ancestors have made tools, those tools were made of stone.

The short answer

The Stone Age is the long stretch of time when people made their tools mainly from stone. It began more than 3 million years ago and lasted until metal tools spread, which happened at different times in different places. For most of it, people lived by hunting animals, fishing and gathering plants, nuts, fruit, roots and honey.

They usually lived in small groups of a few families, perhaps 20 to 50 people. Groups moved with the seasons, following animals and ripening plants. They knew their land in great detail: which berries were safe, where water stayed in the dry season, and when fish would swim upriver.

Surprising but true

The oldest known stone tools, found in Kenya, are about 3.3 million years old. They are older than our own species by about 3 million years.

Tools that lasted

Stone does not rot, so tools are the clues archaeologists find most. The earliest were simple: a stone struck to make a sharp edge for cutting meat. Later, around 1.7 million years ago, people began shaping hand axes, teardrop-shaped tools that fit the palm. They were made the same careful way for over a million years.

India has some of the oldest. At Attirampakkam, near Chennai, researchers found hand axes about 1.5 million years old. Over time, tools grew smaller and smarter: blades, spear points, bone needles with eyes for thread, and fish hooks.

Wood, leather, string and baskets were probably just as important, but most of them rotted away long ago.

Daily life

A day might mean gathering plants and small animals near camp, while others went hunting or fishing. Food was shared. Studies of people who still hunt and gather suggest they often worked fewer hours on food than many farmers later did, though life could be hard in bad seasons.

Fire changed everything. It gave warmth, light, protection from animals and cooked food, which is easier to chew and digest. Evenings round a fire were a time to talk, tell stories and teach children.

People also made beautiful things. They wore shell beads, carved figures from bone and ivory, and painted animals on rock walls.

Who made the very first paintings?Long before paper, pencils or paintbrushes, people were painting on rock. Some of their pictures are more than 50,000 years old.

Living through the ice

For much of the last 2 million years, Earth swung in and out of ice ages. In Europe and northern Asia, people hunted reindeer, horses and woolly mammoths. They sewed fitted clothes with bone needles and built shelters from mammoth bones and hides.

As people spread out of Africa, they reached Asia, Australia and finally the Americas. Many scientists think people reached the Americas at least 15,000 years ago, and some think earlier. About 13,000 years ago, hunters across North America made a distinctive stone spear tip now called the Clovis point.

Bhimbetka: a home for thousands of years

In Madhya Pradesh, about 45 km from Bhopal, a cluster of rock shelters called Bhimbetka was used by people for many thousands of years. Its walls carry hundreds of paintings in red and white: hunters, dancers, deer, bison and elephants. Some are thought to be about 10,000 years old, and others were painted much later.

In 1957 an archaeologist named V.S. Wakankar spotted the rocks from a train window and guessed they might hold old sites. He was right. Bhimbetka became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2003.

The end of the Stone Age

About 12,000 years ago, some groups began to plant seeds and keep animals. Farming led to villages, then towns. Copper, then bronze and iron, slowly replaced stone. But the Stone Age did not end because people ran out of stones. It ended because they found something new to try.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Stone Age people were "cavemen" who all lived in caves.

Truth: Most people lived in the open, in huts or tents made of wood, hides, grass and even mammoth bones. Caves are simply good at preserving tools and paintings, so they are where many finds turn up.
