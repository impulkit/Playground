What are the world's major religions?

Four of the world's religions, Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism, began in India. Followers of almost every other faith live there too.

The short answer

A religion is a set of beliefs and practices about God or gods, the meaning of life, how to live well and what happens after death. Billions of people follow a religion, and many others are not religious at all.

By most counts, the largest religions are Christianity, with about 2.3 billion followers, and Islam, with about 2 billion. Hinduism comes next, with about 1.2 billion, followed by Buddhism. About 1.9 billion people say they have no religion. Smaller faiths include Sikhism, Judaism, Jainism, the Bahá'í faith and Zoroastrianism, along with many local and traditional religions.

Surprising but true

The country with the most Muslims is not in the Middle East. It is Indonesia, and India has one of the largest Muslim populations too.

Faiths born in India

Hinduism has no single founder. It grew over thousands of years from many traditions, texts and gods, and its followers worship in many ways. Its sacred texts include the Vedas, the Upanishads and the Bhagavad Gita.

Around 2,500 years ago, two teachers in what is now Bihar founded new paths. Siddhartha Gautama, who became the Buddha, taught a way to end suffering through wisdom and kindness. Mahavira, the great teacher of Jainism, taught strict non-violence towards every living thing. Buddhism later spread across Asia, to Sri Lanka, China, Japan and beyond.

Who was the Buddha?A prince left his palace at 29 to find out why people suffer. What he taught now guides hundreds of millions of people.

In the 1400s and 1500s, Guru Nanak founded Sikhism in Punjab. Sikhs believe in one God, equality of all people and service to others.

Faiths that share a father

Judaism, Christianity and Islam are sometimes called Abrahamic religions, because all three honour Abraham as an early prophet. Judaism is the oldest of the three. Christianity grew from the teachings of Jesus about 2,000 years ago. Islam began in Arabia in the 600s CE with the Prophet Muhammad, and its holy book is the Quran.

Followers of these faiths reached India very early. Jewish communities lived in Kerala for many centuries, and Kochi's Paradesi Synagogue was built in 1568. Christian tradition holds that the apostle Thomas came to Kerala in the first century. Historians debate this, but Christians have certainly lived there for well over a thousand years. Zoroastrians from Persia, known in India as Parsis, settled in Gujarat more than a thousand years ago.

Who believes what, where

Many religions have sacred places that draw pilgrims from far away. Hindus travel to Varanasi on the Ganga, and Muslims who are able make the Hajj to Mecca. Sikhs visit the Golden Temple in Amritsar, and Buddhists go to Bodh Gaya, where the Buddha is said to have found enlightenment. Christians visit Jerusalem, Bethlehem and Rome.

India is home to most of the world's Hindus. At the 2011 census, about 80% of Indians were Hindu, 14% Muslim, 2% Christian and nearly 2% Sikh, with smaller numbers of Buddhists, Jains and others. In the United States, about six in ten adults describe themselves as Christian, and a growing share say they have no religion.

Shared ideas, different paths

Religions differ in their beliefs about God and the afterlife, and their followers sometimes disagree strongly. But many share ideas about kindness, honesty, giving to others and caring for the poor. Many also mark the year with festivals of light and feasting, like Diwali, Christmas, Eid and Hanukkah. Poets such as Mirabai and Kabir sang about faith in everyday language that ordinary people could understand.

India's Constitution protects the freedom to follow, practise and spread any religion, or none. Learning what your neighbours believe, and why, is often the first step to understanding them.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Most of the world's Muslims are Arabs living in the Middle East.

Truth: Only about one in five Muslims is Arab. The largest Muslim populations are in South and Southeast Asia, in countries like Indonesia, Pakistan, India and Bangladesh.

Try this

Make a calendar of festivals celebrated by people in your class or neighbourhood, from different faiths. Ask friends politely what one of their festivals means to them and how their family celebrates it. Note any shared themes, such as light, giving or feasting.
