What is the Spiti Valley like?

In the village of Langza in Spiti, at over 4,000 metres, children can pick up fossils of sea creatures that lived on the floor of an ocean more than 100 million years ago.

The short answer

Spiti is a high, cold, dry valley in Himachal Pradesh, close to the border with Tibet. Its name is often explained as "the middle land", the land between India and Tibet. Most of it lies above 3,500 metres, among bare brown mountains, deep gorges and a wide river of grey water and stones.

Very little rain falls, because the Himalayan ranges to the south block the monsoon. So Spiti is a cold desert, much like Ladakh. Only a few thousand families live in its small villages of white mud-brick houses, most of them Buddhist. They farm in the short summer and stay indoors through long, freezing winters.

Surprising but true

The post office at Hikkim in Spiti, at about 4,400 metres, is often described as one of the highest post offices in the world. Tourists queue to send postcards from it.

A sea floor in the sky

The rocks of Spiti were once mud at the bottom of an ancient sea called the Tethys. When India crashed into Asia, that sea floor was squeezed and pushed up to form the Himalaya. Fossils came up with it.

Near the village of Langza, at about 4,400 metres, the hillsides are full of fossil shells. Many are ammonites, coiled sea creatures related to squid. Local people sometimes sell them to visitors, though collecting fossils is discouraged so that the sites stay intact for scientists.

Monasteries on the cliffs

Buddhism reached Spiti more than a thousand years ago. Tabo Monastery was founded in 996 CE. From outside its mud walls look plain. Inside, its prayer halls are covered with wall paintings and clay statues that have survived for about a thousand years. Some people call it "the Ajanta of the Himalaya".

Key Monastery, perched on a cone-shaped hill above the Spiti River, looks like a pile of white boxes stacked up a slope. Young monks study there, and visitors are often offered butter tea. The Dhankar Monastery clings to a crumbling ridge so steeply that it has been listed among the world's most endangered monuments.

Life at the top

Spiti's farming season is short, from about May to September. Villagers grow barley, green peas and potatoes, watering fields with channels fed by melting snow. The peas are prized and sold across north India.

Winters are harsh. Temperatures can fall below −30°C on the coldest nights, and roads are often blocked for days. Families store food, dried dung and wood for months. Children in some villages go to boarding schools lower down during the coldest part of the year.

The air is thin, so visitors are told to take things slowly for the first days. The same thin air makes the night sky dazzling. With almost no lights and very dry air, the Milky Way is easily visible on clear nights.

Snow leopards and blue sheep

Spiti is one of the best places in India to try to see a snow leopard. In winter, the big cats follow herds of bharal, or blue sheep, and ibex down towards the valley. Local guides, many of them former herders, now lead small groups of visitors in the snow. The Pin Valley National Park, in a side valley, protects this mountain wildlife.

Getting there

There are two main ways into Spiti. One road climbs up from Shimla through Kinnaur along the Sutlej and Spiti rivers. The other comes from Manali over the Kunzum Pass, about 4,590 metres high, which is closed by snow for much of the year.

Since 2020, the Atal Tunnel under the Rohtang Pass has made the journey from Manali shorter and safer, though Kunzum still blocks the way in winter. Even so, the trip takes a full day of rough mountain roads, with river crossings, landslides and the odd herd of goats. For many travellers, the long drive is part of the adventure.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Spiti is covered in snow all year round.

Truth: The high peaks keep their snow, but the valley floor is brown and dry in summer, with green fields of peas and barley around the villages. Heavy snow comes mostly between November and April.
