What is Gond art?

In Gond paintings, a tree can be full of birds, a tiger can be made of tiny dots, and a deer can have a forest growing inside it.

The short answer

Gond art is a style of painting by artists from the Gond community, one of India's largest indigenous or Adivasi groups. Gond families live across central India, especially in Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh and Maharashtra.

A Gond painting usually shows animals, trees, gods and stories from the forest. The shapes are bold and often bright, and their insides are filled with tiny repeated marks: dots, dashes, curves and scales. These patterns make a still picture feel as if it is shimmering. Most of the famous painters come from Patangarh, a small village in Dindori district.

Surprising but true

Gond painting on paper and canvas is less than 50 years old. It began in the early 1980s, when a young artist from a village in Madhya Pradesh moved his wall designs onto paper.

From mud walls to paper

For generations, Gond women decorated the mud walls and floors of their homes, especially for festivals. They used white clay, coloured earth and charcoal. The designs faded with the rains and were painted again.

Among the Gonds are the Pardhans, a community of bards. They sang the stories of Gond gods and heroes, playing a stringed instrument called the bana. Those songs gave painters a huge store of characters and tales. Gond paintings often show a single moment from a story that everyone in the village already knew.

Jangarh Singh Shyam

In the early 1980s, a team led by the artist J. Swaminathan was looking for village artists for a new arts centre, Bharat Bhavan in Bhopal. In Patangarh they met Jangarh Singh Shyam, a young Pardhan Gond who had been painting on the walls of village homes.

They gave him paper, canvas and modern paints. Jangarh took the wall patterns and turned them into large, dazzling pictures of gods, animals and forests. His work was soon shown in galleries in India and abroad. He also trained relatives and neighbours, and a whole school of artists grew around him. Jangarh died young, in 2001, but his style lives on in dozens of painters.

Patterns that carry stories

A typical Gond painting begins with an outline. Then the artist fills it with a filling pattern, repeated hundreds of times. One painter might use fish scales, another tiny seeds, another fine parallel lines. Experts can often tell who made a painting just from the pattern.

The patterns do more than decorate. Many artists say they show movement, sound or life inside a creature: ripples in water, wind in leaves, the energy of an animal about to leap. Because there is no shading or perspective, every part of the picture looks equally important, from the smallest insect to the biggest tree.

Trees appear again and again, especially the mahua and the banyan, which matter in Gond life and belief. Animals like the tiger, deer, peacock and fish are favourites. Sometimes the pictures surprise you: a tree's roots become snakes, or a bird turns out to be made of leaves.

Gond art on bookshelves

Gond artists have also become well-known book illustrators. In 2004, Bhajju Shyam, who had trained with Jangarh, published The London Jungle Book with Tara Books in Chennai. It shows his first trip to London, painted in Gond style, with the city's buses and clocks turned into creatures of the forest.

Bhajju Shyam, Durga Bai Vyam and Ram Singh Urveti later painted The Night Life of Trees, which won a prize at the Bologna Children's Book Fair in Italy. Bhajju Shyam and Durga Bai Vyam have both received the Padma Shri. Their work now hangs on museum walls in Bhopal and sits on bookshelves far from the forests that inspired it.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Gond art is an ancient style that has always looked the way it does today.

Truth: Gond people have decorated walls and floors for a very long time, but the detailed paintings on paper and canvas seen in galleries began in the 1980s. Today's style is a living art that keeps changing.
