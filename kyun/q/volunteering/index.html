What is volunteering?

In 2007, a Dutch schoolteacher sorting galaxy photos online as a volunteer spotted a glowing green cloud that astronomers had never seen.

The short answer

Volunteering means giving your time and effort to help others, without being paid. Volunteers teach children to read, plant trees, serve food, clean beaches, help at hospitals, rescue animals and assist after disasters. Some give an hour a month, and some give years.

People volunteer for many reasons: to help a cause they care about, to meet people, to learn skills or to give something back to their community. Many organisations, from small local groups to huge international charities, would struggle to work without volunteers.

Surprising but true

In the United States, most firefighters are volunteers, about two in every three.

Helpers in uniform

Some volunteers do serious, risky jobs. In the United States, about two in three firefighters are volunteers, especially in small towns where there are not enough emergencies to pay a full-time crew. They train for many hours and leave work or bed when the alarm sounds.

In India, the National Service Scheme was launched in 1969, the centenary year of Gandhi's birth. College and higher-secondary students in the scheme spend time on community work, such as health camps, literacy drives and cleanliness campaigns. Millions have taken part. The Scouts and Guides movement also trains young people to help others.

Volunteers for science

You do not have to leave home to volunteer. Through "citizen science" projects, ordinary people help researchers by counting birds, recording rainfall or sorting photos.

In 2007, a Dutch schoolteacher named Hanny van Arkel was classifying photos of galaxies for an online project called Galaxy Zoo. She noticed a strange green blob next to one galaxy. Astronomers studied it and found a huge glowing gas cloud, now known as Hanny's Voorwerp, Dutch for "Hanny's object". Volunteers in projects like this have helped discover new planets around other stars too.

How many galaxies are there?In 1995, the Hubble telescope stared for ten days at a patch of sky that looked completely empty, and found thousands of galaxies.

Birdwatchers around the world, including many in India, record what they see in online databases. Scientists use these records to track bird migration and how populations change.

Volunteers for nature

Some of the world's biggest environmental movements were led by volunteers. In the 1970s, villagers in the Himalayan foothills, many of them women, protected their forests by hugging trees to stop them being cut, in what became known as the Chipko movement. Across India, groups of volunteers now clean beaches, revive lakes and plant saplings on weekends.

Going abroad

The US Peace Corps, founded in 1961, sends American volunteers to live and work in other countries for two years, teaching, farming or supporting health projects. Other countries run similar programmes. The United Nations has its own volunteer service, and since 1985 it has marked 5 December as International Volunteer Day.

What volunteers get back

In emergencies, volunteers often arrive before anyone else. After floods in Kerala in 2018, fishermen took their own boats into flooded towns and rescued thousands of people. Students and neighbours ran relief camps, cooked meals and sorted donations. Volunteers also help run huge gatherings like the Kumbh Mela, guiding pilgrims and reuniting lost family members.

Volunteering is unpaid, but people often say they gain a lot: new friends, new skills and a sense of purpose. Studies find that people who volunteer tend to report feeling happier, though it is hard to tell how much volunteering causes that. You can read more about this in Is kindness good for you?. For young people, volunteering can also be a way to try out a kind of work before choosing a career.

Myth or fact?

Myth: You have to be an adult to volunteer.

Truth: Children can volunteer in many ways, usually with a parent, teacher or youth group. Scouts and Guides, school eco-clubs and online science projects all welcome young volunteers.

Try this

With an adult, find one small way to volunteer together for an hour, such as helping at a community clean-up, reading to a younger child, or sorting donations at a local shelter. Afterwards, write down one thing you learned and one thing that surprised you.
