Who was Munshi Premchand?

His most loved story is about a small boy who spends his only coins at the Eid fair on a pair of iron tongs for his grandmother.

The short answer

Munshi Premchand was one of the greatest writers of modern Hindi and Urdu. He lived from 1880 to 1936 and wrote about 300 short stories and more than a dozen novels. His real name was Dhanpat Rai Srivastava.

Before him, much popular fiction in north India was about magic, kings and adventures. Premchand wrote about ordinary people instead: farmers, widows, schoolchildren, clerks and moneylenders. He showed their hopes and troubles honestly, with humour and kindness. Many readers still call him "Upanyas Samrat", the emperor of novels.

Surprising but true

Premchand wasn't his real name. He switched to it after British officials burned copies of his first story collection.

A teacher who wrote at night

Premchand was born in Lamhi, a village near Varanasi. His father was a post office clerk. His mother died when he was about seven, and his father when he was a teenager, leaving the family poor.

He became a schoolteacher and later a deputy inspector of schools, writing stories in his spare time. In 1921, when Gandhi called on Indians to stop working for the British government, Premchand gave up his secure job. He spent the rest of his life writing, editing magazines and running a printing press that often lost money. In 1930 he started a Hindi literary magazine called Hans, meaning "swan". It was revived decades later and is still published.

Two names and a fire

Premchand first wrote in Urdu under the name Nawab Rai. Soon after his first collection of stories, Soz-e-Watan, came out, it was seized by the British authorities. The stories spoke of love for the homeland, and officials thought they could stir up rebellion against British rule. Copies were burned.

After that, he began publishing as Premchand. He also moved more and more into Hindi, though he kept writing in both languages. He used a simple, spoken style that ordinary readers in either language could follow.

Stories every child knows

Many Indian children meet Premchand in school. In Idgah, Hamid is a poor orphan who lives with his grandmother. On Eid morning, his friends set off for the fair with more money than him. He chooses a gift for her instead of a treat for himself. The ending makes readers smile and swallow hard at the same time.

Bade Bhai Sahab is about two brothers. The elder studies all the time and keeps failing, while the younger plays all day and keeps passing. Do Bailon ki Katha follows two bullocks, Hira and Moti, who escape and find their way home. Panch Parmeshwar asks whether friends can judge each other fairly in a village council.

Village life, honestly

His last finished novel, Godaan (1936), follows Hori, a poor farmer who dreams of owning a cow. Debt, caste and greed press in on him from every side. Premchand did not pretend village life was simple or peaceful. He showed unfair customs and the people who suffered under them.

In 1936, the year he died, he gave the main speech at the first meeting of the Progressive Writers' Association in Lucknow. In his speech he argued that writers should care about the lives of ordinary people and the problems of their society.

His stories keep finding new audiences. Satyajit Ray filmed Shatranj ke Khiladi, about two noblemen so busy playing chess that they ignore the end of their kingdom. The film came out in 1977, more than forty years after Premchand's death.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Premchand wrote only in Hindi.

Truth: He began writing in Urdu, and many of his stories appeared in both Urdu and Hindi. He is a major writer in both languages.

Try this

Read Idgah, which is short and easy to find in Hindi, Urdu or English. Then write a page about what you would buy for someone in your family if you had only a little money. Think about what they need, not what they asked for.
