What are lakhs and crores?

In India, ten million is written 1,00,00,000 and called one crore. In the USA, the same number is 10,000,000, with the commas in different places.

The short answer

Lakh and crore are Indian words for big numbers. One lakh is a hundred thousand, written 1,00,000. One crore is ten million, written 1,00,00,000. So one crore is the same as 100 lakh.

They come from the way numbers are grouped. The international system groups digits in threes: thousand, million, billion. The Indian system groups the first three digits, then every two after that: thousand, lakh, crore. That's why the commas fall in different places. People across South Asia use these words every day, for prices, salaries, populations, film box-office records and government budgets.

Surprising but true

An old list of number words in the Yajurveda, one of the oldest Indian texts, counts up by tens all the way to parardha, a million million, which shows how early Indian thinkers were naming very big numbers.

Where the commas go

Take the number 12345678. Written the international way, it's 12,345,678, read as "twelve million, three hundred forty-five thousand, six hundred seventy-eight".

Written the Indian way, it's 1,23,45,678, read as "one crore, twenty-three lakh, forty-five thousand, six hundred seventy-eight". The digits and their place values are exactly the same. Only the grouping changes.

A few handy swaps help when switching between the two. Ten lakh is one million. One crore is ten million. A hundred crore is one billion. When an Indian newspaper says a city has "two crore people", an American paper would write "20 million".

Ancient names for big numbers

The word lakh comes from the Sanskrit laksha, and crore from koti. Both appear in old Indian texts.

Indian thinkers named large numbers very early. A passage in the Yajurveda, composed around 3,000 years ago, lists number words rising by tens from one to parardha, a million million. Later Buddhist and Jain texts went much further, naming numbers so vast they were used to describe the age of the universe. This comfort with big numbers grew alongside the Indian numerals and place value that the world now uses.

Lakh and crore in daily life

Look at any Indian newspaper and you'll see these words everywhere. India's population is more than 140 crore people. In 2025–26, the central government planned to spend about ₹50 lakh crore, a number so big it combines both words. That's 50 followed by 12 zeros.

Films measure success the same way. When a Bollywood film earns more than ₹100 crore in cinemas, it is said to join the "100 crore club", a phrase that became popular after Ghajini in 2008. Property prices, cricket auctions and school fees all get quoted in lakh and crore.

Indian English has borrowed both words, and so have the dictionaries. The British in India used "lakh" so often that it appears in English texts from the 1600s, often spelled "lac".

Above a crore

The old Indian system keeps going. One hundred crore is an arab. One hundred arab is a kharab, and names continue beyond that. Few people use them today. Instead, Indians mostly stack the familiar words, saying "lakh crore" for a million million, or "hazaar crore" for ten billion.

The international system has its own ladder: million, billion, trillion, quadrillion. Even that has changed over time. In Britain, a "billion" once meant a million million, and only in 1974 did the British government officially switch to the American meaning of a thousand million. Number words, it turns out, are habits, and habits differ from place to place.

Two systems, one world

Many Indians switch between the systems without thinking, using lakh and crore at home and million and billion at work or in science. Banks and phones let you choose which comma style to show.

Mistakes still happen. A crore is not a billion, and mixing them up can make a sum seem a hundred times too big. A useful check is to count the zeros. One crore has seven. One billion has nine. When in doubt, write the number out in full.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A crore is the same as a billion.

Truth: A crore is ten million. A billion is a hundred crore, which in the older Indian system is called an arab. Mixing these up can make a number seem a hundred times bigger or smaller than it really is.
