Why is the Mona Lisa so famous?

The Mona Lisa was admired for 400 years, but it became a worldwide celebrity only after someone walked out of the Louvre with it under his coat.

The short answer

The Mona Lisa is a portrait of a woman from Florence, painted by Leonardo da Vinci in the early 1500s. Artists admired it from the start, because of its soft, smoky shading and the mysterious hint of a smile.

But it was not always the most famous painting in the world. That happened slowly, and partly by accident. A daring theft in 1911 put it on newspaper front pages across the globe. After that, copies on postcards, posters, advertisements and cartoons made her face familiar to almost everyone. Today millions of people visit the Louvre in Paris every year, and a huge number of them come mainly to see her.

Surprising but true

When the Mona Lisa was stolen in 1911, crowds queued at the Louvre just to stare at the empty space where it had hung.

Who was she?

Most experts believe the woman is Lisa Gherardini, the wife of a silk merchant in Florence called Francesco del Giocondo. That is why the painting is also called La Gioconda in Italian. "Mona" is short for "Madonna", meaning "my lady".

The strongest clue was found in 2005 in an old book in a library in Heidelberg, Germany. In its margin, an official named Agostino Vespucci wrote a note in 1503 saying Leonardo was working on a portrait of Lisa del Giocondo. Even so, Leonardo never handed the painting over. He kept it for years and took it to France.

The smile that moves

Leonardo blended the corners of her mouth and eyes into shadow, with no sharp lines. This method is called sfumato.

Scientists who study vision have an explanation for why her smile seems to come and go. The centre of your eye is good at seeing fine detail, while the edges of your vision pick up soft shadows better. When you look straight at her mouth, the smile seems smaller. When you look at her eyes, the shadows around her mouth make the smile seem bigger.

The day she disappeared

On a Monday in August 1911, when the Louvre was closed, an Italian handyman named Vincenzo Peruggia, who had worked at the museum, hid inside. He lifted the painting off the wall, removed its frame, and walked out with it under his workman's smock.

Nobody noticed for more than a day. Then newspapers everywhere printed her picture. Crowds came to gaze at the four hooks on the bare wall. For two years, police had no idea where she was.

In 1913 Peruggia tried to sell the painting to an art dealer in Florence, saying he wanted to return it to Italy. The dealer called the police. The Mona Lisa went back to Paris, now famous all over the world.

Fame that keeps growing

After the theft, artists began to play with her image. In 1919 the French artist Marcel Duchamp drew a moustache on a cheap postcard of her, as a joke about how seriously people took old art. Since then she has appeared in thousands of adverts, cartoons and memes.

Today she sits behind bulletproof glass in the Louvre's largest room, which she shares with other paintings, kept at a steady temperature and humidity. Experts use X-rays and other scans to study the layers of paint underneath, just as art detectives do with other old pictures. These scans show that Leonardo made small changes as he worked, painting over his own first ideas.

Is it the best painting ever?

That depends on whom you ask. Many artists think Leonardo's skill with light and shadow is extraordinary. Others say the painting is famous mainly for being famous. Both can be true. Plenty of visitors who squeeze to the front of the crowd find a small, quiet portrait that rewards a long, slow look.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The Mona Lisa is a huge painting.

Truth: It is quite small, about 77 cm by 53 cm, roughly the size of a large school chart. Its fame makes people expect something much bigger.

Try this

Look at a printed picture of the Mona Lisa. Cover her mouth with your finger and look at her eyes, then cover her eyes and look at her mouth. Does she seem to smile more when you look at her eyes? Many people find the smile is easier to see out of the corner of their eye.
