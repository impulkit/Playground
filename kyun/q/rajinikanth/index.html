Why is Rajinikanth so loved?

Before he became one of India's biggest film stars, Rajinikanth punched tickets as a bus conductor in Bengaluru.

The short answer

Rajinikanth is a film actor who has starred in more than 150 films, mostly in Tamil. For nearly fifty years he has been one of the most popular stars in India. In Tamil Nadu, fans simply call him "Thalaivar", meaning leader, or "Superstar".

People love him for several reasons. His screen style is like nobody else's. His life story, from bus conductor to superstar, feels like one of his own films. And off screen he seems humble and ordinary, which makes fans feel he is one of them.

Surprising but true

His 1995 film Muthu became a hit in Japan a few years later, where it was sold as Muthu, The Dancing Maharaja, and earned him fans who had never been to India.

From bus tickets to the big screen

He was born Shivaji Rao Gaekwad on 12 December 1950 in Bengaluru, into a Marathi family of modest means. His mother died when he was a boy. As a young man he did odd jobs, including carpentry and carrying loads, and then worked as a conductor on city buses.

He loved acting in amateur plays, often in Kannada plays based on stories from the epics. Friends encouraged him to join the Madras Film Institute in Chennai. There, the director K. Balachander noticed him and gave him a small role in Apoorva Raagangal in 1975. Balachander also gave him his screen name.

Style that cannot be copied

In his early films, Rajinikanth often played villains. Audiences loved his speed and swagger so much that he was soon cast as the hero. He became famous for tiny tricks done at lightning pace: flipping sunglasses onto his face, tossing a coin, or swinging his jacket over his shoulder.

Fans call it "style", and they cheer every move. Films like Baashha (1995) and Muthu (1995) made him a giant across South India. Enthiran (2010), in which he played both a scientist and a robot, used some of the most ambitious special effects Indian cinema had seen at the time.

Fans like no other

A new Rajinikanth film in Tamil Nadu feels like a festival. Fans gather before dawn for the first show, put up giant cut-outs of him and set off firecrackers. When Kabali came out in 2016, some companies in Chennai and Bengaluru gave their staff the day off.

His fame travelled far beyond India. Muthu was released in Japan in 1998 and became a surprise hit there. Many of his films are dubbed into Telugu, Hindi and other languages, so fans across the country can follow them.

The man off screen

What surprises many people is how plain he looks in real life. He often appears in public without his screen wig or makeup, in a simple white shirt. He is known for being polite to everyone on set, from fellow stars to the crew who carry the lights. He has spoken openly about his early struggles and regularly travels to the Himalayas for spiritual retreats.

In 2020, he announced plans to start a political party, then dropped them, saying his health came first. Supporters were disappointed, and others said it was the right choice. Either way, his fans stayed loyal.

Honours and jokes

The Government of India gave him the Padma Bhushan in 2000 and the Padma Vibhushan in 2016. He received the Dadasaheb Phalke Award, India's highest film honour, named after the father of Indian cinema, for 2019.

He is also the hero of countless internet jokes, which claim he can do impossible things, like counting to infinity twice. The jokes are made with affection, and they spread through emails and websites in the late 2000s, long before most people had smartphones. In a way, they are another sign of how much people across South Indian cinema and beyond enjoy him.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Rajinikanth is Tamil by birth.

Truth: He was born in Bengaluru to a Marathi family. He became the biggest star of Tamil cinema after training in Chennai, and Tamil Nadu adopted him as its own.
