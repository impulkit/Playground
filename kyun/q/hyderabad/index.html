What is Hyderabad famous for?

Some of the world's most famous diamonds passed through the markets of Golconda, near Hyderabad. Today the city is just as well known for biryani and software.

The short answer

Hyderabad, the capital of Telangana, is famous for many things at once. There are the Charminar and the giant Golconda Fort, built by the Qutb Shahi kings. There are pearls and diamonds, the rich food of the Nizams' kitchens, and Hyderabadi biryani, slow-cooked rice and meat sealed in a pot.

In the last 30 years it has also become one of India's biggest technology cities, nicknamed Cyberabad. Around 1 crore people live in its wider area.

Surprising but true

The Koh-i-Noor and the Hope Diamond are both believed to have come from mines in the Golconda region.

The fort of diamonds

Golconda Fort stands on a granite hill on the edge of the city. It was a strong fort of the Qutb Shahi dynasty, with walls running for several kilometres. Its builders designed a clever sound system. A clap at the main gate can be heard at the hilltop palace, which helped guards warn of visitors.

Golconda was also the world's best-known diamond market for centuries. Stones were mined along the Krishna River and brought here to be cut and sold. The Koh-i-Noor and the blue Hope Diamond, now in the Smithsonian museum in Washington, D.C., are both believed to have come from this region.

A new city and its four towers

By the late 1500s, Golconda was crowded and short of water. In 1591, Sultan Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah founded a new city beside the Musi River. At its centre he built the Charminar, a square building of granite and lime with four tall minarets. "Char minar" means "four minarets". Nobody knows for sure why it was built, but it became the city's symbol.

Around it grew the old city's bazaars. Laad Bazaar is known for glittering bangles, and pearl shops line the nearby streets. Hyderabad became a pearl city because its traders bought pearls from the Gulf and from the south, then drilled, sorted and strung them here. During Ramzan, the lanes around the Charminar stay busy late into the night with food stalls selling haleem, a slow-cooked dish of wheat, lentils and meat.

The Nizams

In 1724, a Mughal governor named Asaf Jah declared himself ruler of the Deccan, and his family became known as the Nizams. They ruled Hyderabad State, a kingdom bigger than England, until 1948. The last Nizam, Mir Osman Ali Khan, was once counted among the richest people in the world. He funded Osmania University and hospitals.

At independence in 1947, the Nizam did not want to join India. In September 1948 the Indian Army moved in, and Hyderabad became part of India within days. Later, the state was reorganised along language lines, and in 2014 Telangana was formed with Hyderabad as its capital.

Cyberabad

In 1998, the state opened HITEC City, a set of office towers built for software firms. Big international companies followed, along with Indian ones. Western Hyderabad filled with glass buildings, and the city now competes with Bengaluru for technology jobs.

Hyderabad is also a film city. Ramoji Film City, on the city's edge, has been recognised by Guinness World Records as the largest film studio complex in the world. Telugu films made here, such as Baahubali and RRR, became hits far beyond India.

Lakes and a giant Buddha

Hussain Sagar, a lake built in the 1560s, separates Hyderabad from its twin city, Secunderabad. In the middle stands a large stone statue of the Buddha, about 18 m tall, put up in 1992. In the evening, families stroll along the lakeside road. Hyderabad's food, many languages and mix of Hindu and Muslim traditions are what its people talk about first.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Golconda's diamonds were dug out of mines inside Golconda Fort.

Truth: The fort had no diamond mines. The stones were found along the Krishna River and brought to Golconda, which was the market where they were cut and sold.
