Why is Virat Kohli so famous?

The morning after his father died, an 18-year-old Virat Kohli walked out to bat for Delhi and made 90.

The short answer

Virat Kohli is one of the best batters cricket has ever seen. He is famous for chasing big totals in one-day matches, for scoring more one-day international centuries than anyone else, and for his fierce, emotional style. He captained India in all three formats and made the team one of the fittest in the world.

His fame goes beyond cricket. He is one of the most-followed sportspeople anywhere on social media. In India his face sells everything from shoes to insurance, and children copy his celebration after a century.

Surprising but true

Kohli played for the same IPL team, Royal Challengers Bengaluru, for 18 seasons before it finally won the title in 2025.

A boy from West Delhi

Kohli was born in Delhi in 1988 and joined the West Delhi Cricket Academy when he was about nine. His coach, Rajkumar Sharma, has said the boy hated losing even in practice.

In December 2006 his father died suddenly in the night. Kohli was 18, and in the middle of a Ranji Trophy match for Delhi against Karnataka. The next morning he went back to bat to save his team, made 90, and only then went to the funeral. Many fans see that day as the moment his determination was set.

Chasing is his speciality

In 2008 he captained India to win the Under-19 World Cup in Malaysia. Later that year he made his one-day debut. By 2011 he was part of the team that won the World Cup at home.

In one-day cricket, the team batting second must chase a target. Kohli became the master of this. He planned his innings like a sum, running hard between the wickets and hitting fours along the ground rather than risky sixes. On 15 November 2023 he hit his 50th ODI century, passing Sachin Tendulkar, and he finished that World Cup with 765 runs, a record for a single tournament.

The fitness captain

Around 2012, Kohli has said, he looked in the mirror and did not like what he saw. He gave up most sweets and fried food, trained in the gym and became much fitter.

As Test captain from 2014 to 2022 he made fitness tests compulsory for the team, including the "yo-yo" running test. India's fast bowlers grew stronger, and India won more Tests under him than under any captain before. In 2018–19 India won its first Test series in Australia.

Trophies at last

Kohli's teams often came close but missed out on the big trophies. That changed late in his career. In 2024 he was player of the match in the T20 World Cup final in Barbados, scoring 76, and then retired from T20 internationals. In 2025 India won the Champions Trophy.

He played every IPL season for Royal Challengers Bengaluru, starting in 2008. For years they could not win. In June 2025 they finally beat Punjab Kings in the final in Ahmedabad, and Kohli cried on the field. A month earlier, he had retired from Test cricket.

Not always calm

Kohli shows his feelings openly. He celebrates loudly, argues with opponents and stares down fast bowlers. Some fans love it and say it fires up his team. Critics have said he sometimes goes too far, and he has been fined for on-field behaviour.

Over time he has talked about staying calm and about his mental health. He has admitted that during a bad tour of England in 2014 he felt lonely even in a room full of people. He is married to the actor Anushka Sharma, and he plays like a man who still hates losing, even at practice.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Kohli was always a super-fit athlete.

Truth: Early in his career he has said he ate junk food and was not very fit. Around 2012 he changed his diet and training completely, and he later pushed the whole Indian team to take fitness more seriously.

Try this

Try a simple running-between-wickets test. Mark two lines about 17 metres apart, roughly the length of a cricket pitch, and time how long it takes you to run three lengths. Rest, try again, and see if planning your turns makes you faster. Kohli is famous for turning ones into twos.
