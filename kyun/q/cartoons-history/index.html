What were the first cartoons?

One of the first animated films ever made lasted under two minutes and showed a stick figure turning into an elephant, a house and a flower.

The short answer

Long before cinema, people made pictures seem to move with spinning toys. A drum with slits, called a zoetrope, showed a running horse when you spun it and peered through. These toys worked because your eyes and brain blend quick pictures into one smooth movement, as moving pictures explains. Today's studios still rely on the same trick, as modern animation shows.

The first animated films came in the early 1900s. In 1906, J. Stuart Blackton in the USA filmed a chalk drawing being changed bit by bit. In 1908, the French artist Émile Cohl made Fantasmagorie, a film of stick figures lasting under two minutes, that is often called the first fully animated cartoon.

Surprising but true

The word "cartoon" first meant a full-size drawing made as a plan for a painting or a woven wall hanging. It became a word for funny drawings only after a British magazine used it as a joke in 1843.

Pictures before film

In 1892, the Frenchman Émile Reynaud opened a show in Paris called the Théâtre Optique. He painted pictures on long strips of see-through material and wound them past a lamp, projecting little stories onto a screen with music.

Reynaud's shows ran for years and drew around half a million people. But he worked the strips by hand, and they could not be copied easily. When film cameras arrived, his invention lost out. In despair, he later threw most of his strips into the River Seine.

Stick figures and a dinosaur

Cohl's Fantasmagorie used about 700 drawings, each filmed one at a time. A clown changes into a bottle, then an elephant, then a house, as the lines melt from one shape into the next. It looks like chalk on a blackboard because Cohl filmed black lines and printed them reversed.

Then, in 1914, the American cartoonist Winsor McCay toured with Gertie the Dinosaur. McCay stood on stage beside the screen and gave Gertie orders. She seemed to obey, lifting her leg and swallowing a tree. At the end, McCay walked behind the screen and appeared on film, riding away on her back.

How do your eyes see?The picture at the back of your eye is upside down. Your brain flips it the right way up without you ever noticing.

Felix, Mickey and sound

Drawing every frame on paper was slow, so studios found shortcuts. Artists began drawing characters on clear sheets of plastic called cels, which could be laid over one painted background. This let a studio produce a short cartoon every couple of weeks.

Felix the Cat, a clever black cat drawn much like a comic strip hero, became the first cartoon star known around the world, from 1919. In 1928, Walt Disney's studio released Steamboat Willie, starring Mickey Mouse, with sound carefully matched to the action. Audiences loved hearing a cartoon whistle, and silent cartoons quickly faded away.

The first long cartoons

People once thought no one would sit through a long cartoon. Yet the German artist Lotte Reiniger spent about three years cutting paper silhouettes for The Adventures of Prince Achmed (1926), based on stories from the Arabian Nights. It is the oldest animated feature film that still survives.

In 1937 Disney released Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs, the first full-length cel-animated feature in colour and sound. Many in Hollywood had called it "Disney's folly". It became one of the biggest hits of its time.

Where the word came from

Before films, "cartoon" meant a full-size sketch that artists drew to plan a painting or a woven wall hanging. In 1843, the British humour magazine Punch printed joke drawings it called "cartoons", poking fun at a contest for paintings in Parliament. The name stuck to funny drawings, and later to animated films too.

India has its own long line of cartoonists, from newspaper artists such as R. K. Laxman to the animators of today. Many of them began, like Cohl and McCay, with a pencil, a stack of paper and a lot of patience.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Mickey Mouse was the first cartoon star.

Truth: Felix the Cat was a worldwide star almost ten years earlier, from 1919, and Gertie the Dinosaur was drawing crowds in 1914. Mickey became famous in 1928 because Steamboat Willie was one of the first cartoons with well-matched sound.
