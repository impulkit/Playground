Who was Major Dhyan Chand?

Opponents said the ball seemed glued to his hockey stick. India's National Sports Day falls on his birthday.

The short answer

Major Dhyan Chand was an Indian field hockey player, and many people think he was the greatest ever. He won Olympic gold medals with India in 1928, 1932 and 1936, at a time when India was still under British rule. He was famous for his close control of the ball, his clever passes and the number of goals he scored.

He served in the Indian Army for most of his life and retired as a major. Today India's highest sports award is named after him, and his birthday, 29 August, is celebrated as National Sports Day.

Surprising but true

Dhyan Chand's real name was Dhyan Singh. "Chand", meaning moon, was a nickname, often said to come from his habit of practising by moonlight.

A soldier who played by moonlight

Dhyan Singh was born in Allahabad, now Prayagraj, in 1905. His father was in the army, and the family moved often. Dhyan joined the army himself at 16 and started playing hockey in regimental matches.

Soldiers worked during the day, so he often practised after duty. Stories say he trained under the moon, and friends nicknamed him "Chand", the Hindi word for moon. Whether or not that is exactly how it happened, the name became famous.

Three Olympic golds

India first played Olympic hockey at Amsterdam in 1928. The team did not let in a single goal all tournament, and Dhyan Chand was the top scorer. India beat the Netherlands 3–0 in the final. When the team sailed home, crowds met them at the docks in Bombay. Only a few people had seen them off.

In 1932 the Games were held in Los Angeles. Few teams could afford the journey, so only three took part. India beat Japan 11–1 and the USA 24–1. Dhyan Chand's younger brother, Roop Singh, scored about as many goals as he did.

How did India become a hockey superpower?India's men won eight Olympic gold medals in hockey, six of them in a row, more than any other country.

Berlin 1936

In 1936 Dhyan Chand was captain. The Berlin Olympics were run by Adolf Hitler's Nazi government, which used the Games to show off its power. India lost a warm-up match to Germany, but in the final they beat the hosts 8–1.

Dhyan Chand is said to have taken off his heavy boots and played barefoot or in rubber shoes in the second half, because the ground was wet. He was among India's scorers in the final. Three years later, World War Two began and the Olympics stopped until 1948.

Stories and legends

Many stories are told about Dhyan Chand. One says officials in the Netherlands broke his stick to check for a magnet. Another says Hitler offered him German citizenship. These are retold everywhere, but historians have not found reliable evidence for them.

His goal total is also uncertain. Numbers like 400 or even 1,000 goals are quoted, but many matches had no proper records. What the Olympic results show clearly is that he scored a lot, in the biggest games, and that his teammates and opponents spoke of him with awe.

Remembering him

Dhyan Chand died in Delhi in 1979. The National Stadium in Delhi was renamed after him in 2002. In 2021 India's top sports award, the Khel Ratna, was renamed the Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna.

Some fans have campaigned for years for him to receive the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian award. He received the Padma Bhushan in 1956. His son Ashok Kumar also played hockey for India and scored the winning goal in the 1975 World Cup final, so the family's story continued on the field. Hockey then was played on grass, and the game has since moved to artificial turf, which is faster and flatter.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Hitler offered Dhyan Chand a job and German citizenship after the 1936 final.

Truth: The story is very popular, but historians have found no reliable record of it. What is certain is that India beat Germany 8–1 in the final in Berlin.

Try this

Try dribbling a small ball around a line of shoes with a stick or a rolled-up newspaper, keeping the ball as close to the stick as you can. Time yourself, then try to go faster without losing control. You will see why close control made Dhyan Chand so hard to stop.
