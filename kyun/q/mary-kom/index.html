Who is Mary Kom?

She kept boxing a secret from her father until her photo appeared in the newspaper. She went on to become a six-time world champion.

The short answer

Mary Kom is an Indian boxer from Manipur and one of the most successful women in the history of amateur boxing. She has won six World Championship gold medals, more than any other woman, and a bronze medal at the London Olympics in 2012.

She did it while coming from a poor farming family in a small village, with little equipment and few women boxers to follow. Along the way she raised three sons, served in Parliament and inspired a Hindi film about her life.

Surprising but true

Mary Kom won two of her world titles after becoming a mother of twin boys, and a third after having another son.

Growing up in Manipur

Mary was born in 1982 in Kangathei, a village in Manipur, into a family of the Kom tribe. Her parents worked in the fields, and she helped them as a child. She was good at athletics at school.

In 1998 a Manipuri boxer, Dingko Singh, won gold at the Asian Games in Bangkok. Mary was inspired. Boxing for women was new in India, but in Manipur's capital, Imphal, she found a coach who agreed to train her. She was fast, determined and fearless.

A secret in the newspaper

Mary did not tell her father she was boxing, because she thought he would worry about injuries and her marriage chances. In 2000 she won the state championship. Her photograph appeared in the local newspaper, and her father saw it.

He was upset at first. Over time he came round and backed her fully. Within two years she was world champion.

Money was always short. In her autobiography, Unbreakable, Mary describes training on simple food and travelling to competitions with very little. She also met her husband, Onler Kom, during these years. He later took on much of the care of their children so that she could keep training and travelling.

World champion, again and again

Mary won her first world title in 2002 in Antalya, Turkey. She won again in 2005, 2006 and 2008. In 2007 she had twin boys and took a break, and many people thought her career was over. She came back to win the world title in 2008 and again in 2010.

Her sixth world gold came in 2018 in New Delhi, when she was 35, and she also won a world medal in 2019. People started calling her "Magnificent Mary".

London 2012

Women's boxing joined the Olympics for the first time in London in 2012. Mary usually fought at 48 kg or lighter, but the smallest Olympic class was 51 kg, so she had to move up and face bigger opponents. She trained to put on muscle, worked on staying fast at the heavier weight, and won bronze.

It was one of India's six medals at those Games. The only other Indian woman to win a medal in London was the badminton player Saina Nehwal. Mary lost her semi-final to Britain's Nicola Adams, who went on to take gold.

Beyond the ring

In 2016 the President of India nominated Mary to the Rajya Sabha, the upper house of Parliament, where she served until 2022. She runs a boxing academy in Imphal to train young fighters, especially girls from the northeast.

In 2014 Priyanka Chopra played her in the Bollywood film Mary Kom. Some critics asked why an actor from Manipur was not chosen for the role, a debate that made many people think about how the northeast is shown in films. Mary received the Padma Vibhushan, India's second-highest civilian award, in 2020.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Boxing is only for big, heavy people.

Truth: Boxers compete in weight classes, so they face people of a similar size. Mary Kom is about 1.58 metres tall and won world titles at weights as low as 45 to 48 kg.

Try this

Try some shadow footwork. Stand with one foot slightly in front, knees soft, and move forward, back and sideways in small steps without crossing your feet. Do it for one minute, rest, and repeat three times. Boxers say good footwork matters as much as punching.
