What makes Goan food special?

The name of Goa's famous vindaloo has nothing to do with potatoes. It comes from a Portuguese dish of meat in wine and garlic.

The short answer

Goan food is a mix of Konkan coastal cooking and about 450 years of Portuguese rule. The everyday meal is fish curry and rice, flavoured with coconut and the sour fruit kokum. On top of that, the Portuguese brought vinegar-based dishes, pork, bread, and ingredients from the Americas such as chillies, cashews and potatoes.

The result is food found nowhere else in India: tangy vindaloo, spicy sorpotel, green cafreal chicken, crusty bread rolls and a layered sweet called bebinca. Goan Hindu and Goan Catholic families each have their own cooking traditions, which share many flavours.

Surprising but true

Goa's soft bread rolls, called pão or pav, were introduced by the Portuguese, and they spread from Goa to become Mumbai's vada pav and pav bhaji.

Fish curry rice

Goa sits on India's west coast, where rivers meet the Arabian Sea. Fish is part of almost every meal. A typical Goan fish curry uses coconut ground with red chillies, coriander and turmeric, soured with kokum or tamarind.

Mackerel, kingfish, prawns and pomfret are favourites. Fishing boats bring the catch to beaches and markets early in the morning, and the fish is often cooked the same day. During the monsoon, when fishing at sea pauses, families rely more on dried fish stored earlier in the year. Goans eat their curry with boiled red rice, often with fried fish on the side. For many Goans, "xit kodi" (rice and curry in Konkani) is simply what lunch means.

The Portuguese years

The Portuguese captured Goa in 1510 and ruled it until 1961, when it became part of India. They came for the spice trade, travelling the sea trade routes around Africa.

How did Goa become part of India?India became free in 1947, but Goa stayed a Portuguese colony for another fourteen years, until Indian troops moved in one December night in 1961.

Portuguese ships carried plants from their colony in Brazil and other parts of the Americas. Chillies, potatoes, tomatoes, pineapples and cashews all reached India through ports like Goa. Within a century or two, chillies had spread across the whole subcontinent.

Vindaloo, sorpotel and xacuti

Vindaloo began as the Portuguese "carne de vinha d'alhos", meat soaked in wine and garlic. In Goa, cooks replaced the wine with palm vinegar, added dried red chillies and spices, and made it their own. It's traditionally made with pork.

Sorpotel is a spicy pork dish that tastes better after a few days, so it's often made before Christmas. Xacuti is a rich curry with roasted coconut and many spices. Cafreal is chicken coated in a green paste of coriander, chillies and spices, then fried. Its name is linked to Portuguese soldiers from Africa.

The bakers and their bread

Before the Portuguese, Goans ate mostly rice. The Portuguese brought wheat bread, and Goan bakers called poders learned to make it. Many still cycle through villages in the mornings with baskets of fresh bread, honking a horn.

Goan breads include pão (soft rolls), poi (a round bread with bran) and kankonn, a crisp ring-shaped bread. From Goa, soft rolls spread to Mumbai, where they became the pav in vada pav and pav bhaji.

Sweets and festivals

Bebinca is Goa's best-known dessert. It's made from coconut milk, eggs, sugar and flour, baked one thin layer at a time. A traditional bebinca can have seven to sixteen layers and takes hours to make.

At Christmas, Catholic families make plates of sweets called kuswar, including neureos, filled pastries, and dodol, a dark sweet made with coconut and jaggery. Goan Hindu families celebrate Ganesh Chaturthi and Diwali with their own sweets and dishes. Many Goan Hindu dishes skip vinegar and use kokum and coconut instead, but the coast's love of fish, rice and coconut runs through every Goan kitchen.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Vindaloo is a potato curry, because its name has "aloo" in it.

Truth: The name comes from the Portuguese "vinha d'alhos", meaning wine and garlic. Traditional Goan vindaloo is made with pork and has no potatoes. The "aloo" is a coincidence.
