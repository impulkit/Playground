What is India's Constitution, and why does it matter to you?

It's the rulebook that even the government has to follow, and its first copies were written entirely by hand.

The short answer

The Constitution says how India is governed, what the government can and can't do, and what rights every person has. Every law in India must fit inside it. If a law breaks it, courts can cancel that law.

It promises things like equality before the law, freedom of speech, and free education for children. It also sets up Parliament, the courts, the President and the Election Commission, and explains how they work together.

The original was written by hand by Prem Behari Narain Raizada, a master of calligraphy. It was decorated by the artist Nandalal Bose and his students, who had studied the Ajanta paintings.

Surprising but true

India's Constitution is the longest written national constitution in the world.

Writing a rulebook for a new nation

In December 1946, the Constituent Assembly met in New Delhi to write the Constitution. After Partition, it had 299 members, including 15 women, such as Sarojini Naidu, Hansa Mehta and Ammu Swaminathan.

Dr B.R. Ambedkar chaired the drafting committee. Members studied the constitutions of many countries, including the USA, Britain, Ireland and Canada. They argued over almost every line for nearly three years. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949, now Constitution Day, and came into force on 26 January 1950, now Republic Day.

The Preamble

The opening lines say India is a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic, and promise justice, liberty, equality and fraternity to all its citizens. Fraternity means a feeling of brotherhood and sisterhood.

The words "socialist" and "secular" were added in 1976, by the 42nd Amendment. Many schools across India read the Preamble aloud at assembly.

Who was B.R. Ambedkar?As a boy he wasn't allowed to drink from the school tap. As a man he led the team that wrote India's Constitution.

Fundamental Rights and Duties

Every person gets rights like equality before the law and freedom of speech. Untouchability, the old practice of treating some castes as "unclean", was abolished. Children aged 6–14 have the right to free education, added in 2002.

In 1976, a list of Fundamental Duties was added too. It asks citizens to respect the flag and anthem, protect the environment and develop a scientific temper, which means thinking with evidence.

A living document

The Constitution can be amended, which means changed by Parliament following special rules. It has been amended more than 100 times.

But there are limits. In 1973, the Supreme Court ruled that Parliament cannot change the Constitution's "basic structure", such as democracy and the rule of law.

How it works in daily life

The Constitution can feel far away, but it touches ordinary days. It is the reason elections are held every five years, and why every adult citizen gets one vote, whatever their caste, religion or wealth. It sets up free and fair voting through the Election Commission.

It divides work between the central government and the states. Defence and railways belong to the centre, while police and public health belong mainly to the states. Education is shared. When you go to a government school, ride a train or see a police officer, you are seeing the Constitution at work.

Kept safe

The two handwritten originals, one in Hindi and one in English, are kept in special cases filled with helium gas in the Library of Parliament, to stop them decaying. On 24 January 1950, members of the Assembly signed them one by one. Today, you can read the whole Constitution free online, in many Indian languages.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Dr Ambedkar wrote the whole Constitution by himself.

Truth: Ambedkar chaired the drafting committee and guided the Constitution through the Assembly, and he is rightly called its chief architect. But the adviser B.N. Rau prepared an early draft, and hundreds of Assembly members debated and changed it.
