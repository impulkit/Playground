What was the California Gold Rush?

In January 1848 a carpenter spotted something shiny in a millstream. Within a few years, about 300,000 people from around the world had rushed to California.

The short answer

In 1848 gold was found in the hills of northern California. When the news spread, people poured in from the eastern United States, Latin America, Europe, China and Australia, all hoping to get rich quickly.

They were called forty-niners, after 1849, the year the rush really took off. California changed almost overnight. It became a US state in 1850, and San Francisco grew from a small settlement into a busy port city. But the rush was a disaster for the Native peoples who already lived there.

Surprising but true

The people most likely to get rich in the Gold Rush were usually not the miners but the shopkeepers who sold them shovels, food and clothes.

The find at Sutter's Mill

James Marshall was building a sawmill for a landowner named John Sutter at Coloma, in the Sierra Nevada foothills. On 24 January 1848 he saw shiny flakes in the water channel below the mill. After a few simple tests, the workers were sure it was gold.

Sutter tried to keep it secret, but it was hopeless. A shopkeeper in San Francisco ran through the streets holding up a bottle of gold dust. Soon sailors left their ships in the bay to go digging, and the town nearly emptied.

By 1850 hundreds of abandoned ships crowded San Francisco's harbour. Some were dragged ashore and turned into shops, hotels and even a jail. As the town filled in the shoreline, many hulls were buried, and builders digging foundations downtown still sometimes find their timbers today.

Getting there

Travel was slow and dangerous. Some forty-niners crossed the continent by wagon, a trip of four to six months. Others sailed from New York all the way round South America, about 29,000 km. Some took a shortcut across the jungles of Panama, risking malaria.

Thousands came from across the Pacific too. By 1852, about 20,000 Chinese people arrived in a single year. They often faced unfair treatment, including a special tax on foreign miners.

How mining worked

At first, gold lay in riverbeds where anyone could find it. Miners scooped up gravel in a pan and swirled it with water. Gold is heavy, about 19 times heavier than water, so it sank while the sand washed away.

Soon that easy gold ran out. Miners built long wooden boxes called sluices, and later companies blasted hillsides with powerful jets of water. This hydraulic mining washed huge amounts of mud into rivers and damaged farmland so badly that a court largely banned it in 1884.

Who got rich

Most miners found little. Prices in mining towns were sky-high, so a pair of boots or a dozen eggs could cost a day's earnings. Shopkeepers, hotel owners and traders often did better than diggers.

One of them was Levi Strauss, a young German immigrant who sold cloth and goods in San Francisco. Years later, in 1873, he and a tailor named Jacob Davis patented work trousers strengthened with metal rivets, the ancestors of today's jeans.

The cost for Native Californians

Before the rush, about 150,000 Native people lived in California. Miners took their land and food sources, brought new diseases, and attacked villages, often with little or no punishment. By around 1870, only about 30,000 Native Californians remained. Historians now see this as one of the darkest chapters of Native American history.

India has gold-mining history too. At the Kolar Gold Fields in Karnataka, miners dug for over a century until 2001, reaching more than 3 km underground, among the deepest mines in the world.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Most gold rush miners became rich.

Truth: Most found little or nothing and many went home poorer. The easy surface gold was gone within a few years, and big companies with machines took over.

Try this

Try gold panning without gold. Mix some sand with a few small metal washers or coins in a shallow pan of water. Swirl gently and tip the pan so the light sand washes over the edge. The heavy metal sinks and stays behind, which is how miners found gold.
