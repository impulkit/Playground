How did sneakers become so popular?

Sneakers got their name because their rubber soles were so quiet that you could sneak up on someone wearing them.

The short answer

Sneakers are shoes with soft rubber soles and cloth or leather tops. They became possible in the 1800s, when a way was found to make rubber tough and springy. At first they were simple canvas shoes for sports and games on the beach.

Over the 1900s, athletes made them famous. Runners, tennis players and basketball stars wore them, and companies paid sports heroes to advertise them. By the 1980s, sneakers were fashion as well as sportswear, and today billions of pairs are sold every year. In India, the same shoes are often called "sports shoes" or "PT shoes".

Surprising but true

Adidas and Puma, two of the world's biggest sports shoe brands, were started by two brothers from the same small German town after they fell out with each other.

Rubber that behaves

Natural rubber comes from the sap of rubber trees. On its own, it gets soft and sticky in heat and stiff and cracked in cold. In the USA, the inventor Charles Goodyear spent years trying to fix this, often in debt.

Around 1839, he found that heating rubber with sulphur made it stay strong and springy. He patented the method, called vulcanisation, in 1844. Now rubber could be glued to cloth to make soles that gripped the ground. That grip comes from friction, the force that resists sliding.

The quiet shoe

In Britain, rubber-soled canvas shoes were called plimsolls. In the USA, they got a different name. Because the soles made almost no noise, people said you could sneak about in them, and they became "sneakers". In 1917, two famous canvas shoes went on sale in the USA: Keds and the Converse All Star.

The Converse All Star was made for the new sport of basketball. A player and salesman named Chuck Taylor travelled the country promoting it, and his name was later printed on the ankle patch.

Why do things slow down?A football rolling across grass stops by itself. Voyager 1, launched in 1977, is still coasting through space. The difference is friction.

Two brothers, two brands

In the small German town of Herzogenaurach, the brothers Adolf and Rudolf Dassler made sports shoes together in the 1920s. At the 1936 Berlin Olympics, the American sprinter Jesse Owens wore their shoes while winning four gold medals.

After the Second World War, the brothers quarrelled. In 1948 they split the company. Adolf, called Adi, founded Adidas, and Rudolf founded Puma. The rivalry divided their town for decades, with factories on opposite sides of the river.

Stars and street style

In 1964, an American runner named Phil Knight and his coach began selling running shoes. Their company later became Nike. In 1985, Nike released the Air Jordan, named after the basketball player Michael Jordan. It became one of the best-selling sports shoes ever.

At the same time, hip-hop musicians in New York wore sneakers on stage, and people began collecting rare designs. Some collectors keep shoes in their boxes and never wear them. A few rare pairs have sold for huge sums at auction.

Sneakers in India

In India, the Czech shoe company Bata set up a factory town called Batanagar near Kolkata in the 1930s. Its white canvas shoes became standard school PT shoes for generations of Indian children.

Today India is one of the world's biggest makers of footwear. Sneakers are worn to school, to the office and to weddings. Most soles are now made from synthetic rubber and foams, materials made from oil, as how plastic is made explains. Some companies are trying soles made from recycled plastic and plants, so old shoes don't end up buried in landfills.

Myth or fact?

Myth: More expensive sneakers always make you run faster.

Truth: A good fit and comfort matter most for everyday running. Some very advanced racing shoes do help top runners a little, but for most children an ordinary, well-fitting pair works just as well.

Try this

Test the grip of different shoes in your home. Put each shoe on a smooth board or a big book, and slowly lift one end until the shoe starts to slide. Measure how high the end is. Rubber soles with deep patterns usually grip best, because of friction.
