Who is Sudha Murty?

In 1974 a young engineer named Sudha Kulkarni sent an angry postcard to the head of India's biggest company because a job advert said women need not apply, and it worked.

The short answer

Sudha Murty is an Indian engineer, teacher, writer and philanthropist, someone who gives money and time to help others. She has written dozens of books in Kannada and English, many of them simple, warm stories for children based on real life.

For 25 years she led the Infosys Foundation, a charity that built school libraries, toilets, hospitals and shelters across India. In 2024 she was nominated to the Rajya Sabha, the upper house of India's Parliament. To millions of readers, though, she is best known as the grandmother who tells good stories.

Surprising but true

When Sudha Murty was hired by TELCO in Pune in the 1970s, she became the company's first woman engineer.

A postcard to J.R.D. Tata

Sudha Kulkarni was born in 1950 in Shiggaon, a small town in Karnataka. Her father was a doctor and her mother a teacher. She chose to study engineering in Hubballi at a time when very few women did, and she came first in her class. She then studied computer science at the Indian Institute of Science in Bengaluru.

Near the end of her studies, she saw an advert for engineers at TELCO, a Tata company that made trucks. At the bottom it said women candidates need not apply. Furious, she wrote a postcard to J.R.D. Tata, the head of the group, saying that this was unfair. She was called to Pune for an interview, got the job and became TELCO's first woman engineer.

Infosys and the foundation

In 1978 she married N. R. Narayana Murthy. In 1981 he and six friends started a software company called Infosys, and she lent him ₹10,000 from her savings to help. Infosys grew into one of India's biggest companies, part of the story of how Indian startups can become giants.

In 1996 she became head of the Infosys Foundation. Under her, it built tens of thousands of school library collections, supported hospitals and helped people hit by floods and other disasters. She retired from the foundation at the end of 2021. Before all this, she had also taught computer science to college students in Bengaluru for many years, and she has said that teaching was one of the jobs she loved most.

Stories from real life

Sudha Murty started writing in Kannada and later wrote in English as well. Her books often begin with something that really happened to her or to people she met. In How I Taught My Grandmother to Read, a young girl teaches her grandmother the alphabet so that she can read a serialised novel herself.

Her children's books, such as Grandma's Bag of Stories and The Magic Drum and Other Favourite Stories, borrow the feel of old folk tales and the Panchatantra. The sentences are short and the lessons clear: be honest, work hard, share what you have. Some readers love them for that simplicity. Some critics find them too neat. Either way, they sell in very large numbers and are translated into many Indian languages.

Plain living

She is famous for living simply, despite her family's wealth. She has said in interviews that she stopped buying new saris for many years after a visit to Kashi, and she often travels by ordinary transport. Children write to her, and she often talks at schools about reading, curiosity and kindness. Her talks are full of small stories, the same way her books are.

Honours

The Government of India gave her the Padma Shri in 2006 and the Padma Bhushan, its third-highest civilian award, in 2023. She is one of the few Indian children's writers whose name is as well known as her characters. Her story connects many threads: a small town, a Kannada childhood, engineering, a giant company and a lifetime of books.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Sudha Murty co-founded Infosys.

Truth: Infosys was founded in 1981 by N. R. Narayana Murthy, her husband, and six colleagues. She lent him money to help start it and later led the Infosys Foundation, but she was not one of the founders.

Try this

Ask a grandparent or an older neighbour to tell you a story from when they were your age. Write it down in your own words in about a page, keeping one line they said exactly. Sudha Murty built many of her books this way, from real people's lives.
