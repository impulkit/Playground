How did farmers build Amul?

In 1946, angry dairy farmers in Gujarat refused to sell their milk for two weeks. Out of that strike grew one of India's best-known brands.

The short answer

Amul is owned by dairy farmers in Gujarat. It began in 1946 in the town of Anand, in what was then Kaira district, as a way for farmers to stop being cheated by a private dairy.

The farmers formed village milk societies. Each morning and evening, they brought their milk to a village collection centre, where it was tested and paid for fairly. The societies joined together into a district union, which processed the milk and sold it. Over time, unions across Gujarat joined to sell under one brand name, Amul. Today the system has lakhs of farmer members, many of them women with only one or two buffaloes.

Surprising but true

The film Manthan (1976), about Gujarat's milk cooperatives, was paid for by about five lakh dairy farmers, who each gave ₹2.

Milk, a strike and a freedom fighter

In the 1940s, milk from Kaira was sent to the city of Bombay. A private company held the contract to collect it and paid the farmers very little. Middlemen took much of the rest.

The farmers asked for advice from Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, who came from the same area. He told them to form their own cooperative. Local leader Tribhuvandas Patel helped organise them. In 1946, the farmers refused to sell milk to the company for about two weeks, and the government agreed to buy from their new union instead. It was registered on 14 December 1946.

How did Sardar Patel unite India?In 1947 India was a patchwork of more than 560 kingdoms. In about two years, one man stitched almost all of them into a single country.

An engineer who didn't want to be there

In 1949, a young engineer named Verghese Kurien was posted to a government dairy in Anand. He wanted to leave. Instead, Tribhuvandas Patel persuaded him to help the struggling cooperative, and he stayed for the rest of his working life.

Kurien, along with engineer H. M. Dalaya, solved a big problem. Most of Kaira's milk came from buffaloes, and experts said buffalo milk couldn't be dried into powder. Dalaya found a way, and in 1955 a new plant began making milk powder and butter. The brand name Amul came from the Sanskrit word amulya, meaning priceless.

Spreading the Anand pattern

Prime Minister Lal Bahadur Shastri visited Anand in 1964 and asked Kurien to copy the model across India. The National Dairy Development Board was set up in 1965, and in 1970 it launched Operation Flood. Milk unions on the Anand pattern were started in hundreds of districts, linked to cities by refrigerated road and rail tankers.

This came to be called the White Revolution. India became the world's largest milk producer in the late 1990s and has stayed there since. In Gujarat, the district unions formed a joint federation in 1973 to sell everything under the Amul name, from milk and butter to ice cream and chocolate. Kurien's birthday, 26 November, is now marked as National Milk Day.

The girl in the polka-dot dress

Since 1966, Amul's adverts have starred a chubby little girl in a polka-dot dress, created by the advertising agency of Sylvester daCunha. Her billboards make jokes about the news of the week, often with puns. The slogan "Utterly Butterly Delicious" has run for decades.

The ads are famous, but the bigger story is behind them. A farmer in a Gujarat village who brings a few litres of milk to the local society each morning is a part-owner of the whole business. In 1976, about five lakh of these farmers each paid ₹2 to fund Manthan, a film about their own movement.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Amul is a big private company owned by a rich family.

Truth: Amul is owned by dairy farmers. Village milk societies own district unions, and the unions together own the federation that sells Amul products.

Try this

Make butter in a jar. With an adult, pour some fresh cream into a clean jar with a tight lid, filling it about a third. Take turns shaking it hard for 10 to 15 minutes until a lump of butter separates from a thin liquid, the buttermilk. That is the same basic change that happens in a dairy.
