Who are India's chess champions?

In 1988 India had just one grandmaster. Today it has more than 80, and an 18-year-old from Chennai became world champion.

The short answer

India's chess champions include Viswanathan Anand, the country's first grandmaster and a five-time world champion, and D. Gukesh, who in 2024 became the youngest undisputed world champion in history. Koneru Humpy and Divya Deshmukh are among the strongest women players in the world, and R. Praggnanandhaa and Arjun Erigaisi are top-ranked young stars.

In 1988 India had one grandmaster. It now has more than 80. In 2024 India won both gold medals at the Chess Olympiad, the biggest team event in the game.

Surprising but true

When Viswanathan Anand became India's first grandmaster in 1988, he was 18. In 2024, D. Gukesh became world champion at the same age.

Vishy, the Tiger of Madras

Viswanathan Anand learned chess from his mother in Chennai, then called Madras. He played so fast as a teenager that he was nicknamed "the lightning kid". In 1988 he became India's first grandmaster, the highest title in chess.

He won his first world title in 2000 and became undisputed world champion in 2007, keeping the crown until 2013. Altogether he won five world championships. For a generation of Indian children, Anand showed that a player from India could beat anyone.

He was not the first Indian pioneer. Manuel Aaron, also from Tamil Nadu, became India's first International Master, the rank just below grandmaster, in 1961.

The queen of Indian chess

Koneru Humpy, from Andhra Pradesh, became a grandmaster in 2002 at 15, then the youngest woman ever to earn the title. She has won the women's world rapid championship twice, in 2019 and 2024.

In 2025 Divya Deshmukh of Nagpur, then 19, won the Women's World Cup, beating Humpy in an all-Indian final. The win also made her a grandmaster.

The class of 2024

Inspired by Anand, thousands of Indian children joined chess clubs and online platforms. A wave of teenage grandmasters followed. Gukesh Dommaraju of Chennai won the Candidates Tournament in 2024 to earn a shot at the title.

In December 2024, in Singapore, he beat China's Ding Liren to become world champion at 18. Anand, by then mentoring young players at his own academy, had helped train many of them.

A few months earlier, at the Chess Olympiad in Budapest, India's open team of Gukesh, Praggnanandhaa, Arjun Erigaisi, Vidit Gujrathi and Pentala Harikrishna won gold. The women's team, including Harika Dronavalli, R. Vaishali and Divya Deshmukh, won gold too.

Why Chennai?

Chennai has been called India's chess capital. Anand's success inspired local coaches, schools and clubs to take the game seriously. Gukesh and Praggnanandhaa grew up there, and in 2022 the Chess Olympiad was held at Mamallapuram, just south of the city.

Online chess also helped. During the COVID-19 lockdowns in 2020 and 2021, huge numbers of Indian children started playing on phones and laptops, and many kept going.

What makes a champion?

Chess looks like pure calculation, but research suggests it is more about memory of patterns. In the 1970s psychologists showed that masters can glance at a real game and remember almost every piece, because they see familiar groups of pieces. They are no better than beginners with random boards.

Since 1997, when IBM's Deep Blue beat world champion Garry Kasparov, computers have been stronger than any human. Today's champions use artificial intelligence to prepare, testing millions of moves. Yet only a human champion feels the pressure of a world title match, playing for hours with nerves racing like a final over in cricket. Like the best logic puzzles, the game rewards patience as much as speed.

Myth or fact?

Myth: You have to be a maths genius to be good at chess.

Truth: Studies suggest top players rely mostly on patterns they have learned from thousands of games. Practice and study matter far more than being brilliant at maths.

Try this

Set up a chess position from a real game, look at it for ten seconds, cover it and try to rebuild it. Then scatter pieces at random and try again. If you play chess, you will probably find the real position much easier.
