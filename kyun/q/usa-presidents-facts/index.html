What are some surprising facts about US presidents?

Abraham Lincoln is in a wrestling hall of fame, and he is the only US president to hold a patent, for a gadget to lift boats over sandbanks.

The short answer

The USA has had 47 presidencies since 1789, held by 45 people. Behind the official portraits are some odd and human stories. One president was a champion wrestler. One first lady raised silkworms in the White House. Two founders died on the same day, the country's 50th birthday.

Presidents have been tall and short, rich and poor, young and old. Their stories show that the job has changed hugely, from horses and handwritten letters to jets, television and phones.

Surprising but true

Thomas Jefferson and John Adams, two founders of the USA, both died on the same day, 4 July 1826, exactly 50 years after the Declaration of Independence.

Firsts and lasts

George Washington was the first president, but he never lived in the White House. It was not finished until 1800, and John Adams moved in first. Washington also never lived in Washington, DC, the capital named after him. The White House was not even officially called that until 1901, when Theodore Roosevelt made the name official.

Grover Cleveland was the 22nd and the 24th president, with someone else in between. For more than a century he was the only one to serve two terms that were not back to back. Then Donald Trump, the 45th president, returned as the 47th in 2025. So the numbers keep running ahead of the people.

Records and odd jobs

Abraham Lincoln was the tallest president, at about 193 cm. James Madison was the shortest, at around 163 cm, about the height of a Class 8 student. Lincoln was also a skilled wrestler as a young man, said to have lost only one match in about 300, and is honoured by the US National Wrestling Hall of Fame.

Lincoln remains the only president with a patent. In 1849, he designed inflatable chambers to lift river boats over shallow sandbanks. It was never built, but his wooden model sits in the Smithsonian. Jimmy Carter, the 39th president, was a peanut farmer before politics. He lived to 100, the longest life of any president, and died in December 2024. He also won the Nobel Peace Prize in 2002, more than 20 years after leaving office.

Strange coincidences

Thomas Jefferson and John Adams were close friends, then bitter rivals, then friends again through letters in old age. Both died on 4 July 1826, exactly 50 years after the Declaration of Independence that both had helped create. Adams' last words are said to have been "Thomas Jefferson survives", though Jefferson had died a few hours earlier. A fifth president, James Monroe, also died on 4 July, five years later.

William Henry Harrison gave the longest inauguration speech in history in 1841, nearly two hours in cold weather. He fell ill and died 31 days later, the shortest presidency ever. Doctors at the time blamed a cold. Many modern experts suspect a disease from dirty drinking water.

Pets, toys and teddy bears

Theodore Roosevelt's family kept a zoo of pets at the White House, including snakes, a badger and a pony that once rode the lift upstairs. On a hunting trip in 1902, Roosevelt refused to shoot a bear that had been tied to a tree. A cartoon about it inspired toymakers to sell "Teddy's bear", the first teddy bear.

Later presidents brought dogs, cats, birds and once a raccoon. Calvin Coolidge was given the raccoon to eat for a Thanksgiving dinner. He kept it as a pet instead and named it Rebecca.

Four presidents, Washington, Jefferson, Theodore Roosevelt and Lincoln, are carved 18 metres high on Mount Rushmore. The carving took 14 years and around 400 workers, and none of them died on the job. Washington was the first face finished. How a country with no king learned to pick a leader every four years is a stranger story still, told in US elections.

Myth or fact?

Myth: George Washington had wooden false teeth.

Truth: None of his dentures were wooden. They used ivory, metal and real teeth. Over time they may have stained and cracked, which could have made them look like wood grain.
