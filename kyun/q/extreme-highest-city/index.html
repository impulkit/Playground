Which is the highest city in the world?

In La Rinconada, Peru, tens of thousands of people live at about 5,100 m, higher than the tallest peak in the Alps, where each breath holds about half the oxygen it would at the beach.

The short answer

The highest permanent town in the world is probably La Rinconada, in the Andes mountains of Peru. It sits at about 5,100 m, higher than Mont Blanc, the tallest mountain in the Alps, and right beside a glacier. Tens of thousands of people live there, many of them working in small gold mines.

If you mean a big city, the answer is El Alto in Bolivia, at about 4,150 m. Nearly 900,000 people lived there at the 2024 census, on a flat plain above the city of La Paz. Both places show how far people will go to live and work, even where the air is thin and nights freeze.

Surprising but true

El Alto in Bolivia, at about 4,150 m, is home to about 885,000 people (2024 census), which makes it perhaps the highest big city on Earth.

Life in the clouds

La Rinconada has no proper roads in or out for parts of the year, no full sewer system and no central heating. Temperatures drop below freezing most nights. People come because of gold: miners dig into the mountain hoping for a lucky find.

Many workers there are paid under a system called cachorreo. They work for weeks without regular pay, then get to keep whatever ore they can carry out on one day. The town grew fast in the 2000s when gold prices rose, which is one reason nobody is quite sure how many people live there.

Water boils at only about 83 °C up there, instead of 100 °C, because the air presses down less. That means rice and dal take much longer to cook, and a pressure cooker is a real treasure in a high kitchen.

Half a breath

At sea level in Mumbai or Miami, every breath pulls in plenty of oxygen. Air still has the same mix of gases high up, but the air is thinner, so each breath holds less. At 5,100 m, each lungful carries only about half the oxygen it would at the seaside.

Visitors often get headaches, feel sick and can't sleep. This is altitude sickness. Climbing slowly gives the body time to adjust, by making more red blood cells to carry oxygen. Some visitors to La Paz's airport, at over 4,000 m, feel dizzy the moment they step off the plane.

Why is it hard to breathe on high mountains?At the top of Mount Everest, every breath you take holds only about a third of the oxygen it would at the beach.

Bodies built for the heights

People whose families have lived high up for thousands of years have adapted in different ways. Many Andean people have large chests and more red blood cells. Many Tibetans and Sherpas don't make extra red cells. Instead they breathe faster and move more blood through wider vessels.

Scientists found that many Tibetans carry a gene variant, in a gene called EPAS1, that helps with this. Oddly enough, it seems to have come from the Denisovans, ancient relatives of humans who mixed with our ancestors tens of thousands of years ago.

High towns closer to home

India has high towns too. Leh, the main town of Ladakh, sits at about 3,500 m. Flights there arrive in the morning, and visitors are told to rest for a day or two before doing anything tiring. Villages in Ladakh and Spiti, like Komic and Korzok, sit at well over 4,000 m.

Across the border, Lhasa in Tibet lies at about 3,650 m, the same height as La Paz. In the USA, the highest incorporated town is Alma in Colorado, at about 3,160 m (about 10,400 feet). And the highest people who live anywhere still sit thousands of metres below the summits of the tallest mountains, where no one can live for long.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The highest capital in the world is Lhasa or Kathmandu.

Truth: Kathmandu is only about 1,400 m high, and Lhasa is not a national capital. La Paz in Bolivia, at about 3,650 m, is the highest seat of government. Bolivia's official capital, Sucre, sits lower, at about 2,800 m.

Try this

Run on the spot for one minute and count your breaths for the next 30 seconds. Then rest and count again. Your body asks for more air when it needs more oxygen. High up a mountain, it asks for more even when you are sitting still.
