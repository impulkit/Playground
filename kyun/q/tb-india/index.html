What is tuberculosis, and why does it matter so much in India?

TB is one of the oldest diseases known, found in Egyptian mummies. It can be cured with six months of medicine, yet it still kills over a million people a year.

The short answer

Tuberculosis, or TB, is an infection caused by a bacterium called Mycobacterium tuberculosis. It usually attacks the lungs, but it can affect other parts of the body too. People with TB in their lungs can spread it when they cough, sneeze or even talk, sending tiny droplets into the air.

Most people who breathe in the germ never get sick, because their immune system walls it off. But if the immune system is weakened, the germs can wake up and cause disease. TB can be cured with a course of antibiotics lasting about six months. India has more TB cases than any other country, about a quarter of the world's total, which is why the disease matters so much there.

Surprising but true

About a quarter of all people on Earth carry the TB germ without being ill. In most of them, the immune system keeps it locked away for life.

An ancient enemy

TB has been with humans for thousands of years. Signs of it have been found in ancient Egyptian mummies. In the 1800s it was so common in Europe and America that it was called "the white plague". It was also known as consumption, because it seemed to eat people away. The English poet John Keats died of it in 1821, aged 25.

Nobody knew the cause until 1882. On 24 March that year, the German doctor Robert Koch announced he had found the bacterium, using careful staining and microscopes. That date is now World TB Day.

Signs and treatment

The most common signs of TB in the lungs are a cough that lasts more than two or three weeks, fever, sweating at night, losing weight and feeling very tired. Anyone with a cough like that should see a doctor. In India, TB tests and medicines are free at government health centres.

In 1943, scientists in the USA discovered streptomycin, the first antibiotic that worked against TB. Today patients take a mix of several medicines, because TB germs can learn to resist a single drug. Finishing the full course is essential. Stopping early is one reason drug-resistant TB has become a serious problem.

A discovery from Chennai

Before medicines, patients were often sent to sanatoriums, special hospitals in the hills where they rested in fresh air for months. In 1956, doctors at the Tuberculosis Chemotherapy Centre in Madras, now Chennai, ran a famous study. They compared patients treated in a sanatorium with patients who took their medicines at home.

Both groups did about equally well, and family members at home were not put at extra risk once treatment began. The study changed TB care around the world, showing that patients could be treated at home if they took their medicines regularly.

TB in India today

According to the WHO, about 10.8 million people worldwide fell ill with TB in 2023, and about 1.25 million died. India accounted for around a quarter of the cases. Crowded homes, undernourishment, diabetes and smoking all raise the risk of the disease becoming active.

India's National TB Elimination Programme offers free testing and treatment, tracks patients through a system called Nikshay, and gives money for nutrition during treatment. The government set a goal of ending TB well ahead of the United Nations' 2030 target. Cases and deaths have fallen, but the disease is far from gone.

The BCG vaccine

Most babies born in India get the BCG vaccine soon after birth. It was developed in France and first used in 1921. BCG protects young children well against the most severe forms of TB, but it works less well in adults. Scientists are testing new TB vaccines, and there's no shame in having TB. It's an infection like any other, and getting tested and treated protects your family too.

Myth or fact?

Myth: TB is a disease of the past that only happens to poor people.

Truth: TB is still one of the world's deadliest infectious diseases, and anyone can catch it because it spreads through the air. It does hit hardest where people are crowded, underfed or have other illnesses, and it is curable with treatment.

Try this

Coughs and sneezes spread many germs through the air. Spray a fine mist of water from a plant sprayer in front of a dark wall or board and watch how far the droplets travel. Then try again while holding a tissue in front of the nozzle, to see why covering your mouth when you cough helps.
