How big is the Hoover Dam?

The Hoover Dam holds enough concrete to pave a two-lane road from San Francisco to New York, and it was finished in 1936, two years early.

The short answer

The Hoover Dam is a curved wall of concrete across the Colorado River, on the border between the US states of Nevada and Arizona. It is 221 metres tall, about the height of a 60-storey building, and around 200 metres thick at the bottom. That's nearly the length of two football fields laid end to end.

It holds back Lake Mead, one of the largest reservoirs in the United States. Water rushing through its turbines makes electricity for over a million people, and the lake supplies water to farms and cities in the dry southwest.

Surprising but true

If the Hoover Dam's concrete had been poured in one go, it would have taken over a century to cool. Engineers ran icy water through pipes inside it instead.

Why build it

The Colorado River used to flood in spring and nearly dry up in late summer. Farmers downstream in California could lose their crops either way. A dam could store the floodwater and let it out slowly through the year, while making power.

Work began in 1931, during the Great Depression, when millions of Americans had lost their jobs. Thousands of men came to the hot, empty desert looking for work. At the peak, more than 5,000 people were working on the dam at once.

Moving a river

First the river had to be moved out of the way. Workers blasted four huge tunnels through the canyon walls, each about 15 metres wide, and diverted the Colorado through them. Then they built temporary walls to keep the dry riverbed clear.

Men called high scalers hung from ropes on the cliff faces, drilling and blasting away loose rock. It was dangerous work in temperatures that often passed 45 °C. The official count of deaths during construction is 96.

What is concrete made of?Concrete does not harden by drying out. It hardens through a chemical reaction with water, so it can even set underwater.

A mountain of concrete

The dam was not poured as one giant slab. It was built from hundreds of separate column-shaped blocks, each a metre or two high at a time. That was because concrete gives off heat as it hardens. Poured all at once, engineers worked out, it would have taken more than a hundred years to cool and would have cracked.

So workers laid steel pipes through each block and pumped icy water through them. A plant nearby made ice for the job. About 2.5 million cubic metres of concrete went into the dam. It was finished in 1936, two years ahead of schedule.

Power, water and two clocks

Water from the lake drops through giant pipes and spins 17 turbines at the foot of the dam, the same idea that drives hydroelectric dams everywhere. Together they can produce about 2,000 megawatts of electricity, and no fuel has to be burned to make it. Much of it goes to Nevada, Arizona and California, including the lights of Las Vegas, about 50 kilometres away.

The dam sits right on a state line, and for half the year Nevada and Arizona keep different clocks. Arizona doesn't change its clocks in summer, while Nevada does. So in summer, two clocks on the dam's towers show different times, a quirk of time zones.

A lake running low

Lake Mead depends on snow melting in the Rocky Mountains. After many dry years, the water dropped to its lowest level ever recorded in July 2022, when the lake was only about a quarter full. Old boats and buildings that had been underwater for decades appeared on the shore.

Droughts and growing cities mean the seven states that share the Colorado River must agree on how much water each can take, and those talks are often tough. India has its own giant dams too. The Bhakra Dam on the Sutlej, finished in 1963, is about as tall as Hoover, and the Tehri Dam in Uttarakhand is taller still.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Workers who died building the Hoover Dam are buried inside its concrete.

Truth: No bodies are in the dam. The concrete was poured in small blocks, a little at a time, and the people who died were recovered. The official count of deaths during construction is 96.
