What are the busiest places in the world?

Every day, more people pass through one Tokyo railway station than live in the whole of Goa.

The short answer

The busiest airport in the world is in Atlanta, USA, which handled about 106 million passengers in 2025. The busiest railway station is Shinjuku in Tokyo, where about 3.5 million people a day passed through before the 2020 pandemic, more than the population of Goa. The busiest container port is Shanghai in China.

India holds some records of its own. Mumbai's local trains carry around 70 lakh passenger trips on a normal weekday. And the biggest gathering of people ever recorded happened in 2025, when the Maha Kumbh Mela at Prayagraj reported more than 60 crore visits over 45 days.

Surprising but true

The containers moved through the port of Shanghai in 2025, lined up end to end, would wrap around the Earth about eight times.

Airports that never sleep

Atlanta's airport tops the list almost every year. That's not because Atlanta is huge. It's because one of America's biggest airlines uses it as a hub, a giant meeting point where passengers change planes. The airport says about 80 percent of Americans live within a two-hour flight.

Dubai is second, with about 95 million passengers, and it's the busiest airport for international travellers. Planes land and take off there day and night, linking Europe, Africa, Asia and Australia. Keeping them apart is the job of air traffic controllers, who guide each one in and out.

How do air traffic controllers keep planes apart?Thousands of planes share the sky at once, and each one is kept inside an invisible bubble of empty air.

Stations like small cities

Shinjuku station is a maze of railway lines, underground shopping streets and over 200 exits. Guinness World Records lists it as the world's busiest station. Just one stop away, at Shibuya, a famous crossing can fill with as many as 3,000 people at a single change of the lights.

Mumbai's local trains are one of the most crowded railway systems on Earth. At rush hour, trains carry far more people than they were designed for, with a dozen or more standing in a single square metre. Commuters have learned to step on and off before the train fully stops.

Ports that move the world

Most things you own spent time inside a steel shipping container. Ports are where those boxes are lifted on and off ships by cranes as tall as a 20-storey building. In 2025 the port of Shanghai handled about 55 million standard containers, and it has been the world's busiest container port for 16 years in a row.

Lined up end to end, those containers would stretch about 335,000 kilometres, enough to wrap around the Earth about eight times. Sea ports like Shanghai, Singapore and Mundra in Gujarat are the hidden engines of world trade.

Busy skies and seas

Some of the busiest places aren't buildings at all. Flight-tracking services often show more than 10,000 planes in the air around the world at the same moment. Narrow sea lanes such as the Strait of Malacca, between Malaysia and Indonesia, carry tens of thousands of ships every year.

Air traffic controllers, harbour pilots and traffic police all have the same job in the end: keeping crowds of moving things from bumping into each other.

The biggest crowd ever

Every 12 years, the Maha Kumbh Mela draws pilgrims to Prayagraj, where the Ganga, the Yamuna and the mythical Saraswati are said to meet. In 2025, the Uttar Pradesh government reported more than 60 crore visits during the festival, a figure that counts each visit, so many people came more than once. Even so, it was probably the largest human gathering in history.

A temporary city of tents, roads, hospitals and toilets rose on the riverbanks for the festival and was taken down afterwards. You can find out how pilgrims manage it in the story of the Kumbh Mela. And the strangest part? Some places are busy all the time, simply because they're home to more people than many countries, as the list of the world's biggest cities shows.

Myth or fact?

Myth: The busiest airport must be in the biggest city.

Truth: Atlanta isn't one of America's biggest cities, but it tops the world list because it's a giant connecting hub. Millions of travellers change planes there without ever leaving the airport.

Try this

Do a busyness survey. Stand with an adult near a school gate, a shop entrance or a bus stop and count people passing in five minutes. Multiply by 12 to get an hourly figure. Compare a busy time with a quiet time, then work out how long your spot would take to match one day at Shinjuku station.
