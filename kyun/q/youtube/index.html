How did online videos take over?

The first video ever uploaded to YouTube is 19 seconds long and shows a young man at a zoo, talking about elephants' trunks.

The short answer

Online videos took over when three things came together: faster internet, cheap cameras and a simple website where anyone could share videos. That website was YouTube, started in 2005 by three young engineers in California. Within a few years, it had changed how people learned, laughed and listened to music.

Later, cheap smartphones and mobile data put a video camera and a cinema screen in billions of pockets. Today more than 500 hours of video are uploaded to YouTube every minute, and India has more YouTube users than any other country.

Surprising but true

In 2005 the first YouTube video, "Me at the zoo", was filmed at San Diego Zoo. Less than two years later, Google bought the company for 1.65 billion US dollars.

Nineteen seconds at the zoo

YouTube was founded by Chad Hurley, Steve Chen and Jawed Karim, who had worked together at the online payment company PayPal. On 23 April 2005, Karim uploaded the first video, Me at the zoo. In it, he stands in front of the elephants at San Diego Zoo and says their trunks are "really, really, really long". That's about it.

The site's idea was simple. Upload a video, and it plays straight away in a web page, without a special program. Anyone could copy a small piece of code and put the video on their own blog. Clips spread from site to site.

Why video was hard before

A video file is enormous compared with text or photos. In the early 2000s, many homes connected to the internet through phone lines that took minutes to load a single song. Watching a short clip could mean an hour of downloading.

As broadband spread, connections became many times faster. Engineers also improved video compression, ways of shrinking files by storing only what changes between frames. If a person talks in front of a still wall, the wall need not be sent again in every frame.

The Google years

In late 2006, only about 18 months after the first video, Google bought YouTube for 1.65 billion US dollars. Many people thought the price was far too high for a site full of home videos.

YouTube soon began paying video makers a share of advertising money. This created a new kind of job: the YouTuber. In 2012 a Korean music video, Gangnam Style, became the first video to pass one billion views. Music, cooking, science experiments and video game playthroughs all found huge audiences.

India comes online

For years, most Indians watched little online video because mobile data was costly. Around 2016, data prices in India fell sharply, and cheap smartphones became common. Within a few years, India became one of the biggest online video audiences in the world.

Videos in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bhojpuri and many other languages took off. Farmers watch videos about crops, students watch lessons, and grandmothers share recipes. For some people in villages, online video was their first way of using the internet at all.

Watching wisely

Online video sites recommend what to watch next using computer programs. Their aim is to keep you watching, which can make it hard to stop. Doctors suggest that children balance screen time with sleep, play and time outdoors.

Video sites also carry adverts, false stories and some content that is not meant for children. YouTube Kids, launched in 2015, offers a filtered version for younger viewers. Learning about online safety, and watching with a trusted adult, helps. The best videos teach you something new in a few minutes, and there are millions of those too.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Everything you see on a video site is true, because it is filmed.

Truth: Videos can be edited, staged or made with computers, and some spread false information on purpose. It helps to check who made a video and whether trusted sources say the same thing.

Try this

With an adult's help, look up "Me at the zoo" and watch all 19 seconds. Then plan your own 19-second video about one animal, plant or object near you. Write down exactly what you would say. It is harder than it looks to be interesting in so little time.
