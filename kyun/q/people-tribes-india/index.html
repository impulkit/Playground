Who are India's tribal communities?

India is home to more than 10 crore tribal people, more than the whole population of Germany, belonging to over 700 different communities.

The short answer

India's tribal communities, often called Adivasis, meaning "original inhabitants", are groups with their own languages, customs, beliefs and histories. The government lists over 700 of them as Scheduled Tribes, giving them special protections and benefits in the Constitution.

The 2011 Census counted about 10.4 crore people in Scheduled Tribes, around 8.6 percent of India's population. They live in almost every state, with large numbers in central India, the northeast and hilly or forested regions. The Bhils of western India and the Gonds of central India are the largest groups, each with well over a crore people.

Surprising but true

The Santali language has its own alphabet, Ol Chiki, invented in 1925 by a teacher named Raghunath Murmu, and Santali became one of India's official scheduled languages in 2003.

Hundreds of ways of life

There is no single tribal way of life. The Todas of the Nilgiri hills keep buffaloes and live in barrel-shaped huts. The Bhils are famous archers and farmers. The Khasis of Meghalaya trace family names through the mother's side. The Siddis of Gujarat and Karnataka have African ancestors who arrived centuries ago.

Many communities have deep knowledge of forests, plants and animals. Some hold festivals for seeds, rain or harvest. Bonda women in Odisha wear rows of bead necklaces, and Naga tribes of the northeast build tall carved gateposts and morungs, as seen at the Hornbill Festival.

What is the Hornbill Festival?Every December, Nagaland's tribes gather on a hillside near Kohima for ten days of dances, drums, wrestling and a contest to eat one of the hottest chillies on Earth.

Art from the villages

Some of India's best-known art comes from tribal communities. Warli painters in Maharashtra use circles, triangles and lines to show dancing villagers and harvests. Gond artists in Madhya Pradesh fill animals and trees with patterns of dots and dashes. Saura paintings from Odisha, Bhil art and the metal-casting craft of dhokra are prized around the world.

The Gond artist Jangarh Singh Shyam helped bring Gond art to galleries in the 1980s. Today, the work of many tribal artists sells in cities from Delhi to New York, and hangs in museums in both.

Languages and scripts

Tribal people speak hundreds of languages from several language families. Some, like Santali, Gondi, Bhili and Mundari, have millions of speakers. Others are spoken by only a few hundred people and are in danger of disappearing.

In 1925, Raghunath Murmu, a Santhal teacher, created the Ol Chiki script for Santali. In 2003, Santali was added to the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution as one of India's official scheduled languages. Smaller languages face a harder road, explored in dying languages.

Heroes and struggles

Tribal communities have a long history of standing up for their land. Birsa Munda led the Munda people against British rule and unfair landlords in the 1890s, and died in prison aged about 25. Since 2021, his birthday, 15 November, has been marked as Janjatiya Gaurav Divas, Tribal Pride Day.

Many tribal families have faced loss of land to dams, mines and forest laws, and many still have less access to schools and hospitals. Laws such as the Forest Rights Act of 2006 aim to protect their rights to land and forest produce.

The most protected groups

Seventy-five communities are listed as Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups, because they are small in number or live very isolated lives. They include the Jarawa and Sentinelese of the Andaman Islands. The Sentinelese, on North Sentinel Island, avoid all contact with outsiders. Indian law forbids visiting them, both to respect their choice and to protect them from diseases they have no immunity to.

Many Americas too

The USA has its own first peoples, more than 570 federally recognised tribes, from the Navajo to the Cherokee. Like Adivasis in India, they speak many languages, keep old traditions alive and have struggled for land rights. Comparing the two is a good way to understand the story of Native nations.

Myth or fact?

Myth: India's tribal people all live in forests and follow the same way of life.

Truth: Tribal communities are hugely varied. Some are farmers, herders or fishers, many live in towns and cities, and members work as teachers, doctors, athletes and politicians. India's President Droupadi Murmu, elected in 2022, is from the Santhal community.

Try this

Find out which tribal communities live closest to where you live. Look for one craft, food or festival from that community, and try to learn its real name in their language. If you can, visit a tribal museum or crafts fair, such as a state emporium or a Tribes India shop.
