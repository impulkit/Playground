What happens at the Republic Day parade?

Every 26 January, camels, tanks, dancers and fighter jets parade down one road in New Delhi, and people take their seats before sunrise to watch.

The short answer

The Republic Day parade is India's biggest national ceremony. It takes place every 26 January on Kartavya Path, the long, tree-lined avenue in New Delhi that runs from the President's house towards India Gate.

The President unfurls the national flag, the national anthem plays, and a 21-gun salute booms out. Then the parade begins. Soldiers, sailors and airmen march in step. Tanks, missiles and camels roll and stride past. States send decorated floats, called tableaux. Schoolchildren dance. It ends with fighter jets roaring overhead. The whole show lasts about two hours, and millions more watch it on television.

Before the parade starts, the Prime Minister lays a wreath at the National War Memorial near India Gate, honouring soldiers who died for the country. Then the President arrives, escorted by the President's Bodyguard, a regiment of tall horsemen in red coats. Raised in 1773, it is the oldest regiment in the Indian Army.

Surprising but true

The date 26 January was picked because, twenty years before the Constitution began, the freedom movement had chosen that same day in 1930 to declare it wanted complete independence.

Why this day

India won independence on 15 August 1947, but it still followed laws made under British rule. Over nearly three years, an assembly of Indian leaders wrote a new Constitution, with B.R. Ambedkar chairing the committee that drafted it. It came into force on 26 January 1950, and India became a republic.

That date was chosen on purpose. On 26 January 1930 the Indian National Congress had declared Purna Swaraj, complete self-rule, as its goal. Twenty years later the same day became the birthday of the republic.

The march past

The first parade, in 1950, was held at a stadium. Since 1955 it has used the broad avenue then called Rajpath, renamed Kartavya Path in 2022.

Each contingent marches past the President, who stands on a stage and takes their salute. There are regiments from every corner of the country, police and paramilitary forces, and the Border Security Force's camel riders in bright turbans. Military bands play as they walk, and each regiment has its own marching tune. Each year also has a theme. In 2026 the parade marked 150 years of the song "Vande Mataram". The soldiers practise for months, starting in the cold winter mornings, until their lines are straight enough to lay a ruler along.

Floats and stunts

After the soldiers come the tableaux. Each state or ministry builds a float on a truck, often several metres tall, showing a festival, a craft, a temple or a new idea. Only some are picked each year, and states compete hard to be chosen.

Children who have won national bravery and achievement awards are part of the day too. Then motorcycle teams from the army or police ride past balanced in human pyramids, some riders standing on shoulders as the bikes move. Crowds at the side cheer louder for this than almost anything.

Watching in person takes planning. Tickets go on sale in the weeks before, and spectators arrive long before dawn, wrapped in shawls against Delhi's winter fog, because security checks take time. State capitals hold their own smaller parades, and schools across India hoist the flag and hand out sweets.

Jets and a chief guest

The finale belongs to the Indian Air Force. Helicopters and jets fly over in tight shapes, and fighter planes pull straight up into the sky, trailing smoke.

Each year India invites a foreign leader as chief guest. The first, in 1950, was President Sukarno of Indonesia. In 2026, for the 77th Republic Day, there were two: the presidents of the European Council and the European Commission. The celebrations close three days later at Beating Retreat, when the bands play at sunset near the government buildings on Raisina Hill and the lights come on along the buildings.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Republic Day celebrates India becoming independent.

Truth: That's Independence Day, on 15 August. Republic Day marks the start of the Constitution in 1950, when India began to govern itself by its own written rules.
