What is a ghazal?

A ghazal is a poem made of couplets that could each stand alone, held together by a word that comes back like a heartbeat.

The short answer

A ghazal (said "guh-zul") is a kind of poem built from couplets, pairs of lines called sher. Each couplet is complete in itself, like a tiny poem. What ties them together is sound. Every couplet ends with the same word or phrase, called the radif, and just before it comes a rhyme called the qafia.

The form began in Arabic, flowered in Persian, and came to India with poets and kings who spoke Persian. In Urdu it became one of the best-loved kinds of poetry. Sung to music, the ghazal grew into a style that fills concert halls from Lucknow to Lahore, and film songs too.

Surprising but true

The last couplet of a traditional ghazal often contains the poet's own pen name, a bit like a painter signing the corner of a canvas.

The rules of the game

A ghazal usually has five to fifteen couplets. The first one, the matla, sets the pattern: both of its lines end with the radif. After that, only the second line of each couplet does. The last couplet, the maqta, often contains the poet's pen name.

Say the radif is "by the sea". One couplet might end "I dropped my key by the sea", the next "Nani sang to me by the sea". The rhyming words, key and me, change each time. The radif stays put.

Because each couplet stands alone, a ghazal can jump from love to faith to a joke about the weather. Listeners wait for the radif to come back, and enjoy guessing how the poet will reach it this time. At a gathering called a mushaira, the crowd often shouts "wah wah" and asks to hear a good couplet again.

Ghalib's thousand wishes

Mirza Ghalib, who lived in Delhi from 1797 to 1869, is the most quoted Urdu poet. He saw the last days of the Mughal court and the violence of 1857. His ghazals are clever, sad and often funny at his own expense.

One of his most famous couplets begins "Hazaron khwahishen aisi ke har khwahish pe dam nikle." Roughly: "Thousands of wishes, each one worth dying for. Many came true, and yet too few." Ghalib's house in Old Delhi's Ballimaran lane is now a small museum.

How did Urdu develop?Urdu was born in the markets and army camps of north India, where Persian, Arabic and Turkish words met the everyday speech of Delhi.

From Persia to the Deccan

Long before Ghalib, the Persian poets Rumi and Hafez wrote ghazals that are still recited in Iran. Persian was the language of courts across northern India for centuries, so the form travelled. Amir Khusrau, a poet and musician in Delhi around 1300, wrote ghazals in Persian and mixed in the local tongue.

By the 1600s, poets in the Deccan were writing ghazals in an early form of Urdu. Later, Mir Taqi Mir, Daagh, Faiz Ahmad Faiz and many others kept the form alive. Today ghazals are written in Hindi, Gujarati, Marathi, Punjabi, Bengali and more.

When ghazals are sung

Singing a ghazal is gentler than the roaring qawwali. The singer lingers over the words, so the audience can taste them. Begum Akhtar, from Faizabad, was called Mallika-e-Ghazal, the queen of ghazal, in the mid-1900s. Mehdi Hassan and Ghulam Ali became stars in Pakistan and India alike.

In the 1970s and 80s, Jagjit Singh brought ghazals to millions of new listeners with simple tunes, guitars and a soft voice. Many Hindi film songs borrow the form, with a repeated phrase at the end of each verse.

A ghazal in English

The Kashmiri-American poet Agha Shahid Ali, who taught at universities in the USA, wanted English speakers to write "real ghazals" that followed the old rules. In 2000 he edited a collection of them by American poets. His own ghazals mix memories of Kashmir with life in America. So a form that began in Arabian deserts now turns up in poetry classes in Ohio and Bengaluru.

Myth or fact?

Myth: A ghazal is just a sad Urdu song.

Truth: A ghazal is a form of poetry with strict rules, written in many languages and often read without music. Many are about longing, but others are playful, spiritual or full of jokes.

Try this

Write a three-couplet ghazal in English or Hindi. Pick a short ending word or phrase, like "again" or "phir se", and end both lines of the first couplet with it. Then end only the second line of each later couplet with it. Make each couplet a different thought, and sign the last one with a pen name.
