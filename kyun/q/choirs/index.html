How does a choir sing together?

When people sing together in a choir, a Swedish study found, their heartbeats can start to speed up and slow down together too.

The short answer

A choir is a group of people who sing together. To sound like one voice, they split into parts, follow a conductor, breathe at the same moments and listen to each other all the time. Each singer adjusts their pitch, loudness and timing to blend with the people around them.

Most choirs have four main parts: sopranos sing highest, then altos, tenors and basses. When the parts sing different notes at once, they make harmony. That rich, layered sound is something a single voice can't make on its own.

Surprising but true

The Vienna Boys' Choir was founded in 1498, more than 500 years ago, and boys still join it and tour the world.

Splitting into parts

Sopranos often carry the tune that you would hum. Altos sing a little lower, filling in the middle. Tenors sit higher among men's voices, and basses provide the low notes that hold the music up, like the foundations of a building.

Children's choirs often sing in just two or three parts, because young voices haven't yet split into high and low. Some choirs sing in unison, everyone on the same notes, which sounds simple but is hard to get perfectly together. A round, like "Frère Jacques", is a neat way to start harmony, because everyone sings the same tune but starts at different times.

Watching the conductor

The conductor stands in front and shows the beat with their hands or a stick called a baton. They signal when to start, when to get louder or softer and when to stop. A tiny lift of the hand can tell sixty singers to breathe at the same moment.

Breathing matters a lot. Singers fill their lungs deeply, using the muscle under the ribs called the diaphragm. Then they let the air out slowly and steadily, so long notes don't wobble. In big choirs, singers sometimes take turns to breathe during a long note, so the sound never seems to stop.

How does your voice work?Every word you speak starts as two small folds of tissue in your throat, slapping together hundreds of times a second.

Listening while you sing

The real secret is listening. Each singer has to hear their own voice and the voices next to them at once. If a note is slightly flat, they nudge it up. If they're louder than their neighbours, they ease back.

In 2013 researchers at the University of Gothenburg in Sweden measured singers' heart rates during choir practice. As the choir sang slow, calm phrases together, the singers' heartbeats tended to rise and fall together. Steady, shared breathing seems to be the reason. Many people say singing in a group makes them feel calmer and closer to others.

Choirs around the world

The Vienna Boys' Choir in Austria began in 1498 and still tours the world. In Britain, cathedral choirs have sung daily services for centuries. In the United States, gospel choirs in African American churches sing with clapping, swaying and powerful solo voices.

India has its own group singing traditions. Bhajan and kirtan groups sing devotional songs, with a leader singing a line and everyone repeating it. In a qawwali, a lead singer is backed by a chorus that claps and answers. In north-east India, church choirs are famous, and in 2010 the Shillong Chamber Choir won the TV contest India's Got Talent.

Anyone can join

You don't need to be a star to sing in a choir. Many schools, places of worship and community centres have choirs that welcome beginners. The leader teaches parts by ear or from written music, and singers learn by standing next to someone stronger.

Singing in a choir is a little like playing in an orchestra, except the instrument is inside you. You can't buy a new one, so choir leaders teach singers to warm up gently and not to shout. Many people keep singing in choirs well into their eighties.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Either you're born able to sing, or you can't sing at all.

Truth: Singing is a skill that improves with practice, like riding a bicycle. Only a small share of people have true tone deafness. Most people who think they can't sing just haven't had much practice or teaching.

Try this

Get three friends or family members and sing a simple round like Frère Jacques. One person starts, and the next joins in from the beginning when the first reaches the second line. Keep going until all four voices overlap, then try to finish together.
