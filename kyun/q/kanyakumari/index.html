Where do three seas meet in India?

At the southern tip of mainland India, you can watch the sun rise out of the sea and, that evening, watch it sink back into the sea from the same shore.

The short answer

Kanyakumari is a small town at the very southern tip of mainland India, in Tamil Nadu. Here the Arabian Sea to the west, the Bay of Bengal to the east and the Indian Ocean to the south all meet. Hindus call this meeting point a sangam, and many pilgrims bathe there.

Because the land narrows to a point, the sea lies to the east, south and west. That makes Kanyakumari one of the few places in India where you can watch both sunrise and sunset over water. Out in the waves stand two rocky islets, one topped by a memorial to Swami Vivekananda and the other by a giant statue of the Tamil poet Thiruvalluvar.

Surprising but true

Kanyakumari is not the southernmost point of India. That title belongs to Indira Point on Great Nicobar Island, about 150 km north of Indonesia's Sumatra.

The goddess at land's end

The town takes its name from Kanya Kumari, the virgin goddess, a form of the goddess Parvati. Her temple stands near the shore. In the best-known legend, she was to marry the god Shiva at midnight. When the wedding did not happen, she remained unmarried, waiting at the tip of India forever. Some versions say the rice and grains for the wedding feast turned into the multicoloured sand found on the beach.

Pilgrims from all over India visit the temple. People of many faiths come to see the sunrise.

The monk on the rock

In December 1892, a young monk named Swami Vivekananda reached Kanyakumari after travelling across India. He swam out to a rock about 500 metres from the shore and meditated there for three days, thinking about the country's poverty and its future. The next year, he sailed to the USA and spoke at the World's Parliament of Religions in Chicago, where he became famous.

In 1970, the Vivekananda Rock Memorial opened on that rock. Ferries carry visitors across. A stone footprint there is believed by devotees to be the goddess Kanya Kumari's.

The poet in the sea

On the neighbouring rock stands a statue of Thiruvalluvar, author of the Thirukkural, a book of 1,330 short couplets about good living, wealth and love. The statue was unveiled on the first day of 2000. Its total height, 133 feet, stands for the 133 chapters of his book. It was built to stand up to storms and survived the 2004 tsunami, which killed many people along this coast.

Since December 2024, a glass-floored bridge about 77 metres long has linked the two rocks. Visitors walk across with the waves visible beneath their feet.

Sunrise, sunset and a full moon

Crowds gather on the beach before dawn. As the sun clears the horizon, cameras click and tea sellers do brisk business. In the evening, people move to Sunset Point on the western side.

On Chitra Pournami, the full moon of the Tamil month of Chithirai in April or May, there is a rarer sight. Sometimes the moon rises over one sea while the sun sets over another, both visible at once. The orange sunset and the pale moon face each other across the sky.

Gandhi's ashes and a beam of sun

After Mahatma Gandhi died in 1948, part of his ashes were kept at Kanyakumari before being scattered in the sea. The Gandhi Mandapam was built on the spot. It was designed so that on 2 October, his birthday, sunlight falls through a hole in the roof onto the place where the urn stood.

Look at a map of India's shape and Kanyakumari is its sharp point. Sail due south from here and, after about 5,000 km of open ocean, the first land is a tiny French island called Île Amsterdam. Keep going and the next stop is Antarctica.

Myth or fact?

Myth: You can see three different coloured seas meeting in neat stripes at Kanyakumari.

Truth: The three bodies of water flow into each other without clear lines. On some days, currents, sand and light make patches of slightly different colour, which is where the idea comes from.
