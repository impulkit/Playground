Why does Mysore Palace light up?

On Sunday evenings at seven, about 97,000 bulbs switch on at once and Mysore Palace glows gold against the dark sky.

The short answer

Mysore Palace lights up because its royal family loved to show off a new wonder of the age: electric light. Its outline is covered in about 97,000 bulbs. When they all switch on together, every dome, arch and tower shines gold.

The lights come on for an hour on Sunday evenings and public holidays. During Dasara, the city's great autumn festival, they glow for two hours every night, and the whole city of Mysuru fills with visitors.

The palace was home to the Wodeyar family, who ruled the kingdom of Mysore for most of the past 600 years. It's now among the most visited places in India.

Surprising but true

The palace you see today is the second on the site. The old wooden palace burned down in 1897, during a royal wedding.

A palace rebuilt

There has been a royal palace on this spot for centuries. The older one was made mostly of wood. In 1897, during the wedding of a princess, it caught fire and burned down.

The young maharaja's mother, who was ruling for him at the time, ordered a new palace in stone. The British architect Henry Irwin designed it, and it was finished in 1912. It is a three-storey building of grey granite, with marble domes and a tall central tower about 44 metres high.

Its style is called Indo-Saracenic. It mixes Hindu temple shapes, Mughal domes and arches, Rajput balconies and European glass and iron. Inside are halls with stained-glass ceilings, carved doors and floors of polished stone. Visitors take off their shoes before going inside.

A city that took to electricity early

The princely state of Mysore was quick to bring in electricity. In 1902 it opened a hydroelectric power station at Shivanasamudra, on the Kaveri river. It was one of the first in Asia, and sent power about 140 km away to the Kolar gold mines.

Soon after, Bangalore became one of the first cities in India to light its streets with electric lamps, and Mysore followed. Covering the new palace in light bulbs was a way of saying the kingdom was modern.

Lighting 97,000 bulbs

Today the bulbs run along every edge of the palace, like dots in a join-the-dots drawing. They trace the domes, the arches, the balconies and the tall central tower, so that after dark the whole outline of the building hangs in the air.

Keeping it working is a big job. Electricians check for fused bulbs and replace them, especially before Dasara. Many people say the best view is from the lawns in front, just as the lights come on and the crowd lets out a gasp.

Dasara and the elephants

Mysuru Dasara is the city's biggest festival, held over ten days around September or October. It celebrates the goddess Chamundeshwari's victory over the demon Mahishasura, who gave the city its name. Across India the same season brings Navaratri and Durga Puja.

On the last day, called Vijayadashami, a decorated elephant carries a golden howdah, a seat said to weigh about 750 kg, holding an image of the goddess. It walks in a long procession called the Jamboo Savari, with bands, dancers and floats, from the palace through the city.

The rulers of Mysore

The Wodeyars ruled Mysore from the 1300s. In the 18th century, the general Hyder Ali and then his son Tipu Sultan took power, until Tipu was killed fighting the British in 1799. The British then put the Wodeyars back on the throne.

After India's independence, Mysore joined India and the old kingdom became part of today's Karnataka. The palace is now run by the state government, though the royal family still lives in one part and still leads Dasara prayers inside.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Mysore Palace is lit up with its thousands of bulbs every night.

Truth: The full illumination happens only on Sunday evenings, public holidays and during the Dasara festival. On other nights the palace is mostly dark.
