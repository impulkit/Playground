How do countries change their constitutions?

In 1982, a university student in Texas got a C for an essay about a forgotten amendment from 1789. He spent ten years campaigning, and in 1992 it became part of the US Constitution.

The short answer

A constitution is a country's highest set of rules. Because it matters so much, it is deliberately hard to change. Each change is called an amendment.

Most countries require a bigger majority than for ordinary laws. In India, most amendments need a two-thirds majority of members present and voting in each house of Parliament, and some also need approval from half the state legislatures. In the USA, an amendment needs two-thirds of both houses of Congress, and then three-quarters of the 50 states must agree. Some countries, like Ireland and Switzerland, put changes to a public vote, a referendum.

Surprising but true

India has amended its Constitution more than 100 times since 1950, while the US Constitution has been amended only 27 times since 1789.

America's slow changes

The US Constitution came into force in 1789. Two years later, the first ten amendments, known as the Bill of Rights, were added, protecting freedoms like speech and religion. Since then, only 17 more have passed.

Some changed the country deeply. The 13th Amendment ended slavery in 1865. The 19th gave women the vote in 1920. The 26th lowered the voting age from 21 to 18 in 1971. Only one, the 18th, which banned alcohol, was later cancelled.

How did women win the right to vote?In some countries women campaigned for more than 70 years to vote. In India, they could vote in the very first national election.

The C-grade essay

In 1982, Gregory Watson, a student at the University of Texas, wrote a paper about an amendment first proposed by James Madison in 1789. It said that if members of Congress voted themselves a pay rise, it couldn't take effect until after the next election. The amendment had never been ratified by enough states, but it had no deadline.

Watson's teacher gave him a C. He began writing to state lawmakers across the country, asking them to ratify it. One by one, they did. In 1992, the 27th Amendment became part of the Constitution, more than 200 years after it was proposed. In 2017, the university changed his grade to an A.

India's many amendments

India's Constitution is much longer and more detailed than America's, so it needs changing more often. Its makers expected this. In the Constituent Assembly, B. R. Ambedkar argued that each generation should be able to adapt the Constitution to its own needs, so India chose a path between very rigid and very easy. The 1st Amendment came in 1951. The 61st, in 1988, lowered the voting age from 21 to 18. The 73rd, in 1992, made panchayats a part of the Constitution. The 86th, in 2002, made education a fundamental right for children aged 6 to 14. The 106th, in 2023, reserved a third of seats in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies for women, to take effect after the next census and redrawing of constituencies.

The 42nd Amendment of 1976, passed during the Emergency, made so many changes that it was called a "mini-constitution". It added the words "socialist" and "secular" to the Preamble. Many of its other changes were reversed in 1978.

Limits on change

Can an amendment change anything at all? In 1973, in a case brought by Kesavananda Bharati, the head of a monastery in Kerala, the Supreme Court of India said no. Parliament can amend the Constitution, the judges ruled, but not destroy its "basic structure", such as democracy, federalism, secularism and judicial review.

The decision was close, seven judges to six. It remains one of the most important judgments in Indian history, and courts in some other countries have since borrowed the idea.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Parliament can change anything in India's Constitution if enough members vote for it.

Truth: In 1973, the Supreme Court ruled that Parliament cannot change the Constitution's "basic structure", such as democracy, the rule of law and the independence of courts. Courts can strike down amendments that do.
