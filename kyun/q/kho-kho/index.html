How is kho-kho played?

In kho-kho, the chasers sit in a line facing opposite ways, and the hunt passes from player to player with a tap on the back and a shout.

The short answer

Kho-kho is a chasing game from India played by two teams. One team chases and the other defends. Eight chasers sit or squat in a line down the middle of a long court, facing alternate directions. A ninth chaser is the active one, running after the defenders.

The active chaser cannot turn back or cut across the middle line. To change direction, they touch a seated teammate on the back and shout "kho". That teammate leaps up and takes over the chase. Defenders come on three at a time, and each one tagged is out.

Surprising but true

In January 2025, New Delhi hosted the first Kho Kho World Cup, and India won both the men's and women's titles.

The court and the poles

The court is a rectangle, about 27 metres long and 16 metres wide for adults, longer than a cricket pitch is from stumps to stumps. A central lane runs down the middle with a wooden pole at each end. The eight seated chasers fill squares along that lane.

The poles are the only places where a chaser can turn around. A clever chaser uses them to swing round the end of the line and surprise a defender from behind.

How a turn works

Each team gets a turn to chase and a turn to defend, usually twice. The defending team sends in three players at a time. When all three are out, the next three come in.

Defenders dodge, weave between the seated chasers and run round the poles. They try to stay "alive" as long as possible. The chasing team scores a point for every defender tagged, and the team with the most points at the end wins. Under newer rules, a defender who survives long enough can also earn points.

A game of tricks

Kho-kho is much more than running fast. Chasers plan "kho" chains, passing the chase down the line faster than a defender can sprint. A good team corners defenders between two chasers coming from opposite directions.

Defenders practise sudden stops and turns. They watch the seated players closely, because the next chaser could spring up from anywhere. Diving and stretching to tag a defender, called a pole dive or a sky dive, can look like a gymnastics move. All this needs powerful leg muscles and quick thinking.

From Pune to the world

Children across India have played chasing games like kho-kho for generations. The first written rules were drawn up in 1914 by the Deccan Gymkhana, a sports club in Pune. In 1936 a group from Amravati demonstrated kho-kho, along with kabaddi, at the Berlin Olympics.

The Kho Kho Federation of India was founded in 1955, and national championships followed. The game is played in schools across Maharashtra, Karnataka, Kerala and beyond. It is also a medal sport at India's national school games.

A new professional era

In 2022 a professional league, Ultimate Kho Kho, started. It changed the game for television with shorter turns, a smaller squad on court and bonus points for exciting play. Players who had played on mud grounds suddenly appeared on prime-time screens.

In January 2025, New Delhi hosted the first Kho Kho World Cup, with teams from about two dozen countries. India won both the men's and women's titles, beating Nepal in both finals. A game once played in school yards, with nothing but chalk lines and two sticks, now has a world champion.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Kho-kho is only played in India.

Truth: It is popular in Nepal, Bangladesh and Sri Lanka, and has federations in many other countries. Teams from about two dozen nations took part in the 2025 World Cup.

Try this

Play a tiny version with six friends in a safe open space. Four sit in a row facing alternate ways, one chases and one runs. The chaser may not turn back but can tap a sitter and shout "kho" to swap. Notice how quickly the chase changes direction.
