Did dinosaurs live in India?

Lots! India has bones, footprints, eggs, and one of the biggest dinosaur nurseries in the world.

The short answer

Yes. India's first dinosaur bones were found in Jabalpur in 1828. The word "dinosaur" was only invented in 1842.

In the Narmada valley, scientists found hundreds of eggs laid by giant long-necked sauropods. India also has footprints, fossil poo and bones from many kinds of dinosaur.

When most of these dinosaurs lived, India was an island, slowly drifting towards Asia. That makes its dinosaurs special. Some have close cousins in Madagascar and South America, but nowhere else.

Surprising but true

India's dinosaurs lived on a giant island floating across the ocean.

The first finds

The bones from Jabalpur were collected by William Henry Sleeman, a British army officer. Nobody knew what they were. Decades later, in 1877, the scientist Richard Lydekker named them Titanosaurus indicus, "titanic lizard of India". It was the first dinosaur named from India, and it gave its name to the whole group of titanosaurs.

Since then, Indian scientists have led most of the work. Teams from the Geological Survey of India, the Indian Statistical Institute, Panjab University and others have found new species in Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Telangana and Tamil Nadu.

Dinosaurs across time

India's dinosaurs span most of the dinosaur age. Some of the oldest come from the Maleri rocks of Telangana, about 220 million years old, when dinosaurs were small and new. Barapasaurus, one of the earliest big long-necks, lived by the Godavari about 190 million years ago.

In 2023, scientists named Tharosaurus from rocks near Jaisalmer, about 167 million years old. It may be one of the oldest known members of the group that includes Diplodocus. At the very end of the age came Rajasaurus, a horned meat-eater from Gujarat, and the great titanosaurs.

A land of giants

Long-necked titanosaurs were India's most common big dinosaurs near the end of the age of dinosaurs. Isisaurus, named after the Indian Statistical Institute, had unusually long front legs. Titanosaurs laid eggs in huge nesting grounds in Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh. Near Dhar, scientists found 92 nests holding 256 eggs.

Their eggs were about the size of a small football. At Dholi Dungri in Gujarat, a fossil snake was found coiled in a titanosaur nest, beside a hatchling.

Clues beyond bones

Some of India's most important finds are not bones at all. At Pisdura in Maharashtra, fossil dinosaur poo held tiny bits of grass, showing that grass grew in India while dinosaurs were alive. Near Jaisalmer in Rajasthan, three-toed footprints are pressed into rock that was once a sandy shore. Each kind of clue fills in a different part of the picture.

An island world

When these dinosaurs lived, India was drifting alone across the ocean, after breaking away from Madagascar about 88 million years ago. That's why its animals have cousins in Madagascar. Rajasaurus and Majungasaurus of Madagascar belong to the same family of short-armed hunters.

Near the end, India passed over a hot spot that fed the Deccan eruptions. Many fossils lie in a layer of rock called the Lameta Formation, sandwiched just below the lava.

Fossil parks you can visit

The Balasinor Dinosaur Fossil Park in Gujarat and the Dinosaur Fossil National Park in Dhar, Madhya Pradesh, protect places where fossils were found. Visitors can see eggs still in the rock. Some sites have suffered from people taking eggs and bones, which destroys clues forever. Protecting these sites, and keeping fossils in museums where scientists can study them, is now a big part of Indian palaeontology.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Dinosaurs mostly lived in America and China, not in India.

Truth: India has dinosaur fossils from almost the whole age of dinosaurs, from the Triassic to the very end. Its egg sites in Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh are among the largest in the world.
