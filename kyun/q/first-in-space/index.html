Who was the first person in space?

On 12 April 1961, a 27-year-old pilot shouted "Let's go!" and became the first human being to see Earth from space.

The short answer

The first person in space was Yuri Gagarin, a pilot from the Soviet Union. On 12 April 1961, he was launched in a small spacecraft called Vostok 1. He circled Earth once, travelling at about 27,000 km/h, and landed safely 108 minutes after lift-off.

As the rocket roared off the pad, Gagarin called out "Poyekhali!", Russian for "Let's go!". He was 27 years old. After his flight he became one of the most famous people on Earth, and 12 April is now celebrated by the United Nations as the International Day of Human Space Flight.

Surprising but true

Yuri Gagarin did not land inside his spacecraft. He ejected about 7 km above the ground and came down on his own parachute, which the Soviet Union kept quiet for years.

A farm boy who flew

Yuri Gagarin was born in 1934 in a village called Klushino, west of Moscow. His parents worked on a collective farm. During the Second World War, German soldiers occupied his village, and his family lived for a time in a dugout in the ground.

After the war he trained as a metalworker, then joined a flying club and later the Soviet Air Force as a fighter pilot. In 1960 he was chosen as one of 20 young pilots to train as cosmonauts, the Russian word for astronauts. One reason he was picked for the first flight was practical: he was only about 1.57 m tall, which helped in the cramped capsule. He was also calm and cheerful under pressure.

108 minutes

Vostok 1 was launched from Baikonur, in what is now Kazakhstan, the same site that had sent up Sputnik four years earlier. The spacecraft was a ball about 2.3 m wide, with a small window. Most of the flight was controlled automatically, because nobody knew whether a human could think clearly while weightless. Gagarin had a sealed envelope with a code to switch on the manual controls in an emergency.

He looked out at Earth and reported what he saw, including the thin blue band of the atmosphere at the edge of the planet. He said he felt well, and that weightlessness did not stop him working, writing notes or thinking clearly.

At about 7 km above the ground, he ejected from the capsule and floated down by parachute, wearing a bright orange spacesuit. He landed in a field near the town of Engels, in southern Russia. A farm woman and her granddaughter were the first people to meet him. He told them not to be afraid, because he was a Soviet citizen like them.

The space race heats up

Gagarin's flight was a huge victory for the Soviet Union in its space race with the United States. Less than a month later, on 5 May 1961, Alan Shepard became the first American in space, on a 15-minute flight that went up and came straight back down. In February 1962, John Glenn became the first American to orbit Earth.

Before people flew, both countries had sent animals. In August 1960 two Soviet dogs, Belka and Strelka, orbited Earth and came home safely, which helped convince engineers that a human could survive the trip.

The first woman

On 16 June 1963, Valentina Tereshkova, a former textile factory worker and keen parachutist, flew Vostok 6. She orbited Earth 48 times in nearly three days, longer than all the American astronauts up to then put together. She was 26. Nearly 20 years passed before another woman went to space.

After the flight

Gagarin never flew in space again. He was too precious to risk. He toured the world, including India in 1961, where he met Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru. In 1968 he died in a training jet crash, aged 34.

Less than ten years after Gagarin's 108 minutes, astronauts were walking on the Moon during Apollo 11. India plans to launch its own astronauts through its Gaganyaan programme.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Neil Armstrong was the first person in space.

Truth: Armstrong was the first person to walk on the Moon, in 1969. Yuri Gagarin had flown into space eight years earlier, in 1961.
