Why are bananas curved?

Bananas start out pointing at the ground. Then each one bends upwards to reach for the sunlight, and that turn gives it its curve.

The short answer

Bananas are curved because of the way they grow. They grow in big bunches on a stalk that hangs down from the top of the plant. At first, each little banana points towards the ground. As it grows, it starts to turn up towards the sunlight.

Scientists call this kind of growth negative geotropism: growing away from the pull of gravity. Plant stems do it all the time. For a banana, the fruit has already started off pointing down, so when it turns up, it ends up shaped like a smile. In dense rainforests where bananas first grew wild, reaching for light helped the plant survive.

Surprising but true

The banana plant isn't a tree. It's a giant herb with no wood in it, and its "trunk" is made of tightly rolled leaves.

A herb taller than a bus

The banana plant can grow taller than a double-decker bus, but it isn't a tree. It has no wood at all. What looks like a trunk is a "pseudostem", made of the bases of huge leaves wrapped tightly around each other, like a rolled-up newspaper.

After a plant flowers and fruits once, that stem dies. A new shoot, called a sucker, grows from the underground base to take its place. A single banana field can keep going this way for many years.

Hanging upside down

A banana bunch starts as a giant purple flower bud that droops down on a long stalk. Rows of small flowers open under the petals. Each female flower has a tiny green banana behind it.

At first these little fingers point down, because the whole stalk hangs. Then, over a few weeks, they turn and grow upwards. Cells on the lower side of each fruit grow faster than those on the top. That makes the banana bend, just as uneven growth makes a shoot bend towards a window. It is one of many slow ways that plants move without muscles.

Can plants move?Touch a chhui-mui leaf and it folds shut in seconds. Plants move all the time; most are just too slow for us to notice.

A berry with no seeds

Botanists call the banana a berry, because it grows from a single flower and has seeds inside. Strawberries, oddly, are not true berries. Wild bananas are full of hard black seeds, as big as peppercorns, and hardly any flesh.

The bananas we eat have been bred over thousands of years to have tiny, soft seeds that can't grow. The black specks in the middle are what's left of them. So bananas can't be planted from seed. Farmers grow new plants from suckers or from tissue grown in labs. In a sense, every Cavendish banana in a shop is a copy of the same plant.

India, land of bananas

India grows more bananas than any other country, about a quarter of the world's total in 2022. Most stay in India, eaten as a snack, cooked as a vegetable, or fried into chips in Kerala. Raw banana, the flower and even the inner stem are all cooked in South Indian kitchens.

There are hundreds of kinds, from tiny sweet Elaichi bananas to red bananas and the large Nendran of Kerala. In South Indian meals, food is often served on a fresh banana leaf, which is big, waterproof and thrown away afterwards with no washing up.

One kind, one risk

Most bananas sold around the world, including in the US, are one kind: the Cavendish. Before the 1950s, the favourite was a different kind called Gros Michel. A fungus called Panama disease wiped out many Gros Michel farms, and growers switched to Cavendish.

Now a new form of the same disease is attacking Cavendish plants in Asia, Africa and Latin America. Because every Cavendish is a copy, none of them can fight it off. Scientists are racing to breed or engineer bananas that can survive.

Bananas have one more odd secret. They contain potassium, and a tiny part of that potassium is naturally radioactive. It's far too little to hurt you, but scientists even made a joke unit out of it: the banana equivalent dose.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Bananas grow on trees.

Truth: The banana plant is the world's biggest herb. Its tall stem is made of layers of leaf bases wrapped tightly together, with no wood inside. After it fruits once, the stem dies and a new shoot grows from the base.

Try this

Plant a bean in a clear cup of damp soil and lay the cup on its side once the shoot appears. Over a few days, watch the shoot bend to grow upwards again and the root bend to grow down. Bananas use the same kind of bending.
