What's the difference between a president and a prime minister?

India has a President and a Prime Minister. America has only a President. Britain has a Prime Minister and a King. Who is really in charge?

The short answer

A president and a prime minister can both lead a country, but they get their power in different ways. In a presidential system, like the United States, voters choose the president separately from the lawmakers, and the president is both head of state and head of the government.

In a parliamentary system, like India or Britain, voters elect a parliament. The leader of the majority there becomes prime minister and runs the government. A separate head of state, a monarch or a president, stays mostly above party politics.

Surprising but true

In 2022, Britain had three different prime ministers in a single year, and there was no general election in between.

The American way

In the United States, the President is elected for four years and can serve at most two terms. The President leads the government, commands the armed forces and represents the country abroad. But the President is not a member of Congress and cannot make laws alone.

A US President cannot be removed just because Congress dislikes their policies. The only way to remove one early is impeachment, a trial for serious wrongdoing, and no president has ever been removed that way.

The parliamentary way

In India, the Prime Minister must keep the support of a majority in the Lok Sabha, the elected house of Parliament. Ministers are themselves members of Parliament, and they must answer questions there. If the majority votes "no confidence", the government falls. A party can also swap its leader, so the prime minister can change without a national election.

That is what happened in Britain in 2022, when three people held the job in one year. The British Prime Minister lives at 10 Downing Street in London, while King Charles III is head of state.

What makes London famous?London has the world's oldest underground railway, a castle where ravens are kept by royal tradition, and a clock bell called Big Ben that most people mistake for a tower.

India's President

India's President is elected for five years, but not by ordinary voters. An electoral college of MPs and members of state assemblies chooses them. The President signs bills into law, appoints the Prime Minister, and is supreme commander of the armed forces.

In practice the President acts on the advice of the Prime Minister and ministers. Still, the job carries weight. A President can send a bill back to Parliament once to be reconsidered, and decides who to invite to form a government when no party has a clear majority. The President's official home in Delhi, Rashtrapati Bhavan, was designed by Edwin Lutyens and has about 340 rooms. The White House has 132.

Mixing the two

Some countries use a bit of both. France has a powerful elected president and also a prime minister who needs parliament's support. Sometimes the two come from rival parties and have to share the work. The French call this "cohabitation", which means living together.

Other countries have a president with almost no political power at all. Germany's president, for example, signs laws and greets visiting leaders, while the chancellor, Germany's name for a prime minister, runs the government. The title alone does not tell you how much power someone has. You have to read the country's constitution.

Which is better?

Political scientists disagree. A presidential system gives a clear leader for a fixed term, and it is hard to remove them early. A parliamentary system can replace a leader quickly, but governments may fall if the majority splits. Each country chose its system from its own history. India picked the British-style system it already knew when it wrote its Constitution, and every democracy keeps arguing about how to make its own version work better.

Myth or fact?

Myth: India's President runs the government day to day.

Truth: Under the Constitution, real executive power lies with the Prime Minister and the council of ministers. In most matters the President acts on their advice, much like Britain's monarch, though the President has a few important powers of their own.
