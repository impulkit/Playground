How did ballet begin?

The most famous ballet dancer of the 1600s was a king. Louis XIV of France danced on stage as the rising Sun.

The short answer

Ballet began about 500 years ago as entertainment in the royal courts of Italy, during the Renaissance. Rich families put on grand shows with music, poetry, costumes and dancing at weddings and feasts. When the Italian noblewoman Catherine de' Medici married into the French royal family, she helped bring these spectacles to France.

In France, ballet became a serious art with strict rules. King Louis XIV, who loved to dance, founded a royal dance academy in 1661. Teachers there set down the basic positions and steps, with French names still used everywhere today. Later, ballet grew in Russia, where famous works such as Swan Lake and The Nutcracker were created, and then spread around the world.

Surprising but true

A professional ballerina dancing a lead role can wear out a pair of pointe shoes in a single performance.

Dancing kings

Early court ballets were performed by nobles, not professionals. They wore heavy costumes, masks and high-heeled shoes, so the steps were slow and graceful rather than athletic.

The young Louis XIV took part in many ballets. In 1653, aged 14, he danced the role of the rising Sun in a ballet that lasted all night. The part gave him his famous nickname, the Sun King. His dancing master, Pierre Beauchamp, is credited with describing the five basic positions of the feet that every ballet student still learns.

What was the Renaissance?In one Italian city, within a single lifetime, artists worked out how to paint depth, and an architect raised the largest brick dome ever built.

Rising onto the toes

Over time, professional dancers replaced nobles, and ballet moved from palace halls to public theatres in Paris and elsewhere. Skirts became shorter so audiences could see the feet. Dancers began to jump higher and spin faster.

In the early 1800s, ballerinas started dancing on the very tips of their toes, called dancing en pointe. In 1832, the Italian-Swedish ballerina Marie Taglioni danced La Sylphide in Paris, floating on her toes as a fairy spirit. It made her a star. Pointe shoes have a stiff box at the toe, built from layers of fabric, paper and glue, and a firm sole for support.

The Russian years

In the 1800s, the Russian tsars spent fortunes on ballet. A French choreographer, Marius Petipa, worked in St Petersburg for decades and created grand ballets with huge casts. The composer Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky wrote the music for three of the most famous: Swan Lake (1877), The Sleeping Beauty (1890) and The Nutcracker (1892).

In the early 1900s, a Russian company called the Ballets Russes toured Europe, working with modern painters and composers. Ballet became bold, new and sometimes shocking.

Ballet in America and India

In the United States, Russian-born choreographer George Balanchine co-founded the School of American Ballet in 1934 and later New York City Ballet, creating a fast, sleek American style. The Nutcracker became a Christmas tradition across the country, and for many ballet companies it now sells more tickets than any other show.

In India, ballet schools have grown in big cities such as Mumbai, Delhi and Bengaluru, and a few Indian dancers have trained abroad and joined professional companies. Ballet is very different from Bharatanatyam. Ballet dancers lift away from the floor, reaching up and outward. Bharatanatyam dancers sink into a deep half-sitting stance and strike the ground with their feet. Both, though, take many years of daily practice to master.

Strong as athletes

Ballet looks light and effortless, but it is one of the most demanding forms of dance. Dancers train their legs, back and core for hours each day, often from childhood. Professional dancers can go through many pairs of pointe shoes in a season, sometimes a pair a performance, because the box softens with sweat and pressure.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Ballet is only for girls.

Truth: Ballet began with men. Kings and noblemen danced in the earliest court ballets, and many famous ballet stars, choreographers and teachers have been men. Ballet training demands huge strength from every dancer.

Try this

Hold the back of a chair for balance and stand with your heels together and toes pointing outwards, as far as is comfortable. That is first position. Slowly bend your knees over your toes, keeping your back straight, then straighten up. You have just done a plié, the first exercise in almost every ballet class.
