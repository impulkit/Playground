What if we got rid of all mosquitoes?

Mosquitoes spread diseases that kill more people every year than any other animal does. So what would happen if every single one vanished?

The short answer

If all mosquitoes disappeared, the biggest change would be for people. Mosquitoes spread malaria, dengue, chikungunya, Zika and yellow fever. Malaria alone caused about 282 million cases and 6,10,000 deaths in 2024, according to the World Health Organization. Most of those who died were young children in Africa. A world without mosquitoes would save hundreds of thousands of lives every year.

For nature, the answer is less clear. Mosquitoes are food for fish, frogs, birds, bats and spiders. Some scientists think other insects would quickly fill the gap. Others worry that a few food chains, especially in the Arctic and in wetlands, would be badly shaken. Nobody knows for sure.

Surprising but true

Of the roughly 3,500 kinds of mosquito, only a small number bite people, and only the females bite at all. They need blood to make their eggs.

The deadliest animal

Sharks, snakes and tigers get all the scary headlines. But mosquitoes are linked to far more human deaths than any other animal, because of the diseases they carry.

A female mosquito bites to get blood, which she needs to make her eggs. If she has already bitten someone with malaria, tiny parasites may travel from her saliva into the next person's blood. Dengue and Zika viruses spread in a similar way.

In India, malaria cases have dropped sharply since 2000, but dengue outbreaks still fill hospitals in many cities after the monsoon. In the USA, mosquitoes spread West Nile virus.

Who would go hungry

Mosquito larvae live in water. Fish, tadpoles, dragonfly nymphs and water beetles eat them in huge numbers. Adult mosquitoes are snapped up by birds, bats, spiders, lizards and frogs.

In the Arctic tundra, mosquitoes hatch in vast clouds each summer. Some scientists think migrating birds rely on them while raising chicks there. Others say mosquitoes make up only a small part of most animals' diets, and that midges and other flies could take their place.

The swarms can be so thick that herds of caribou change their routes across the tundra to escape them. Without mosquitoes, those great herds might walk different paths, trampling different plants.

Flowers and mosquitoes

Male mosquitoes never bite. Both males and females drink nectar from flowers for energy. As they do, some carry pollen from one flower to another.

A few plants, such as certain orchids in North America's northern forests, are pollinated mainly by mosquitoes. Losing mosquitoes might hurt them, though other insects could help.

Why are mosquitoes so dangerous?No shark, snake or tiger comes close. Through the diseases they carry, mosquitoes cause more human deaths than any other animal.

New ways to fight back

People have tried many ways to control mosquitoes: draining swamps, spraying insecticides, using bed nets and putting fish in ponds. These help, but mosquitoes breed fast and some become resistant to sprays.

Scientists are now testing new methods. One uses a gene drive, a way of changing mosquitoes' genes so that a trait, such as producing only male babies, spreads quickly through a population. A project called Target Malaria is studying this for the mosquitoes that carry malaria in Africa. These ideas are being tested carefully before any large release.

Another method infects mosquitoes with a common bacterium called Wolbachia. It doesn't kill them, but it makes it much harder for them to pass on dengue. Cities in Indonesia, Brazil and Australia have seen big drops in dengue after releasing these mosquitoes.

Remove some, not all

Many scientists suggest a middle path. Out of about 3,500 mosquito species, only a few dozen spread serious human diseases. Targeting just those could save most of the lives while leaving the rest of nature mostly alone.

Some countries have already beaten malaria without wiping out mosquitoes. Sri Lanka, India's neighbour, was declared malaria-free by the WHO in 2016, through careful testing, treatment and mosquito control.

Mosquitoes are just one small group of insects. Remove all insects, though, and the effects would be far more dramatic, as the thought experiment in a world without insects shows.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Mosquitoes are useless and nothing would miss them.

Truth: Mosquito larvae are food for many fish and insects, adult mosquitoes are eaten by birds, bats and spiders, and some mosquitoes drink nectar and help pollinate flowers. Whether nature could cope without them is still debated.

Try this

Do a mosquito check around your home with an adult. Look for small pools of standing water: plant pot trays, old tyres, buckets, coolers and blocked drains. Tip them out or cover them. Mosquitoes need only a bottle cap of water to lay eggs, so you have just removed a nursery.
