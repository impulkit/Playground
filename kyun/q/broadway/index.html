What is a Broadway musical?

The Phantom of the Opera played on Broadway for 35 years, and its giant chandelier crashed towards the audience nearly 14,000 times.

The short answer

A Broadway musical is a stage show that tells a story through songs, dance and spoken scenes, performed in one of the big professional theatres of New York. The name comes from Broadway, a long avenue in Manhattan. Most of the theatres sit on the streets around Times Square, in an area called the Theater District.

To count as a Broadway theatre, a venue needs at least 500 seats. There are 41 of them. Shows run eight times a week, sometimes for years, with live singers, dancers and an orchestra in a pit below the stage. Musicals that succeed there often travel the world, play in London's West End and become films.

Surprising but true

Broadway is not one theatre but a group of 41 large theatres, most of them on side streets near Times Square rather than on Broadway itself.

How the songs tell the story

In a musical, characters burst into song when their feelings are too big for ordinary talk. A song might show a hero deciding to leave home, two people falling in love or a villain plotting.

Early American musical shows were mostly a string of songs, jokes and dances with a thin plot. That changed in 1927 with Show Boat, which told a serious story about life on the Mississippi River. In 1943, Oklahoma! by Richard Rodgers and Oscar Hammerstein went further, using every song and dance to move the story on. It set the pattern for many musicals that followed.

Famous shows

West Side Story (1957) retold Shakespeare's Romeo and Juliet as a story of two rival gangs in New York, with explosive dancing. The Phantom of the Opera opened in 1988 and ran until 2023, the longest run in Broadway history. The Lion King turned a Disney film into a stage show with giant puppets of animals, and has earned more money than any other Broadway production.

Hamilton, in 2015, told the story of an American founding father using hip-hop, rap and R&B, with a cast of mostly Black and Latino actors. It sold out for years and brought a new generation into theatres.

Behind the curtain

A big Broadway musical is like a factory that runs every night. Besides the actors, there are musicians, stage crews who move scenery, people who run the lights and sound, costume makers and wig makers. Many performers have "understudies", who learn a role in case the lead actor is ill.

Opening a new musical can cost millions of dollars, and many shows close within months because they do not sell enough tickets. The ones that succeed can run for decades. Each June, the best shows of the season win Tony Awards, named after the actress and producer Antoinette Perry.

The Great White Way

Broadway's nickname, the Great White Way, comes from the bright electric lights that lit its theatre signs from the late 1800s onwards. Times Square, with its giant glowing screens, is still one of the most photographed places in the city.

On show nights, crowds pour out of the subway and queue under the marquees, the big lit-up signs above theatre doors. Some people line up for cheap same-day tickets, and others enter lotteries run by popular shows. When the curtain falls, fans wait at the stage door for autographs, a Broadway habit that is decades old.

Broadway and India

Indian audiences already know the magic of a story told through song. Bollywood films, with their song-and-dance numbers, have a lot in common with musicals. In 2004, the musical Bombay Dreams, with music by A.R. Rahman, opened on Broadway after a run in London. In Mumbai, theatres such as the NCPA now host touring musicals, and Indian companies have staged their own big musicals based on Bollywood stories.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Broadway is the name of one big theatre in New York.

Truth: Broadway is a long avenue, and the name means the cluster of 41 professional theatres in Manhattan's Theater District. Smaller theatres in the city are called Off-Broadway and Off-Off-Broadway.
