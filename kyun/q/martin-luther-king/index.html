Who was Martin Luther King Jr.?

He started college at 15, led a city-wide boycott at 26, and won the Nobel Peace Prize at 35.

The short answer

Martin Luther King Jr. was a Baptist minister who became the best-known leader of the American civil rights movement. He fought to end laws that kept Black and white Americans apart and to win equal rights, including the right to vote.

King believed in non-violence, which he learned partly from Gandhi. He led boycotts and marches, went to jail many times, and gave speeches that moved millions. He was killed in 1968, aged 39. Today the third Monday of January is Martin Luther King Jr. Day, a national holiday in the USA.

Surprising but true

King was named Michael at birth. His father changed both their names to Martin Luther after a trip to Germany in 1934.

A preacher's son

King was born in Atlanta, Georgia, in 1929. His father and grandfather were both preachers at the same church. As a child he saw segregation close up. A white friend's parents told him they could no longer play together once school started, because the schools were separate.

He was a quick student and skipped two grades, entering Morehouse College at 15. He later earned a doctorate at Boston University and became a pastor in Montgomery, Alabama, in 1954.

The bus boycott

In December 1955, Rosa Parks was arrested in Montgomery for refusing to give up her bus seat. Local leaders organised a boycott of the city's buses and chose the 26-year-old King to lead it. His house was bombed during the boycott, though his family was unhurt. He urged his supporters not to answer violence with violence.

After 381 days, the courts ended segregation on Montgomery's buses, and King became known across the country.

A pilgrim in India

In 1959, King and his wife Coretta spent about a month in India. They met Prime Minister Nehru and visited places linked with Gandhi. On arriving he said, "To other countries I may go as a tourist, but to India I come as a pilgrim."

He came home even more committed to non-violent protest, which he saw as a way to change laws and also change hearts. He later wrote that India had convinced him that non-violent resistance was the most powerful weapon available to oppressed people.

"I have a dream"

In 1963, King was jailed in Birmingham, Alabama, during protests. In his cell he wrote a long letter, on scraps of newspaper at first, explaining why people could not wait patiently for justice. His "Letter from Birmingham Jail" is still studied in schools.

That August, about 2.5 lakh people joined the March on Washington. He stood on the steps of the Lincoln Memorial, built to honour Abraham Lincoln. There he spoke of his dream that his four children would one day "not be judged by the colour of their skin but by the content of their character."

Prize and loss

In 1964 King won the Nobel Peace Prize, the youngest winner up to then. He gave the prize money to the movement.

What is the Nobel Peace Prize?The man who invented dynamite left his fortune to reward people who make the world more peaceful.

Laws protecting civil rights and voting passed in 1964 and 1965.

King then spoke out about poverty and against the Vietnam War, which cost him support. On 4 April 1968 he was shot in Memphis, Tennessee, where he had gone to support striking rubbish collectors.

Like other famous people, he had faults. Researchers later found that parts of his doctoral thesis copied other writers without credit. His achievements, though, are not in doubt.

A memorial to him opened in Washington, D.C. in 2011, near the Lincoln Memorial. It shows him carved from a huge block of granite.

Myth or fact?

Myth: King was popular with most Americans during his lifetime.

Truth: In polls in the 1960s, many Americans disapproved of him, and the FBI secretly watched him for years. His reputation grew after his death, and his birthday became a national holiday in 1986.
