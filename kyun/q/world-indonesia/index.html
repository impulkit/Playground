How many islands does Indonesia have?

Indonesia has so many islands that if you visited a new one every day, you would need almost 48 years to see them all.

The short answer

As of 2024, Indonesia's government counts 17,380 islands. That makes it the largest country made entirely of islands, an archipelago. Its official number has changed over the years, because counting islands is harder than it sounds. Some appear only at low tide, some are tiny rocks, and new surveys keep finding more. Each island must be named, measured and checked by experts before it is counted, and newly verified islands are still being added to the official records.

Most islands are small and empty. People live on perhaps a few thousand of them. The biggest, like Sumatra, Java, Borneo and Sulawesi, hold most of the country's roughly 280 million people. Java alone is home to more than half of them, packed onto an island a little smaller than the Indian state of Odisha.

Surprising but true

When Krakatoa erupted in 1883, the bang was heard on an island in the Indian Ocean about 4,800 km away.

Why so many islands?

Indonesia sits where several of Earth's huge plates push against each other. As one plate slides under another, rock melts and rises, building chains of volcanoes. Over millions of years, many of those volcanoes grew into islands.

The country has about 130 active volcanoes, more than almost anywhere else. They make the soil rich for farming but also cause earthquakes and eruptions. In 2004, an undersea earthquake off Sumatra set off the tsunami that also struck India's coasts.

Two giant blasts

In 1883, the volcano Krakatoa, between Java and Sumatra, blew itself apart. The sound was heard about 4,800 km away, on Rodrigues Island in the Indian Ocean. It may be the loudest sound in recorded history. Ash in the sky turned sunsets red around the world for months.

Far bigger was Toba, on Sumatra, about 74,000 years ago. It was one of the largest eruptions of the last few million years. Its ash fell as far as India. The crater it left is now Lake Toba, about 100 km long, with an island inside it about the size of Singapore.

Dragons and giant flowers

Indonesia's islands are full of wildlife found nowhere else. Komodo dragons, the world's largest lizards, live on Komodo and a few nearby islands. They can grow about 3 m long, nearly as long as a small car. They hunt deer and wild pigs, and their saliva carries venom that stops blood from clotting. Only a few thousand are left in the wild.

The rainforests of Sumatra and Borneo hold orangutans and the Rafflesia, which has the largest single flower in the world, nearly 1 m across. It smells of rotting meat, which attracts the flies that pollinate it.

What was the Srivijaya empire?For about 600 years, a Buddhist kingdom on the island of Sumatra controlled one of the busiest sea lanes on Earth, and the world almost forgot it existed.

Old links with India

Ships crossed the Indian Ocean between India and Indonesia for more than 1,500 years. Traders carried spices, cloth and ideas, and Hindu and Buddhist kingdoms grew on Sumatra and Java. The sea empire of Srivijaya became a famous centre of Buddhist learning.

On Java stands Borobudur, a huge Buddhist monument from the 800s. Nearby, the Hindu temples of Prambanan are carved with scenes from the Ramayana, which dancers still perform there on moonlit nights. Most Indonesians today are Muslim, making it the country with the most Muslims in the world. Bali remains mostly Hindu.

So many voices

Indonesia has more than 700 languages. To help everyone understand each other, the country uses one national language, Bahasa Indonesia, taught in all schools. Many Indonesians speak a local language at home and the national one at school.

Jakarta, the capital on Java, is so crowded and sinking so fast that the government has been building a new capital, Nusantara, on Borneo. And India is closer than you might think. Indira Point, at the southern tip of the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, is less than 200 km of sea from the Indonesian island of Sumatra.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Indonesia is a small country of beach islands.

Truth: It is huge. Its islands stretch more than 5,000 km, and some of them are giants. Borneo, which Indonesia shares with Malaysia and Brunei, is the third largest island on Earth, and New Guinea, which it shares with Papua New Guinea, is the second.
