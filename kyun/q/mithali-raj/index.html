Who is Mithali Raj?

She trained to be a Bharatanatyam dancer. Instead she played international cricket for 23 years and scored more one-day runs than any woman in history.

The short answer

Mithali Raj is one of the greatest batters in the history of women's cricket. She played for India from 1999 to 2022, about 23 years, the longest international career of any woman cricketer. She scored 7,805 runs in one-day internationals, more than any other woman.

She captained India for many years and led them to two World Cup finals, in 2005 and 2017. Both were lost, but the 2017 run changed how Indians watched women's cricket. Millions tuned in, and a new generation of girls picked up bats.

Surprising but true

Mithali Raj scored a century in her very first one-day international, in 1999, when she was 16.

From dance class to the nets

Mithali was born in Jodhpur, Rajasthan, in 1982, and grew up in Secunderabad, part of Hyderabad. As a little girl she learnt Bharatanatyam, the classical dance of Tamil Nadu, for several years.

Her father, an officer in the Indian Air Force, took her to cricket practice partly to cure her habit of sleeping late. Her brother was already training. Coaches noticed her neat, calm batting, and she soon had to choose between dance and cricket. She chose cricket, though she has said dance helped her footwork and balance at the crease.

A century on debut

In 1999, aged 16, she played her first one-day international, against Ireland. She scored 114 not out. Three years later, at Taunton in England, she made 214 in a Test, then the highest score in women's Test cricket.

Mithali was famous for timing rather than power. She leaned into drives and placed the ball into gaps between fielders. She was rarely in a hurry, which made her very hard to get out. Bowlers knew that if they tried to tempt her with wide balls, she would simply leave them and wait. Her ODI batting average was above 50, a mark of great consistency in any kind of cricket. She became the first woman to pass 6,000 ODI runs, and later 7,000.

The 2017 World Cup

In 2017 India reached the Women's World Cup final at Lord's in London. Before batting in some matches, Mithali was photographed calmly reading a book in the dressing room, and fans loved it. The final against England was tight, but India lost by nine runs.

It still felt like a turning point. Stadiums and television audiences grew. Players like Harmanpreet Kaur and Smriti Mandhana became household names. In 2022 the Indian cricket board announced equal match fees for men and women playing for India. In 2023 the Women's Premier League began, paying women cricketers far more than before.

Awards and a film

Mithali received the Arjuna Award in 2003 and the Padma Shri in 2015. In 2021 she was given the Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna, India's highest sports award. She retired from international cricket in June 2022.

The same year, a film about her life, Shabaash Mithu, came out. Her career also connects two eras: when she started, women cricketers often travelled in crowded second-class trains and were barely paid. When she stopped, they were signing big contracts.

What came next

Mithali never won a World Cup as a player. In November 2025 the women's team did it, beating South Africa in the final in Navi Mumbai, with Harmanpreet Kaur as captain.

Many of the players had grown up watching Mithali. Her long years at the crease helped prove that women's cricket in India deserved a big stage, and that a girl from a dance class could become a record-breaker.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Women's cricket in India only started recently.

Truth: India's women played their first Test in 1976 and their first one-day international in 1978. What changed recently is the attention, money and television coverage.

Try this

Try "batting" with a book balanced on your head. Stand side-on as if holding a bat, and practise a slow forward step and swing without dropping the book. Dancers and batters both need a steady head, and Mithali has said dance helped her footwork.
