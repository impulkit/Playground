What is the difference between a zoo and a sanctuary?

In a zoo, people come to see the animals. In a good sanctuary, the animals mostly get left alone.

The short answer

A zoo keeps wild animals in enclosures so that people can see them. Good zoos also breed rare animals, do research and teach visitors about nature.

A sanctuary is a place where animals are protected for their own sake. The word means two slightly different things. In India, a wildlife sanctuary is a large wild area, like a forest or lake, protected by law, where animals live freely and you might never spot them. Elsewhere, a sanctuary often means a rescue centre that gives a lifelong home to animals that cannot survive in the wild, such as injured, orphaned or once-captive animals. Real sanctuaries don't buy, sell or breed animals for show.

Surprising but true

The Arabian oryx was wiped out in the wild in 1972. Zoo-bred oryx were released in Oman in 1982, and more than a thousand now live wild in the Arabian Peninsula again.

From royal menageries to modern zoos

For thousands of years, kings collected strange animals to impress visitors. The oldest zoo still open today, at Schönbrunn Palace in Vienna, began as an emperor's collection in 1752. Kolkata's Alipore Zoo opened in 1876. For a long time it was home to Adwaita, a giant tortoise said to be well over 150 years old when he died in 2006.

Older zoos kept animals in small cages with bars. Many modern ones use larger spaces with plants, water and places to hide. Keepers give animals puzzles, hidden food and new smells to stop boredom. This is called enrichment. A bear might have to work out how to open a log stuffed with honey, the way it would rip into a bees' nest in the wild. In India, every zoo must be approved by the Central Zoo Authority, set up in 1992, which sets rules for space and care.

How did zoos begin?Over 5,000 years ago, rulers in Egypt kept hippos, baboons and elephants. The zoo you visit today grew out of these private animal collections.

Wild places protected by law

India's Wildlife (Protection) Act of 1972 lets governments declare forests, grasslands and wetlands as sanctuaries or national parks. India now has over 500 sanctuaries and over 100 national parks. In a sanctuary, local people may sometimes collect firewood or graze cattle. A national park is stricter, with almost no human use allowed.

In the USA, wild areas are protected as national parks, wildlife refuges and wilderness areas. Yellowstone, set up in 1872, was the world's first national park and helped save the last wild bison.

Rescue sanctuaries

Until the 2000s, some performers in India captured sloth bear cubs and trained them to "dance" for coins on the roadside. The practice was illegal, but it continued. Conservation groups worked with the government and the bear handlers' families, helping the families find new jobs. By 2009 the last known dancing bears had been rescued. Many now live out their lives in sanctuaries such as the bear rescue centre near Agra, where they can dig, climb and forage.

Should zoos exist?

Supporters point to animals saved by zoo breeding, like the Arabian oryx and the California condor. Zoos also raise money for conservation, and for many children a zoo is the first place they see a living tiger.

Critics argue that some animals suffer in captivity however good the enclosure. Big, far-roaming, intelligent animals, such as elephants and polar bears, are hardest to keep well. In 2009, India's Central Zoo Authority directed zoos to move their elephants to forest camps and rescue centres, where they would have more room. The argument about what we owe wild animals is still going on. Many people now agree on one thing: whatever we decide about zoos, protecting animals' wild homes matters most.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Zoo animals could simply be set free in the wild if people wanted.

Truth: Animals born in zoos often have never hunted, found water or avoided predators. Releasing them takes years of careful preparation, a safe wild home and close monitoring afterwards, and many attempts still fail.
