What is American food?

Corn, turkey and cranberries were in America long before burgers, and almost everything else on the plate arrived with immigrants.

The short answer

There is no single American food, in the same way there is no single Indian food. The United States is a huge country of immigrants. Its food is a mix of what Native Americans grew, what Europeans brought, what enslaved Africans cooked, and what newer arrivals from Mexico, China, Italy, India and elsewhere added.

Some of it is famous everywhere: burgers and hot dogs, fried chicken, pancakes, apple pie and pizza. But a fisherman in Maine, a rancher in Texas and a grandmother in Louisiana would each give you a very different answer if you asked what "American food" means at home.

Surprising but true

The fortune cookie, which most Americans link with Chinese restaurants, was popularised in California and is almost unknown in China.

The first American foods

Long before Europeans arrived, the Native nations of North America were skilled farmers. Many grew the "Three Sisters" together in one field. Maize grew tall, beans climbed up the maize stalks, and broad squash leaves shaded the soil and kept weeds down.

The Americas also gave the world turkey, potatoes, tomatoes, chillies, peanuts, vanilla and chocolate, though many of these were first grown farther south, in Mexico and South America. Wild rice, cranberries and maple syrup come from the north. When Indians eat aloo or tamatar today, they are eating foods that first crossed an ocean from the Americas.

A plate of many journeys

Each new wave of people added dishes. German immigrants brought sausages that became hot dogs. Italian families in New York and Chicago turned simple Neapolitan pizza into something huge and cheesy. Jewish bakers made bagels a New York breakfast. Chinese workers, many of whom came for the gold rush and the railways, opened restaurants that served chop suey.

Enslaved Africans and their descendants shaped Southern cooking deeply. They brought knowledge of rice farming and foods such as okra and black-eyed peas, and developed what is now called soul food. Today you can find dosa in New Jersey, pho in Texas and tacos almost everywhere.

What happened at Ellis Island?For more than 60 years, a small island in New York harbour was the front door to America. About 12 million people walked through it to start new lives.

North, south, east and west

American food changes from region to region. New England, in the north-east, is known for clam chowder, a creamy seafood soup, and lobster rolls. The South has fried chicken, cornbread, biscuits and slow-smoked barbecue.

In Louisiana, French, Spanish, African and Native cooking blended into Creole and Cajun food. Gumbo is a thick stew of seafood or sausage, thickened with okra, served over rice. New Orleans is famous for it. In the south-west, food mixes with Mexican cooking, and in California, fresh vegetables and Asian flavours are everywhere.

Fast food goes global

In 1940, two brothers named Richard and Maurice McDonald opened a drive-in restaurant in San Bernardino, California. A few years later they redesigned it to make burgers quickly on an assembly line, like a factory. Many other chains copied the idea, and American fast food spread around the world.

In India, these chains had to change their menus. Many outlets sell no beef, and offer paneer, aloo tikki and spicy chicken burgers instead. It is a good example of how food bends to fit wherever it goes.

The biggest meal of the year

On the fourth Thursday of November, many American families eat a Thanksgiving dinner. Roast turkey sits beside stuffing, mashed potatoes, cranberry sauce and pumpkin pie. Almost every one of those foods has roots in the Americas. Families from Indian, Mexican or Chinese backgrounds often add their own dishes, which may be the most American touch of all.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Apple pie was invented in America.

Truth: English cookbooks had apple pie recipes by the late 1300s, long before Europeans settled in America. Apples themselves came to North America with European settlers. The saying "as American as apple pie" is much newer than the pie.
