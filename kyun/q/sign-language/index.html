How does sign language work?

There isn't one sign language. There are hundreds, and American and British Sign Language are so different that their users can't easily understand each other.

The short answer

Sign languages are full languages, as rich as Hindi or English, that use the hands, face and body instead of the voice. Every sign is built from a few parts: the shape of the hand, where it is placed, how it moves, which way the palm faces, and the expression on the face.

Sign languages have their own grammar and their own vocabulary. They are not spoken languages turned into gestures. Deaf communities around the world use about 300 different sign languages. In India, the main one is Indian Sign Language, or ISL. In the USA, it is American Sign Language, or ASL.

Surprising but true

In Nicaragua in the 1970s and 1980s, deaf children brought together in new schools created a brand new sign language, and scientists watched it grow.

Not English on the hands

Many people assume that ASL is English acted out with the hands. It isn't. ASL has its own word order and its own ways of showing time, questions and who did what. Raised eyebrows can turn a statement into a yes-or-no question. Puffed cheeks can show that something is big or fat.

Signers also use the space around them like a stage. You might place "my sister" to your left and "the shop" to your right, then move the sign for "go" from left to right to show that she went to the shop. Information that a spoken language puts into word endings or word order, a sign language can put into space and movement.

A French connection

British Sign Language and ASL are very different, even though both countries speak English. The reason lies in history. In 1817, an American teacher, Thomas Hopkins Gallaudet, brought a deaf French teacher named Laurent Clerc to Hartford, Connecticut. Together they opened the first permanent school for deaf children in the USA.

French Sign Language mixed with the signs that American students already used, and ASL grew from that blend. In 1864, a college for deaf students was founded in Washington, D.C. Today it is Gallaudet University, where classes are taught in ASL and deaf students lead campus life.

Indian Sign Language

In India, millions of deaf people use Indian Sign Language, though signs vary from region to region, much as spoken words do. For a long time, many deaf schools taught children to lip-read and speak rather than sign. Attitudes have changed. In 2015, the government set up the Indian Sign Language Research and Training Centre in Delhi. It has published a dictionary of thousands of signs and trains interpreters.

India's 2016 law on the rights of people with disabilities calls for sign language to be promoted. Some TV news bulletins now show a sign language interpreter in a corner of the screen, and more schools are teaching ISL to deaf children from an early age.

A language born in a school

In the late 1970s and early 1980s, Nicaragua opened new schools that brought deaf children together in large numbers for the first time. Their teachers tried to teach lip-reading, without much success. But in the playground and on the school buses, the children began to sign to each other, mixing the home gestures each had used with their families.

Each new group of younger children took what the older ones did and made it more regular and more complex. Within a few years, a full language had formed: Nicaraguan Sign Language. Scientists who studied it saw something rare, the birth of a language, with grammar created by children themselves. Babies who see signing from birth pick it up just as easily as hearing babies learn to speak.

Myth or fact?

Myth: Sign language is the same all over the world.

Truth: There are about 300 different sign languages. Each grew up in its own deaf community, with its own vocabulary and grammar, just as spoken languages did.

Try this

Learn to fingerspell your name. With an adult's help, find an official fingerspelling chart for Indian Sign Language and one for American Sign Language. Practise your name in both. You'll notice that ISL uses two hands for most letters, while ASL uses one.
