## The short answer

Elephants use [infrasound](?infrasound), sounds lower than humans can hear, to stay in touch across long distances.

Experiments show elephants can notice vibrations through the ground.

India has around 22,400 wild Asian elephants, more than any other country. That figure comes from the first nationwide count using DNA from elephant dung, published in 2025.

## The throbbing in the air

In 1984, an American scientist named Katy Payne was watching Asian elephants at a zoo in Portland, Oregon. Standing beside their enclosure, she kept feeling a strange throbbing in the air, like the deep rumble of a pipe organ she remembered from her childhood church. She could feel it, but not quite hear it.

Payne came back with recording equipment that could pick up very low sounds. When the tapes were sped up, they were full of elephant calls nobody had known about. Elephants were talking in [sounds](sound-travel) below 20 hertz, too deep for human ears. Later, she and her colleagues showed that wild African elephants use these calls to find each other over several kilometres.

## Why low sounds travel so far

Low-pitched sounds lose less energy than high ones as they travel through air, and they bend around trees and hills more easily. On a calm evening, an elephant's rumble can carry for several kilometres across a savanna or forest. [Blue whales](whale-songs) use the same trick in the ocean.

A rumble also shakes the ground. Elephants are so heavy that their calls, and even their footsteps, send tiny vibrations through the soil. These ground waves move a bit like very faint [earthquake](earthquakes) waves, and they can sometimes travel further than the sound in the air.

## Listening with feet

Caitlin O'Connell-Rodwell, an American biologist, noticed wild elephants in Namibia sometimes froze, leaned forward and lifted a foot, as if listening. She and her team played recorded warning calls through the ground. The elephants grew alert and bunched together, even though the sound in the air was barely there.

Elephant feet and trunk tips are packed with nerve endings that sense pressure and vibration. Bones may carry the vibrations up to the [ear](ears-hear) too. Scientists still don't know exactly how far elephants can "hear" this way, or how often they use it in daily life.

## What elephants say

Elephant rumbles carry meaning. Some say "let's go", some greet a returning friend, and some warn of danger. A study of African elephants in Kenya found that females recognised the calls of around 100 other elephants, and reacted differently to friends and strangers. A 2024 study suggested that wild African elephants may even use name-like calls for particular members of their family.

Asian elephants have their own sounds. Besides rumbles and trumpets, they make high squeaks and chirps, a little like a bird.

## Elephants in India

India's wild elephants live mainly in the forests of the Western Ghats, the north-east and the eastern and central states, with Karnataka holding the most. Project Elephant began in 1992 to protect them and the corridors they use to travel between forests.

Those journeys sometimes bring elephants into farms and villages, where they can damage crops and homes, and both people and elephants get hurt. Understanding how elephants talk and find each other may one day help warn villages before a herd arrives. Like [crows](crow-smart), elephants have long memories, and herds follow old routes their grandmothers knew.
