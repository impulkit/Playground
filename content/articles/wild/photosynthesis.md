## The short answer

This is called [photosynthesis](?photosynthesis). It's how almost every [food chain](?foodchain) on Earth begins.

A leaf takes in carbon dioxide from the air through tiny holes on its surface, and water comes up from the roots. Inside the leaf's cells, a green pigment called [chlorophyll](?chlorophyll) soaks up sunlight. The plant uses that energy to join carbon dioxide and water into [sugar](sugar), its food. Oxygen is left over and floats out into the air.

That's why [the asteroid](extinction)'s darkness was so deadly: no sunlight, no plant food, no food for anyone.

Tiny ocean plants called plankton make about half of all the oxygen on Earth.

## Why leaves are green

Chlorophyll soaks up red and blue light for energy but reflects green. That's why leaves look green. In autumn, some trees stop making chlorophyll and other colours show.

The chlorophyll sits inside tiny green packets in each cell, called chloroplasts. A single leaf can hold millions of them. Seen under a microscope, they look like green beads drifting inside the cells.

## A tree made of air

In the early 1600s, a Flemish scientist named Jan Baptist van Helmont planted a willow sapling in a big pot of soil that he had dried and weighed. For five years he gave it nothing but rainwater. The willow grew from about 2 kg to about 77 kg. When he dried and weighed the soil again, it had lost only around 60 grams.

He decided the tree was made from water. He was partly right. Later scientists found the missing ingredient. In 1771, the English chemist Joseph Priestley showed that a sprig of mint could "restore" air in a sealed jar, so that a [candle](candles) could burn in it again. A few years later, the Dutch doctor Jan Ingenhousz showed that plants only do this in light, and only in their green parts.

So a giant [redwood](redwoods) or a [banyan](banyan) is built mainly from carbon dioxide pulled out of the air, plus water. The soil supplies only small amounts of minerals.

## Every meal starts here

Rice, wheat, fruit, and even milk and meat. All your food energy started as sunlight captured by plants. A cow eats grass, you drink the milk, and the energy in it began in a leaf. The energy in that sunlight began in the [Sun](sun-what), where atoms crash together deep inside.

Fossil fuels such as coal are photosynthesis too, in a way. They are the remains of plants and tiny sea creatures that captured sunlight hundreds of millions of years ago.

## Oxygen for the planet

Early Earth had almost no oxygen in its air. Then, more than 2.4 billion years ago, tiny ocean microbes called cyanobacteria began using sunlight to make food. They released oxygen as waste, and over hundreds of millions of years it built up in the air. Every animal that breathes, including you, depends on that change.

Today forests, grasslands and crops do a lot of the work on land, but microscopic plankton drifting in the sunlit top of the ocean make about half of the world's oxygen. Some [coral](coral-reefs) even keep tiny algae living inside their bodies, which share the sugar they make.

## Catching light, two ways

A [solar panel](solar-panels) also catches sunlight, but it turns it into [electricity](electricity-made), not food. The best panels turn about a fifth of the light into electricity. A typical leaf stores only about 1% of the sunlight that falls on it. The leaf, though, builds and repairs itself, makes its own copies and runs on nothing but air, water and light.
