## The short answer

Yes. India has two kinds of wolf. The Indian wolf lives on the dry plains and plateaus of central, western and southern India. The Himalayan wolf lives high in the mountains of [Ladakh](ladakh), Himachal Pradesh and Sikkim.

Both are rare. A 2022 study estimated about 3,100 Indian wolves, roughly as many as there are wild tigers in India. Himalayan wolves number perhaps a few hundred in India. Yet wolves get far less attention than tigers, partly because they live in places most people overlook: dusty [grasslands](grasslands), thorny scrub and farmers' fields.

## Small, quiet hunters

The Indian wolf is much smaller than the big grey wolves of Canada or Russia. An adult weighs about 20 to 25 kilograms, about the same as a large village dog. It has a short, sandy coat that keeps it cool in the heat and blends with dry grass.

Packs are small, often a breeding pair and their young, sometimes up to eight or so. They hunt [blackbuck](wild-blackbuck) and chinkara, two kinds of fast Indian antelope, as well as hares and rodents. They also take sheep and goats, which brings them into conflict with herders. They often hunt in the cool hours of dawn and dusk, and spend the hot day resting in dens, ravines or tall grass.

## The oldest wolves?

Scientists have studied the DNA of wolves around the world, and India's wolves stand out. Both the Indian and Himalayan wolves appear to have split from other wolves a very long time ago, making them some of the oldest surviving wolf lines anywhere.

Because of this, some scientists argue they should be treated as separate species. Others say more evidence is needed. Either way, losing them would mean losing a unique branch of the wolf family tree, the same family from which all [dogs](dogs-domestic) descend.

## Wolves in stories

The most famous wolves in India are fictional. In Rudyard Kipling's [The Jungle Book](jungle-book), written in the 1890s, the boy Mowgli is raised by a wolf pack in the Seeonee hills, based on the real Seoni district of Madhya Pradesh. Mother Wolf, Father Wolf and the pack leader Akela are kinder than most real wolves would be to a human child.

In villages, wolves have a mixed reputation. Herders lose animals to them. Attacks on people are very rare, but they do happen, usually where wolves have lost their natural prey. In 2024, a series of attacks near Bahraich in Uttar Pradesh frightened villagers, and forest officials spent weeks tracking and catching several wolves.

## Losing the grasslands

The biggest threat to Indian wolves is the loss of their home. For a long time, government maps marked open grasslands as "wasteland", land that seemed empty and useless. Much of it has been ploughed, planted with trees, built on or covered in solar farms.

A few protected places help. Velavadar Blackbuck National Park in Gujarat is famous for its wolves and blackbuck. The Deccan grasslands near Solapur in Maharashtra and parts of Rajasthan and Karnataka also hold packs. Wolves are protected under India's wildlife law, and killing them is a crime.

## Wolves around the world

In the United States, wolves were wiped out from most states in the early 1900s. In 1995, they were brought back to [Yellowstone](yellowstone-wolves) National Park, and scientists watched the whole ecosystem change, from elk herds to riverbank trees.

India's wolves never disappeared completely, but they need room. Like wolves everywhere, they keep in touch with [howls](animal-communication) that can carry across kilometres of open land. One recent study found that wolves living near busy villages howl less often, as if keeping their voices down.
