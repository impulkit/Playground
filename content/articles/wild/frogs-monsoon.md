## The short answer

Frogs need water for their eggs and tadpoles, so the monsoon is their breeding season.

Each frog [species](?species) has a different call, so females can find the right partner.

The Western Ghats are home to hundreds of frog species found nowhere else.

Most of the singers are males. Each one pumps air back and forth over his vocal cords and into a stretchy throat pouch called a vocal sac. The sac swells like a [bubble](bubbles-round) and makes his call louder. On a wet monsoon night, a single pond can hold hundreds of calling males, each trying to be heard over the rest.

## Waiting for the rain

For much of the year, India is too dry for frog eggs. Frog eggs have no shell, and tadpoles must live in water, so a frog that breeds in the dry season would lose its young. So many frogs spend the hot months hidden, buried in soil, tucked under rocks or curled inside cracks, using very little energy.

When the [monsoon](monsoon) arrives, ponds, ditches and paddy fields fill almost overnight. Frogs pour out to mate and lay eggs while the water lasts. The whole frog year is timed to the rains.

## The purple frog

The purple frog of the Western Ghats lives underground, eating termites. It surfaces only during the monsoon to breed. It has a pointed snout, a tiny head and a puffy purple body, a bit like a deflated balloon with legs.

Local people knew it, but scientists only described it in 2003. Its closest relatives live in the Seychelles, islands in the Indian Ocean thousands of kilometres away. Long ago, India and the Seychelles were joined. When India [drifted away](india-drift) across the ocean, the frogs' ancestors were split apart, and the two families have been evolving separately ever since.

## Frogs that dance

Beside fast, noisy streams in the Western Ghats live the dancing frogs. Over the roar of the water, a call is hard to hear. So the males stretch out a back leg and wave their webbed toes at females, a move called foot-flagging. In 2014, Indian scientists led by S.D. Biju described 14 new species of these frogs at once.

In the plains, the big Indian bullfrog has its own monsoon costume. For a few days of breeding, the males turn bright lemon yellow, with two balloon-like blue vocal sacs puffing out at their cheeks.

## Choruses around the world

Frogs everywhere sing to find partners. In the eastern USA, tiny frogs called spring peepers start singing in ponds as winter ends, so loud that a marsh sounds like jingling bells. [Birds](birds-sing) have dawn choruses too, but frog choruses usually come at night.

In some parts of India, people have long held mock "frog weddings" in dry years, hoping to bring rain. The custom shows how closely frogs and rain are linked in people's minds.

## Silent ponds

Frogs are in trouble around the world. Over the past few decades, a disease caused by a chytrid fungus has wiped out or badly harmed hundreds of frog species, especially in Central America and Australia. Frogs also lose their homes when [wetlands](wetlands) are drained and streams are polluted.

Frogs breathe partly through their thin, damp [skin](skin), so poisons in water reach them easily. When frogs disappear from a place, it is often an early warning that something has gone wrong with its water. A noisy pond on a monsoon night is a sign of a healthy one.
