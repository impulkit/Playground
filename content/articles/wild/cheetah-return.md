## The short answer

Cheetahs went [extinct](?extinct) in India by 1952.

Project Cheetah brought African cheetahs to Kuno National Park to help restore grassland wildlife.

It's an experiment. Some scientists support it; others worry about whether there's enough space and prey. It was the first time a large wild cat was moved from one continent to another to live in the wild.

## How India lost its cheetahs

Asiatic cheetahs once ran across grasslands and open scrub from central India to Arabia. For centuries, Indian kings caught them to hunt [blackbuck](wild-blackbuck) and deer. The Mughal emperor [Akbar](akbar) is said to have kept about a thousand at one time. Captured cheetahs rarely bred in captivity, so more and more had to be taken from the wild.

Hunting, the loss of grasslands to farms and the shrinking of their prey slowly emptied the land. In 1947, the Maharaja of Koriya, in present-day Chhattisgarh, is recorded as shooting three cheetahs at night. They are often described as the last confirmed wild cheetahs in India. In 1952, the government declared the animal extinct in the country.

## A long flight

India first tried to get Asiatic cheetahs from Iran, but Iran had too few to share. Today only a handful survive there, in remote deserts and hills, and they are among the rarest big cats on Earth. So India turned to Africa, home to most of the world's cheetahs.

On 17 September 2022, eight cheetahs from Namibia landed in Madhya Pradesh in a specially fitted cargo plane. They were moved to Kuno National Park, where they lived first in fenced enclosures, then were released into the open forest and grassland. Twelve more arrived from [South Africa](south-africa) in February 2023. A month later, the first cubs were born on Indian soil in over seventy years.

## Hard lessons

The first years were difficult. Several adult cheetahs and many cubs died. Some deaths came from fights and from heat. Others were blamed on infections under the [radio](radio-invented) collars, as some cheetahs grew thick coats meant for an African winter and moisture got trapped beneath them.

Some cheetahs also wandered far outside the park, into farms and villages, and had to be caught and brought back. In 2025, a few were moved to a second home, the Gandhi Sagar Wildlife Sanctuary, also in Madhya Pradesh. Scientists are learning what helps cheetahs survive here.

## Why people disagree

Supporters say cheetahs are a symbol for India's neglected grasslands. Protecting a big, eye-catching animal can also protect [wolves](wolves-india), foxes, blackbuck, the great Indian bustard and the grasses themselves.

Critics point out that Kuno is much smaller than the vast open spaces where cheetahs roam in Africa. [They worry about leopards](leopards-cities), which kill cheetahs and their cubs, and about too little prey. Some argue the money would do more good spent on animals already struggling in India. Both sides agree that the cheetahs' future depends on large, connected grasslands where people and wildlife can share the land.

## Fastest on land

The cheetah is the [fastest land animal](fastest-animal), reaching about 100 km/h in short bursts. A deer or blackbuck that can run long enough usually escapes. Its spots break up its outline in dry grass, a different trick from a [tiger's stripes](tiger-stripes). If Project Cheetah works, some of those sprints may soon be happening on Indian grasslands again, as they did in Akbar's time.
