## The short answer

The internet grew out of a small American network called ARPANET, which linked four university computers in 1969. Over the next 20 years, engineers worked out common rules, called protocols, so that different networks could connect into one giant "network of networks".

Email arrived in the early 1970s. The rules that still run the internet, called TCP/IP, were adopted in 1983. In 1989 a British scientist invented the World Wide Web, which made the internet easy for anyone to use. The public could [get online in India](internet-india) from 1995. Today billions of people use it every day.

## A race in space

The story starts with a beeping metal ball. In 1957 the Soviet Union launched [Sputnik](sputnik), the first artificial satellite. The United States was alarmed and set up a new agency, ARPA, to fund advanced research.

Computers then were huge, rare and expensive, and each one spoke its own language. ARPA wanted researchers across the country to share them. In the late 1960s it paid for a network to link them: ARPANET.

## Messages in packets

Old telephone networks kept a line open for each call. Engineers Paul Baran in the US and Donald Davies in Britain, working separately, came up with a different idea. Chop each message into small blocks, called packets, and send them separately by whatever route is free. At the other end, put them back together.

This is called packet switching, and it is still how the internet works. If one route is blocked, packets simply go another way. When you watch a video, it arrives in thousands of packets that may travel by different paths.

## LO and behold

On 29 October 1969, a student named Charley Kline at the University of California, [Los Angeles](los-angeles), tried to log in to a computer at the Stanford Research Institute. He typed L, then O, then the system crashed. So the first message on the network was "LO". Within weeks, four computers were linked.

In 1971, programmer Ray Tomlinson sent the first network [email](email) between two computers. He chose the @ sign to separate a person's name from their computer's name, because it was rarely used for anything else.

## One language for all

By the 1970s, other networks were springing up, including ones using radio and [satellites](satellites-help). They could not talk to each other. Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn designed a shared set of rules, TCP/IP, describing how packets should be addressed and checked. On 1 January 1983, ARPANET switched over. Many people call that the true birthday of the internet.

Most of the internet's data now travels as flashes of light through glass fibres. [Cables run along the ocean floor](undersea-internet) between continents, some as thick as a garden hose. They carry far more data than satellites do.

## The web and the world

The internet was still hard to use until 1989, when Tim Berners-Lee, working at the CERN laboratory near Geneva, invented the [World Wide Web](web-invented). The internet is the network of connections. The web is the pages and links that run on top of it.

India's public internet service was launched by VSNL, the state telephone company, on 15 August 1995, Independence Day. Connections were slow dial-up lines and costly. Today India has hundreds of millions of internet users, most of them on mobile phones. To see how your messages find their way today, read [how the internet works](internet-how).
