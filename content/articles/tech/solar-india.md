## The short answer

India gets strong sunshine for most of the year, and it is putting that sunlight to work. Huge fields of [solar panels](solar-panels) now cover parts of Rajasthan, Gujarat, Karnataka and other states. Millions of homes, schools, railway stations and farms have panels on their roofs too.

In 2014 India had less than 3 gigawatts (GW) of solar power. By August 2026, it had about 168 GW, according to the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy. One gigawatt is enough to light many lakhs of homes. In 2023, India made more solar electricity than any country except China and the USA. The aim is to cut the use of [coal](oil-coal), which still makes most of India's electricity.

## Fields of glass in the desert

Bhadla Solar Park, in the [Thar Desert](thar-formed) of Rajasthan, is one of the largest solar parks anywhere. It covers about 56 square kilometres, an area bigger than many towns, with rows and rows of dark blue panels tilted towards the Sun. It can produce about 2,245 megawatts at full sunshine.

Deserts are good for solar parks because the land is flat and the skies are clear. But deserts are dusty, and dust blocks light. Many parks send small cleaning robots trundling along the panel rows at night. Workers also have to keep the panels from overheating, since very hot panels work a little less well.

## Power on your roof

Not all solar power comes from giant parks. In 2024 the government started a scheme called PM Surya Ghar, which helps families pay for rooftop panels. A home can use the electricity it makes, and in many states it can sell extra power back to the grid. By August 2026, rooftop panels across India added up to about 33 GW.

Farmers are using solar pumps to water fields instead of diesel pumps. Some villages far from power lines run on small solar grids of their own. [Modhera](india-wonder-sun-temple-modhera) in Gujarat was declared India's first round-the-clock solar-powered village in 2022, using rooftop panels and a big battery.

## How a panel makes power

A solar panel is a sandwich of thin slices of silicon, the same material used in computer chips. When sunlight strikes the silicon, it knocks tiny charged particles called electrons loose, and wires collect them as an electric current. This is called the [photovoltaic](?photovoltaic) effect, from the Greek word for light and the name of the Italian scientist Alessandro Volta.

For years, most panels and cells used in India were made in China. The government now offers money and rules that favour Indian-made panels, and new factories have opened in Gujarat, Tamil Nadu and other states. Making more of the parts at home is meant to create jobs and protect India from shortages.
## The trouble with sunset

The Sun sets every evening, and clouds roll in during the [monsoon](monsoon). Solar panels can't help when people switch on lights and fans at night. So India is building giant [batteries](batteries-future) to store daytime power, and pairing solar with wind farms, which often blow hardest in the evening and in the rainy season.

Land is another puzzle. Solar parks need a lot of space, and India has many people and farms. One answer is floating solar, with panels placed on reservoirs and lakes. A large floating project has been set up, in stages, on the Omkareshwar reservoir on the [Narmada river](narmada) in Madhya Pradesh.

## Sharing the sunshine

In 2015, at the climate talks in Paris, India and France launched the International Solar Alliance, a group of countries working together to spread solar power. Its headquarters are in Gurugram, near Delhi. More than 100 countries have signed up, many of them sunny nations in Africa, Asia and South America.

Solar power is one of the cheapest ways to make electricity today. Each panel that replaces coal means less smoke in the air and less of the gas that drives [climate change](climate-change).
