## The short answer

The World Wide Web was invented by Tim Berners-Lee, a British computer scientist working at CERN, a big physics laboratory near Geneva in [Switzerland](switzerland).

In 1989, he proposed a way for scientists to share documents by linking them together across [the internet](internet-history). By the end of 1990, he had written the first web browser, the first web server and the first web pages. He created three key ideas still used today: web addresses (URLs), the language for writing pages (HTML), and the rules computers use to send pages to each other (HTTP).

The internet already existed. Berners-Lee's invention made it easy for anyone to publish and explore information on it.

## A lab full of lost information

CERN brings together thousands of scientists from many countries. In the 1980s, they used many kinds of computers, which often couldn't share files. Information got lost, and people had to keep asking each other where things were.

Berners-Lee wanted a system where any document could link to any other, on any computer. In March 1989, he wrote a proposal called "Information Management: A Proposal". His boss, Mike Sendall, wrote "Vague but exciting" on the front and let him keep working on it.

## Building the first website

Berners-Lee built the web on a NeXT computer, a powerful black machine of the time. To stop anyone switching it off by accident, he stuck a label on it saying, in red capitals, that it was a server and must not be powered down.

[The first website](hist-first-website), at CERN, explained what the web was and how to use it. It went public in 1991. A Belgian engineer at CERN, Robert Cailliau, worked with Berners-Lee to promote the project. At first, most users were physicists.

## Giving it away

On 30 April 1993, CERN announced that the web's software would be free for anyone to use, with no fees. This decision mattered enormously. Companies, universities and ordinary people could build websites and browsers without asking permission or paying.

The same year, a browser called Mosaic, made at the University of Illinois, showed pictures and text together on the same page and was easy to use. The web began to spread fast beyond science labs.

## The web and the internet

People often mix up the web and [the internet](internet-how). The internet is the physical network: cables, routers and computers linked around the world, which grew from the late 1960s. The web is one way of using it, made of pages joined by links.

Before the web, people used the internet for [email](email) and file sharing, mostly with typed commands. The web made it point-and-click. It's a bit like how the [printing press](printing-press) didn't invent writing but made it far easier to share.

## The web today

There are now more than a billion websites, though many are inactive. To find anything among them, people rely on [search engines](search-engines).

In 1994, Berners-Lee founded the World Wide Web Consortium, which still agrees on standards so websites work on every browser and device. He was knighted in 2004 and has campaigned for the web to stay open to everyone, and for people to have control over their own data. At the [2012 London Olympics](olympics-host) opening ceremony, he appeared at a computer, and a message appeared in lights around the stadium: "This is for everyone".
