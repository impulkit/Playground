## The short answer

Most apps simply ask for your birthday and trust the answer. That is easy to fool.

Stronger checks ask for more proof. It might be a photo of an ID card, a selfie that software uses to guess your age, or approval from a parent. Each method is harder to trick, but it also asks you to share more about yourself.

## Why apps ask

Most big [social media](social-media) apps say users must be at least 13. That comes from a 1998 American law that limits how companies collect personal data from younger children. India's 2023 data law says companies need a parent's consent to handle the personal data of anyone under 18.

Some countries go further and ban social media for younger teens. Australia's ban for under-16s began in December 2025, and Greece passed a ban for under-15s in October 2026. Supporters say heavy scrolling harms sleep, mood and [screen habits](screen-time). Critics worry about privacy and whether bans can really work.

## Ways to check

**Self-declaration.** You type your age. Quick and private, but anyone can lie.

**ID checks.** You upload an ID card or log in with an official ID. Accurate, but it hands over sensitive information, and not every child has an ID.

**Face age estimation.** An [algorithm](?algorithm) looks at a selfie and guesses your age, using the same kind of maths as [face recognition](face-recognition). Good systems can delete the photo straight away. They are less accurate close to the age limit.

**Parent approval.** A parent confirms the account from their own phone, often linked to their own verified age.

## The privacy puzzle

The stronger the check, the more personal data changes hands. If an app stores your ID and is then hacked, that data could leak. This is why [privacy](privacy) experts prefer methods that prove only one fact, such as "over 15: yes", without revealing your name or birthday, often protected by [encryption](?encryption).

Some governments are building digital "age tokens" that do exactly this. It is a new and fast-changing area. Learning how [apps use your data](app-safety) helps you understand what you are sharing, whichever check you meet.
