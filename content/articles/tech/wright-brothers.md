## The short answer

Wilbur and Orville Wright learned to fly by studying, testing and practising, step by step, for about four years. They were bicycle makers from Dayton, Ohio, with no university training in engineering. They read everything about flight they could find, watched birds, built kites and [gliders](gliders), and even made their own wind tunnel.

Their big insight was that the hard part of flying was not lifting off but controlling the machine in the air. On 17 December 1903, near Kitty Hawk, North Carolina, Orville made the first powered, controlled flight. It lasted 12 seconds. By the end of that day, Wilbur had flown for 59 seconds.

## Brothers with a bicycle shop

The Wrights' interest in flight started young. In 1878 their father brought home a small toy helicopter powered by a twisted rubber band. The boys played with it until it broke, then built copies.

As adults they ran the Wright Cycle Company, building and repairing [bicycles](bicycles-history). The work taught them about light, strong frames, chains and balance. A bicycle is unstable until a rider controls it. They guessed an aeroplane would be the same: a pilot would need to keep balancing it all the time.

## Watching birds and twisting boxes

Wilbur spent hours watching birds such as buzzards glide. He saw that they kept their balance by twisting the tips of their wings. One day, while idly twisting a long empty cardboard box in the shop, he realised that wings could be twisted the same way. They called this wing warping.

From 1900 they tested gliders at Kitty Hawk, chosen for its steady winds and soft sand dunes for landing. The gliders didn't lift as well as published tables said they should. So in 1901 the brothers built a small wind tunnel in their shop and tested about 200 model wing shapes. Their own measurements were far better than anyone else's at the time.

## Twelve seconds in December

No company could sell them a light enough engine, so their mechanic, Charlie Taylor, built one from [aluminium](aluminium) in about six weeks. The brothers carved their own propellers, treating them as spinning wings.

On the cold, windy morning of 17 December 1903, they took turns. Orville went first, lying on the lower wing. The Flyer lifted, swooped and landed about 37 m away after 12 seconds, less than the length of many modern jets. Only five local people were there to watch. On the fourth flight, Wilbur flew about 260 m in 59 seconds before a gust of wind damaged the plane.

## From laughter to fame

At first few people believed them. [Newspapers](newspapers) ignored or doubted the story. The brothers kept improving their planes in a field near Dayton. In 1908, Wilbur flew in public in France, making smooth turns and figure-of-eight loops. Crowds were astonished, and the Wrights became world-famous.

Flight spread fast. In February 1911, a French pilot [flew bags of mail](hist-first-flight-india) about 10 km from Allahabad across the Yamuna to Naini, the first official airmail flight anywhere. In 1932, J.R.D. Tata flew mail from Karachi to Bombay, the start of what became Air India.

The original 1903 Flyer now hangs in the [Smithsonian](smithsonian) National Air and Space Museum in Washington, D.C. Pieces of its fabric have travelled with [Apollo 11](apollo-moon) to the Moon and with NASA's [Ingenuity helicopter](mars-helicopter) to [Mars](mars-rovers), where in 2021 it made the first powered flight on another planet.
