## The short answer

A robot is a machine that can sense the world around it, decide what to do, and then move to do it. Engineers call this "sense, think, act".

To sense, a robot uses [sensors](?sensor): cameras, microphones, touch pads, or beams of light that measure distance. To think, it uses a computer running a [program](programming), a set of instructions written by people. To act, it uses motors that turn wheels, bend [joints](joints) or squeeze grippers. Take away any one of the three and you have something simpler: a camera, a computer or a toy car.

## Arms that never get tired

The first robot to work in a factory was called Unimate. In 1961 it started lifting hot pieces of metal at a General Motors car plant in New Jersey, a job that was dangerous for people. It was basically a strong arm that repeated the same movements again and again.

Most robots today are still arms like that. In a modern car factory, rows of them weld the body together in showers of sparks, faster and steadier than any person. They don't need lunch breaks. But they are also not very clever. Move the car a few centimetres out of place, and an old-style robot arm will weld thin air.

## Giving robots eyes

To work in messier places, robots must notice what is going on around them. [A robot vacuum cleaner](robots-home) bumps into a sofa leg, feels the bump and turns away. Newer ones scan the room with cameras or [lasers](lasers) and build a little map, so they can clean in neat rows.

The hardest part is understanding what the sensors see. A camera gives a robot millions of coloured dots. Working out that some of those dots are a child, a ball or a step is difficult. This is where [artificial intelligence](ai-what) comes in: programs that learn to recognise things by studying huge numbers of examples. The same problem faces [self-driving cars](self-driving), which are really robots on wheels.

## Robots far from home

Some robots go where people can't. [NASA's rovers](tech-robots-space) have driven across Mars for years, steered by teams on Earth. Radio messages take several minutes to reach Mars, so the rovers must handle some decisions on their own, like stopping before a rock.

India has a robot on the Moon too. In August 2023, Chandrayaan-3 landed near the [Moon's south pole](moon-water) and released the [Pragyan rover](chandrayaan). It was about the size of a large suitcase. Over roughly ten days it rolled about 100 metres and tested the soil for elements such as sulphur.

## Why most robots don't look like us

In films, robots usually have two arms, two legs and a face. Real engineers build whatever shape suits the job. A robot that cleans a swimming pool looks like a small tank. One that operates inside the body is a set of thin arms that [a surgeon controls from a console](robots-surgery).

Walking on two legs, it turns out, is extremely hard. You balance without thinking, but a machine must correct its weight many times every second or topple over. A few companies are now testing human-shaped robots for warehouses. Even so, the humble wheel is still the robot's best friend.

Hands are another puzzle. Picking up an egg without crushing it, or a sock without dropping it, needs a delicate sense of touch. Your fingertips have thousands of [nerve endings](nervous-system). Robot grippers are only starting to catch up, which is why [folding laundry](life-chores-world) is still harder for a robot than welding a car.
