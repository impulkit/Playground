## The short answer

Homes got electricity in stages, over more than a century. First, scientists worked out how to make electric current with spinning magnets and coils of wire. Then inventors built practical [light bulbs](light-bulb). Finally, companies and governments built power stations and strung wires along streets, into houses, and out to villages and farms.

The first big public power station opened in New York in 1882. Rich city homes in America, Europe and India got electricity in the early 1900s. But it took many more decades to reach the countryside. In the United States, most farms were connected only in the 1940s and 1950s. In India, the last village was connected in 2018.

## Faraday's spinning magnet

In 1831, the English scientist Michael Faraday found that moving a magnet near a coil of wire makes electricity flow in the wire. This is called [electromagnetic induction](induction-stoves). It sounds simple, but it is the basic idea behind almost every power station on Earth. Spin a magnet inside coils of wire, using steam, falling water or wind, and you get a flow of electric current.

For decades the discovery was mostly a lab experiment. People needed a reason to want electricity in their homes. That reason was light.

## Edison lights up New York

By 1879, Thomas Edison and his team in New Jersey had made a light bulb that could glow for many hours. Edison knew a bulb was useless without a supply of electricity, so he designed a whole system: generators, cables, meters, switches and fuses.

On 4 September 1882, his Pearl Street Station in Lower Manhattan began sending power through underground cables to about 80 nearby customers, lighting roughly 400 bulbs. Within a few years, power stations were opening in cities around the world. Edison's system used direct current, which led to a fierce contest with a rival system, a story told in [the war of the currents](tesla-edison).

## Electricity comes to India

Calcutta saw a public demonstration of electric lighting in 1879. In 1897, India's first hydroelectric power station opened at Sidrapong, near Darjeeling, using water rushing down the hills.

In 1902, a hydroelectric plant at Shivanasamudra, on the Kaveri river in [Karnataka](karnataka), began sending power about 150 km to the Kolar Gold Fields. A few years later, in 1905, Bangalore became one of the first cities in Asia to light its streets with electric lamps. Today hydropower from [dams](dams-power) still supplies part of India's electricity, alongside coal, solar, wind and [nuclear power](nuclear-power).

## Wires to the countryside

Cities got electricity first because many customers lived close together. Villages and farms were spread out, so each kilometre of wire served few people.

In the United States, the government set up the Rural Electrification Administration in 1935. It lent money to farmers' groups to build their own power lines. Within about fifteen years, most American farms had electricity, along with electric pumps, milking machines and radios.

In India, after independence, the government spent decades building power lines to [villages](villages-india). On 28 April 2018, the government announced that Leisang, a small village in Manipur, had become the last inhabited village to be connected. A village counted as electrified once public buildings and at least 10 percent of homes had power. A later scheme called Saubhagya worked to connect the remaining homes.

Electricity changed daily life more than almost any other invention. It brought [fans](ceiling-fans), pumps, [refrigerators](how-fridges-cold) and lights to study by after dark.
