## The short answer

The Ramayana is one of India's two great [epics](?epic), long poems about heroes and gods. It tells the story of Rama, a prince of Ayodhya, who is sent into the forest for 14 years. His wife Sita and his brother Lakshmana go with him. Ravana, the ten-headed king of Lanka, carries Sita away. With the help of Hanuman and an army of monkeys and bears, Rama builds a bridge across the sea, defeats Ravana and brings Sita home.

The oldest known version is in [Sanskrit](sanskrit) and is traditionally said to be by the sage Valmiki. For many Hindus, Rama is a form of the god Vishnu, and the story is sacred.

## Valmiki's poem

Valmiki's Ramayana has about 24,000 verses, divided into seven books called *kandas*. Scholars disagree about its date. Many think it took shape over several centuries, starting somewhere between about 500 and 300 BCE, with the first and last books probably added later. Tradition calls Valmiki the *adikavi*, the first poet.

A famous story tells how his poetry began. Valmiki saw a hunter shoot one of a pair of birds. His grief poured out in a new verse form, the *shloka*, the [rhythm](rhythm-beat) in which most of the Ramayana is written. The story is told in the Ramayana itself, so it is a legend about the poem's own birth.

## Many Ramayanas

Across India, poets retold the story in their own languages. Kamban wrote a Tamil version around the 1100s. Krittibas Ojha retold it in Bengali in the 1400s. In the 1500s, Tulsidas wrote the *Ramcharitmanas* in Awadhi, a form of [Hindi](hindi-language), and it became one of the most loved books in north India.

Each version differs. In some, Ravana is a pure villain. In others, he is a learned king with a fatal flaw. Jain and Buddhist traditions have their own tellings too. The scholar A. K. Ramanujan wrote a well-known essay called "Three Hundred Ramayanas" about this rich variety.

## Across the sea

The story travelled with traders and priests to Southeast Asia more than 1,000 years ago. In [Thailand](thailand) it became the *Ramakien*, and murals of it run around the walls of the Temple of the Emerald Buddha in Bangkok. In Cambodia it is the *Reamker*, and long stone carvings of the great battle cover a gallery wall at [Angkor Wat](angkor-wat). On the [island of Bali](bali), where most people are Hindu, it is performed in dance.

Shadow [puppet](puppetry) plays of the Ramayana are performed in Indonesia, Malaysia, Kerala and Karnataka. In Indonesia, a Muslim-majority country, a Ramayana [ballet](ballet) is still staged in the open air beside the Prambanan temple in Java.

## Alive in homes and on screens

In north India, Ramlila performances act out the story each year in the days before Dussehra, which celebrates Rama's victory over Ravana. Many people link Diwali with Rama's return to Ayodhya, when lamps were lit to welcome him home.

In 1987, Ramanand Sagar's *Ramayan* began on Doordarshan, India's national [TV channel](tv-india). On Sunday mornings, streets emptied as families gathered around [television](television-invented) sets, sometimes the only one in the neighbourhood. When the series was shown again during the 2020 lockdown, it drew huge audiences once more. A story first sung by poets in Sanskrit had found new listeners on screens, more than two thousand years later.
