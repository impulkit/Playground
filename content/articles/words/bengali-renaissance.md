## The short answer

The Bengal Renaissance was a burst of new ideas in Bengal, especially in [Calcutta](kolkata), from about 1800 to the 1920s. Writers, reformers, teachers and scientists questioned old customs, started schools and newspapers, and created modern [Bengali](bengali-language) literature.

The name borrows from the European [Renaissance](renaissance), when Italy rediscovered old learning and made new art. Bengal's version mixed ideas from Indian tradition and from Western books that arrived with British rule. Not everyone agrees the name fits, but the change was real.

## Why Calcutta?

Calcutta was the capital of British India until 1911 and the headquarters of the [East India Company](east-india-company). It had printing presses, colleges and a new class of educated Bengalis who worked as clerks, [lawyers](jobs-lawyer) and teachers. They were called the bhadralok, meaning "gentle folk".

Hindu College, founded in 1817, taught science and English literature. Its students argued loudly about religion, caste and freedom. Bengali, once used mainly for religious songs and poems, became a language for essays, novels and plays. The first Bengali newspapers appeared around 1818, and [Ram Mohan Roy](raja-ram-mohan-roy) soon started journals of his own in Bengali and Persian.

## Reformers who took on custom

Raja Ram Mohan Roy, born in 1772, read Sanskrit, Persian, Arabic and English. He campaigned against sati, the practice of a widow dying on her husband's funeral pyre, which the British banned in 1829. In 1828 he founded the Brahmo Sabha, later the Brahmo Samaj, which worshipped one God without idols.

[Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar](ishwar-chandra), a Sanskrit scholar, fought for widows to be allowed to remarry. He also pushed for girls' schools and simplified the Bengali alphabet. His primer, Barnaparichay, is still used.

## A flood of words

Writers created new kinds of Bengali literature. Michael Madhusudan Dutt brought blank verse into Bengali poetry. Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay wrote novels, including Anandamath (1882), which contains the song "Vande Mataram", later India's national song.

Then came Rabindranath [Tagore](tagore), who wrote poems, songs, stories and plays. In 1913 he became the first non-European to win the Nobel Prize in Literature. His songs became the national [anthems](anthems) of both India and [Bangladesh](bangladesh).

## Science and women's voices

The ideas spread beyond literature. Jagadish Chandra Bose studied [radio waves](radio-waves) and plant responses. Satyendra Nath Bose's work in 1924 gave his name to a whole family of particles, the bosons. [Swami Vivekananda](swami-vivekananda), a Calcutta graduate, spoke at the World's Parliament of Religions in Chicago in 1893, and many Americans heard about Indian philosophy for the first time.

In 1886 Kadambini Ganguly became one of the first Indian women qualified in Western medicine. Begum Rokeya Sakhawat Hossain wrote "Sultana's Dream" (1905), a short story about a land run by women, and opened a school for Muslim girls in Calcutta.

## A movement with limits

Historians point out that most of these changes reached a small group of mostly upper-caste, urban Hindu men. Peasants, many Muslims and most women were left out at first. Some argue the movement depended too much on British ideas, while others say Bengalis made those ideas their own. Others note that reformers in Maharashtra, Punjab and the Tamil country were questioning caste and custom at about the same time, so Bengal was far from alone.

Whatever the label, its schools, newspapers and debates helped prepare the ground for India's [freedom struggle](freedom-struggle). The arguments of that century can still be heard in Indian politics today.
