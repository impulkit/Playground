## The short answer

Tinkle is an Indian comics magazine that started in November 1980. It was founded by Anant Pai, a chemical engineer turned publisher whom readers knew as Uncle Pai, at India Book House in Bombay. He had already created *Amar Chitra Katha*, the comics that retell Indian myths and history.

Tinkle was meant to be lighter and more playful. Each issue mixed funny comic strips, folk tales, puzzles, quizzes and short science facts. It soon became one of the most popular [children's magazines](childrens-magazines) in India, and characters like Suppandi and Shikari Shambu became household names. Many parents who read it as children now buy it for their own kids.

## Uncle Pai and a quiz show

Anant Pai often told how [Amar Chitra Katha](amar-chitra-katha) began. In 1967 he saw a quiz on television in which Indian children could answer questions about Greek myths but not about the [Ramayana](ramayana). He felt children were losing touch with their own stories. Pai already knew comics well. Born in 1929 in Karkala, in Karnataka, he had worked at The Times of India, where he was involved with Indrajal Comics, which brought the American hero the Phantom to Indian readers.

Comics, he decided, were the answer. *Amar Chitra Katha* became a huge success. By the late 1970s, Pai wanted a magazine that would do more than retell old legends. He wanted something that was funny, that taught a little science and that let children take part.

So in 1980, *Tinkle* was born. Pai signed his messages to readers as Uncle Pai, the name generations of children came to know him by.

## Suppandi and friends

Tinkle's most famous character is Suppandi, a simple, good-hearted village boy who keeps getting new jobs and keeps losing them. Tell him to "keep an eye on the milk" and he will stare at it as it boils over. The early Suppandi stories grew out of folk tales sent in by readers and were drawn by the artist Ram Waeerkar.

Shikari Shambu is a "great hunter" who is actually terrified of animals and succeeds only by accident. Kalia is a clever crow who protects smaller animals from Chamataka the jackal and Doob Doob the crocodile. Real [crows](crow-smart) are among the cleverest birds, so Kalia fits right in. Tantri the Mantri is a minister who keeps plotting to seize the throne and always fails.

## A magazine its readers wrote

Tinkle's big idea was that readers could join in. Children sent jokes, stories and facts, and the best were printed with their names. Seeing your name in a national magazine was a thrill many readers still remember.

Each issue also had puzzles, "Tell me why" style science questions and stories from across [India's regions](india-states-tour). Tinkle came out in English and, at various times, in other Indian languages too.

## From paper to screens

Over the years, Tinkle launched thicker "digests" that collected old favourites, and holiday specials packed with extra stories. India Book House's comics later became part of a company called Amar Chitra Katha Pvt Ltd, which publishes Tinkle today.

Characters like Suppandi have appeared in animated shows and online. Anant Pai died in 2011, but his magazine kept going. Along with [Indian comics](comics-india) such as *Chacha Chaudhary* and later TV heroes like [Chhota Bheem](chhota-bheem), Tinkle showed that Indian children wanted funny stories set in places that looked like home.
