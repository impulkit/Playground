## The short answer

Yoga and Ayurveda are two old Indian traditions that both grew up around the same ideas: that body and mind are connected, and that health comes from balance.

Ayurveda is a system of medicine. Its name means "knowledge of life" in [Sanskrit](sanskrit). It uses herbs, diet, massage, daily routines and, in its classical form, even surgery.

Yoga is a set of practices for the body and mind: breathing [exercises](exercise), meditation and physical postures. Today it is practised by millions of people around the world, many of whom think of it mainly as exercise.

## Three doshas and a cook's spice box

Ayurveda's classical texts, the *Charaka Samhita* and the *Sushruta Samhita*, were written down roughly 2,000 years ago, though they were revised over centuries. The second is famous for its surgery, which you can read about in [Sushruta's story](sushruta).

Ayurveda teaches that the body is governed by three *doshas*, called vata, pitta and kapha, which are linked to qualities like movement, heat and stability. Illness is seen as these falling out of balance, and treatment tries to restore it. Many Indian kitchen habits, such as adding [turmeric](turmeric) and ginger to food, are tied to Ayurvedic ideas.

## Patanjali and the eight limbs

The most famous early yoga text is the *Yoga Sutras* of Patanjali, a collection of short sayings. Scholars disagree on its date, but many place it somewhere between 1,600 and 2,200 years ago.

Patanjali describes eight "limbs" of yoga. They include rules for behaviour, breath control, withdrawing the senses and, finally, deep concentration and meditation. Posture is only one limb, and he says little about it beyond sitting steady and at ease. Breath control matters more, and works because [your lungs](lungs-breathe) and heart rate are closely linked.

## How yoga went global

Yoga's ideas reached America before its poses did. In 1893, the Indian monk [Swami Vivekananda](swami-vivekananda) spoke at the Parliament of the World's Religions in Chicago and became famous overnight. He went on to teach yoga philosophy and meditation in the USA, and in 1896 published a book on it called *Raja Yoga*. For many Americans, it was their first meeting with these ideas.

Modern posture yoga took shape in the early 1900s. In Mysore, a teacher named T. Krishnamacharya developed flowing sequences of poses. His students, including B.K.S. Iyengar and K. Pattabhi Jois, taught them around the world.

In the USA, yoga grew from a curiosity to a common activity. A 2017 US government survey found that about one in seven American adults had practised it in the past year. In 2014, the [United Nations](united-nations) declared 21 June the International Day of Yoga, and the first was held in 2015.

## What science says

Researchers have studied [yoga](yoga-science) a lot. There is good evidence that it can improve flexibility and balance, and some evidence that it helps with back pain and [stress](stress). It is not a cure for serious illness.

Ayurveda is harder to test, because treatments are often tailored to each person. Some herbs are being studied seriously. Others have little evidence behind them, and a 2004 study found that about one in five Ayurvedic products bought in Boston shops contained harmful metals such as lead. Ayurveda practitioners say the traditional knowledge has value; many doctors want stronger proof from [careful trials](statistics-lies).

India's government supports both through the Ministry of AYUSH, set up in 2014. Anyone with a real illness should still see a qualified doctor.
