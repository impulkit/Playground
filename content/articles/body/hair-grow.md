## The short answer

Each hair grows out of a tiny pocket in your skin called a [follicle](?follicle). At the bottom of the follicle is a bulb of living cells that divide again and again. As new cells are made, they push the older ones up and out.

As those cells move up, they fill with a tough protein called [keratin](?keratin) and die. So the hair you can see and comb is dead. That's why a haircut doesn't hurt, while pulling a hair out by the root does.

Hair keeps you warm, protects your skin and eyes, and helps you feel things touching you.

## About a centimetre a month

Hair on your [scalp](gross-dandruff) grows about 1 cm a month, around 15 cm a year. Each follicle works in cycles. It grows a hair for several years, rests for a few months, and then lets the old hair fall out as a new one starts underneath.

Because all your follicles are at different stages, you shed about 50 to 100 hairs a day without going bald. The growing stage decides how long your hair can get. In some people it lasts much longer, which is how a few people grow hair down to the floor.

## What hair is for

Eyebrows steer [sweat](sweat) and rain away from your eyes. Eyelashes catch dust and trigger a blink when something comes close. Nose hairs filter the air you breathe.

The hair on your head shades your scalp from the sun and helps keep it warm. Every hair also has nerves wrapped around its root, so it acts like a tiny feeler. That's how you can sense an ant on your arm before you see it.

When you're cold or scared, tiny muscles pull your hairs upright. You get [goosebumps](goosebumps). In furrier animals this traps a thicker layer of warm air, or makes the animal look bigger.

## A mammal thing

Hair is one of the things that makes a mammal a mammal. Cats, elephants, whales and bats all have some. It may have helped early [mammals](mammals-rise) stay warm at night while dinosaurs ruled the day.

Humans have about as many follicles as chimpanzees, but most of ours grow short, fine hairs. Scientists think losing thick fur may have helped our ancestors keep cool while walking and running in hot places, using sweat instead.

## Colour, curls and grey

[Hair colour](hair-colour) comes from melanin, the same pigment that colours skin. Black and brown hair have lots of one kind. Red hair has more of another. As people get older, the colour-making cells slowly stop working, and new hairs grow in [grey](grey-hair) or white.

Curly hair grows from oval or curved follicles. Straight hair grows from rounder ones. Your genes decide which you have.

## Hair and tradition

Hair matters in many cultures. In many Hindu families, a child's first haircut, called the *mundan*, is a ceremony. At the Tirumala temple in Andhra Pradesh, many pilgrims offer their hair as a sign of devotion, and the temple sells the hair to [make wigs](oddity-history-hair). Many Sikhs keep their hair uncut as part of their faith. Other [religions](religions-world) have their own customs, from shaved heads to covered hair.

## A clue for detectives

A hair pulled out by the root carries living cells, and those cells contain [DNA](dna). Forensic scientists can use it to help identify a person. A hair that simply fell out, or a cut piece, usually has much less DNA to work with, but it can still show things like whether someone dyed it.
