## The short answer

When someone gets hurt, the first thing to do is stay calm and check that you're safe. Don't run onto a road or touch a broken wire to help. Then get an adult as fast as you can: a parent, a teacher or a nearby grown-up.

If it's serious, call for help. In India, the national emergency number is 112, and in most states 108 brings an ambulance. In the USA, it's 911. Tell them what happened and where you are. For small injuries, simple first aid makes a big difference: [clean water](water-purify) for cuts, cool water for burns, and comfort for the person who is hurt. Being kind and staying with them matters more than you might think.

If you do call an emergency number, try to stay on the line and answer the questions slowly. The person on the phone is trained to guide callers, step by step, until help arrives. They may ask your address, what happened and whether the person is awake and breathing.

## Cuts and scrapes

Most cuts and grazes are small. Rinse the cut with clean running water to wash out dirt. Then press on it with a clean cloth or tissue to stop the bleeding. Once it stops, a plaster or bandage keeps germs out while it heals.

Your body does a lot of the work itself. Blood thickens at the cut, and within a day a [scab](scabs) forms to seal it. A deep cut, one that keeps bleeding or one made by something dirty or rusty needs an adult to check it, and maybe a doctor.

## Burns

If someone burns their [skin](skin) on a hot pan or with hot water, cool it under cool running tap water for about 20 minutes. That may feel like a long time, but it stops the heat from going deeper. Take off rings or tight clothes near the burn, unless they're stuck to the skin.

In many homes in India and elsewhere, people reach for toothpaste, butter or ice. These can make burns worse. Large burns, burns on the face or hands, and any burn on a young child need medical help.

## Bumps, nosebleeds and sprains

For a bump or a twisted ankle, rest the injured part. Hold something cold against it, such as an ice pack wrapped in a cloth. That reduces swelling and [pain](pain). The [bruise](bruises) may change colour over the next few days as the body clears the blood under the skin.

For a [nosebleed](nose-bleeds), sit up, lean forward and pinch the soft part of the nose for about 10 minutes. Don't tip the head back. Any bang to the head that makes someone sleepy, confused or sick needs a doctor straight away.

## Where first aid came from

In 1859, a Swiss businessman named Henry Dunant passed the battlefield of Solferino in northern Italy. Thousands of wounded soldiers lay there with almost no one to help. He organised local people to care for them, then wrote a book calling for trained volunteers in every country. This led to the founding of the [Red Cross](red-cross) in 1863. Dunant shared the first [Nobel Peace Prize](nobel-peace) in 1901.

Today, the Red Cross and Red Crescent work in almost every country, and the Indian Red Cross Society dates from 1920. Many schools teach first aid, and organisations like St John Ambulance run classes for children. Knowing how to [call for help](telephone) and what to say is the most useful first-aid skill of all.
