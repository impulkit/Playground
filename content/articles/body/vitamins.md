## The short answer

Vitamins are substances your body needs in tiny amounts to work properly. You need much less of them than of protein or carbs, often just a few thousandths of a gram a day, but without them things go wrong.

Each vitamin has its own jobs. Vitamin A helps your [eyes see](eyes-see) in dim light. Vitamin C helps build the tough protein that holds skin, gums and blood vessels together. Vitamin D helps your [bones](bones) take up calcium. The B vitamins help your cells turn food into energy. Your body can't make most vitamins, or can't make enough, so they have to come from food. There are 13 that people need.

## The sailors' sickness

On long sea voyages in the 1500s to 1700s, sailors often came down with scurvy. Their gums bled, their teeth fell out and old wounds opened up. On [Magellan's voyage](magellan) around the world, many of the crew suffered from it.

In 1747, a Scottish ship's doctor named James Lind tried an experiment on HMS Salisbury. He split 12 sick sailors into pairs and gave each pair something different: cider, vinegar, seawater, oranges and lemons, and more. The pair given [citrus fruit](citrus) recovered fast. It took decades, but by the 1790s the British navy was giving its sailors lemon juice. Today we know scurvy comes from a lack of vitamin C.

## Chickens and polished rice

In the 1890s, a Dutch doctor named Christiaan Eijkman was studying beriberi in Java, in what is now Indonesia. Beriberi weakened people's nerves and hearts. Eijkman noticed that chickens fed white, polished [rice](rice) fell ill with something similar, but chickens fed brown rice stayed healthy.

Something in the brown outer layer of rice was protecting them. That something was vitamin B1. In 1912, the Polish scientist Casimir Funk called these missing substances vital amines, or vitamines. When it turned out that not all of them were amines, the final "e" was dropped. Eijkman later shared a Nobel Prize for his work.

## Sunshine vitamin

Vitamin D is unusual. Your [skin](skin) makes it when sunlight falls on it. Without enough, children's bones can grow soft and bent, a disease called rickets. Long ago, it was common in smoky industrial cities in Britain and the USA, where children saw little sun. Today, milk in many countries has vitamin D added.

You might think everyone in sunny India gets plenty. But studies have found many Indians low in vitamin D, partly because people spend much of the day indoors and cover up from the heat. A little time outside each day helps.

## A colourful plate

Most people get all the vitamins they need from a mix of foods. [Fruits and vegetables](fruits-vegetables) carry many of them: vitamin C in amla, [guava](guava-papaya) and oranges, vitamin A in [carrots](food-why-carrots-orange), mangoes and green leafy vegetables. Whole grains and dal carry B vitamins. Vitamin B12 comes mostly from animal foods like milk, curd and eggs, which matters for people who eat no animal products at all.

Some vitamins can harm you in very large doses, especially A and D, which build up in the body's fat and [liver](liver). That's why vitamin tablets should only be taken if a doctor or parent says so. Vitamin A deficiency is still a leading cause of preventable blindness in children worldwide, and many countries give children vitamin A drops to stop it.
