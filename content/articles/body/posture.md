## The short answer

Posture is the way you hold your body when you sit, stand or move. A comfortable posture lets your bones carry your weight while your muscles work without straining.

It matters, but perhaps not quite in the way you have often been told. Doctors used to think there was one correct way to sit or stand. Newer research suggests the most important thing is not to stay frozen in any position for too long. Bodies like to move. Getting up, stretching and changing position often seem to help more than sitting perfectly straight.

## Your S-shaped spine

Your spine is a stack of 33 bones called vertebrae. Some at the bottom are fused together, so an adult has 24 that move. Between them sit rubbery discs that act as shock absorbers.

Seen from the side, a healthy spine isn't straight. It has three gentle curves, in the neck, upper back and lower back, making a soft S shape. These curves work like a spring, helping you absorb bumps when you walk, run and jump.

Through the day, the weight of your body slowly squeezes water out of the discs. [You lose about a centimetre of height by evening](body-weird-facts), and get it back overnight while you lie down.

## Heads, necks and phones

An adult head weighs about 5 kg, about as much as a big watermelon. When you hold it straight above your shoulders, your neck carries it easily. When you bend forward to look at a phone or book, your neck muscles must work much harder to hold the head up.

One surgeon's calculation in 2014 suggested the strain on the neck could be several times greater when the head tips far forward. Scientists still debate how much harm this really does. But many people notice stiff necks and shoulders after long sessions bent over [screens](screen-time). Holding a phone closer to eye level and taking breaks helps.

## The school bag problem

[Heavy school bags](life-school-bags) are a worry for parents in both India and the USA. In 2020, India's government issued a School Bag Policy recommending that bags weigh no more than about 10% of a child's body weight. It also suggested that the youngest children in pre-primary classes carry no bag at all.

Wearing a backpack on both shoulders spreads the load evenly. Packing the heaviest books closest to your back helps too. If your back hurts after carrying your bag, tell a parent or teacher.

## Strong muscles, easy posture

Good posture is held up by muscles, especially those in your back, belly and hips, often called your core. People who run, swim, [climb](sport-climbing) or dance tend to find comfortable posture easier.

Some activities train posture on purpose. [Ballet](ballet) dancers practise standing tall for hours. [Yoga](yoga-science) includes poses like tadasana, the mountain pose, which is all about standing balanced and still, and many people find that [yoga](ayurveda-yoga) helps them feel looser.

## A lesson from space

Astronauts aboard the International Space Station can grow up to about 5 cm taller, because their spinal discs spread out without [gravity pressing down](what-if-gravity-doubled). Some report backaches as a result. When they return to Earth, they shrink back to normal, as explained in [space and the body](space-body). Gravity, it turns out, shapes our posture every moment of the day.
