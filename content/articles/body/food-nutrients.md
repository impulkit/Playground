## The short answer

Food gives your body three big kinds of [nutrients](?nutrient): carbohydrates, proteins and fats. Each has its own main job.

Carbohydrates, or carbs, are mostly fuel. Rice, roti, potatoes and fruit are full of them. Your body breaks them into sugar, which your cells burn for energy. Proteins are building material. Dal, eggs, milk, [paneer](cheese-paneer), fish and meat give your body the pieces it needs to build muscle, skin, hair and much else. Fats are a store of energy and a building material too. They form the outer skin of every cell and help your body take in some vitamins. A healthy diet mixes all three.

## Carbs: the fuel

When you eat rice or [bread](wheat-bread), your gut breaks the starch down into a sugar called glucose. It passes into your [blood](blood) and travels to every cell. Your brain runs almost entirely on glucose, using about a fifth of all your body's energy, even though it weighs only about 2% of your body.

Some carbs, like white sugar, are absorbed very fast. Others, like whole grains, [millets](millets) and beans, break down slowly and keep you going longer. Fibre is a carb you can't digest at all, but your gut microbes feed on it and it keeps your digestion moving.

## Proteins: the building blocks

Proteins are long chains made from smaller pieces called amino acids, a bit like beads on a string. There are 20 kinds. Your body can make 11 of them itself. The other 9 must come from food.

Animal foods like milk and eggs contain all 9 in good amounts. Many plant foods are low in one or two, but different plants fill each other's gaps. Rice is low in one amino acid that [dal](pulses-dal) has plenty of, and dal is low in one that rice has. So the everyday plate of dal-chawal, or rice and beans in Mexico and the American South, gives a complete set. Families worked this out by taste and habit long before scientists did.

## Fats: the store and the wall

Fat holds about twice as much energy per gram as carbs or protein. That makes it the body's long-term store, packed under the skin and around the organs. It also keeps you warm and cushions your organs.

Every cell in your body is wrapped in a thin skin made largely of fat. Your brain is about 60% fat by dry weight. Some fats, called essential fatty acids, can't be made by the body and must come from food, such as nuts, seeds, mustard oil and fish.

## Counting energy

The energy in food is measured in kilocalories, often just called calories. One kilocalorie is the energy needed to warm one litre of water by one degree Celsius. In the late 1800s, an American chemist named Wilbur Atwater burned foods in sealed containers and measured the heat. He also measured what people ate and what left their bodies. His numbers, 4 for carbs and protein and 9 for fat, are still printed on food labels today.

A balanced [thali](thali) does a neat job of all this: rice or roti for fuel, dal and curd for protein, a little ghee or oil, and vegetables for vitamins and fibre. India's [mid-day meal](mid-day-meal) scheme in schools is planned around similar ideas, to give children enough energy and protein each school day.
