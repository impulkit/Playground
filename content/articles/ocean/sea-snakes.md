## The short answer

Sea snakes are snakes that live in the ocean. There are about 70 species, found in warm, shallow seas from the east coast of Africa, across the Indian Ocean and around India, to Australia and the islands of the Pacific. There are none at all in the Atlantic.

They are [reptiles](ocean-reptiles), related to cobras, and like cobras most are venomous. But their bodies have changed in clever ways for life in water. Their tails are flat like paddles. Their nostrils close with little flaps when they dive. And instead of slithering, they swim with smooth side-to-side waves of their bodies.

## Two kinds of sea snake

Biologists split sea snakes into two groups. **True sea snakes** are fully at home in the ocean. They give birth to live babies in the water, and many would be clumsy and helpless if washed onto a beach. **Sea kraits** live a double life. They hunt at sea but crawl onto rocks and beaches to rest, digest their food and lay eggs. They are easy to spot, with bold black and blue-grey bands.

Most sea snakes hunt among [coral reefs](coral-reefs), rocks and mangroves, poking their small heads into cracks for eels and fish. A few eat only fish eggs, and have tiny fangs and almost no venom.

## Holding their breath

Sea snakes breathe with lungs, so every so often they have to swim up for air. One lung runs almost the whole length of the body and works like an air tank. Some species can also absorb a little oxygen straight through their skin.

With these tricks, some sea snakes stay underwater for an hour or more, and a few go much longer while resting. Compare that with your own [lungs](lungs-breathe): most people can hold their breath for less than a minute. Divers watching sea snakes see them rise slowly, snatch a quick breath at the surface and glide back down, like a tiny whale coming up to blow.

## Thirsty in the sea

Sea water is far too [salty](why-sea-salty) to drink. Sea snakes have a gland under the tongue that pushes out extra salt, but scientists found that many still get dangerously thirsty. In experiments, some refused salt water even after weeks without a drink, then gulped fresh water eagerly.

In the wild, they seem to rely on rain. After a heavy downpour, a thin layer of fresh water can float on top of the sea for a while, and sea snakes come up to drink it. In long dry spells they may go months without a proper drink.

## The snake that drifts across an ocean

The yellow-bellied sea snake, black on top and bright yellow below, spends its life drifting in the open ocean, often near floating lines of seaweed and rubbish where small fish gather. It has the widest range of any snake. It is found from East Africa all the way to the Pacific coast of Mexico and Central America. During warm [El Niño](el-nino) years, a few have even washed up on beaches in California.

## Sea snakes and people

Sea snake [venom](snakes-venom) is powerful, but the snakes are usually calm. Divers often film them swimming past without trouble. Off India's coasts, most bites are from the beaked sea snake, and nearly all happen to fishermen sorting tangled nets, especially at night.

A sea snake on a beach should be left alone and reported to an adult, a lifeguard or the forest department. Along the coasts of Odisha, Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Gujarat, sea snakes are a regular part of the catch that fishermen carefully throw back.
