## The short answer

A port is where the sea meets the land, and its whole job is moving cargo between the two as quickly as possible. A giant ship arrives, a harbour pilot guides it in, and tugboats nudge it against the dock. Then enormous cranes lift steel containers off the ship and onto trucks or trains, and load new ones back on.

Ports have deep channels so ships don't scrape the bottom, long quays where ships can tie up, and huge yards where containers are stacked like building blocks. They also have customs officers who check what comes in, since almost everything you own may have spent part of its life on a ship.

## The box that changed the world

For thousands of years, ships were loaded by hand. Workers carried sacks, barrels and crates up gangplanks, one at a time. A big ship could sit in port for a week or more, and goods were often lost or stolen.

In 1956, an American trucking businessman named Malcolm McLean sent a converted tanker called the Ideal X from Newark to Houston carrying 58 metal boxes. Each could be lifted straight off a truck and onto the ship. Soon containers came in standard sizes, 20 or 40 feet long, so the same box could ride a ship, a train and a truck without being opened. Loading costs dropped sharply, and [world trade](trade-world) grew fast.

## Giant cranes and moving stacks

Today [the largest container ships](ships-tech) are about 400 m long, longer than three football fields, with room for more than 20,000 of the smaller containers. Even so, they [float](floating-ships), because their huge hulls push aside an enormous weight of water.

At the dock, ship-to-shore cranes taller than a 15-storey building lift each box. A skilled operator can move a container every minute or two. Computers plan exactly where every box goes, keeping heavy ones low and balancing the ship so it doesn't tip. Each container has its own code, and its journey is tracked from the factory gate to the shop.

## Ports old and new

People have built harbours for thousands of years. At Lothal in Gujarat, archaeologists found a large brick basin over 4,000 years old. Many think it was a dockyard where Harappan boats loaded goods, which would make it one of the oldest known docks anywhere. Others suggest it was a reservoir. Either way, the [Harappans traded](harappan-trade) by sea with lands as far as Mesopotamia.

Today, Shanghai in China is the world's busiest container port. In the United States, the side-by-side ports of [Los Angeles](los-angeles) and Long Beach handle a large share of goods arriving from Asia. In India, big ports include Jawaharlal Nehru Port near Mumbai and Mundra in Gujarat. In 2024, a new deep-water port at Vizhinjam in Kerala began receiving huge container ships, close to one of the world's busiest shipping routes.

## When a port goes quiet

Ports are so connected that trouble in one spot can ripple around the world. In 2021, a giant container ship called the Ever Given got stuck across [the Suez Canal](suez-canal) in Egypt. For six days, hundreds of ships waited at either end, and deliveries of everything from furniture to car parts were delayed for weeks.

That is why port workers, pilots and planners work around the clock. A port that runs smoothly is invisible to most people. A port that stops can empty shop shelves on the other side of the planet.
