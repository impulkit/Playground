## The short answer

A kelp forest is an underwater forest made of [giant seaweed](seaweed-food). Kelp grows in cool, shallow coastal water, anchored to rocks on the sea floor. It reaches up towards the light, sometimes more than 40 m, and spreads out at the surface like a leafy roof. Beneath it, sunlight filters down in green beams, a bit like a forest on land.

Kelp is not really a plant. It is a large brown seaweed, a kind of [algae](?algae). But like trees, it gives food and shelter to a huge number of living things: fish, crabs, snails, [sea stars](starfish), sea urchins, seals and sea otters. Kelp forests grow along about a quarter of the world's coastlines, from California and Chile to South Africa, Australia and Norway.

## How kelp is built

Kelp has three main parts. At the bottom is the holdfast, a tangle of root-like grips that clings to rock. It does not suck up water or food like a real root. It just holds on. Above it rises a long, bendy stalk called the stipe. Leafy blades grow from the stipe, and many have small gas-filled bulbs that float them upward.

Kelp makes its own food from sunlight, using [photosynthesis](photosynthesis), just like plants do. It soaks up nutrients from the water through its whole surface. That is why kelp needs cold water: cold water rising from the deep is usually rich in nutrients. Warm, clear tropical seas have too few for kelp to grow well.

## The fastest growers

Giant kelp is one of the fastest-growing living things on Earth. In good conditions, a frond can grow about half a metre in a day. Within a few months, a young kelp can reach the surface from the sea floor many metres below.

A grown kelp forest can be as tall as a big forest tree on land. Along the coast of northern California, the [redwoods](redwoods) on land and the giant kelp just offshore are both among the tallest living things in their worlds. Storms tear some kelp loose every winter, but the forest grows back fast as long as conditions are right.

## Otters, urchins and starfish

The health of a kelp forest depends on a balance between hungry animals. Sea urchins, spiny balls that crawl along the bottom, love to chew through kelp holdfasts. When there are too many urchins, they can wipe out a forest and leave bare rock known as an urchin barren.

Sea otters and sunflower sea stars eat urchins and keep their numbers down. When otters were hunted almost to extinction for their fur, many kelp forests collapsed. Where otters have returned, kelp often comes back too, as you can read in [sea otters](sea-otters). Since 2014, a sea star disease and a long [ocean heatwave](heatwaves) hit northern California hard. Bull kelp forests there shrank by more than 90 per cent, and urchins took over.

## Kelp in your kitchen

People use kelp and other seaweeds in many ways. In Japan and Korea, seaweeds have been eaten for centuries. A substance taken from kelp, called alginate, helps thicken foods. It can help keep [ice cream](ice-cream) smooth, and it turns up in some [toothpastes](made-toothpaste) and medicines.

India has no giant kelp forests, because its seas are too warm. But seaweeds of other kinds grow along its coasts. In Tamil Nadu, around the Gulf of Mannar, many families farm and collect seaweed, which is sold to make thickeners for foods and other products. Seaweed farming is growing in India, and scientists are studying how to do it without harming the [reefs](coral-reefs) nearby.
