## The short answer

Your heart is a [muscle](muscles) about the size of your fist, pumping blood all around your body.

Small animals usually have faster hearts; big animals have slower ones. A mouse's heart can race at around 500 beats a minute, while an elephant's plods along at about 30. Yours, resting, is somewhere in between, and a child's heart usually beats faster than an adult's.

Put two fingers on your wrist and count for 15 seconds, then multiply by 4. That's your heart rate!

## Two pumps side by side

Your heart is really two pumps joined together. The right side sends tired blood, low in oxygen, to your [lungs](lungs-breathe) to pick up fresh oxygen. The left side takes that bright red blood and pushes it out to the rest of your body.

Each side has two rooms, or chambers: a smaller one on top that collects blood and a stronger one below that pumps it out. Flaps called valves snap shut after each push to stop blood flowing backwards. The "lub-dub" sound of a heartbeat is the noise of those valves closing.

At rest, an adult's heart moves about 5 litres of [blood](blood) every minute, roughly the whole body's supply.

## Its own spark

Your heart doesn't wait for orders from your brain. A tiny patch of special cells in its upper right chamber fires an electrical signal about once a second. The signal spreads across the heart and makes it squeeze. Doctors call this patch the heart's natural [pacemaker](accident-pacemaker).

Your brain can still speed it up or slow it down. When you run, feel scared or get excited, your heart beats faster to deliver more oxygen to your muscles.

## The biggest and the fastest

The [blue whale](blue-whale) has the largest heart of any animal. One preserved by a museum in Canada weighed about 180 kg, about as much as two grown men. In 2019, scientists stuck a sensor on a wild blue whale and found its heart slowed to just 2 beats a minute on deep dives, saving oxygen. At the surface it sped up again to over 30.

At the other end, a flying [hummingbird's](hummingbirds) heart can beat more than 1,000 times a minute, faster than you can tap a finger.

Small animals lose heat quickly and burn energy fast, so their hearts must work harder. Big animals are the opposite.

## A doctor who was too shy

Before 1816, doctors listened to a patient's heart by pressing an ear to their chest. That year, a French doctor named René Laennec felt this was too awkward with a young woman patient. He rolled up a sheet of paper into a tube, put one end on her chest and listened at the other. The heartbeat came through clearly.

He went on to build a wooden tube and called it a stethoscope. Doctors in every hospital from Delhi to Denver still carry its descendant around their necks.

## Three billion beats

Your heart beats about 1 lakh times a day. Over a long life, that adds up to about 3 billion beats. It's hard to picture a number that big, as you can see in [big numbers](big-numbers). Through all of them, your heart never stops to rest. Between beats, it relaxes for a fraction of a second, and that tiny pause is all the rest it gets.
