## The short answer

Frida Kahlo (1907–1954) was a Mexican painter best known for her vivid self-portraits. She painted her own face again and again, with dark joined eyebrows, flowers in her hair and colourful traditional Mexican clothes. Around her she painted monkeys, parrots, plants and symbols from Mexican history and folk art.

Her life was full of pain. Childhood illness and a terrible accident left her with lasting injuries, and she had many operations. She turned those experiences into paintings that are honest, strange and bright all at once. Not very famous outside Mexico in her own time, she is now one of the most recognised artists in the world.

## Polio and a bus crash

Frida was born in Coyoacán, then a quiet town on the edge of Mexico City, in a house her family painted bright blue. At about six she caught [polio](polio-india), a disease that can damage [muscles](muscles). It left her right leg thinner and weaker than her left, and she later wore long skirts partly to hide it.

At 18, in 1925, she was travelling home from school when the bus she was on crashed into a tram. Her spine, pelvis and one leg were badly broken. She spent months lying flat in plaster.

## Painting from bed

Frida had planned to study medicine. Now, stuck in bed, she began to paint. Her mother had a special easel made that let her paint lying down, and a [mirror](mirrors) was fixed above the bed so she could see herself.

That was the start of her self-portraits. Around 55 of her paintings show her own face. She painted herself with her pets, in hospital, with her heart outside her body, or as two Fridas holding hands. Each picture told a piece of her story.

## Frida and Diego

In 1929 Frida married Diego Rivera, already Mexico's most famous painter. He was known for giant [murals](?mural) on public walls showing workers, farmers and Mexican history. Their marriage was stormy. They separated, divorced in 1939 and remarried the next year.

They travelled together to the United States, where Diego painted murals in [San Francisco](san-francisco), Detroit and New York. Frida felt homesick there, and painted herself standing between busy American factories and the ancient world of Mexico.

## Proudly Mexican

Frida loved Mexico's past, including the cultures of the [Aztecs and the Maya](aztecs-maya), and its living traditions. She often wore the long embroidered skirts and blouses of the Tehuana women of southern Mexico. She painted in the style of small Mexican folk paintings called *retablos*, which ordinary people made to give thanks for being saved from accidents or illness.

In 1939 she had an exhibition in [Paris](paris). The Louvre bought one of her works, *The Frame*, a small self-portrait surrounded by painted birds and flowers. Famous artists there praised her, though she did not think much of the Paris art scene.

## The Blue House

In her last years Frida's health grew worse, and doctors had to remove part of her right leg. In 1953 she had her first solo exhibition in Mexico. Too ill to walk, she arrived by ambulance and was carried in on her own bed.

She died the next year at 47, in the Blue House where she was born. It is now the Frida Kahlo Museum and receives visitors from all over the world. Many people, especially those living with illness or disability, see her as someone who refused to hide what made her different.
