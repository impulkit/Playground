## The short answer

Manipuri is one of India's officially recognised classical dances, from the state of Manipur in the north-east. It is famous for its gentle, rounded movements. Dancers never stamp hard on the floor. Instead they glide, sway and turn softly, with knees bent and arms moving in smooth curves.

The best-known Manipuri dances are the Ras Lilas, which show the love of Krishna, Radha and the gopis, the cowherd girls. The female dancers wear a stiff, shining, barrel-shaped skirt called the *potloi*, decorated with mirrors and embroidery, and a fine see-through veil. Manipuri also includes energetic drum dances and much older dances for the gods of Manipur's own Meitei religion.

## A valley full of dance

Manipur is a green valley ringed by hills, close to the border with Myanmar. Its capital is Imphal, and its largest lake, [Loktak](india-wonder-loktak), is famous for its floating islands. The people of the valley, the Meitei, have a long tradition of music, martial arts and dance.

One of the oldest celebrations is Lai Haraoba, which means "the merrymaking of the gods". Priestesses called *maibis* lead dances that tell the story of how the world, and human life, were created. Many of Manipuri dance's gentle movements are thought to come from these ancient rituals.

## The Ras Lila

In the 18th century, Vaishnavism, the worship of Vishnu and Krishna, became the main religion of Manipur's royal court. King Bhagyachandra, also called Rajarshi Bhagyachandra, is credited with creating the Manipuri Ras Lila. Tradition says the dance and its costumes came to him in a dream.

Ras Lilas are performed at temples on full-moon nights in autumn and spring, and can go on for many hours. The dancers keep their faces calm and gentle, with no big expressions. The devotion is shown through the flow of the whole body.

## Dancers who drum

Manipuri music is led by the *pung*, a barrel-shaped [drum](drums). In the dance called *pung cholom*, male drummers become dancers too. With drums hung from their shoulders, they leap, squat, whirl and spin, all while playing complex rhythms.

Cymbal players do something similar in the *kartal cholom*. These dances are thrilling to watch and show the athletic side of Manipuri, which is otherwise known for softness. The region's martial art, *thang-ta*, has also influenced some movements.

## Tagore and the wider world

In 1919, the poet [Rabindranath Tagore](tagore) watched a Manipuri dance performance in Sylhet, in what is now [Bangladesh](bangladesh). He was so moved that he invited Manipuri teachers to his school at Santiniketan in Bengal. This helped introduce the dance to the rest of India and, through Tagore's own dance-dramas, to audiences abroad.

In 1954, the Jawaharlal [Nehru](nehru) Manipur Dance Academy opened in Imphal to train new dancers. Today Manipuri is taught across India and performed on stages from Kolkata to New York.

## Costume and colour

The potloi skirt is made of stiff cloth layers, stiffened at the bottom so it stands out like a drum. It is covered in [mirror](mirrors)-work, gold and silver thread and sequins that catch the light of the lamps. Krishna wears yellow, with a crown of peacock feathers. Because the skirt hides the feet completely, the dancers seem to float, almost like dolls.

Manipur's music uses a singer, the pung drum, cymbals and sometimes a flute. Together with the soft steps, it creates the feeling of a dream, which many Manipuris say is what the dance is meant to be. For the people of Manipur it is also a matter of deep pride, and one of the best-known arts of [north-east India](northeast-india).
