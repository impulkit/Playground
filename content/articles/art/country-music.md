## The short answer

Country music is a style of American popular music that grew out of the folk songs of the rural South, especially the [Appalachian](appalachians) mountains. It is known for story-telling lyrics about everyday life, love, family, hard work, faith and small towns. Its sound comes from the fiddle, banjo, acoustic [guitar](guitar) and the sliding, crying steel guitar.

Its roots lie in the ballads brought by settlers from [Scotland](scotland), Ireland and England, mixed with the banjo and musical styles of African Americans. In the 1920s, radio and records turned these local sounds into a national music. Today country is one of the best-selling kinds of music in the United States, centred on Nashville, Tennessee.

## Mountain songs

In the 1700s and 1800s, many settlers from Scotland, Ireland and England made homes in the Appalachian mountains. They brought old ballads, long songs that told stories of love, murder and adventure, and dance tunes played on the fiddle.

In the American South, they met the music of enslaved Africans and their descendants. The banjo came from African instruments made of a gourd and a skin, with a long neck. Black musicians also brought rhythms, the bluesy sliding notes and new ways of singing. Mixed together over generations, these sounds became "old-time" or "hillbilly" music, played at dances, churches and on porches.

## The big bang in Bristol

In 1927, a record producer named Ralph Peer set up a [recording studio](behind-music-recording) in an empty building in Bristol, a town on the border between Tennessee and Virginia. He put an advert in the local paper inviting musicians to come and record.

Among those who turned up were the Carter Family, who sang mountain songs in close harmony, and Jimmie Rodgers, a former railway worker who yodelled. Both became stars. Historians often call the Bristol Sessions "the big bang of country music", and Bristol now has a museum about them.

## The Grand Ole Opry

In 1925, a radio station in Nashville started a Saturday night show of fiddle and string-band music. It became the Grand Ole Opry. Families across the South gathered around their radios to listen, and singers who appeared on it became famous.

The Opry is still broadcast every week, making it one of the longest-running [radio shows](radio-shows) in the world. Nashville grew into the home of country music recording, and is now nicknamed Music City.

## Many kinds of country

Country music has many branches. Bluegrass, created by Bill Monroe in the 1940s, is fast and acoustic, with brilliant banjo and fiddle playing. Honky-tonk, made famous by Hank Williams, is the sound of bars and broken hearts. Johnny Cash sang about prisoners and the poor, and Dolly Parton wrote hits including "Jolene" and "9 to 5".

Modern country often blends with pop and rock. [Taylor Swift](taylor-swift) began as a teenage country singer before moving to pop. In 2019, Lil Nas X's country-rap song "Old Town Road" spent 19 weeks at number one in the US. In 2025, Beyoncé's country album *Cowboy Carter* won the Grammy for Album of the Year.

## Country in India

Country music has a loyal following in parts of north-east India, especially in Meghalaya and Nagaland, where Western music has been loved for generations. In [Shillong](shillong), local bands play country and rock songs at cafés and festivals. Its songs about land, family and home have a lot in common with India's own [folk songs](folk-music-india).
