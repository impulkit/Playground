## The short answer

Ravi Shankar was an Indian sitar player and composer who did more than anyone to bring Indian classical music to the rest of the world. From the 1950s, he toured Europe and America, playing ragas for audiences who had never heard them before.

He worked with the violinist Yehudi Menuhin, taught [the Beatles](beatles-music)' George Harrison, and played at the famous Woodstock festival in 1969. He also wrote film music, including for Satyajit Ray's *Pather Panchali*. In 1999, India gave him the Bharat Ratna, its highest civilian honour. He kept performing almost until he died in 2012, at the age of 92.

## A boy who danced

Ravi Shankar was born in 1920 in Benares, now [Varanasi](varanasi), on the banks of the Ganga. His older brother, Uday Shankar, was a famous dancer who mixed Indian and Western styles.

When Ravi was about ten, he moved to [Paris](paris) with Uday's dance troupe. For several years, he danced on stages across Europe and America and played a little music on the side. He saw the world early, which helped him later explain Indian music to Western audiences.

## Seven years with a strict guru

In 1938, Ravi gave up dancing to study sitar with Allauddin Khan, a great musician who lived in the small town of Maihar, in central India. He was famous for being very strict.

Ravi practised for many hours a day, for about seven years. He learned the old rules of ragas, the families of notes that each have their own mood, as described in [ragas and time](raga-time).

## Music for radio and film

After his training, Ravi Shankar worked as music director for [All India Radio](radio-india) in Delhi from 1949 to 1956, writing music for its [orchestra](orchestra). He also composed film music. His score for [Satyajit Ray](satyajit-ray)'s *Pather Panchali* (1955), with its haunting sitar themes, helped make the film famous around the world.

He then began touring abroad. In the 1960s, he and the violinist Yehudi Menuhin recorded *West Meets East*, which won [a Grammy award](music-awards).

## A Beatle comes to learn

In 1966, George Harrison of the Beatles began learning sitar from Ravi Shankar, and travelled to India to study with him. Suddenly, millions of young people in Britain and America wanted to hear Indian music.

Ravi Shankar played at huge rock festivals, including Monterey in 1967 and Woodstock in 1969. He was not always comfortable there. He asked audiences to listen quietly and respectfully, and some Indian critics felt he was simplifying classical music for Western fans.

## A concert for refugees

In 1971, war in East Pakistan, now [Bangladesh](bangladesh), forced millions of people to flee to India as [refugees](refugees). Ravi Shankar's own family came from Bengal, and he wanted to help.

He asked George Harrison to help him raise money. In August 1971, they organised the Concert for Bangladesh at Madison Square Garden in New York, with famous musicians including Bob Dylan. It was one of the first giant benefit concerts, and it inspired many later ones. Ravi Shankar opened the show with Ali Akbar Khan on sarod and Alla Rakha on [tabla](tabla). Today, his daughter Anoushka Shankar carries on the sitar tradition, while her half-sister Norah Jones became a famous singer in the USA.
