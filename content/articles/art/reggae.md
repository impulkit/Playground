## The short answer

Reggae is a kind of popular music that began in Jamaica, an island in the Caribbean, in the late 1960s. You can recognise it by its rhythm. Guitars and keyboards play short, choppy chords on the off-beats, a deep bass guitar carries the melody, and the drums often leave the first beat of the bar empty. The effect is a slow, swaying, heavy groove.

Reggae songs cover everything from love and dancing to poverty, protest and religion. Bob Marley and his band, the Wailers, made reggae famous worldwide in the 1970s. Its sound systems, dub mixing and talking-over-the-beat style went on to shape [hip-hop](hip-hop) and dance music. In 2018, UNESCO added reggae music of Jamaica to its list of the world's intangible cultural heritage.

## From ska to reggae

Jamaica became independent from Britain in 1962. In those years, young Jamaican musicians mixed American rhythm and blues with the island's own folk music, called mento, to create ska. It was fast, with horns and a jumpy off-beat.

In the mid-1960s, the music slowed down into rocksteady, cooler and heavier on the bass. By about 1968 it had slowed and changed again into reggae. A song by Toots and the Maytals called "Do the Reggay" was one of the first to use the name. Nobody is completely sure where the word came from.

## Sound systems

In Kingston, Jamaica's capital, many people could not afford records or tickets to clubs. Instead, DJs built "sound systems", huge stacks of speakers loaded onto trucks, and held open-air dances in yards and streets.

DJs began talking and chanting over the music to excite the crowd, a style called toasting. Producers such as King Tubby and Lee "Scratch" Perry created dub, stripping songs down to drums and bass and adding echoes. In 1973, a Jamaican-born DJ named Kool Herc used these ideas at a party in the Bronx, New York, and helped start hip-hop.

## Bob Marley

Bob Marley grew up poor in Trench Town, a neighbourhood of Kingston. With the Wailers, he wrote songs such as "One Love", "Three Little Birds", "Get Up, Stand Up" and "Redemption Song". His music mixed [catchy tunes](behind-theme-music) with messages about freedom, equality and hope.

Marley toured the world, and in 1980 he sang at the celebrations for Zimbabwe's independence. He died of cancer in 1981, aged 36. His album *Legend*, released after his death, is one of the best-selling albums of all time.

## Faith and roots

Many reggae musicians follow Rastafari, a faith that began in Jamaica in the 1930s. Rastafari teaches pride in African heritage and honours Haile Selassie, who was emperor of [Ethiopia](ethiopia), as a holy figure. That is why the red, gold and green of Ethiopia's flag appear so often on reggae posters and clothes. Not every reggae artist is Rastafarian, and the music has many different messages.

## Jamaica, India and the world

Jamaica's people have many roots. Most Jamaicans are descended from Africans brought to the island in [slavery](slavery-freedom). From 1845, thousands of workers from India also arrived to work on plantations, and Indo-Jamaicans are still part of the island's mix. Jamaican curry goat is one of their legacies.

Reggae has travelled everywhere. There are reggae bands in Japan, Brazil, [Nigeria](nigeria) and Britain. In India, reggae and dub groups play at clubs and [music festivals](festivals-music), and Bob Marley songs are familiar on college campuses across the country.
