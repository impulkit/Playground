## The short answer

Lata Mangeshkar was one of India's greatest singers. For about seven decades, from the late 1940s, she sang thousands of songs for Hindi films and in more than 30 languages. Her clear, high, sweet voice became the sound of countless heroines on screen.

She was a playback singer, which means her songs were [recorded in studios](behind-music-recording) and actors moved their lips to them on screen. People called her the "Nightingale of India". In 2001, she received the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour. When she died in 2022, aged 92, the government declared two days of national mourning.

## A childhood cut short

Lata was born in 1929 in Indore, in what is now [Madhya Pradesh](madhya-pradesh). Her father, Deenanath Mangeshkar, was a well-known classical singer and actor in [Marathi theatre](marathi-gujarati). He began teaching her music when she was very young.

In 1942, when she was about 13, her father died. As the eldest of five children, Lata had to support the family. She acted in small roles and sang in Marathi films. Her sisters Asha, Usha and Meena and her brother Hridaynath all became musicians too.

## Too thin, then everywhere

The family moved to Bombay, now [Mumbai](mumbai). Lata's voice was high and light, very different from the heavier voices then popular in films. One producer is said to have rejected her for being too thin.

The composer Ghulam Haider believed in her. Then, in 1949, she sang *Aayega Aanewala* in the film *Mahal*. The haunting song became a massive hit, and studios began asking for her. Through the 1950s and 1960s, she sang for almost every major [composer](film-music-composers) in Hindi films.

## A song that moved a nation

In January 1963, a few months after India's war with China, Lata sang *Ae Mere Watan Ke Logon* at a public event in Delhi. The song, written by the poet Pradeep, honoured soldiers who had died.

Prime Minister [Jawaharlal Nehru](nehru) was in the audience and was reportedly [moved to tears](body-music-chills). The song is still sung on Republic Day and Independence Day across India.

## The secret of her voice

Lata's voice was famous for staying pure and steady even on very high notes. She could sing softly and still be heard clearly, which suited film [microphones](microphones). She sang with careful pronunciation of Hindi and Urdu words, so every line was easy to follow. Learn how voices reach high notes in [singing](singing).

She practised daily and protected her voice. She also had a strong sense of what was fair to singers. In the 1960s, she argued that playback singers should receive a share of royalties from record sales, which led to a long disagreement with some composers and fellow singers.

## A long shadow

For decades, Lata and her sister Asha Bhosle sang a huge share of the female songs in Hindi films. Some critics and singers felt that their dominance made it hard for other women to get chances. Others say they simply had the voices composers wanted.

She kept singing into her eighties. Composers from S.D. Burman to [A.R. Rahman](ar-rahman) worked with her. Today, songs she recorded more than seventy years ago are still played at weddings, on radios and on streaming apps around the world.
