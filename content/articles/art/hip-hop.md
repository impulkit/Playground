## The short answer

Hip-hop began in the Bronx, a part of New York City, in the early 1970s. Young people, mostly African American and Latino, held parties in community rooms, parks and streets. DJs played funk and soul records, and dancers waited for the "break", the short section where the singing stopped and only the drums kept going.

A teenage DJ called Kool Herc started using two turntables to repeat the break over and over. Soon, other performers began talking in rhyme over these beats to hype up the crowd. That rhyming became rap. Dancing, art and [fashion](fashion-history) grew alongside, and together they formed hip-hop.

## A tough time in the Bronx

In the 1970s, many neighbourhoods of the South Bronx were struggling. A huge highway had cut through communities, many families had little money, and fires destroyed thousands of buildings. Clubs were expensive, and young people had few places to go.

So they made their own fun. With borrowed speakers, records and a lot of creativity, they turned schoolyards and apartment-block rooms into dance floors. Hip-hop was born from people making something exciting out of very little, in one of the most famous cities on Earth, [New York](new-york).

## The famous party

On 11 August 1973, Clive Campbell, known as DJ Kool Herc, played at his sister's back-to-school party in the recreation room of 1520 Sedgwick Avenue. He had moved to New York from [Jamaica](reggae) as a boy, where huge outdoor sound systems were common.

That night is now celebrated as hip-hop's birthday, and the 50th anniversary in 2023 was marked with concerts across the USA. Other DJs, such as Grandmaster Flash, invented new tricks. Some cut quickly between records, and others scratched a record back and forth under the needle to make rhythmic sounds.

## Four elements

Hip-hop is often described as having four elements. DJing is creating beats from records. MCing, or rapping, is rhyming over the beat. Breaking is an athletic dance with spins, freezes and footwork, done by b-boys and b-girls.

The fourth element is graffiti, bold painted words and images, often on trains and walls. Painting on property without permission is against the law, but many graffiti artists now create large murals with permission.

## From the Bronx to the world

In 1979, a song called *Rapper's Delight* by the Sugarhill Gang became a hit, and many people heard rap for the first time. Over the next decades, hip-hop became one of the most popular kinds of music in the USA.

Rappers have been praised as poets. In 2018, the rapper Kendrick Lamar won a Pulitzer Prize for Music, the first winner from outside classical music and jazz. Like [poetry](poetry), good rap depends on rhythm, rhyme and clever wordplay. Breaking made its debut as a medal sport at the 2024 Paris [Olympics](olympics).

## Hip-hop in India

Hip-hop reached India through music videos, films and the [internet](internet-how). In the 2010s, young rappers in [Mumbai](mumbai), some from neighbourhoods like [Dharavi](slums-housing), began rapping in Hindi and other Indian languages about their own streets and lives.

The 2019 Hindi film *Gully Boy* was inspired by the stories of real Mumbai rappers. Today, Indian rap is sung in Punjabi, Tamil, [Malayalam](malayalam), Marathi and many other languages. B-boys and b-girls practise breaking in cities from Delhi to [Bengaluru](bengaluru), and some now compete internationally.
