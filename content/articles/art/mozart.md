## The short answer

Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart was an Austrian composer who lived from 1756 to 1791. He was one of the most famous child performers in history, touring Europe from the age of six. As an adult, he wrote music of almost every kind: symphonies, [operas](opera), piano concertos, church music and pieces for small groups.

He wrote more than 600 works in just 35 years. Many are still among the most performed pieces of classical music. People love his music for its clear tunes, its playfulness and the way it can turn suddenly serious or sad. His operas, like *The Magic Flute*, are staged all over the world.

## A child on tour

Mozart was born in Salzburg, in today's Austria. His father, Leopold, was a musician and teacher. Wolfgang and his older sister, Nannerl, were both brilliant at the keyboard. Wolfgang was writing short pieces by the age of five.

Leopold took his children on long tours to show them off at royal courts. Between 1763 and 1766, the family travelled through Germany, [Paris](paris) and [London](london), where Wolfgang met the composer Johann Christian Bach. In London, aged about eight, he wrote his first symphony. Travel in coaches over bumpy roads was slow and tiring, and the children were often ill.

## The famous memory

In 1770, the Mozarts visited Rome. In the [Sistine Chapel](michelangelo), they heard the *Miserere* by Gregorio Allegri, a piece the [Vatican](vatican)'s [choir](choirs) sang only during Holy Week. Copies weren't supposed to leave the chapel.

According to his father's letters, Wolfgang, aged 14, wrote down the whole piece from memory after hearing it, then went back to check. Stories like this made him seem almost magical. Scientists today say such skills come from huge amounts of practice from a very young age. A brain trained this way gets very good at music, as you can read in [how the brain works](brain-works).

## Freelance in Vienna

As a young man, Mozart worked for the Archbishop of Salzburg, but he hated being treated like a servant. In 1781, he left and moved to Vienna to earn his living by teaching, performing and selling his music. This was unusual and risky.

In Vienna, he wrote many of his greatest works, including piano concertos he played himself on the [piano](piano) of his day. His operas *The Marriage of Figaro* and *Don Giovanni* followed. He earned good money at times, but spent freely and often had to borrow.

## The last year

In 1791, his opera *The Magic Flute* was a success in Vienna. It includes the Queen of the Night's fiery song, one of the hardest pieces for a soprano, with notes far above most voices, as explained in [singing](singing).

He was also writing a Requiem, music for the dead, when he fell ill. He died on 5 December 1791, aged 35. The Requiem was finished by one of his students.

## Myths and legends

The 1984 film *Amadeus* showed a rival composer, Antonio Salieri, plotting against Mozart. It is a great story, but historians have found no evidence that Salieri harmed him. The two men knew each other and seem to have been polite rivals.

Another myth says that playing Mozart to babies makes them cleverer. A small study in 1993 sparked the idea, but later research did not find any lasting effect. The young composer [Beethoven](beethoven) may have visited Vienna hoping to meet Mozart in 1787. What happened, if they met, nobody knows for sure.
