## The short answer

Leonardo da Vinci (1452–1519) was an Italian painter, engineer and scientist. He lived during the [Renaissance](renaissance), when artists and thinkers in Italy were rediscovering ancient learning and studying the world with fresh eyes.

He painted the *Mona Lisa* and *The Last Supper*, two of the best-known paintings on Earth. Yet he finished very few paintings. Most of his energy went into notebooks, thousands of pages full of drawings and ideas about birds, water, light, plants, machines and the human body. He wanted to understand how everything worked, and he believed drawing was the best way to find out.

## A boy from Vinci

Leonardo was born near Vinci, a small hill town in Tuscany, in 1452. His parents were not married, which meant he could not follow his father into a respected profession like law. That turned out to be lucky.

As a teenager he was sent to Florence to train in the workshop of Andrea del Verrocchio, a leading artist. There he learned painting, [sculpture](sculpture), metalwork and how to grind colours. A famous story says that when young Leonardo painted an angel in one of Verrocchio's pictures, it was so good that his teacher was astonished.

## Painting slowly

Leonardo painted with great care and often took years. He had studied how light falls on round objects, and he blended shadows so softly that there are no hard outlines. Italians call this *sfumato*, from a word meaning smoke.

*The Last Supper*, painted on a wall in Milan in the 1490s, shows Jesus and his disciples at the moment he says one of them will betray him. Leonardo tried a new method, painting on dry plaster instead of wet. The paint soon began to flake off, and the picture has needed careful repair ever since. It still uses [perspective](perspective) so well that the room seems to continue beyond the wall.

## Notebooks full of everything

Leonardo carried a notebook almost everywhere. Thousands of pages survive, though many more are lost. He wrote right to left in mirror writing, and he was [left-handed](left-handed).

The pages jump from topic to topic. A design for a flying machine sits beside notes on how water swirls. He drew a spinning screw meant to lift into the air, which reminds many people of a [helicopter](helicopters). He planned [bridges](bridges), canals and weapons for the rulers who employed him. Many ideas were never built, and some would not have worked.

## Drawing the inside of the body

Leonardo wanted to know what lay under the skin. Over many years he dissected (carefully cut open) human bodies, mostly of people who had died in hospitals, and drew what he found. His drawings of bones, [muscles](muscles) and the heart were more accurate than anything else of his time.

He studied how the valves of the heart open and close, and drew blood swirling as it flows. Doctors who look at those drawings today are struck by how much he got right. Sadly, they stayed hidden in his notebooks and were not published for centuries, so they did not help medicine in his own day.

## His last years

In 1516 the French king Francis I invited Leonardo to France and gave him a house near the royal castle at Amboise. He took the *Mona Lisa* with him and kept working on it. He died there in 1519.

His notebooks were later split up and scattered. One of them, the Codex Leicester, was bought in 1994 by Bill Gates. The pages still show a mind that never stopped asking questions.
