## The short answer

Kathakali is a dance-drama from [Kerala](kerala). Its name means "story-play". Actors in huge skirts, tall crowns and brightly painted faces act out stories from the [Ramayana](ramayana), the [Mahabharata](mahabharata) and the Puranas. They never speak. Singers at the back of the stage sing the words, and drummers drive the action.

The actors tell the story through *mudras*, a language of hand gestures, and through very detailed facial expressions. They train their eyes, eyebrows, lips and cheeks to move separately, as if each had a mind of its own. The painted make-up also tells you who each character is the moment they appear, before they make a single gesture.

## A face that tells you who's who

[Kathakali make-up](art-kathakali-makeup) follows strict colour codes. Noble heroes, kings and gods have green faces, called *pacha*. Proud, powerful villains, such as Ravana or Duryodhana, have green faces with red knife-shaped marks on the cheeks, called *kathi*.

The most violent or evil characters wear a red beard and a fierce red-and-black face, called *chuvanna thadi*. Hanuman, the wise monkey god, wears a white beard. Female characters and sages have a soft, glowing yellow-orange face, called *minukku*.

Many characters also wear a *chutti*, a white frame along the jaw that makes the face look bigger and stand out by lamplight. It was traditionally built from rice paste and lime, and today is often cut from layers of paper.

## Three hours in the green room

Make-up for the main characters can take three hours or more. The actor lies flat while an expert artist paints the face with colours ground from natural materials, such as minerals and plant dyes, mixed with [coconut](coconut) oil.

Then comes the costume: a wide, layered skirt, a padded jacket, heavy ornaments and a tall crown. Fully dressed, an actor can barely sit down. Some actors put a seed from a plant called the chundapoovu under their eyelids, which reddens the whites of their eyes and makes expressions more striking.

## Eyes that dance

Kathakali training begins in childhood and is famously tough. Students wake before dawn for exercises and oil massage that make their bodies strong and flexible. They practise eye movements daily: rolling, darting and quivering the eyeballs, following a lamp or a finger.

Actors learn 24 basic mudras, from an old text called the *Hastalakshana Deepika*. By combining them, they can "say" hundreds of words. They also master the nine main [emotions](emotions), or *navarasa*, such as love, courage, disgust, fear and wonder, each shown with a precise expression.

## All-night shows

Traditionally, Kathakali was performed outdoors at temples and in courtyards, starting after sunset and ending at dawn. The only light came from a tall brass oil lamp at the front of the stage. Its flickering flame made the painted faces glow.

The music comes from the chenda, a loud cylindrical drum, the maddalam, a barrel drum, a gong called the chengila and small cymbals. Singers chant the lines in Manipravalam, a mix of [Malayalam](malayalam) and Sanskrit.

## Saving the art

By the early 20th century, Kathakali was losing royal patrons. In 1930, the poet Vallathol Narayana Menon founded Kerala Kalamandalam to train new artists. The school soon moved to Cheruthuruthy, on the banks of the Bharathappuzha river. It is still one of the most famous arts schools in India. Kathakali was traditionally performed only by men, but today women train and perform too, and short shows for visitors are staged every evening in [Kochi](kochi).
