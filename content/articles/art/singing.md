## The short answer

Singing starts in your voice box, or larynx, the bump at the front of your throat. Inside are two small bands of tissue called vocal folds. When you breathe out and start to sing, the folds come together and air from your lungs makes them buzz, very fast.

To sing higher, small [muscles](muscles) stretch the folds longer, thinner and tighter. Tight, thin folds vibrate faster, and faster vibrations make a higher note. It works like a rubber band: stretch it more and pluck it, and the note rises. Trained singers learn to control these tiny muscles very finely, while keeping a steady flow of air.

## Air is the fuel

Every sung note needs air, supplied by your [lungs](lungs-breathe). Singers practise breathing low and deep, using the dome-shaped muscle under the lungs, the diaphragm, along with the muscles of the belly and ribs.

The aim is a steady, controlled stream of breath, not a big gust. Long notes need careful saving of air. A singer holding a note for fifteen seconds is like a cyclist pedalling steadily uphill, not sprinting.

## Why voices are different

Adult men usually have longer, thicker vocal folds than women, so their voices are lower. Children's folds are shorter still. During the teenage years, a boy's larynx grows quickly and his folds get longer, so [his voice drops](body-voice-change), sometimes cracking along the way. Girls' voices change too, but less.

The sound from the folds is only a thin buzz. Your throat, mouth and nose shape it into a rich voice, like the body of a guitar shaping a string's sound. Moving your tongue and lips changes the vowels.

## The highest notes

Very high notes need the folds to be stretched tight and to vibrate in only part of their length. A soprano's high C vibrates about 1,000 times a second.

In [Mozart](mozart)'s opera *The Magic Flute*, the Queen of the Night sings a furious song that climbs to an F above that, about 1,400 vibrations a second. Few singers can perform it well. Above the normal range, some singers can reach a thin, piercing sound called the whistle register.

Trained [opera singers](opera) also learn to make a strong ring in their voice, which helps them be heard over a whole [orchestra](orchestra) without a [microphone](microphones).

## Practice, Indian style

Indian classical singers do *riyaz*, daily practice, often early in the morning. One famous [exercise](exercise) is *kharaj* practice: singing very low, steady notes to build breath and control. Singers also practise sliding smoothly between notes.

Playback singers in Hindi films have sung in huge ranges. [Lata Mangeshkar](lata-mangeshkar) was known for a light, clear, high voice that she kept for decades. Singers in Carnatic music practise fast runs of notes called *brigas*, which need very quick, precise control of the vocal folds.

## Looking after a voice

Vocal folds are delicate. Shouting for a long time, or singing hard when you have a cold, can make them sore and swollen, and your voice goes croaky. Most of the time, they recover with rest.

Singers drink water, warm up gently and avoid straining to reach notes. If someone's voice stays hoarse for weeks, it is a good idea to see a doctor. A well-cared-for voice can last a lifetime. Some singers keep performing into their eighties.
