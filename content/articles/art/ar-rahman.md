## The short answer

A.R. Rahman changed Indian film music by making it sound new. In the early 1990s, he used [digital recording](behind-music-recording), electronic keyboards and careful layers of sound in a way few Indian composers had tried. He mixed Carnatic music, [Sufi songs](qawwali), Western pop, [reggae](reggae) and much more.

His first film soundtrack, for the Tamil film *Roja* in 1992, was a huge hit and won a national award. Soon he was writing music for Hindi films too. Over the next twenty years, his songs became some of the best-loved in India. In 2009, he won two Oscars for the film *Slumdog Millionaire*.

## A childhood surrounded by instruments

Rahman was born in 1967 in Madras, now [Chennai](chennai), as A.S. Dileep Kumar. His father, R.K. Shekhar, arranged and conducted music for [Malayalam](malayalam) and Tamil films. Young Dileep sat beside him at the keyboard.

When he was nine, his father died. To earn money, the family rented out his father's instruments. As a teenager, Dileep started working as a keyboard player for film composers, including the famous Ilaiyaraaja, and studied [Western classical music](classical-western). In his early twenties, he and his family embraced Sufi Islam, and he took the name Allah Rakha Rahman.

## The studio at home

Rahman built a recording studio next to his home and called it Panchathan Record Inn. There he composed hundreds of [advertising](advertising) jingles, learning to say [something catchy](behind-theme-music) in thirty seconds.

He worked mostly at night and treated the studio like an instrument. He recorded parts one at a time and layered them, the way a painter adds coats of colour. His method meant he could spend weeks on a single song, adjusting tiny details until it felt right.

## A new sound for Indian cinema

In 1992, the director Mani Ratnam asked him to compose music for *Roja*. The songs sounded fresh and clean, with crisp drums and unusual sounds. They were a huge success, first in Tamil and then dubbed into Hindi.

Hindi films followed: *Rangeela*, *Dil Se*, whose song *Chaiyya Chaiyya* was filmed on top of a moving train, *Taal* and *Lagaan*. His music drew on [Carnatic music](carnatic-hindustani), Sufi singing and folk songs, along with [hip-hop](hip-hop) and [electronic beats](electronic-music). In 1997, for fifty years of India's independence, he released the album *Vande Mataram*, with the song *Maa Tujhe Salaam*.

## Two Oscars in one night

In 2008, the British director Danny Boyle made *Slumdog Millionaire*, set in [Mumbai](mumbai), and asked Rahman to compose the music. At the Academy Awards in 2009, he won Best Original Score and, with the lyricist Gulzar, Best Original Song for *Jai Ho*.

He went on to write music for films and stage shows in Britain and [Hollywood](hollywood). Earlier, he had written the music for *Bombay Dreams*, [a musical](broadway) produced in London by Andrew Lloyd Webber.

## Teaching the next generation

People sometimes call Rahman "the Mozart of Madras", comparing him to [Mozart](mozart) for his gift with melody. He is known for being quiet and shy in interviews, and for thanking God when he wins awards.

In 2008, he founded the KM Music Conservatory in Chennai to train young musicians in both Indian and Western music. Many composers who grew up listening to his songs say he showed them that Indian film music could take ideas from anywhere and still sound Indian. His work is a big part of the story of [Bollywood songs](bollywood-music).
