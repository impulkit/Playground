## The short answer

Every baby dinosaur hatched from an egg. Some, like many of the giant long-necks, seem to have been left to look after themselves from the start. Others, especially the bird-like dinosaurs, had parents that [sat on their nests](wild-penguin-dads), the way birds do now.

Once hatched, dinosaur babies grew fast. [Rings inside their bones](bones-growth) show that a young T. rex went through a huge [growth spurt](body-growth-spurts) in its teenage years. Most dinosaurs reached adult size in their teens or twenties, far quicker than big reptiles today.

## Egg Mountain

In 1978, the owner of a small rock shop in Montana showed some tiny bones to the fossil hunter Jack Horner and his friend Bob Makela. They had come from a nest with the bones of many babies inside. Nearby, the pair later found a hill so full of eggs and nests that it became known as Egg Mountain.

The babies were too big to be newly hatched, so they seem to have stayed in the nest for a while. Horner suggested their parents brought them food. He named the dinosaur Maiasaura, "good mother lizard". Not every scientist agrees the babies were fed, but Maiasaura is now the state fossil of [Montana](states-usa).

## A parent on the nest

In 1923, an expedition in [Mongolia](mongolia) found a toothless dinosaur lying on a clutch of eggs. Scientists assumed it had been caught stealing them and named it Oviraptor, "egg thief". Seventy years later, in the same desert, a close relative was found crouched over a nest of its own eggs, arms spread wide.

It looked exactly like a hen sheltering her chicks. The eggs had been its own all along. Birds sit on their eggs because [birds are dinosaurs](birds-dinosaurs), and this habit is far older than birds themselves.

## Waiting to hatch

Dinosaur eggs were slow. [Baby teeth](teeth) inside fossil eggs have tiny daily growth lines, like ticks on a calendar. Counting them showed that some dinosaur embryos spent around three to six months in the egg. A [hen's egg](eggs) hatches in about three weeks.

Long-necked [sauropods](?sauropod) laid many eggs in pits and covered them with soil and plants. In India, the [titanosaur nests](dino-eggs) of the Narmada valley held several eggs each, and some held more than a dozen. A long wait in an unguarded nest was risky, so laying lots of eggs helped. It is a plan [sea turtles](sea-turtles) still use.

## Growing up fast

Scientists cut thin slices from fossil bones and count the yearly growth rings. In 2004, a study of tyrannosaur bones found that a T. rex grew slowly for its first ten years or so. Then, as a teenager, it gained about 2 kg a day for several years. By about 20 it was fully grown, around the weight of an elephant.

A baby titanosaur from Madagascar, about the size of a big dog, had legs already built for walking long distances. It was probably on its own soon after hatching. Small and alone, baby dinosaurs were easy prey, and many never grew up at all.

## Who looked after whom

Dinosaur families were probably as varied as animal families today. [Crocodiles](croc-ancient), the dinosaurs' living cousins, guard their nests and carry their hatchlings gently in their jaws. Most birds feed their chicks for weeks. One fossil from China shows more than 30 young Psittacosaurus huddled together with one older animal. Some scientists see a babysitter. Others think the group simply died together.
