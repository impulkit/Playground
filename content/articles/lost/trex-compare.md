## The short answer

T. rex was bigger: about 12 m long and about as heavy as an elephant or more. Rajasaurus was about 8–9 m long and much lighter.

They never met. T. rex lived in North America and [Rajasaurus](rajasaurus) in India, which was an island at the time. They weren't close cousins either. Big hunters evolved separately in different parts of the world.

## Side by side

Put the two next to each other and the difference is clear. T. rex was as long as a city bus, with a skull about 1.4 m long. Rajasaurus was as long as two cars parked end to end, with a shorter, deeper skull topped by a rounded crest.

Weight is where the gap is biggest. Studies of complete T. rex skeletons suggest adults weighed about 6 to 8 tonnes, more than an African elephant. Rajasaurus is known only from part of a skeleton, so nobody can weigh it with confidence. Its closest relatives were slimmer than tyrannosaurs, so it was probably a few tonnes at most.

## How scientists estimate size

For T. rex, scientists have several nearly complete skeletons, like Sue in Chicago. John Hutchinson's team made 3D [computer models](computers-how) of them in 2011, adding flesh around the bones, and calculated their weight.

The heaviest T. rex estimate so far comes from a skeleton nicknamed Scotty, found in Saskatchewan, Canada, in 1991. In 2019, scientists suggested Scotty weighed nearly 9 tonnes, though other experts think that figure may be too high.

For Rajasaurus, only some bones were found: parts of the skull, backbone, hips and legs. Scientists compare these with close relatives like Majungasaurus, whose skeletons are more complete, and scale them up or down. It is careful [estimation](estimation), and the answer comes with a range rather than a single number.

## Two families, one job

T. rex belonged to the tyrannosaurs, a family that lived mainly in North America and Asia. Rajasaurus was an abelisaur, a family that lived mainly on the southern continents: South America, Africa, Madagascar and India.

These families had been separate for a very long time. Yet both became the top hunters in their lands, and both had big heads and very short arms. When different families evolve similar bodies for a similar way of life, scientists call it convergent evolution.

## Why they never met

About 67 million years ago, India had broken away from Madagascar and was drifting north through the ocean, as you can read in [India's long journey](india-drift). North America was thousands of kilometres away, across oceans.

So the two lived at the same time, but in different worlds, a bit like [lions and tigers](lions-gir) today. Wild lions live in Africa and in India's Gir forest, while tigers live only in Asia. India is the only country with both, and even there they live in different forests.

## Who was the better hunter?

There is no way to stage a contest, and it would be a strange question to scientists. Each was suited to its own prey and land. T. rex had a bone-crushing bite. Abelisaurs like Rajasaurus had shorter, very strong skulls and may have held on to prey rather than tearing big chunks. Both were at the top of their food chains until [the asteroid](extinction) hit. After that, neither family left any descendants, and the job of top hunter passed to others: giant ground birds and [crocodiles](croc-ancient) in some places, and later mammals.
