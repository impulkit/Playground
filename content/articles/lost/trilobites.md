## The short answer

Trilobites were sea animals with hard, jointed shells and lots of legs. They were arthropods, the huge group that today includes insects, [crabs](crabs) and spiders. They [first appeared about 521 million years ago](cambrian) and lived for around 270 million years, far longer than the dinosaurs.

Most were about the size of a coin or a matchbox. Some were smaller than a grain of rice. The biggest known was about as long as your arm. They crawled on the sea floor, swam in open water or burrowed in mud. Then, about 252 million years ago, the last of them vanished.

## Three lobes

The name trilobite means "three lobes". Look at one from above and its body is split into three strips running from head to tail: a raised middle and two flatter sides. The body was also divided from front to back into a head shield, a segmented middle and a tail shield.

Underneath were many pairs of legs, each with a branch that probably helped with breathing. Their outer skeleton was strengthened with calcite, the same mineral found in limestone and chalk. That is one reason they fossilised so well.

## Eyes of stone

Trilobites had some of the earliest complex eyes we know of. Like an insect's eye, many trilobite eyes were made of lots of tiny lenses packed together. The lenses were clear crystals of calcite.

A crystal of calcite usually splits light into two images. Trilobite lenses were lined up in a special direction that avoided this, so they saw clearly. Some kinds had huge eyes that wrapped round the head. Others, living in deep, dark water, had no eyes at all. Compare that with how [your own eyes](eyes-see) work with soft, living lenses.

## Rolling up

Trilobites were hunted by sea scorpions, early fish and other predators. Many could roll themselves into a tight ball, tucking their soft underside inside their shell, as a pill bug does. Lots of fossils are found rolled up like this, as if they were frightened at the moment they were buried.

Like crabs, trilobites moulted to grow. They split their old shell and crawled out of it. So a single animal could leave several fossils behind, and many trilobite fossils are discarded jackets rather than bodies.

## Where they are found

Trilobites have been found on every continent. Rocks in Utah are so full of one small kind that its fossils are sold in shops around the world. The trilobite Isotelus is the state fossil of Ohio, and one huge kind found in Canada was about 70 cm long. Also in Canada, in the Burgess Shale high in the [Rocky Mountains](rocky-mountains), some trilobites were buried so gently that their legs and feelers were preserved too. Soft parts like these almost never fossilise, so these rare finds showed scientists what the underside of a trilobite really looked like.

In India, trilobites are found in the [Spiti valley](spiti) of [Himachal Pradesh](himachal) and in [Kashmir](kashmir), in rocks over 500 million years old. These rocks formed on an ancient sea floor and were later lifted up when India pushed into Asia and the [Himalayas](himalaya-form) rose.

## The end

Trilobites were already less common when the [Great Dying](great-dying) struck, 252 million years ago. That disaster killed off the last few kinds. Later seas filled with other shelled animals, such as [ammonites](ammonites), but nothing quite like a trilobite ever crawled the sea floor again.
