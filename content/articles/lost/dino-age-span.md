## The short answer

Dinosaurs ruled for about 165 million years. Humans have been around for about 300,000 years.

Even among dinosaurs, there were huge gaps. [Stegosaurus](stegosaurus) lived about 85 million years before T. rex. That's why dinosaurs from different times never met. When we say "the age of dinosaurs", we mean a stretch of time over 500 times longer than humans have existed.

## Three chapters

Scientists split the age of dinosaurs into three periods. The Triassic ran from about 252 to 201 million years ago. The Jurassic came next, until 145 million years ago. The Cretaceous ended 66 million years ago, when [the asteroid](extinction) struck.

The first dinosaurs appeared in the Triassic, about 230 million years ago. Fossils like Eoraptor and Herrerasaurus from [Argentina](argentina) show small, quick, two-legged animals. At first they shared the land with many other reptiles, some bigger and fiercer than they were. The world had only just recovered from [the Great Dying](great-dying), the worst extinction ever.

## Taking over

Around 201 million years ago, another mass extinction wiped out many of the dinosaurs' rivals. Dinosaurs came through and spread everywhere. During the Jurassic, giant long-necked sauropods like [Barapasaurus](barapasaurus) in India grew enormous, and Stegosaurus roamed North America.

The Cretaceous brought new kinds: horned dinosaurs, duck-billed dinosaurs, armoured ankylosaurs and the great tyrannosaurs. Flowering plants appeared and spread during this time. Many dinosaurs we know best, like T. rex and [Triceratops](triceratops-horns), lived only in the last few million years of it.

## Squeezing time into a day

Big numbers are slippery. A [million years](big-numbers) is already far longer than all of written history, which goes back only about 5,000 years. The age of dinosaurs is 165 of those millions.

Here is one way to picture it. Squeeze the whole dinosaur age into a single day. Each hour stands for nearly 7 million years. Stegosaurus appears around midday. T. rex arrives in the last half-hour before midnight. Modern humans, if they were on the clock, would take up less than three minutes.

## How we know the dates

Fossils do not come with labels, so how can anyone say "66 million years"? The answer is in volcanic ash. When a volcano erupts, its ash settles in layers and contains tiny crystals. Some of those crystals hold [uranium](accident-radioactivity), which slowly turns into lead at a steady, known rate, a process explained in [half-life](half-life).

By measuring how much uranium has turned into lead, scientists can tell when the crystal formed. A fossil found between two ash layers must be younger than the one below and older than the one above. Thousands of these measurements, checked against each other around the world, give the dates for each period.

## India's dinosaur timeline

India's own dinosaurs span almost the whole age. Barapasaurus walked beside the Godavari about 190 million years ago, early in the Jurassic. More than 120 million years later, [Rajasaurus](rajasaurus) hunted in Gujarat, near the very end. In between, India split from Africa and Antarctica and began its long drift north as an island.

## After the dinosaurs

When the asteroid struck 66 million years ago, the giant dinosaurs vanished. Birds, which are dinosaurs too, survived. With the big reptiles gone, [mammals took over](mammals-rise), slowly growing larger and more varied. Our own ancestors came very late in that story. The oldest known [stone tools](stone-age) are about 3.3 million years old, and modern humans appeared about 300,000 years ago.
