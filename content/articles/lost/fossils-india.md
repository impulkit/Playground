## The short answer

India is one of the best places in the world to find fossils, because its rocks record a long and unusual journey. Over the last 150 million years or so, [India drifted](india-drift) north across an ocean as an island, then crashed into Asia and pushed up the Himalaya. Each chapter left fossils behind.

The most famous sites include dinosaur egg and bone beds at Raiyoli in Gujarat and around Dhar in Madhya Pradesh, and Jurassic dinosaurs in the Pranhita–Godavari valley. There are also early whales and mammals in Kutch, fossil forests in Rajasthan and Tamil Nadu, and sea shells high in the Himalaya. Many are protected as National Geological Monuments.

## Dinosaur nurseries

In 1981, geologists from the Geological Survey of India were working at a quarry near the village of Raiyoli, in the Balasinor area of Gujarat. They found dinosaur bones and eggs, and the area turned out to be one of the richest dinosaur egg sites anywhere. Bones from Raiyoli later helped describe [Rajasaurus](rajasaurus), a meat-eater with a small horn on its head.

At a nearby site, scientists found a snake fossil curled around a crushed dinosaur egg, next to the bones of a hatchling. The snake, named *Sanajeh*, seems to have been raiding a titanosaur nest when it was suddenly buried by sliding mud and sand. Further east, in Dhar district of Madhya Pradesh, a 2023 study described 92 nesting sites with 256 eggs, some still lying in their clutches.

## Older giants

Much older dinosaurs lie in the Pranhita–Godavari valley, where Telangana and Maharashtra meet. Here scientists dug up [Barapasaurus](barapasaurus), a long-necked plant-eater that lived about 190 million years ago in the early Jurassic. Its name means "big-legged lizard", and it is one of the earliest sauropods known from a large number of bones, dug from the remains of several animals.

## Whales, horses and amber

In Kutch, in western Gujarat, rocks about 40 to 50 million years old hold fossils of early whales that still had legs. They show how [whales walked](whales-walked) on land before returning to the sea. Other early whales of that age are known from Pakistan, then part of the same coastline.

In the Vastan lignite mine near Surat, scientists found amber about 54 million years old, with insects and spiders trapped inside. The same mine yielded *Cambaytherium*, a small animal related to the ancestors of horses and rhinos, suggesting that this group may have started in India.

## Mountains full of shells

Spiti, in Himachal Pradesh, has rocks packed with [ammonites](ammonites), coiled sea shells from creatures related to squid. They lived in the Tethys Ocean, which once lay between India and Asia. When the [Himalaya rose](himalaya-form), the sea floor was pushed high into the sky. In Nepal, smooth black ammonite fossils from the Gandaki river are revered as *shaligrams*.

Along the foothills of the Himalaya, the Siwalik Hills have yielded fossil elephants, rhinos, apes and *Sivatherium*, a giant relative of the giraffe named after Shiva, described in the 1830s.

## Forests of stone

At Akal near [Jaisalmer](india-wonder-jaisalmer), logs of trees turned to stone lie scattered on the desert ground. They are around 180 million years old, from a time when the Thar was a green coast. At Tiruvakkarai in Tamil Nadu, large fossil tree trunks lie in a protected park. Around Ariyalur, also in Tamil Nadu, Cretaceous sea fossils weather out of the rocks.

Many sites face threats from quarrying and illegal fossil selling. Scientists keep asking for stronger laws, because once a fossil bed is dug up for building stone, the record it held is gone for good.
