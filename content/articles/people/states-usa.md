## The short answer

The United States has 50 states because it grew, step by step, [from 13 colonies](usa-statehood) on the Atlantic coast to a country stretching across a continent and into the Pacific. Each time new land was settled and organised, it could ask to join as a state.

Each state has its own constitution, governor, lawmakers and courts. Under the [US Constitution](us-constitution), powers not given to the national government belong to the states or the people. So states make many of their own rules, from driving ages to school calendars.

## Thirteen to start

In 1776, 13 British colonies [declared independence](usa-wonder-philadelphia) and fought the [American Revolution](american-revolution). After winning, they at first worked together loosely, almost like separate countries. In 1787 they wrote a new Constitution to bind themselves into one nation. Delaware approved it first, on 7 December 1787, so it calls itself "the First State".

## Growing west

In 1803, the United States bought a huge area called Louisiana from France. The Louisiana Purchase roughly doubled the size of the country. Later came Florida, [Texas](texas), land in the west after a war with Mexico, and the Oregon Territory in the northwest. Settlers moved in, and new states were formed one after another.

This growth had a painful side. The land was already home to many Native American nations. Through wars, broken treaties and forced removals, most were pushed off their land as the states spread west.

## Joining up

A new state usually began as a territory run by the national government. When enough people lived there, it wrote a constitution and asked [Congress](congress-usa) to admit it. Arguments over [slavery](us-civil-war) made this very tense in the 1800s, because each new state could tip the balance in the Senate.

[Arizona](grand-canyon-state) joined in 1912, completing the 48 states that touch one another. Then came two that do not touch the rest at all. [Alaska](alaska), bought from Russia in 1867 for 7.2 million dollars, became a state in January 1959. [Hawaii](hawaii-islands), a chain of Pacific islands once ruled by its own monarchs, followed in August 1959.

## A flag that keeps up

Every time a state joins, the flag gains a star. The 50-star flag came into use on 4 July 1960. Robert Heft, then a high school student in Ohio, said he designed it for a class project and first got a B minus. His design was later chosen, and his teacher changed the grade.

## Big, small and not states

Alaska is the largest state, more than twice the size of Texas. Rhode Island is the smallest, smaller than many Indian districts. California has the most people, about 4 crore.

Because states make so many of their own laws, life can differ a lot across a border. The age for a driving licence, the sales tax on a toy, and even the date school starts can change when you cross from one state into the next. Each state also has its own nicknames and symbols. Texas calls itself the Lone Star State, and Hawaii is the Aloha State.

Some places are part of the United States but are not states. Washington, D.C. is a federal district created to be the capital. [Puerto Rico](puerto-rico) and Guam are territories. People there are US citizens but do not have voting members in Congress. India took a different route, redrawing its map from inside rather than adding new land, as you can read in [Why is India divided into states?](states-india).
