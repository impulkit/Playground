## The short answer

News reaches us through a chain of people. A [reporter](journalism) sees something happen or talks to people who did. An editor checks the story, asks questions and decides how important it is. Then it is published in a newspaper, broadcast on radio or TV, or posted online, where we read, watch or hear it.

Big news organisations also rely on news agencies, which have reporters in many countries and sell their stories to hundreds of newspapers and channels. Today, a lot of news also reaches us through phones and social media, often shared by friends, which brings new speed and new problems.

## From town criers to printing

For most of history, news travelled by word of mouth, by messengers on horseback or by town criers reading announcements in the street. Rulers in ancient Rome posted daily notices in public places. After the [printing press](printing-press) spread in Europe in the 1400s, printed news sheets became possible, and the first regular newspapers appeared in the 1600s.

## India's first papers

India's first newspaper was *Hicky's Bengal Gazette*, started in Calcutta, now [Kolkata](kolkata), by an Irishman named James Augustus Hicky in 1780. It was full of gossip and sharp attacks on officials of the [East India Company](east-india-company). Hicky was sued and jailed, and the paper was shut down within two years.

Many more papers followed. *The Bombay Times*, founded in 1838, later became *The Times of India*. During the [freedom struggle](freedom-struggle), leaders such as Gandhi, Tilak and Ambedkar ran their own papers to spread their ideas. Today India has more registered publications than almost any other country, in dozens of languages.

## Wires and agencies

The [telegraph](telegraph) made news fast. From the 1840s, messages could travel along wires in minutes rather than days. News agencies grew up to use it. The Associated Press began in New York in 1846. In 1850, Paul Julius Reuter used [pigeons](pigeons-homing) to bridge a gap in the telegraph line between Aachen and Brussels, then founded Reuters in London in 1851. India's own agency, the Press Trust of India, began in 1947.

## Radio, TV and the internet

Radio brought news into homes in the 1920s and 30s. All India Radio got its name in 1936. [Television](television-invented) followed: [Doordarshan](doordarshan-tech) began in Delhi in 1959. For decades, many Indians got national news from just one TV channel. Today there are hundreds of news channels, and millions read news on phones.

## Checking the facts

News organisations have to decide what counts as news. Editors look for stories that are new, important to many people, close to home, or surprising. That means some events get a lot of attention while others are ignored. Reading news from several sources helps you see a fuller picture.

Some countries protect the press by law. The First Amendment to the [US Constitution](us-constitution) protects freedom of the press, and India's Constitution protects it as part of [freedom of speech](free-speech).

[Good journalists](jobs-journalist-day) try to check facts before publishing. They look for more than one source, ask people on different sides for their views, and correct mistakes openly. Newspapers have editors whose job is to catch errors.

Social media lets anyone publish anything instantly. That can spread important news from places reporters cannot reach. It also spreads rumours, edited photos and [fake news](fake-news). A useful habit is to ask three questions: who made this, how do they know, and do other trusted sources report it too?
