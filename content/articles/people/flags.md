## The short answer

A flag is a quick way to say "this belongs to us" or "we are here". A piece of coloured cloth can be seen from far away, it is easy to make, and anyone can recognise it without reading a word.

Flags began as markers on battlefields and ships, so soldiers and sailors could tell friend from enemy. Over time, countries adopted national flags to stand for the whole nation. Today a flag flies over government buildings, at sports events, on spacecraft and at the United Nations. People stand for it, cheer for it and sometimes argue about how it should be treated.

## From battle to nation

Ancient armies carried standards, poles topped with banners or symbols, and Roman legions carried a metal eagle. Ships flew flags to signal which country they belonged to. National flags, the kind that stand for a whole people, became common only in the last few hundred years. Denmark's red flag with a white cross is often called the oldest national flag still in use, and a legend dates it to 1219.

The study of flags is called vexillology, from the Latin word for a banner.

## India's tricolour

India's flag has three equal bands: saffron at the top, white in the middle and green at the bottom. In the centre is a navy-blue wheel with 24 spokes, the [Ashoka](ashoka) Chakra, taken from a stone pillar carved for Emperor Ashoka over 2,000 years ago. The [Constituent Assembly](constituent-assembly) adopted it on 22 July 1947, a few weeks before independence. It grew out of earlier flags of the freedom movement, including a design by Pingali Venkayya.

For decades the flag had to be made of [khadi](khadi), hand-spun cloth linked with Gandhi and the [freedom struggle](freedom-struggle). At the end of 2021, the rules changed to allow machine-made and polyester flags, so more people could fly one.

## Who may fly it

For years, private citizens in India could fly the flag only on certain national days. Industrialist Naveen Jindal challenged this after being told to take down a flag at his factory. In 2004, the Supreme Court ruled that flying the flag with respect is part of a citizen's freedom of expression. The Flag Code of India, 2002, now lets people fly it on any day, as long as they treat it with dignity.

## Stars and stripes

The US flag has 13 red and white stripes for the original 13 colonies and [50 white stars](usa-statehood) on a blue square, one for each state. A star is added whenever a state joins, so the flag has changed 27 times. The current version has flown since 1960. The US celebrates Flag Day each year on 14 June.

## Flags far from home

In 1969, the [Apollo 11](moon-landing-sites) astronauts planted a US flag on the [Moon](apollo-moon). Because there is no air there, a metal rod along the top held it out so it looked like it was waving. Flags have also been carried to the top of [Mount Everest](everest-first) and painted on spacecraft, including India's lunar landers.

## Simple shapes

Most flags use only a few colours, and many are symmetrical, the same on the left as on the right. That makes them easy to sew, easy to see and easy to draw. If you look through the [flags of the world](flags-world), you will find red is one of the most common colours, and stripes and crosses appear again and again.
