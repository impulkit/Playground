## The short answer

India is far too large and varied to run from one office in Delhi. So it is divided into 28 states, each with its own elected government, plus 8 union territories that are governed more directly from the centre.

This is called a [federal](?federal) system. The [Constitution](constitution) shares out the work. The central government handles things like defence, railways, currency and foreign affairs. States handle police, farming, public health and much of education. Some subjects, like forests, are shared by both.

## A patchwork at independence

In 1947, India was a jumble. There were provinces ruled directly by the British, and more than 560 [princely states](princely-states) ruled by maharajas, nawabs and nizams. Some were as large as a European country, and some were just a few villages.

Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel and the civil servant V. P. Menon persuaded almost all of them to join India, sometimes with long negotiation and occasionally with force. By 1950 the country had a new map, but it was drawn mostly along old colonial lines, not around the people who lived there.

## The language question

Many Indians wanted states that matched the language they spoke. Telugu speakers in the Madras region campaigned hard for their own state. In 1952, Potti Sriramulu began a fast to demand it. He died after 58 days, and the protests that followed pushed the government to create Andhra State in 1953.

A commission then studied the whole country. The States Reorganisation Act of 1956 redrew much of the map so that states roughly followed [languages](languages-india). [Kerala](kerala) brought together Malayalam speakers, and Mysore State, later renamed Karnataka, united Kannada speakers.

## The map keeps changing

Language was not the only reason for new states. In 1960, Bombay State split into Maharashtra and Gujarat. Several states in the northeast were formed to give tribal communities their own governments. In 2000, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh and [Uttarakhand](uttarakhand) were created, partly so that people in hilly or forested regions far from their old state capitals would be governed closer to home. Telangana, formed in 2014, is the newest.

Union territories have changed too. In 2019, Jammu and Kashmir became a union territory, and [Ladakh](ladakh) became a separate one.

## Big and small

States vary hugely. Rajasthan is the biggest by area, almost as large as Germany. Uttar Pradesh has the most people, around 20 crore at the 2011 census, more than most countries. Goa is the smallest by area, and [Sikkim](sikkim) has the fewest people.

Each state has a Chief Minister, an elected assembly and a Governor appointed by the President. Most states have a single assembly, but a few, such as Maharashtra, Karnataka and Bihar, also have an upper house called a legislative council. States make their own laws on subjects in the State List. They set up their own school boards and run their own bus corporations. That is why a bus ticket or a school textbook can look quite different from one state to the next. Below the state, power is handed down further, to districts and to village councils called [panchayats](panchayat).

## Unity in difference

Some people at independence worried that states based on language would pull India apart. Others argued that people would feel more at home in a state that spoke their tongue. More than 60 years later, India has stayed together. Compare its story with [the USA's 50 states](states-usa), which grew mostly by adding land rather than by redrawing it.
