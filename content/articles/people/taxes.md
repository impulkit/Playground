## The short answer

Taxes are money that people and businesses pay to the government. The government uses it to pay for things that everyone shares and that would be hard to buy alone: roads, schools, hospitals, police, the army, clean water, streetlights and space programmes.

You could not easily buy your own highway or hire your own fire brigade. When crores of people each pay a share, the country can afford them together. Taxes also help pay for [pensions](pensions-old-age) for the elderly, free school meals and help after floods and earthquakes.

## Taxes you see and taxes you don't

Some taxes are paid directly. Income tax is taken from what people earn, and richer people usually pay a bigger share of their income. Companies pay tax on their profits.

Other taxes are hidden inside prices. India's Goods and Services Tax, or GST, began on 1 July 2017. It replaced a tangle of separate state and central taxes with one system. Different goods carry different rates, shown as [percentages](percentages). In India GST is included in the price you see. In most of the United States, sales tax is added at the till, and it varies by state. A few states, such as Oregon and Delaware, have no statewide sales tax at all.

## Taxes that changed history

People have argued about taxes for thousands of years, and some arguments became revolutions. In 1773, American colonists angry at a British tax on tea threw crates of it into [Boston Harbour](boston). Their slogan was "no taxation without representation": they would not pay taxes set by a parliament they could not vote for. Within a few years the [American Revolution](american-revolution) had begun.

In 1930, [Mahatma Gandhi](gandhi) led thousands of people on a walk of about 385 km to the sea at Dandi to make salt, breaking a British law that taxed it. Salt was something even the poorest family needed, so the tax touched everyone.

## Strange taxes

In 1696, England introduced a window tax. Houses with more windows paid more, on the idea that bigger houses had more windows. Many owners simply bricked some up. Doctors later complained that dark, airless homes made people ill, and the tax was abolished in 1851.

## Where the money goes

Every February, India's Finance Minister presents the Union Budget in Parliament. It lists how much the government expects to collect and how it plans to spend it, on things like defence, railways, farming, health and [roads](roads-built). A [budget](budget-government) works much like a family's monthly plan, just with far more zeros. You can learn how to make one in [budgets](budgets).

India's modern income tax began in 1860, when the British introduced it to pay debts after the [revolt of 1857](revolt-1857). In the United States, a federal income tax became permanent after the 16th Amendment to the Constitution in 1913. Americans file their returns each spring, usually by 15 April.

## Fair shares

Nobody enjoys paying taxes, and people disagree strongly about how much tax is fair and what it should be spent on. Some want lower taxes and a smaller government. Others want higher taxes to pay for more public services. Those disagreements are a big part of what elections are about. Most people do agree on one thing: a country where nobody paid tax would have no schools, roads or hospitals for anyone.
