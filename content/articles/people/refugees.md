## The short answer

A refugee is someone who has had to flee their own country because they are in danger there, from war, violence or [persecution](?persecution). Persecution means being hunted, jailed or hurt because of your religion, ethnic group, politics or who you are.

Refugees are different from migrants who choose to move for work or study. A refugee leaves because staying could cost them their life or freedom. People who flee but stay inside their own country are called internally displaced.

The [United Nations](united-nations) says more than 10 crore (100 million) people around the world have been forced from their homes. A world treaty from 1951 protects refugees, and a UN agency called UNHCR helps them.

## The promise of 1951

After the [Second World War](world-war-two), millions of people across Europe had no home to go back to. In 1950, the United Nations set up its refugee agency, UNHCR. The next year, countries agreed the Refugee Convention.

Its most important rule is that a refugee must not be sent back to a country where they face serious danger. Countries that joined it, including the USA, promise to give refugees fair treatment, such as the right to go to court and send children to school. The USA joined in 1968, through a 1967 update to the treaty. UNHCR has won the [Nobel Peace Prize](nobel-peace) twice for its work.

## India's long history of sheltering people

India has not signed the 1951 Convention, but it has taken in huge numbers of people. During [Partition](partition) in 1947, about 1.5 crore people crossed the new borders between [India and Pakistan](pakistan-history), in both directions. Many arrived with nothing.

In 1959, the Dalai Lama fled Tibet and came to India, followed by thousands of Tibetans. Their community at McLeod Ganj, above Dharamshala in [Himachal Pradesh](himachal), is still home to many of them. In 1971, during the war that created [Bangladesh](bangladesh), about a crore people fled into India.

## Famous refugees

Some of the world's best-known people were once refugees. [Albert Einstein](einstein) left Germany when the [Nazis](holocaust) came to power in 1933. The writer [Anne Frank](anne-frank)'s family fled Germany for the Netherlands. The former US Secretary of State Madeleine Albright arrived in America as a child refugee from Czechoslovakia.

These names are famous. Most refugees are not, but they too bring skills, food, languages and stories to the places that take them in.

## Life after fleeing

Some refugees live in camps for years, in tents or simple shelters, waiting for peace. Others rent rooms in cities and look for work. Children may miss years of school, which is why aid groups set up classrooms in camps.

In the end, refugees usually hope for one of three things: to go home safely, to settle in the country where they first sheltered, or to be welcomed in a third country. The USA has resettled millions of refugees since 1980, when it passed a law setting out how.

## Asking for safety

A person who arrives in a new country and asks to be protected as a refugee is called an asylum seeker. Officials then check their story, which can take months or even years. If they are accepted, they are given refugee status and allowed to stay.

Countries argue a lot about how many people to accept and how fast to decide. Those debates are often in the news. The 1951 Convention sets the basic rules, but each country runs its own system.

## A team without a flag

In 2016, ten refugee athletes competed at the Rio Olympics under the Olympic flag. The Refugee Olympic Team has returned at every Games since. Every year on 20 June, World Refugee Day honours the courage of people who had to start again somewhere new.
