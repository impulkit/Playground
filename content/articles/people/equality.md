## The short answer

Equality is the idea that every person has the same worth and should have the same basic rights and chances, whatever their religion, [caste](caste-history), race, gender, wealth or disability. Nobody is born more important than anyone else.

In practice it has several parts. Equality before the law means the same rules apply to a minister and [a street vendor](people-street-vendors). Equality of opportunity means everyone gets a fair chance at school, jobs and public life. And many people add a third idea: that a fair society should help those who started further behind.

## Written into the rulebook

India's Constitution makes equality a Fundamental [Right](rights-duties). Article 14 promises everyone equality before the law. Article 15 bans discrimination by religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth. Article 17 abolished untouchability, the old practice of treating some castes as "impure" and keeping them away from temples, wells and schools.

In the United States, the 14th Amendment of 1868 promised "equal protection of the laws". For almost a century, though, many states still kept Black and white Americans apart in schools, buses and restaurants.

## Water, a school and a bus seat

In 1927, B. R. [Ambedkar](ambedkar) led thousands of people to the Chavdar Tale, a public water tank in Mahad, Maharashtra. People of so-called "untouchable" castes were forbidden to drink from it. They drank anyway, claiming a right that was theirs by law. The Mahad Satyagraha became a landmark in the fight against caste discrimination.

In 1954, the US Supreme Court ruled in Brown v. Board of Education that separate schools for Black and white children were unequal and against the Constitution. In 1960, six-year-old [Ruby Bridges](usa-ruby-bridges) became the first Black child at William Frantz Elementary School in New Orleans. Angry crowds shouted at her, and marshals walked her to class. The [civil rights movement](civil-rights) that followed changed American law.

## Votes for all

For most of history, women could not vote. [New Zealand](new-zealand) gave women the vote nationally in 1893, the United States in 1920, and Britain on equal terms with men only in 1928. India's Constitution gave every adult the vote from 1950, so its first general election, in 1951–52, included women from the start.

## Same or fair?

People often disagree about how to reach equality. Some say the fairest thing is to treat everyone the same and let them compete. Others say that groups held back for centuries need extra help to catch up, like reserved seats in colleges or jobs. India's system of [reservations](reservation-india) for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and other backward classes comes from this second view. Supporters say it has opened doors for millions. Critics argue about who should benefit and for how long. Both sides usually agree the goal is the same.

## Equality is still unfinished

Women around the world still earn less on average than men. People with disabilities often find buildings, buses and websites built without them in mind, as you can read in [disabilities and inclusion](disabilities-inclusion). In 2018, a law came into force in Iceland requiring larger companies to prove they pay men and women equally for the same work. Each generation finds new places where the promise of equality has not yet reached, and new people willing to push for it.
