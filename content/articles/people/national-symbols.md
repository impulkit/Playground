## The short answer

National symbols are pictures, animals, plants and songs that a country picks to stand for itself. They appear on coins, passports, government buildings and school textbooks, and they help people feel they belong to one nation.

India's main official symbols include the national flag and the national emblem, the Lion Capital of Ashoka. There is also a national anthem, a national song and a national calendar, and animals such as the tiger and the peacock. Each was chosen for a reason, and most have long roots in Indian history and nature.

## Four lions, three visible

The national emblem comes from a stone pillar top carved more than 2,200 years ago for the emperor [Ashoka](ashoka) at Sarnath, near [Varanasi](varanasi), where the Buddha gave his first sermon. Four lions sit back to back, facing the four directions. Below them, on a round base, are carvings of an elephant, a bull, a horse and a lion, separated by wheels.

In the flat version printed on coins and documents, only three lions show. On the base, a wheel sits in the middle with a bull on one side and a horse on the other. The words *Satyameva Jayate*, "Truth alone triumphs", are written below in Devanagari script. India adopted the emblem on 26 January 1950, the day [the Constitution](constitution) began. The original carving is kept in the Sarnath Museum.

## Animals and plants

The tiger became the national animal in 1973, the year India launched Project Tiger to save the big cats from vanishing. It replaced the lion, which had held the title before. You can find out why every tiger's coat is different in [tiger stripes](tiger-stripes).

The Indian peafowl, usually called the [peacock](peacock-tail), became the national bird in 1963. It is protected by law. In 2009, the Ganges river dolphin, a nearly blind dolphin that finds food by sound, was named the national aquatic animal. The elephant was declared the national heritage animal in 2010.

The lotus is widely recognised as the national flower, and the banyan as the national tree, with its roots dropping from branches to form new trunks.

## Songs and a calendar

*Jana Gana Mana*, written by Rabindranath [Tagore](tagore), is the national anthem. *Vande Mataram*, written by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay and published in his 1882 novel *Anandamath*, is the national song and was a rallying cry of the freedom movement. Both have equal honour.

In 1957, India also adopted a national calendar based on the Saka era. Government gazettes and All India Radio use its dates alongside the usual Gregorian ones.

## A new symbol for the rupee

In 2010, India chose a symbol for [its currency](life-money-world), ₹, after a public competition. It was designed by D. Udaya Kumar and blends the Devanagari letter "ra" with the Roman letter "R". A line across the top echoes the flag.

## Official or not

Some things on popular lists are not official at all. The best-known example is the claim that [hockey](hockey-india) is the national game. India's eight Olympic [hockey](dhyan-chand) gold medals made it feel like one, but the government has said no sport holds that title. The [mango](mango-tree) is often called the national fruit for similar reasons. When a list of national symbols seems very long, it is worth checking which ones a government actually adopted.
