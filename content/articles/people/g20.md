## The short answer

The G20, or Group of Twenty, is a club of the world's biggest economies. Its members are 19 countries, including India, the USA, China, Brazil, Japan and Saudi Arabia, plus the European Union and, since 2023, the African Union. Together they make up about 85% of the world economy and about two-thirds of its people.

Their leaders meet once a year at a summit to discuss problems no country can fix alone, such as financial crises, trade, climate change, debt and health. The G20 can't pass laws. It works by getting powerful countries to agree on shared goals and then nudging each other to follow through.

## Born from a crisis

In 1997 and 1998, a financial crisis spread across Asia, sinking currencies and businesses from [Thailand](thailand) to [South Korea](seoul). Rich countries realised that big emerging economies, like India, China and Brazil, needed to be part of the conversation.

So in 1999, finance ministers and central bank heads from 19 countries and the EU began meeting as the G20. For years these meetings were quiet and technical. Then came the global financial crisis of 2008, the worst since the [Great Depression](great-depression). In November 2008, the US invited G20 leaders to Washington, D.C., for the first summit of presidents and prime ministers.

## How it works

The 19 member countries are Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, France, Germany, India, Indonesia, Italy, Japan, Mexico, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, South Korea, Turkey, the UK and the USA. The G20 has no office building and no permanent staff. Instead, the presidency rotates every year. The host country plans hundreds of meetings, sets the themes and holds the leaders' summit at the end.

Each leader has a personal representative called a sherpa, named after [the Sherpa mountain guides](people-sherpas) of Nepal who help climbers reach the top. The sherpas do the hard work of negotiating, line by line, the joint statement leaders sign at the summit. The past, present and next hosts work together as a team of three called the troika, so plans carry over.

## India's year

India held the presidency from December 2022 to November 2023. Its theme was *Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam*, a Sanskrit phrase meaning "the world is one family", with the motto "One Earth, One Family, One Future".

Instead of holding everything in [Delhi](delhi), India spread more than 200 meetings across about 60 cities, from [Srinagar](kashmir) to Kumarakom. The leaders' summit was held at the new Bharat Mandapam convention centre in Delhi in September 2023. There, leaders agreed a joint declaration and welcomed the African Union as a permanent member.

## Why it matters, and its critics

When the G20's biggest economies act together, the effect can be large. In 2008 and 2009, their joint spending and rescue plans helped stop the crisis from getting even worse. During the COVID-19 pandemic, they let the poorest countries pause some debt payments.

Critics say a group of 20 can't speak for nearly 200 countries, and that its promises are often vague and not kept. Others point out that agreeing on anything among [rivals like the USA, China and Russia](superpower-summits) is an achievement in itself. In 2025, South Africa hosted the first G20 summit held in Africa, and in 2026 the presidency passed to the USA. Debates over [climate change](climate-change) and world [trade](trade-world) are likely to fill its agenda for years.
