## The short answer

Bread began as a flat, chewy cake of crushed wild grains, cooked on hot stones. The oldest bread crumbs ever found come from a fireplace at a site called Shubayqa in Jordan. They are about 14,400 years old.

The bakers were hunter-gatherers called the Natufians. They had no farms yet. Some scientists think the work of gathering grain for bread may have helped push people toward [farming](farming-began). Within a few thousand years, wheat was being planted across West Asia, and bread spread with it.

## A grain that changed everything

Wheat was one of the first crops ever farmed, about 10,000 years ago in the Fertile Crescent, an arc of land running through what is now Iraq, Syria and Turkey. Early kinds, called einkorn and emmer, had small seeds and tough husks.

Over generations, farmers kept the plants with the biggest, easiest grains. Wheat reached the [Indus cities](indus-cities) in what is now Pakistan and India by about 5,000 years ago. Today India is the second biggest wheat grower in the world, after [China](china-today), and much of it comes from Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh and [Madhya Pradesh](madhya-pradesh).

## Why wheat dough stretches

Mix wheat flour with water and two proteins join up into gluten. Gluten makes dough stretchy, a bit like [chewing gum](chewing-gum). Knead it and the strands line up into a strong net.

Barley, rice and millet have little or no gluten, so they make flatter breads. That is why bajra and jowar rotis are rolled thicker and patted out by hand. A few people cannot eat gluten at all, because their bodies react badly to it. This is called coeliac disease, and doctors can test for it. This is why wheat became the grain of choice for puffy loaves, and why a [roti](roti-chapati) made from wheat atta can blow up like a balloon.

## The rise of the loaf

Leave a wet dough lying around and [wild yeast from the air](food-science-bread-rise) will start to grow in it. The yeast eats sugars and [burps out carbon dioxide](burps). The gluten net catches the bubbles, and the dough swells. This kind of [fermentation](fermentation) was probably discovered by accident.

Ancient Egyptians baked many kinds of risen bread in clay pots. Bread was so central to life that it was a kind of pay. Records and the remains of bakeries near Giza show that pyramid workers received bread and beer as part of their wages.

## From tandoor to toaster

Bread took different shapes around the world. Clay ovens like the tandoor, sunk into the ground, baked naan on their hot walls. The Portuguese brought yeast-raised loaves to Goa in the 16th century, which is where the soft pav of Mumbai's vada pav comes from.

In 1928, a bakery in Chillicothe, Missouri, sold the first loaves cut by Otto Rohwedder's slicing machine. [Sliced bread](bread-factory) caught on so fast that Americans still call a new idea "the best thing since sliced bread".

## Old ways come back

Sourdough is bread raised by a "starter", a jar of flour paste full of wild yeast and bacteria that bakers keep feeding for years. It is how most bread was made before factory yeast went on sale in the 1800s. Home bakers in many countries took it up again during the COVID-19 lockdowns of 2020, when shops ran short of packets of yeast.
