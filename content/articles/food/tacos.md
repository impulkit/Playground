## The short answer

A taco is a small, soft tortilla wrapped or folded around a filling. In Mexico the tortilla is usually [made of corn](corn-maize), warmed on a flat griddle called a comal, much like a tawa. On top go meat, beans, cheese or vegetables, then fresh onion, coriander, a squeeze of lime fruit and a spoonful of salsa.

Tacos are everyday [street food](food-street-food-world) across [Mexico](mexico). Stands sell them from morning until late at night, often for a few pesos each. They are eaten standing up, [with your hands](food-eating-with-hands), and the trick is to tip your head sideways so nothing falls out. Anyone who has eaten a kathi roll in Kolkata knows the feeling.

## Corn, the ancient grain

Corn, or maize, was first farmed in southern Mexico about 9,000 years ago, from a wild grass called teosinte with tiny, hard ears. Over thousands of years, farmers chose the best plants and seeds, and the ears grew bigger and softer. Corn became the most important food of the [Aztecs, the Maya](aztecs-maya) and many other peoples.

A [Maya creation story](myths-maya) even says the first humans were made from maize dough. Corn was eaten at almost every meal, and the tortilla was the plate, [spoon](food-cutlery-history) and bread all in one.

## The lime trick

Raw dried corn is hard and difficult to digest, and grinding it does not fix that. Thousands of years ago, people in Mexico discovered that cooking corn in water with lime makes a big difference. This lime is calcium hydroxide, a white mineral powder made from limestone or shells.

The process is called [nixtamalisation](?nixtamalisation). It loosens the corn's tough skin, makes the dough stick together, adds calcium and frees niacin, one of the B [vitamins](vitamins). When corn spread to Europe and the American South without this trick, many poor people who ate mostly corn fell ill with a disease called pellagra, caused by a lack of niacin.

## Fillings from everywhere

Mexican tacos change from region to region. Carnitas is slow-cooked pork. Barbacoa is meat steamed for hours, traditionally in a pit. Baja California, on the Pacific coast, is famous for fried fish tacos.

Tacos al pastor have a surprising story. In the early 20th century, immigrants from Lebanon brought shawarma to Mexico: meat stacked on a tall turning spit and sliced thin. Mexican cooks swapped lamb for pork, added chilli and pineapple, and put the slices in tortillas. The spit is called a trompo, meaning [spinning top](yo-yo-tops).

## Where the word comes from

Nobody is sure where the word "taco" comes from. One idea, put forward by the historian Jeffrey Pilcher, links it to silver mines of the 1700s, where a "taco" was a paper-wrapped charge used for blasting rock. Others think the word simply meant a plug or wad, something rolled up. The name is younger than the food, which is thousands of years old.

## The crunchy shell

In the United States, Mexican American cooks were frying tortillas into crisp shells by the early 1900s. In the 1950s, restaurant owners found ways to make pre-shaped U-shaped shells, which could be filled quickly. [Fast-food chains](fast-food-history), such as Taco Bell, which opened in 1962, made them famous across the country.

Those shells are a big part of [Tex-Mex](tex-mex) food today. Many Mexicans think of them as a foreign idea, which is a funny fate for a dish named in [Spanish](spanish-language).
