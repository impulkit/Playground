## The short answer

Burgers and hot dogs came to the USA with German [immigrants](ellis-island) in the 1800s. In Hamburg, people ate a kind of minced beef patty, and in Frankfurt and Vienna they ate long, thin sausages. Millions of Germans moved to America, bringing these foods with them.

In the busy cities of the late 1800s, working people needed quick, cheap meals they could eat standing up or on the move. Putting a beef patty or a sausage inside a bun did the trick. Fairs, [baseball](baseball-world-series) games and, later, [roadside restaurants for car drivers](fast-food-history) made them part of everyday American life.

## Who invented the hamburger

Nobody knows for sure. Several places in the USA claim the first hamburger. A restaurant in New Haven, Connecticut, says it served one in 1900. A man in Seymour, Wisconsin, is said to have flattened meatballs into a bun at a fair in 1885. Two brothers from Ohio say they sold one in Hamburg, New York, the same year.

In 2000, the Library of Congress recorded New Haven's claim in a project on local traditions. But the other towns still disagree, and historians say there is no real way to settle the question. The likeliest answer is that many people had the same simple idea around the same time. By the early 1900s, hamburgers were being sold at fairs across the country.

## Hot dogs and Coney Island

German sausage sellers worked the streets of [New York](new-york) in the 1800s. Charles Feltman, a German immigrant, sold sausages in rolls at Coney Island, the seaside amusement area in Brooklyn, from around the 1870s.

In 1916, one of his workers, Nathan Handwerker, opened his own stand nearby and sold hot dogs for half the price. Nathan's Famous still stands there. Every 4 July it holds a hot dog eating contest watched by millions on television. Hot dogs also became the classic snack at baseball parks.

## The fast-food boom

In 1921, a restaurant called White Castle opened in Wichita, Kansas. It sold small, cheap burgers made in a clean kitchen customers could see, and grew into the first burger chain.

After the [Second World War](world-war-two), Americans bought [cars](cars-history) in huge numbers and drove along new [highways](route-66). Roadside burger stands grew into big chains. In 1948, Richard and Maurice McDonald rebuilt their restaurant in California around a fast assembly-line kitchen. A businessman named Ray Kroc later turned McDonald's into one of the biggest restaurant companies in the world.

## Burgers in India

When McDonald's came to India in 1996, it had to change its menu. Many Hindus don't eat beef and many Muslims don't eat pork, so neither was served. Chicken, fish and vegetable patties took their place, and kitchens kept vegetarian food separate.

The McAloo Tikki, a spiced potato patty in a bun, became one of its best sellers. In a country where many people are [vegetarian](vegetarian), the potato burger made sense.

## Mumbai's own burger

India had a bun snack long before the chains arrived. Vada pav, a spiced potato fritter in a soft pav roll, is often called [Mumbai](mumbai)'s burger. It is usually traced to a stall near Dadar station in the 1960s, where it fed textile mill workers on the go.

It's cheap, filling and quick, the same reasons Americans took to burgers a century ago. Today, vada pav stalls sit outside almost every railway station in Mumbai, as you can read in [Indian street food](street-food).
