## The short answer

Milk is food that [mammals](mammals-rise) make in their bodies to feed their babies. A cow, buffalo or goat makes milk after giving birth, in special glands in her udder. The milk has water, fat, protein, vitamins, minerals and a sugar called [lactose](?lactose), everything a newborn needs to grow.

Farmers milk the animals once or twice a day, by hand or with machines. The milk is cooled, collected and sent to dairies. There it is usually heated to kill germs, packed, and trucked to shops before dawn. In India, much of it still arrives in a steel can on the back of a bicycle or motorbike.

## The country with the most milk

India makes about a quarter of all the milk in the world, more than any other country. Unusually, a big share of India's milk, about 44% in 2024, comes from buffaloes. Buffalo milk is thicker and richer in fat than cow's milk, which is why it makes such creamy curd and [paneer](cheese-paneer).

Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh produce the most. In the USA, California makes the most milk, and Wisconsin calls itself "America's Dairyland".

## How India got its milk

In the 1950s and 1960s, India was short of milk. Then a young engineer named Verghese Kurien began working with a [cooperative](?cooperative) of farmers in [Anand](amul), Gujarat. Each farmer owned a few animals and brought milk to a village centre twice a day. The cooperative, later known by its brand name Amul, collected it, tested it and paid fairly.

In 1970, the government spread this model across the country in a programme called Operation Flood. It grew into the [White Revolution](white-revolution), and by the late 1990s India had become the world's biggest milk producer. Kurien's birthday, 26 November, is now National Milk Day.

## Making milk safe

Fresh milk can carry harmful bacteria. In the 1860s, the French scientist Louis Pasteur showed that gentle heating kills many germs, and the idea was later applied to milk. [Pasteurised](?pasteurise) milk is typically heated to about 72°C for 15 seconds, then cooled fast.

Many Indian families boil milk at home before drinking it, which also kills germs. Watch a pot of milk heat up and you'll see [a skin form on top](food-science-milk-boil). That is protein and fat cooking at the surface.

## Can everybody drink milk?

Babies make plenty of lactase, a chemical in the [gut](digestion-gut) that breaks down milk sugar. In most of the world, people make much less of it after childhood. If they drink a lot of milk, they may get tummy aches. This is called lactose intolerance.

But some groups whose ancestors kept cattle, in northern Europe, parts of Africa and north-western India, often keep making lactase as adults. It is one of the best examples of a change in human [genes](genes-family) spreading because of food. Curd and cheese have less lactose, which is one reason people everywhere turned milk into them.

## Milk in your body

Milk has plenty of [calcium](bones-growth), a mineral your body uses to build [bones](bones) and teeth. It also has protein for muscles. That is why it is part of many school meal programmes. People who don't drink milk can get calcium from foods such as ragi, sesame, leafy greens and some pulses.

Milk is also the starting point for a long list of foods: [curd](curd-yogurt), butter, ghee, paneer, khoa and most [Indian sweets](sweets-india). A few litres of milk on an Indian kitchen counter can become a whole meal by evening.
