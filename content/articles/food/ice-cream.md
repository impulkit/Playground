## The short answer

Ice cream is made by freezing a mixture of milk, cream and sugar while stirring it all the time. The stirring, called churning, does two jobs. It keeps the ice crystals tiny, so the ice cream feels smooth on your tongue instead of icy. It also whips in air, which makes it soft and light.

In a factory, the mixture is first heated to kill germs, then mixed so the fat is spread evenly. It is cooled, flavoured and churned in a freezer that pumps in air. Finally it is hardened at very low temperature. A good ice cream is a careful balance of [ice crystals](?crystal), fat, sugar syrup and air.

## Why sugar and fat matter

Sugar does more than sweeten. It lowers the freezing point of water, so not all the water turns to ice. A little stays as thick, sweet syrup, which keeps ice cream soft enough to scoop.

Fat coats the tiny ice crystals and air bubbles and keeps them from joining up. That's why ice cream made with cream feels richer than ice made from fruit juice.

## The salt trick

Before electric freezers, people made ice cream with [ice and salt](salt-ice-roads). In 1843, an American named Nancy Johnson in Philadelphia patented a hand-cranked freezer. A metal can of cream sat inside a wooden bucket packed with ice and salt. Turning the handle churned the cream.

Salt lowers the melting point of ice. As the salty ice melts, it soaks up heat from the cream, chilling it well below 0°C. The same science is why roads are salted in snowy parts of the USA. Today, electric [refrigeration](refrigerator) does the cooling instead.

## Kulfi, India's frozen dessert

Kulfi is made differently. Milk is boiled slowly for a long time until much of its water evaporates and it thickens. Sugar, [cardamom](cardamom-pepper), pistachios or [saffron](saffron) are added. Then it is poured into cone-shaped moulds and frozen without churning.

With little air and less water, kulfi is dense and creamy, and melts slowly even on a hot afternoon. It is often linked with the [Mughal](mughals) court. Records from [Akbar](akbar)'s time describe ice and snow brought down from the mountains to cool drinks in the royal kitchen. Kulfi sellers, called kulfiwalas, still [push carts](food-trucks) with clay pots packed with ice and salt, the old trick at work.

## Ice cream in America

Ice cream became a luxury treat in the young United States. Records show that [George Washington](george-washington) spent about 200 dollars on ice cream in one summer in 1790, a large sum then. Ice cream was made with ice cut from frozen lakes in winter and stored in icehouses.

By the 1900s, factories and home fridges made it cheap. Ice cream cones became popular at the 1904 World's Fair in St. Louis.

## Brain freeze

Eating ice cream too fast can cause a sudden, sharp headache, often called [brain freeze](body-brain-freeze). Scientists think the cold on the roof of your mouth makes blood vessels nearby narrow and then widen quickly, which nerves sense as pain.

It goes away in less than a minute. Pressing your tongue against the roof of your mouth can help warm it up. Eating more slowly prevents it, and it gives you more time to enjoy the ice cream.
