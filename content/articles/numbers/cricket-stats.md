## The short answer

Cricket is a game of numbers, and two of the most important are the average and the strike rate. A batting average tells you how many runs a batter scores, on average, each time they get out. A strike rate tells you how fast they score: how many runs they would make if they faced 100 balls.

Bowlers have their own numbers. A bowling average is the runs they give away for each wicket they take, so lower is better. An economy rate is the runs they give away per over. Together, these numbers help selectors pick teams, captains plan tactics and fans argue about who is the greatest.

## Working out a batting average

The rule is simple: divide total runs by the number of times out. If a batter scores 450 runs and is dismissed 9 times, their average is 450 ÷ 9 = 50.

The tricky part is "not out". If a batter finishes an innings without being dismissed, their runs still count, but that innings is not added to the number you divide by. So two batters with the same runs and innings can have very different averages. This helps batters who often finish not out, and it is one reason averages should be read with care, a point made in many guides to [statistics](statistics-lies).

## The Bradman puzzle

[Australia](australia)'s Don Bradman played 52 Test matches between 1928 and 1948. He scored 6,996 runs and was dismissed 70 times, giving an average of 99.94. Most of the greatest batters in history average somewhere in the 50s in Tests, so Bradman stands far apart.

The story of his last innings is famous. Before his final Test, at The Oval in London in 1948, he had been dismissed 69 times. If he scored just 4 runs, even if he then got out, his average would be 7,000 ÷ 70 = exactly 100. He was bowled second ball for a duck by the English leg-spinner Eric Hollies, and his average stopped at 99.94.

## Strike rate and the T20 age

In a [Test match](test-cricket), batting for a long time matters, so average is king. In shorter formats, speed matters too. A batter's strike rate is runs ÷ balls faced × 100. Score 30 runs off 20 balls and your strike rate is 150. Score 30 off 60 and it is 50.

In T20 leagues like the [IPL](ipl), a strike rate of 150 or more is often seen as excellent, while in Test cricket many batters score at well under 60. A bowler's economy rate matters most in T20 too. Giving away 6 runs an over is very good, while 10 or more is expensive.

Bowlers also have a strike rate, but it means something different: the number of balls they bowl, on average, for each wicket. A bowler who takes 10 wickets in 300 balls has a strike rate of 30. For a bowler, unlike a batter, a lower strike rate is better.

## Big numbers, careful comparisons

[Sachin Tendulkar](sachin-tendulkar) holds the records for most runs in both Test cricket, with 15,921, and one-day internationals, with 18,426. His Test average was 53.78. But comparing players across eras is hard. Pitches, bats, rules, the number of matches played and the quality of opposing teams have all changed.

That is why [statisticians invent new measures](maths-sports-stats), such as averages in matches a team won, or runs scored against the strongest bowlers. Numbers can settle some arguments, but in cricket they usually start a few more.
