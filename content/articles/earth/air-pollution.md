## The short answer

Air is mostly nitrogen and oxygen. It becomes polluted when other things get mixed in: soot, dust, smoke, and gases such as nitrogen dioxide, sulphur dioxide and ground-level ozone. Most of it comes from burning things. Car and truck engines, coal power plants, factories, cooking fires and burning crop stubble all send smoke into the sky.

The worst part is often invisible. Tiny particles called PM2.5 are so small they get deep into our lungs. We clean the air mostly by burning less, and by filtering the smoke from what we still burn.

## Why some days are worse

The same smoke can be harmless one day and choking the next. Weather decides. On windy days, pollution blows away and spreads thin. On cold, still winter nights, a layer of warm air can sit on top of cooler air near the ground, like a lid on a pot. Smoke gets trapped underneath.

That is part of why [Delhi](delhi) has some of its dirtiest air in November and December. Vehicles, building dust, industry and the burning of leftover straw on farms in [Punjab](punjab) and Haryana all add to it, and the still air keeps it close to the ground.

Nature makes some pollution too. Dust storms blow in from the [Thar Desert](desert-life) before the monsoon. [Volcanoes](volcano) puff out ash and gas. In September 2020, smoke from [huge wildfires](forest-fires) turned the daytime sky over San Francisco a deep orange, and streetlights stayed on at noon. But in most big cities, people's own burning is the main source, and that is the part we can change.

## The fog that killed

In December 1952, a thick yellow [smog](fog-smog) settled over London for five days. Homes there burned coal for heat, and the smoke mixed with fog. People could barely see across a street. Doctors later counted thousands of extra deaths.

That disaster changed the law. Britain passed the Clean Air Act in 1956, which moved homes away from smoky coal. [London](london) slowly became a clearer city. Big cities in the USA had their own smog problems, especially [Los Angeles](los-angeles), and the USA passed its Clean Air Act in 1970.

## What dirty air does

When you breathe in, air travels down to millions of tiny bags in your [lungs](lungs-breathe), where oxygen passes into your blood. Fine particles can travel all the way there. Over years, dirty air makes asthma worse and harms the heart and lungs. Children breathe faster than adults and are closer to car exhausts, so they take in more.

The World Health Organization says almost everyone on Earth breathes air dirtier than its guidelines. On bad days, health officials advise staying indoors more and skipping hard exercise outside.

## How cities clean up

Cleaner air has a few proven recipes. Filters on power plant chimneys catch soot. Devices called catalytic converters, fitted to car exhausts, turn some poisonous gases into safer ones. Better fuels and [electric cars](electric-cars) help, and so do buses and metros that move many people at once.

In the early 2000s, Delhi switched its buses and autorickshaws to a cleaner gas called CNG. Across India, millions of families have swapped smoky wood and dung stoves for gas cooking, which helps the air inside homes. Air pollution also has a big cousin: the same burning that makes smog adds the gases behind [climate change](climate-change).
