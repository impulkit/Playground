## The short answer

A desert is any place that gets very little rain or snow. Whether it ends up sandy or rocky depends mostly on two things: what kind of rock is around, and what the wind does.

In most deserts, wind blows away the loose [sand](sand-where) and dust, leaving behind rock, gravel and pebbles too heavy to move. The sand piles up somewhere else, usually in low basins where the wind slows down. Those places become seas of [dunes](earth-weird-moving-dunes). So rocky deserts and sandy deserts are often two halves of the same story.

## Wind, the great sorter

With few plants to hold the ground together, desert wind can move a lot of material. It works like a sieve. Fine dust is lifted high and can be carried across oceans. Dust from the Sahara regularly reaches the Caribbean and even the [Amazon rainforest](brazil-amazon).

Sand grains are heavier. They bounce along close to the ground, rarely rising higher than your waist. Pebbles and stones are too heavy for the wind to shift at all. Over thousands of years, the wind strips away the small grains and leaves a tightly packed layer of pebbles on the surface. Geologists call this desert pavement. It can look as if someone laid it out by hand.

## Where the sand comes from

Most desert sand is made of quartz, a hard mineral that survives after softer [rocks](rocks-types) have crumbled. Desert rocks break down as they heat up by day and cool at night, and in sudden rainstorms that rush through [dry valleys](valleys).

Rivers, often from long ago, also carry sand into deserts. Much of the sand in the [Thar Desert](thar-formed) of Rajasthan is thought to have come from rivers and the dry beds of old streams. Strong summer winds from the southwest then shaped it into dunes.

## Seas of sand

Where the [wind](wind-blows) slows down, in a basin or behind a mountain range, the sand it carries drops out and gathers. Big sandy areas are called ergs, after an Arabic word. The largest are in the [Sahara](sahara) and in Arabia's Empty Quarter, where dunes can be taller than a 50-storey building.

Dunes move. The wind pushes sand up the gentle side and it tumbles down the steep side, so the whole dune creeps forward. In the Thar, people plant rows of trees and shrubs, called shelterbelts, to slow the moving sand and protect fields and roads.

## Rocky deserts

Most of the world's desert land is rocky. There are wide plains of gravel, bare rock and mountains, and dry lake beds that shine white with salt. [Death Valley](mystery-sailing-stones) in California is mostly rock, [salt flats](earth-salt-flats) and stony fans spreading out from canyons. It has dunes too, but they cover only a small part of the park.

The [Ladakh](ladakh) region of India is a high, cold desert, with bare mountains and rocky valleys. It gets so little rain because the Himalaya blocks [the monsoon](monsoon) clouds before they arrive.

## Cold deserts count too

Because a desert is about dryness, not heat, the largest desert on Earth is [Antarctica](antarctica). Very little snow falls there each year, but because it hardly ever melts, it has built up into [ice sheets kilometres thick](what-if-ice-melted). The Gobi Desert in [Mongolia](mongolia) and China is rocky and gravelly, with scorching summers and bitterly cold winters.

## Life on the gravel

Deserts are not empty. Plants grow wide, shallow roots to catch rare rain, or deep ones to reach water underground. Animals hide from the heat by day. People have lived in deserts for thousands of years, from the Rajasthani villages of the Thar to the [Navajo Nation](navajo-nation) in the American Southwest. [Life in the desert](desert-life) depends on knowing exactly where water can be found.
