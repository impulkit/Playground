## The short answer

Deep underground, rock gets so hot it melts into [magma](?magma). Magma is lighter than solid rock, so it rises.

Magma holds gas, like a [fizzy drink](soft-drinks). Runny magma lets the gas escape gently. Sticky magma traps it, until it bursts.

India's only active volcano is on Barren Island, in the Andaman Sea.

## Inside the shaken bottle

A sealed bottle of soda has gas dissolved in it, kept in by pressure. Open it, and bubbles fizz out. Magma deep underground holds water vapour and other gases in the same way. As it rises and the pressure drops, the gas forms bubbles.

What happens next depends on how sticky the magma is. Runny magma, like the kind in Hawaii, lets bubbles escape easily. It pours out as glowing rivers of lava. Thick, sticky magma traps the bubbles. Pressure builds and builds, until the magma shatters into ash and rock and blasts out of the volcano.

## Where volcanoes happen

Most volcanoes form where plates pull apart or where one plate sinks under another. Some, like Hawaii's, sit over hot spots in the mantle.

About three-quarters of Earth's active volcanoes ring the Pacific Ocean, in a horseshoe called the Ring of Fire, from New Zealand and Indonesia to Japan, Alaska and the Andes. There, ocean plates sink beneath other plates and melt as they go down. [Iceland](iceland) sits on a ridge where two plates are pulling apart.

## India's volcanoes

Barren Island, about 135 km north-east of Port Blair in the [Andaman](andaman) Sea, is India's only active volcano. After more than a century of quiet, it began erupting again in 1991 and has had several eruptions since. Nobody lives there, though a herd of wild goats does, and scientists sail out to study it from the sea. Further north lies Narcondam Island, another volcano that has been quiet for a very long time.

India's biggest volcanic story is far older. About 66 million years ago, enormous cracks in western India poured out lava again and again. The flows piled up into the [Deccan](deccan) Traps, layers more than 2 km thick in places. The [rich black soil](soil-types-india) of Maharashtra, so good for cotton, formed from that weathered lava rock.

## Famous eruptions

In the year 79, Mount Vesuvius in Italy buried the Roman town of [Pompeii](pompeii) under ash. The ash preserved streets, bread in ovens and paintings on walls, giving us a snapshot of Roman life.

In April 1815, Mount Tambora in Indonesia had the largest eruption in recorded history. Its ash and gas spread around the world and cooled the climate. In 1816, crops failed in Europe and North America, and people called it "the year without a summer".

On 18 May 1980, Mount St. Helens in Washington state, USA, blew out its side in a huge sideways blast. The mountain lost about 400 m of height in minutes. Scientists had been watching it for weeks and had closed the area, which saved many lives.

## Good and bad

Eruptions can be dangerous, but volcanic ash makes some of the richest soils on Earth, and volcanoes built many islands.

In places like Java in [Indonesia](bali), millions of farmers live on the slopes of active volcanoes because the soil is so fertile. Iceland uses underground volcanic heat to warm homes and make electricity. And [Hawaii](hawaii-islands) is still growing, one lava flow at a time.
