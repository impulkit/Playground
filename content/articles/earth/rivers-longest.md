## The short answer

Most [encyclopedias](encyclopedias) and atlases say the Nile is the longest river in the world, at about 6,650 km. It flows north through eastern Africa and Egypt into the Mediterranean Sea.

But the Amazon in South America is a close rival. Britannica gives it about 6,400 km, yet some scientists have measured it at nearly 7,000 km by choosing a different source high in the Andes mountains of [Peru](peru). So the honest answer is: probably the Nile, possibly the Amazon. What nobody disputes is that the Amazon carries far more water than any other river.

## Why it's hard to measure

Measuring a river sounds simple, but it isn't. First, you have to decide where it begins. Big rivers are fed by many streams, and choosing which one is the true source can add or remove hundreds of kilometres.

Then you must decide where it ends. The Amazon spreads into a huge mouth, more than 200 km wide in places, mixed with islands and channels. Where exactly does the river stop and the sea begin?

Finally, rivers wiggle. The more closely you follow every bend, the longer the river gets. This is like the puzzle of [fractals](fractals), where a coastline seems to grow longer the more carefully you measure it. Rivers also shift their courses over time.

## The Nile

The Nile has two main branches. The White Nile begins in the region of Lake Victoria in East Africa, and its farthest headwaters are in Rwanda and Burundi. The Blue Nile starts at Lake Tana in [Ethiopia](ethiopia) and brings most of the water. They meet at Khartoum, in Sudan.

From there the Nile flows north through the desert to Egypt, where nearly everyone lives close to its banks. Without it, there would have been no [ancient Egypt](egypt-nile). Each year, its [floods](floods-why) once spread rich mud over the fields, until the Aswan High Dam was finished in 1970.

## The Amazon

The Amazon begins as tiny streams in the Andes of Peru, just 160 km or so from the Pacific Ocean, and flows east across South America to the Atlantic. It carries about a fifth of all the river water that reaches the world's oceans.

Its basin holds the largest [rainforest](rainforests) on Earth. In the wet season, the river floods vast areas of forest, and fish swim between the tree trunks. There are no [bridges](bridges) across its main stream. Much of the land around it is forest and swamp, with few roads that would need one.

## India's great rivers

The Ganga flows about 2,500 km from the Gangotri Glacier in the Himalaya to the Bay of Bengal. The Brahmaputra and the Indus are both longer, each roughly 2,900 to 3,200 km. All three begin in or near the Himalaya and are fed by melting snow and ice, as you can read in [the rivers of the Himalaya](rivers-himalaya).

The Ganga and the Brahmaputra join in [Bangladesh](bangladesh) and pour into the sea through the largest river [delta](deltas) in the world.

## America's great river

In the United States, the longest river is the Missouri, at about 3,800 km. It flows into the [Mississippi](mississippi), and together they form one of the longest river systems in the world, roughly 6,000 km from the [Rocky Mountains](rocky-mountains) of Montana to the Gulf of Mexico.

Steamboats once carried cotton, people and goods up and down the Mississippi. The writer [Mark Twain](mark-twain) worked as a river pilot on it as a young man, and later wrote about life on the river in his books.

## A friendly rivalry

People sometimes plan expeditions to settle the question, paddling or tracking the rivers from source to sea. With satellite images and GPS, the measurements keep improving. For now, most atlases still give the crown to the Nile, while the Amazon wins easily as the biggest river by water.
