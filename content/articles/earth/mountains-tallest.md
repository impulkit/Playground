## The short answer

It depends on how you measure. [Mount Everest](mountaineering), on the border of Nepal and China, has the highest summit above sea level: 8,848.86 m. That makes it the highest mountain on Earth.

But Mauna Kea in Hawaii is taller from bottom to top. It rises about 10,200 m from its base on the floor of the Pacific Ocean, though most of it is underwater. And if you measure from the centre of the Earth, the summit of Chimborazo in Ecuador reaches farthest out into space.

## The highest: Everest

Everest sits in the Himalaya, which formed when India crashed slowly into Asia. In Nepal the mountain is called Sagarmatha, and in Tibet, Chomolungma.

In 2020, survey teams from Nepal and China announced a new joint measurement: 8,848.86 m. A Chinese team had carried instruments to the top that year, while Nepali surveyors had climbed it in 2019. Both used satellites and ground measurements. [Earthquakes](earthquakes) and the slow push of India northward can change the height very slightly.

## Finding the highest

In the 1800s, the Great Trigonometrical Survey of India measured mountains from far away, using careful [angles](geometry-everyday) and a lot of arithmetic. In 1852, Radhanath Sikdar, a Bengali mathematician working for the survey, calculated that a peak listed as "Peak XV" was the highest in the world.

It was later named after George Everest, a former head of the survey, even though he had little to do with measuring it and reportedly objected to the honour. The first people known to stand on top were Tenzing Norgay and Edmund Hillary, in 1953, as told in [the first Everest climb](everest-first).

## The tallest: Mauna Kea

Mauna Kea is a [volcano](volcano) on the Big Island of [Hawaii](hawaii-islands). It began on the dark floor of the Pacific Ocean, around 6,000 m down, and slowly built itself up from layer after layer of lava. Its summit is about 4,200 m above sea level, so from a beach it doesn't look nearly as big as Everest.

Measured from base to peak, though, it is about 10,200 m tall, more than 1,300 m taller than Everest. Its summit has some of the world's most important [telescopes](telescopes-how), because the air there is thin, dry and clear. The mountain is also sacred to many Native Hawaiians, and building more telescopes there has caused long disagreements.

## Farthest from the centre: Chimborazo

Earth isn't a perfect ball. Its spin makes it bulge at the equator, so the ground there is farther from the centre than at the poles. Chimborazo, a volcano in Ecuador, sits almost on the equator.

Its summit is only about 6,260 m above sea level, but it is about 2 km farther from Earth's centre than Everest's peak. If you want to be as close to the stars as possible while standing on Earth, Chimborazo is the place.

## High points nearer home

[Kangchenjunga](sikkim), on the border of India and Nepal, is India's highest mountain at 8,586 m, and the third highest on Earth. India's official maps also claim K2, the second highest, but it lies in the part of Kashmir run by Pakistan. The people of Sikkim consider it sacred, and many climbers stop just short of the very top out of respect.

In North America, the highest mountain is Denali in [Alaska](alaska), about 6,190 m high. In 2015, the US Geological Survey measured it again with modern equipment and found it was about 3 m shorter than people had thought.

## Even taller on Mars

The tallest known mountain in the solar system is [Olympus Mons](space-weird-tallest-mountain) on Mars, a giant volcano about 22 km high, more than twice the height of Mauna Kea from base to peak. It is so wide that if you stood on its slopes, the peak would be hidden beyond the horizon. [Mars rovers](mars-rovers) haven't visited it yet, but spacecraft have photographed it from above.
