## The short answer

The driest place on Earth that isn't covered in ice is the Atacama Desert in northern Chile, a long strip squeezed between the Pacific Ocean and the Andes mountains. The coastal city of Arica averages less than 1 mm of rain a year. That is less rain in a whole year than Mumbai can get in a minute during a heavy monsoon downpour.

If you count the polar regions, the driest spots are the McMurdo Dry Valleys of [Antarctica](antarctica). These valleys get almost no rain or snow, and fierce winds sweep away what little falls. Some scientists think parts of them have stayed dry for millions of years.

## Why the Atacama is so dry

Three things work together. First, the Andes block rain clouds from the Amazon side, creating a rain shadow. Second, a cold ocean current runs up the coast, cooling the air above the sea so it cannot rise to form rain clouds. Third, a zone of high pressure sits overhead, pressing down on the air and keeping skies clear.

The result is a land of salt flats, gravel and bare red hills. In the driest parts, scientists have found soil where almost no living things survive, even bacteria. Abandoned mining towns and old machines decay very slowly, because there is so little moisture to rot or corrode them.

## A Mars on Earth

The Atacama looks and behaves so much like Mars that NASA and other space agencies test their [rovers](mars-rovers) there. The soils are dry, salty and full of chemicals similar to those on the red planet. Scientists practise searching for tiny traces of life, which helps them design instruments to send to Mars.

Its clear, dry skies also make it one of the best places on Earth for telescopes. The giant ALMA observatory sits on a plateau at about 5,000 m, high above most of the water vapour in the air.

## Mummies older than Egypt's

The dryness preserves things. About 7,000 years ago, the Chinchorro people of the Atacama coast began mummifying their dead. They removed organs, rebuilt bodies with sticks and plant fibres, and covered them in clay masks painted black or red. That is about 2,000 years before the Egyptians began making [mummies](mummies).

Scientists think the Chinchorro may have started mummifying partly because the desert itself dried bodies naturally, and people saw the dead stay recognisable for years. In 2021, UNESCO made the Chinchorro sites a World Heritage Site.

## When the desert blooms

Every few years, often during [El Niño](el-nino), warm Pacific water brings a little extra rain. Within weeks, seeds that have waited in the soil for years sprout. Pink, purple and yellow flowers carpet the desert in what Chileans call the desierto florido, the flowering desert.

Some towns on the coast collect water from fog. Fine nets strung on hillsides catch droplets from sea mists called camanchaca, which drip into pipes and tanks below.

## Dry places in India

India's driest regions are the Thar Desert in Rajasthan and the cold deserts of [Ladakh](ladakh), where parts get less than 100 mm a year. In Leh, much of the water comes from melting snow and glaciers, not rain. Even so, Ladakh gets far more than the Atacama.

Then there is the largest desert of all. Antarctica gets so little snowfall that, even though it is covered in ice, it counts as a desert. It covers about 14 million square km, bigger than the Sahara or any other [hot, sandy desert](deserts-sand). Its ice built up slowly, a thin dusting at a time, over hundreds of thousands of years.
