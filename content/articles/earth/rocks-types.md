## The short answer

Geologists sort rocks into three big families by how they were made. Igneous rocks form when hot, molten rock cools and hardens. Sedimentary rocks form when [sand](sand-where), mud, shells and other bits settle in layers and slowly turn to stone. Metamorphic rocks are older rocks that have been changed by heat and pressure deep underground, without melting.

Rocks don't stay in one family forever. Over millions of years, any rock can be worn down, buried, squeezed or melted and turned into another kind. Geologists call this slow [recycling](recycling) the rock cycle.

## Born of fire

Igneous comes from the [Latin](latin-roots) word for fire. Deep underground, rock can be hot enough to melt into [magma](?magma). When magma cools slowly, far below the surface, its crystals have time to grow big. That gives granite, the speckled stone used for kitchen counters and many old buildings.

When magma bursts out of a [volcano](volcano) as [lava](?lava), it cools fast and the crystals stay tiny. That gives dark, fine-grained [basalt](earth-weird-giants-causeway). Much of the Deccan Plateau in western India is made of basalt, laid down in giant eruptions about 66 million years ago. Some lava is so full of gas bubbles that it hardens into pumice, a rock light enough to float on water.

## Built in layers

Rain, wind, ice and rivers slowly break rocks into grains. Rivers carry the grains away and drop them as [sediment](?sediment) in lakes and seas. Layer after layer piles up, and the weight squeezes the lower layers. [Minerals in the water](minerals-everyday) glue the grains together.

Sand becomes sandstone. Mud becomes shale. Shells and the skeletons of tiny sea creatures become limestone. The red sandstone of Delhi's Red Fort and the walls of the [Grand Canyon](grand-canyon) are sedimentary rocks.

Because sediment settles gently, it can bury animals and plants whole. That is why nearly all [fossils](fossils-form) are found in sedimentary rock. Only a thin skin of Earth is made of these rocks, yet they cover about three quarters of the land surface.

## Changed under pressure

Metamorphic means "changed in form". When rocks are buried deep, or caught where continents collide, heat and pressure change them. Their minerals rearrange into new crystals, while the rock stays solid, a bit like clay hardening in a kiln without melting.

Limestone becomes marble. The marble for the [Taj Mahal](taj-mahal) came from Makrana in Rajasthan, and it began as sea-floor limestone. Shale becomes slate, which splits into flat sheets once used for school writing slates. Granite can become gneiss, a rock with wavy stripes.

## Very old rocks

Much of southern India sits on some of the [oldest rock on Earth](rocks-oldest). The Lalbagh Rock in [Bengaluru](bengaluru) is a mound of gneiss about 3,000 million years old. The Geological Survey of India has declared it a National Geological Monument.

The oldest known rocks, in northern Canada, are about 4 billion years old. Older rocks have been recycled away. Earth itself is about 4.5 billion years old, so the rock cycle has been turning for a very long time.

## The cycle keeps turning

A grain of sand on a beach in Goa might once have been part of a granite mountain. In millions of years, it could be buried and turned into sandstone, squeezed into a metamorphic rock called quartzite, or carried down into Earth to melt.

[Mountains rise](mountains-types) as continents push together, and rain and rivers wear them down again. The Himalaya is still rising, while its rocks are already crumbling into the sand that will become new rock. Some of that crumbled rock ends up in fields as [soil](soil-made).
