## The short answer

Earth is getting warmer mainly because people are burning coal, oil and natural gas. Burning them releases [carbon dioxide](carbon-cycle), a gas that traps heat in the air, like a blanket around the planet. [Cutting down forests](forests-india) adds more.

Since the late 1800s, Earth's average temperature has risen by about 1.3 °C, according to a 2024 estimate by the World Meteorological Organization. That sounds small, but it is an average across the whole planet, all year round. It is already melting glaciers, raising the sea and making [heatwaves](heatwaves) more common.

## The heat-trapping blanket

Sunlight passes through the air and warms the ground and the sea. The warm Earth then gives off heat as invisible infrared light. Some gases in the air, especially carbon dioxide, water vapour and methane, absorb that heat and send part of it back down. These are called [greenhouse gases](?greenhouse-gas).

This is natural, and it is a good thing. Without any greenhouse gases, Earth would be about 33 °C colder, and mostly frozen. The problem is that people have added much more of them, so the blanket has grown thicker.

## Who worked it out

In 1856, the American scientist Eunice Foote filled glass jars with different gases and set them in the sun. The jar with carbon dioxide got the hottest. She wrote that more of this gas in the air would make Earth warmer. A few years later, the Irish scientist John Tyndall measured how gases absorb heat in more detail.

In 1958, Charles David Keeling began measuring carbon dioxide on Mauna Loa in [Hawaii](hawaii-islands), far from cities and factories. His measurements, continued to this day, form a famous rising zigzag called the Keeling Curve. Before factories, the air held about 280 parts per million of carbon dioxide. Now it holds more than 420.

## How we know it's us

Natural causes, like changes in the Sun or volcanic eruptions, can't explain the warming. The Sun's output has barely changed in decades. Carbon from burning [coal and oil](oil-coal) has a slightly different chemical fingerprint from other carbon, and scientists can see that fingerprint growing in the air.

Bubbles of ancient air trapped in Antarctic ice show that carbon dioxide is now higher than at any time in at least 800,000 years. The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, which brings together thousands of scientists from around the world, says it is clear that human activity has warmed the planet.

## What is changing

Most [glaciers](glaciers) are shrinking, from Alaska to the Himalaya. Arctic sea ice at the end of summer covers far less area than it did when [satellites](satellites-help) began watching it in the late 1970s. As ice on land melts and warm water expands, the sea has risen by about 20 cm since 1900, and it is [rising faster now](sea-level-rise).

Heatwaves are hotter and more frequent in many places, including India and the United States. [Warm oceans](ocean-and-climate) can make [coral reefs](coral-reefs) turn white and die. The ten warmest years on record have all happened since 2015.

## What people are doing

In 2015, almost every country agreed in Paris to try to keep warming well below 2 °C. Countries are building [solar farms](renewable-energy), [wind turbines](wind-turbines) and electric buses, and finding [new technology](green-tech) to use less energy. India has set up some of the world's largest solar parks, such as Bhadla in Rajasthan.

People disagree about how fast to change and who should pay, and countries are at very different stages. Those are questions of fairness and politics. The science of why Earth is warming is much more settled.

## Not the same as weather

One cold winter doesn't mean warming has stopped, and one hot summer doesn't prove it. Weather is what happens day to day. Climate is the pattern over 30 years or more. Scientists look at the long trend across the whole planet, and that trend points clearly upward.
