## The short answer

The World Meteorological Organization (WMO), a United Nations agency, keeps the official list of Earth's weather extremes. A team of experts checks each claimed record carefully, looking at the instruments, the logbooks and the weather at the time.

The hottest air temperature on its list is 56.7 °C, measured at Furnace Creek in [Death Valley](mystery-sailing-stones), California, on 10 July 1913. The coldest is −89.2 °C, recorded at Russia's Vostok Station in Antarctica on 21 July 1983. India holds rain records: Mawsynram in [Meghalaya](meghalaya) is the wettest place on average, at nearly 12 metres of rain a year.

Records keep changing as new readings come in and old ones are rechecked. Some are still argued over.

## The hottest, and the doubts

For 90 years, the hottest record was 58 °C at El Azizia in Libya, from 1922. In 2012, a WMO team studied the original notes and found that the observer had probably misread his thermometer. The record was cancelled.

The title passed to Death Valley's 1913 reading. Some weather historians doubt that one too, because nearby stations were much cooler that day. But the WMO has not removed it. Death Valley has reached about 54 °C in recent summers, and those readings were measured with modern equipment.

India's highest recorded temperature is 51.0 °C, at Phalodi in Rajasthan, on 19 May 2016.

## The coldest place

Vostok Station sits high on the ice sheet in the middle of [Antarctica](antarctica), more than 3 km above sea level. On 21 July 1983, which is winter there, the air reached −89.2 °C. At that temperature, breath freezes into crystals almost at once.

Satellites have spotted even colder spots on the ice nearby, close to −98 °C. But those are measurements of the snow surface from space, not of the air from a [thermometer](thermometer), so they are kept on a different list.

## Where it rains the most

[Mawsynram](wettest-place) and Cherrapunji sit on the edge of the Khasi Hills, about 15 km apart. Moist monsoon winds from the Bay of Bengal hit these hills and are forced upwards, dropping enormous amounts of rain. Mawsynram averages around 11,800 mm a year, about five times as much as rainy Mumbai.

Cherrapunji holds the record for the most rain in a single year: over 26 metres, between August 1860 and July 1861. The record for a single day goes to the island of Réunion, in the Indian Ocean. In January 1966, a cyclone dropped 1,825 mm there in 24 hours, enough water to reach a tall adult's head.

At the other extreme, parts of the [Atacama Desert](extreme-driest) in Chile get almost no rain at all, less than 1 mm a year on average in some towns.

## Wind and ice from the sky

The [strongest gust of wind ever measured](extreme-windiest), apart from inside [tornadoes](tornadoes), was 408 km/h. It struck Barrow Island, off Western Australia, during Tropical Cyclone Olivia in 1996.

Hailstones have records too. The heaviest on record fell in Gopalganj, Bangladesh, on 14 April 1986, weighing about 1 kg. The widest, found in Vivian, South Dakota, in 2010, measured 20 cm across, about the size of a volleyball. [Hail like that](hailstorms-india) [forms](snow-hail) when strong updrafts in a thundercloud carry ice up and down again and again, adding a new layer each time.
