## The short answer

The Maya and the Aztecs were two great civilisations of Mesoamerica, the region covering much of today's [Mexico](mexico) and Central America. They were different peoples who lived at different times.

The Maya built cities with stone pyramids, palaces and ball courts in the [rainforests](rainforests) of southern Mexico and Central America. They flourished especially from about 250 to 900 CE, and they had a full writing system, advanced mathematics and precise calendars. Maya people did not vanish: millions live in the region today. The Aztecs, who called themselves Mexica, rose much later. In 1325 they founded [Tenochtitlan](aztec-tenochtitlan) on an island in a lake, and built a powerful empire. It fell to Spanish invaders and their local allies in 1521.

## Maya cities in the forest

At its height, the Maya world held dozens of city-states, each ruled by a king. Tikal, in Guatemala, had temple-pyramids rising above the treetops, the tallest about 65 metres high.

Cities fought wars and made alliances. Kings carved their deeds on tall stone slabs called stelae, with dates. Around 900 CE, many great cities in the southern lowlands were abandoned. Historians think a mix of long [droughts](droughts), war and overuse of land played a part. Further north, cities such as Chichen Itza kept going.

## Writing, numbers and time

Maya writing used hundreds of signs called glyphs, some standing for whole words and some for sounds. For a long time no one could read them. From the 1950s to the 1980s, scholars including Yuri Knorozov and Tatiana Proskouriakoff cracked much of the code.

The Maya counted in twenties and used a shell sign for [zero](zero-invent). They tracked the Sun, the Moon and [Venus](venus-hot) carefully. Their Long Count calendar counted days from a starting point in 3114 BCE. One long cycle ended in December 2012, which led to a modern myth about the end of the world.

## A city on a lake

The Aztecs arrived in the Valley of Mexico as wanderers. Their legend says their god told them to settle where they saw an eagle on a cactus, eating a snake. That image is on Mexico's flag today.

Tenochtitlan grew on an island in Lake Texcoco. Causeways linked it to the shore, and canals ran through it like streets. Farmers built *chinampas*, plots of fertile mud and plants piled up in the shallow lake, where they grew maize, beans and flowers. By 1519, perhaps 2 lakh or more people lived there, more than in any Spanish city of the time.

## Empire, tribute and gods

The Aztecs ruled by demanding tribute, regular payments of goods, from conquered towns: cotton cloth, feathers, cacao beans and more. Cacao beans were so valuable they were used as money.

At the centre of the city stood the Templo Mayor, a great double pyramid for the rain god and the war god. The Aztecs believed the gods needed offerings, including human sacrifice, to keep the Sun moving. This made many neighbouring peoples hate their rule.

## Conquest and survival

In 1519, [Hernán Cortés](spanish-conquest) landed with a few hundred Spanish soldiers. Thousands of Tlaxcalans and other enemies of the Aztecs joined him. A [smallpox](smallpox) [epidemic](disease-history), brought from Europe, killed huge numbers of people who had no immunity to it. In 1521, Tenochtitlan fell.

Mexico City was built on its ruins. Yet Aztec and Maya heritage is everywhere. Words such as chocolate, [tomato](tomatoes) and avocado come from Nahuatl, the Aztec language, which over a million Mexicans still speak.
