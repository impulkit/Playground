## The short answer

Ancient Greece was not one country but hundreds of small city-states, such as Athens, Sparta and Corinth, spread across mainland Greece, the islands and the coasts of the Mediterranean. They shared a language, gods and the [Olympic Games](oddity-ancient-olympics-weird), and often fought each other.

From them we inherited a surprising amount: [democracy](democracy-athens), theatre, the [Olympic Games](olympics) and much of Western philosophy. They also left ideas in maths and science, styles of building still copied today, and thousands of English words, from "telephone" to "dinosaur".

## Rule by the people

In 508 BCE, Athens began a system in which ordinary male citizens met to vote on laws and decisions. They called it *demokratia*, rule by the people. It left out women, enslaved people and foreigners, so only a minority could vote. Even so, the idea that citizens, not a king, should decide has shaped governments from Delhi to Washington.

## Plays, masks and philosophers

Greek theatre grew out of festivals for the god Dionysus. Writers such as Sophocles wrote tragedies about heroes and fate, and Aristophanes wrote comedies that mocked politicians. [Actors wore masks](art-masks-world), and huge open-air theatres were built into hillsides. At Epidaurus, a theatre for about 14,000 people still has such good sound that an actor speaking normally on stage can be heard in the top rows.

Philosophers asked big questions: what is a good life, what is the world made of, how can we know anything? [Socrates](socrates) questioned everyone in the marketplace. His student Plato founded a school, and Plato's student Aristotle studied everything from animals to politics. Aristotle later taught a young prince who became Alexander the Great.

## Geometry and measuring Earth

Greek mathematicians loved proof: showing step by step why something must be true. Around 300 BCE, [Euclid](euclid) wrote *Elements*, a geometry book used in schools for over 2,000 years.

Eratosthenes, a librarian in Alexandria, measured Earth's size using shadows. His answer was close to the real distance around the planet, about 40,000 km. Others studied the stars, [levers](lever-lift) and the human body. Hippocrates, often called the father of medicine, taught doctors to look for natural causes of illness, not curses.

## Columns everywhere

Greek temples stood on rows of stone columns, in styles named Doric, Ionic and Corinthian. [The Parthenon in Athens](acropolis), built for the goddess Athena, is the most famous. Its builders bent its lines very slightly so that it would look perfectly straight from below.

Look at the Supreme Court building in Washington, D.C., or many old banks, museums and colleges in India, and you will see Greek-style columns. The Lincoln Memorial is modelled on a Greek temple.

## Greeks in India

In 326 BCE, Alexander the Great crossed into Punjab and fought King Porus near the Jhelum River. His tired army refused to march further east, and he turned back. Soon after, [Chandragupta Maurya](mauryan-empire) built a great empire in India. A Greek ambassador, Megasthenes, lived at his court in Pataliputra and wrote a book about India.

Greek kings later ruled parts of north-west India. In the region of Gandhara, sculptors mixed Greek and Indian styles, carving some of the earliest statues of [the Buddha](buddha) with wavy hair and flowing robes.

## Words and letters

The Greeks took the [alphabet of the Phoenicians](words-alphabet-order), traders from the eastern Mediterranean, and added letters for vowels. Our own [alphabet](alphabet-history) comes from theirs, through the Romans. Even the word "alphabet" is made of the first two Greek letters, alpha and beta.
