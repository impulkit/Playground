## The short answer

Napoleon Bonaparte was a French general who rose to power after the [French Revolution](french-revolution) and crowned himself Emperor of the French in 1804. Born in 1769 on the island of Corsica, he became one of the most successful military commanders in history, conquering much of Europe.

He also changed how France was governed. His Napoleonic Code set out laws that treated citizens more equally, and it shaped legal systems in many countries. But his wars cost millions of lives. After a disastrous invasion of Russia in 1812, he was defeated, exiled, returned, and finally beaten at Waterloo in 1815. He died in exile on the remote island of St Helena in 1821.

## From Corsica to commander

Napoleon was born in Ajaccio, Corsica, a year after France took over the island from Genoa. His first language was Corsican, a dialect close to Italian, and he spoke French with an accent all his life.

He trained as an artillery officer. In 1793, aged 24, he helped recapture the port of Toulon from British forces, and was quickly promoted. In 1796 he led a French army into Italy and won battle after battle. In 1798 he invaded Egypt, bringing more than a hundred scientists and scholars with him. In 1799 his soldiers found the Rosetta Stone, the key that later let scholars read Egyptian [hieroglyphs](hieroglyphs).

## Emperor of the French

In 1799 Napoleon returned to France and took power in a coup. Five years later, at Notre-Dame Cathedral in Paris, he took the crown from the Pope's hands and placed it on his own head.

He reorganised France. The Napoleonic Code of 1804 replaced a jumble of local rules with one set of laws. It ended privileges based on birth and protected property, though it gave women fewer rights than men. He set up state secondary schools called lycées, founded the Bank of France and spread use of the [metric system](metric-system). He also restored slavery in French colonies in 1802, which had been abolished in 1794.

## A deal with America

In 1803, needing money for war with Britain, Napoleon sold France's vast Louisiana Territory to the United States for 15 million dollars. It stretched from the [Mississippi river](mississippi) to the [Rocky Mountains](rocky-mountains).

The [Louisiana Purchase](usa-statehood) roughly doubled the size of the United States. Parts of 15 present-day states came from it. Today, the state of Louisiana still bases its civil law on French traditions, and the city of [New Orleans](new-orleans) keeps French street names and food.

## The road to Moscow

By 1810 Napoleon controlled or dominated much of Europe, from Spain to Poland. Only Britain, protected by its navy, held out. Napoleon also looked further east. In 1799 he wrote to [Tipu Sultan](tipu-sultan) of Mysore, promising help against the British in India.

In 1812 he invaded Russia with an army of around half a million men or more. The Russians retreated, burning crops and villages as they went. Napoleon reached Moscow, but it was largely burned and empty. Forced to retreat through a freezing winter, his army was destroyed by cold, hunger and attacks. Only a small fraction of his soldiers came back.

## Exile, return and Waterloo

In 1814 his enemies captured Paris and exiled him to the small Mediterranean island of Elba. Within a year he escaped, returned to France, and took power again for about a hundred days.

On 18 June 1815, at Waterloo in today's Belgium, he was defeated by a British-led army under the Duke of Wellington and a Prussian army under Blücher. This time the British sent him to St Helena, a tiny island in the South Atlantic, about 1,900 kilometres from Africa. He died there in 1821. His tomb now lies under the golden dome of Les Invalides in Paris.
