## The short answer

The space race was a contest between the USA and the Soviet Union, mostly from 1957 to 1969, to achieve firsts in space. It was part of the [Cold War](cold-war), when the two countries competed to prove whose system was better.

The Soviet Union took an early lead. It launched the first satellite, sent the first animal into orbit, and flew the first man and the first woman into space. The USA answered by setting itself a huge goal: landing people on the Moon. It did so in July 1969. By then the race had produced new rockets, computers and materials, and the first photos of Earth from far away.

## The beep that shocked America

On 4 October 1957, the Soviet Union launched Sputnik 1. It was a shiny metal ball about 58 cm across, with four long antennas. It did little more than send out a beeping radio signal as it circled Earth every 96 minutes. Radio fans around the world could hear it.

Americans were alarmed. If the Soviets could put a ball in orbit, could their rockets carry bombs across the world? In 1958 the USA created [NASA](nasa-centres) to run its space programme and poured money into science education.

## The first people in space

On 12 April 1961, Soviet pilot Yuri Gagarin flew once around Earth in Vostok 1, a flight of 108 minutes. He became an instant world hero. Less than a month later, Alan Shepard became the first American in space, on a short up-and-down flight.

In 1963, [Valentina Tereshkova](space-first-woman), a former textile factory worker and amateur parachutist, became the first woman in space. She circled Earth 48 times in nearly three days. In 1965 Alexei Leonov made the first spacewalk.

## "We choose to go to the Moon"

In May 1961, President John F. Kennedy asked Congress to land a man on the Moon before the decade ended. At the time, the USA had sent one astronaut into space for 15 minutes.

NASA built the giant Saturn V rocket, as tall as a 36-storey building. Around 4 lakh people worked on the Apollo programme. There were terrible setbacks, including a fire in 1967 that killed three astronauts during a test.

On 20 July 1969, Apollo 11 landed. Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walked on the Moon while Michael Collins circled overhead. About 60 crore people watched on [television](television-invented), one of the largest audiences ever at the time.

## India joins in

While the giants raced, India started small. Scientist [Vikram Sarabhai](vikram-sarabhai) chose [Thumba](space-rocket-bicycle), a fishing village near Thiruvananthapuram, because it sits close to Earth's magnetic equator, useful for studying the upper atmosphere. A local church became an office and workshop.

On 21 November 1963, a small American-made rocket lifted off from Thumba. India had joined the space age. Its programme grew into [ISRO](isro-missions). In 1984, Rakesh Sharma became the first Indian in space, aboard a Soviet spacecraft.

## The race ends

The USA landed twelve astronauts on the Moon between 1969 and 1972. The Soviet Union sent [robot rovers](tech-robots-space) there instead and turned to building space stations.

In July 1975, an American Apollo spacecraft and a Soviet Soyuz docked in orbit, and the crews [shook hands](life-handshake) through an open hatch. Rivals had become partners. Today astronauts from the USA, [Russia](russia) and many other countries live and work together on the International [Space Station](space-station).
