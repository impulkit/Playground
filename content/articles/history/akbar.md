## The short answer

Akbar was the third Mughal emperor. He ruled from 1556 to 1605, nearly 50 years, and turned a shaky kingdom into one of the strongest empires in the world.

He is called "the Great" for several reasons. He conquered and organised a huge territory. He made alliances with Hindu [Rajput](rajputs) kings and gave them high posts. He set up a fairer, better-measured land tax. Most of all, he treated people of different religions with unusual respect for his time, abolishing a tax on non-Muslims and inviting scholars of many faiths to debate. His idea of *sulh-i-kul*, "peace with all", still sounds modern. But he could also be ruthless in war.

## A boy on the throne

Akbar was born in 1542 in a desert fort in Sindh, while his father Humayun was on the run. In 1556, Humayun fell down the stairs of his library and died. Akbar, just 13, became emperor.

Within months, a general named Hemu captured [Delhi](delhi). At the Second Battle of Panipat, Hemu was hit in the eye by an arrow, his army scattered and Akbar's side won. For a few years, Akbar's guardian, Bairam Khan, ran the empire. Then the teenager took charge himself.

## Friends, wives and generals

Akbar saw that the Rajput kings of Rajasthan were too strong to beat forever. So he made alliances. In 1562 he married a Rajput princess from Amber, near today's [Jaipur](jaipur), and her family became powerful nobles. Raja Man Singh of Amber became one of his greatest generals.

Not everyone agreed. The [siege of Chittorgarh](chittorgarh) in 1568 ended with a terrible slaughter. Maharana Pratap of Mewar refused to submit and fought Akbar's army at Haldighati in 1576. He kept resisting for the rest of his life and is a hero in Rajasthan today.

## The house of worship

In 1575 Akbar built a hall at Fatehpur Sikri called the Ibadat Khana, the House of Worship. At first only Muslim scholars debated there. Later he invited Hindu pandits, Jain monks, Zoroastrian priests and [Jesuit missionaries from Goa](india-wonder-goa-history).

Akbar listened to them all. He ordered books such as the [Mahabharata](mahabharata) and [Ramayana](ramayana) translated into Persian, with rich paintings. His Persian Mahabharata is called the *Razmnama*, the "Book of War". He seems to have believed that every religion held part of the truth.

## Nine jewels at court

Stories say Akbar had nine special courtiers, the *navratnas*. The list varies, but it usually includes Birbal, famous for his wit; Tansen, the great singer; Todar Mal, who organised the land tax; and Abul Fazl, who wrote Akbar's history.

Todar Mal's system measured fields and set [taxes](taxes) based on average crops over ten years. Birbal was a real minister and friend of Akbar, though the funny stories about him were mostly made up later. Tansen's music shaped [Hindustani classical music](carnatic-hindustani) for centuries.

## A reader who couldn't read

Akbar never learned to read or write well, perhaps because he spent his childhood on the move. Yet he loved books and had them read aloud to him every day. His library held thousands of manuscripts.

He built a red sandstone city at Fatehpur Sikri, with a giant gateway called the Buland Darwaza. The court left it after about 14 years, possibly because of water shortages. Its empty palaces still stand, a UNESCO [World Heritage](unesco-sites) Site near Agra.
