## The short answer

The Vikings were seafaring people from Scandinavia, today's [Norway](norway-fjords), Denmark and Sweden, who sailed out between roughly 793 and 1066 CE. Some raided monasteries and towns. Many more traded, farmed and settled new lands.

In their fast longships they reached Britain, Ireland and France, sailed rivers deep into [Russia](russia) and Ukraine, and traded as far as Constantinople and Baghdad. They settled [Iceland](iceland) and [Greenland](world-greenland), and around 1000 CE they reached North America. The word "Viking" probably meant someone who went on sea raids. Most Scandinavians of the time were farmers, and only some went "a-viking".

## The raid that started it all

In 793, raiders landed at Lindisfarne, a monastery on an island off northeast England. They took treasures and killed or captured monks. An English scholar wrote that nobody had thought such a voyage possible.

For the next two centuries, Viking fleets raided coasts and rivers across Europe. They sacked [Paris](paris) in 845. Later, many settled where they had raided. A Viking leader named Rollo was given land in northern France around 911, which became Normandy, "the land of the Northmen".

## Ships that could go anywhere

The Viking longship was long, narrow and light, built of overlapping oak planks. With a square sail and rows of oars, it could cross open sea and then row up shallow rivers, or be dragged overland between them.

The Gokstad ship, found in a burial mound in Norway, is about 23 metres long, roughly the length of two school buses. In 1893, a copy of it crossed the Atlantic in about four weeks on its way to a world's fair in Chicago, showing how seaworthy the design was.

## Vinland, long before Columbus

Icelandic sagas tell of Leif Erikson sailing west from Greenland around 1000 CE to a land he called Vinland. For centuries, people wondered if the story was true.

In 1960, explorers Helge and Anne Stine Ingstad found the remains of Viking-style buildings at L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland, [Canada](canada). In 2021, scientists studied wood cut with metal tools at the site. A burst of radiation from the Sun in 993 left a marker in tree rings, letting them count forward to the exact year the trees were cut: 1021. That is 471 years before [Columbus](columbus) crossed the Atlantic.

## A Buddha in Sweden

Vikings traded furs, [amber](amber-fossils), walrus ivory and slaves for silver, silk and spices. Tens of thousands of Arabic silver coins have been found in Scandinavia.

On Helgö, an island in a lake near Stockholm, archaeologists in 1954 dug up a small bronze statue of the [Buddha](buddha), made in northern South Asia around the 6th century. It reached a Swedish trading settlement that later became part of the Viking world, passed along trade routes from hand to hand.

## Words and gods that remain

Vikings worshipped gods such as Odin, Thor and Freya. Three English days of the week are named after Norse gods: Wednesday after Woden, or Odin; Thursday after Thor; and Friday after Frigg. English also borrowed Norse words such as sky, egg, knife and window.

They wrote with letters called runes, carved on stones and sticks. In Iceland, Viking settlers founded an assembly called the Althing in 930, which still meets as the country's parliament. The Viking Age ended around 1066, as Scandinavian kingdoms became Christian and settled into European ways.
