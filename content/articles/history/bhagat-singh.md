## The short answer

Bhagat Singh was a young revolutionary who fought British rule in India in the 1920s. He believed that non-violent protest alone would not free India, and he joined a group that used armed action.

He is best known for two acts. In 1928 he and his comrades shot a British police officer in Lahore to avenge the death of the leader Lala Lajpat Rai. In 1929 he threw harmless bombs into the Central Legislative Assembly in Delhi and let himself be arrested. In jail he led a long hunger strike and wrote essays that are still read today. He was hanged in Lahore in 1931, aged only 23. For many Indians, he became a symbol of courage and sacrifice in the [freedom struggle](freedom-struggle).

## A family of rebels

Bhagat Singh was born in 1907 in Banga, a village in [Punjab](punjab) that is now in Pakistan. His father and uncles were part of the freedom movement, and his uncle Ajit Singh had been exiled by the British.

In April 1919, when he was eleven, British troops killed hundreds of unarmed people at [Jallianwala Bagh](jallianwala) in Amritsar. Bhagat Singh is said to have visited the site afterwards, and the massacre affected him deeply. As a teenager, he left college to join the movement.

## A death avenged

In 1928, a British commission arrived in India to discuss reforms, with no Indian members. Protesters in Lahore marched against it, led by Lala Lajpat Rai. Police beat him with lathis, and he died a few weeks later.

Bhagat Singh's group, the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association, decided to take revenge on the police superintendent they blamed. On 17 December 1928, they shot a young officer named John Saunders by mistake. Bhagat Singh escaped Lahore in disguise, wearing Western clothes and a hat, and with his hair cut.

## Bombs that were meant not to kill

On 8 April 1929, Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt entered the Central Legislative Assembly in Delhi, the building that later became India's [Parliament](parliament-india) House. They threw two small bombs into empty spaces between the benches. No one was killed.

They shouted "Inquilab Zindabad!", "Long live revolution!", and showered leaflets. One leaflet said that it takes a loud voice to make the deaf hear. Then they stood and waited for the police.

## A voice from prison

During his trial, Bhagat Singh was linked to the Saunders killing too. In prison, he and other inmates began a hunger strike to demand better treatment for political prisoners, such as books, newspapers and decent food. His comrade Jatin Das died after 63 days without food. Bhagat Singh kept going for about 116 days.

He read constantly, borrowing books on history, politics and philosophy. His prison notebook is full of quotes from writers around the world. He wrote essays explaining his ideas, including "Why I Am an Atheist".

## The last day

Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were sentenced to death. Many Indians begged for mercy, and [Gandhi](gandhi) raised their case with the Viceroy. Whether Gandhi could have done more is still argued about.

On 23 March 1931, the three were hanged in Lahore jail, a day earlier than planned. Their bodies were secretly cremated near Hussainiwala on the Sutlej river. A memorial now stands there, close to the border with [Pakistan](pakistan-history), and 23 March is marked as Martyrs' Day in their memory.
