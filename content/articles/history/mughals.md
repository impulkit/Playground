## The short answer

The Mughals were a [dynasty](?dynasty) of emperors who ruled much of the Indian subcontinent from 1526 until the 1700s, and in name until 1857. They were Muslims, descended from the Central Asian conquerors Timur and [Genghis Khan](genghis-khan).

At its biggest, around 1700, their empire covered almost all of today's India, Pakistan and [Bangladesh](bangladesh), and part of Afghanistan. It was one of the richest and most populous states in the world. The Mughals built the [Taj Mahal](taj-mahal) and the Red Fort, supported painters, poets and musicians, and left a mark on everything from Indian food to the [Hindi](hindi-language) and Urdu languages. After about 1720, their power faded, and the British took over.

## A prince who lost everything

Babur was born in 1483 in Fergana, a valley in today's Uzbekistan. He became a king at 11, lost his kingdom, won Samarkand and lost it again. Eventually he settled in Kabul.

From there he raided India. In 1526, at Panipat near Delhi, his small army defeated the last [Delhi Sultan](delhi-sultanate), Ibrahim Lodi, using cannons and guns. Babur wrote an unusually honest diary called the *Baburnama* in his own Turkic language. He describes battles, gardens, animals and his longing for the fruits of home.

## Lost, then found again

Babur died in 1530. His son Humayun lost the empire to the Afghan ruler Sher Shah Suri and spent fifteen years in exile. Sher Shah used his short reign well, building roads, rest houses and a new silver coin called the rupiya, ancestor of today's rupee.

Humayun won back Delhi in 1555 but died months later, falling down the stairs of his library. His tomb in Delhi, with its garden laid out in four parts, inspired the Taj Mahal.

## Four great emperors

Humayun's son [Akbar](akbar) ruled for nearly 50 years and made the empire strong. He worked with [Rajput](rajputs) kings and tried to bring people of different faiths together.

Jahangir, his son, loved nature and art and had painters record birds, flowers and animals in fine detail. Shah Jahan built the Taj Mahal in Agra as a tomb for his wife Mumtaz Mahal, and a new walled city in Delhi with the Red Fort. Aurangzeb ruled for 49 years and pushed the empire to its largest size. He was a strict Muslim ruler. Historians still argue about his policies, including the return of the jizya tax on non-Muslims and the wars he fought for decades in the Deccan.

## Gardens, paintings and food

The Mughals loved gardens divided into four parts by water channels, called *charbagh*. You can see them at Humayun's Tomb, the Taj Mahal and the Shalimar Bagh in Srinagar.

Court painters made tiny, detailed [miniature paintings](mughal-miniatures). Persian, Central Asian and Indian cooking blended into rich dishes such as kormas and kebabs. Many words in Hindi and Urdu came from the Persian spoken at court.

## How the empire faded

After Aurangzeb died in 1707, the empire weakened. Provinces broke away, the [Marathas](marathas) grew powerful, and in 1739 the Persian ruler Nader Shah sacked Delhi and carried off the jewel-covered [Peacock Throne](peacock-tail).

The [East India Company](east-india-company) gradually took control. By 1857, the emperor Bahadur Shah Zafar, a poet, ruled little beyond the Red Fort. After the revolt that year, the British sent him into exile in Rangoon, where he died in 1862. The dynasty ended there, far from Fergana and far from Delhi.
