## The short answer

The fastest animal on Earth is the [peregrine falcon](sky-peregrine). It flies high, spots a pigeon or duck below, folds its wings and dives. This dive is called a stoop. Peregrines have been measured at well over 320 km/h, faster than a car on any highway. That is close to 90 metres every second, fast enough to cross a cricket ground in under two seconds.

On land, the record goes to the cheetah, which can reach about 100 km/h, but only for a short sprint of a few hundred metres. In India, the [blackbuck](wild-blackbuck) antelope can run at around 80 km/h. The [fastest humans](body-speed-limit) reach about 45 km/h for a few seconds. To see how all these numbers are worked out, read about [speed and distance](speed-distance).

## Built for the dive

At such speeds, air slams into a bird's nostrils very hard. Peregrines have small bony cones inside their nostrils, which are thought to slow and steer the air so they can keep breathing. A see-through third eyelid sweeps across each eye like a windscreen wiper, keeping it clean and moist.

The falcon's body is smooth and pointed, with stiff feathers that stay flat in the rushing air. Near the end of the dive it opens its wings slightly to steer, then strikes its prey with its feet. Engineers studying [how planes fly](airplanes-fly) have looked closely at its shape.

## Falcons in the city

Peregrines live on every continent except Antarctica. Like [bald eagles](bald-eagle), they nearly vanished from much of North America in the 1960s because of the pesticide DDT, and came back after it was banned.

Some now [live in cities](urban-wildlife). Tall buildings are a lot like cliffs, and cities are full of pigeons. [New York](new-york) has around 30 nesting pairs on [skyscrapers](skyscrapers) and bridges. In India, a resident form called the shaheen falcon nests on cliffs, and other peregrines arrive from the north each winter. Some cities put nest boxes and webcams on tall buildings, so that people can watch the chicks grow.

## Why speed is hard to measure

Measuring a wild animal's top speed is tricky. Animals rarely run flat out in front of a scientist with a stopwatch. Many older records came from people chasing animals in cars, or simply guessing by eye, so they can be too high or too low.

Today scientists use radar guns, very fast cameras and collars with GPS. In 2013, researchers put GPS collars on wild cheetahs in Botswana and recorded hundreds of hunting runs. The fastest was about 93 km/h, a little lower than the figure often quoted. The study showed that a cheetah's real skill is how quickly it can speed up, brake and turn. A falcon's dive is easier to measure, because scientists can film a trained bird diving from a known height.

## The fastest runners

The [cheetah](cheetah-return) is a sprinter. Its long tail works like a rudder in sharp turns. It accelerates quickly but tires within a minute and must rest before it can eat. India brought cheetahs from Africa to Kuno National Park in [Madhya Pradesh](madhya-pradesh) in 2022.

Over long distances, the pronghorn of North America is the champion. It can keep up a fast pace for many kilometres. In the sea, the sailfish is often called the fastest fish at around 110 km/h. Newer studies of its muscles suggest that number is far too high. Measured by body size, a tiny mite from California may beat them all: it runs over 300 times its own body length every second.
