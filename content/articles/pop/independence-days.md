## The short answer

India celebrates Independence Day on 15 August, the day in 1947 when British rule ended. The USA celebrates on 4 July, the day in 1776 when its Congress approved the [Declaration of Independence](usa-wonder-philadelphia) from Britain. Both countries broke away from the same empire, 171 years apart.

The celebrations look different. In India, the Prime Minister raises the flag at the [Red Fort](red-fort) in Delhi and gives a speech, schools hold flag ceremonies, and the sky fills with kites. In the USA, people hold [parades](parades), family barbecues and picnics, and end the day watching fireworks. India also has a second great national day, Republic Day, on 26 January.

## Midnight in Delhi

India became independent at midnight between 14 and 15 August 1947. In the [Constituent Assembly](constituent-assembly) in New Delhi, Jawaharlal [Nehru](nehru) gave his speech beginning "Long years ago we made a tryst with destiny". It was a day of joy, but also of great sorrow, because the [Partition](partition) of [India and Pakistan](pakistan-history) forced millions from their homes.

The next morning, 16 August, Nehru raised the flag at the Red Fort. Every year since, the Prime Minister has done the same there on 15 August. After the flag goes up, the national anthem is sung, a 21-gun salute is fired, and the Prime Minister speaks to the country.

## Kites and sweets

In Delhi and some other northern cities, 15 August is [kite day](pop-kites-patang). Rooftops in Old Delhi are crowded from morning, and many kites are printed in saffron, white and green. It's a cousin of the January [kite festival](kites-festival) of Gujarat.

Schools across India hold flag hoistings, sing patriotic songs and hand out sweets. Buildings are lit up in the colours of the [flag](flags) at night, and many families watch the Red Fort speech live on TV.

## Republic Day

India's Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950, making India a republic, a country led by elected representatives with no king or queen. That date was chosen because on 26 January 1930, the Indian National Congress had declared the goal of full independence, "Purna Swaraj".

Republic Day brings a [grand parade](republic-day-parade) in New Delhi along Kartavya Path, the avenue once called Rajpath. Soldiers march, aircraft fly past, and decorated floats from each state roll by. The President takes the salute. Three days later, the Beating Retreat ceremony, with military bands, closes the celebrations.

## The Fourth of July

In 1776, the 13 American colonies were in the middle of the [American Revolution](american-revolution) against Britain. On 2 July, the Continental Congress voted for independence, and on 4 July it approved the Declaration, mostly written by [Thomas Jefferson](thomas-jefferson). It said that "all men are created equal", words people have argued over and fought for ever since.

The first anniversary in 1777, in Philadelphia, already had fireworks and bells. Today, Americans hang flags, march in parades, hold picnics, and watch fireworks shows over cities and lakes. In New York City, a famous [hot-dog](burgers-hotdogs) eating contest takes place on Coney Island.

## A strange coincidence

John Adams and Thomas Jefferson both helped create the Declaration. Later, both became President. They were rivals in politics but became friends again in old age, writing long letters to each other.

Both men died on 4 July 1826, exactly 50 years after the Declaration was approved, a few hours apart. Adams's last words were reported to be "Thomas Jefferson survives". He did not know that Jefferson had died a few hours earlier.
