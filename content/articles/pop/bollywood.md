## The short answer

Bollywood is the Hindi-language film industry based in Mumbai. It began in 1913, when a printer and photographer named Dhundiraj Govind Phalke, later known as [Dadasaheb Phalke](dadasaheb-phalke), made *Raja Harishchandra*, India's first full-length feature film. It was silent and in black and white, and it told a story from Indian mythology.

The name "Bollywood" came much later. It mixes Bombay, the old name of [Mumbai](mumbai), with Hollywood, and it became common in the 1970s. Today the industry makes hundreds of films a year and is watched from Lagos to London.

## A man who pawned his wife's jewellery

Phalke watched a film about the life of Jesus in a Bombay hall around 1910. He came out wondering why India did not make films about its own gods and heroes. He travelled to London to buy a camera and learn the trade.

Money was always short. Phalke borrowed, and his wife Saraswatibai sold her jewellery to help pay for the film. She also worked behind the scenes, developing film and cooking for the cast. When no woman would play the queen, a young man named Anna Salunke took the role. *Raja Harishchandra* opened in May 1913 and was a hit.

## The films learn to speak

For almost 20 years, Indian films were silent. Then in 1931 came *Alam Ara*, directed by Ardeshir Irani, India's first "talkie", or film with sound. It had seven songs. Audiences loved them, and [film songs](film-songs) have been part of Hindi cinema ever since.

Sound changed everything. Films now had to choose a language, and Bombay's studios settled on Hindustani, a mix of Hindi and Urdu understood across much of north India. Other cities, such as Madras and Calcutta, built strong film industries in Tamil, Telugu and Bengali.

## The golden years

The 1950s and 1960s are often called Hindi cinema's golden age. Directors such as [Raj Kapoor](raj-kapoor), Guru Dutt and Bimal Roy made films about poverty, love and a newly independent country. *[Mother India](bollywood-classics)* (1957) was nominated for an Oscar for Best Foreign Language Film. Playback singers such as [Lata Mangeshkar](lata-mangeshkar) recorded the songs, while actors moved their lips on screen.

In 1975 came *Sholay*, an action film set in a dusty village. It ran in one Bombay cinema, Minerva, for about five years without a break.

## How a Bollywood film is made today

A big Bollywood film looks very different from Phalke's. It may be shot in [Switzerland](switzerland), London and [Ladakh](ladakh), with crews of hundreds. The songs are often recorded before filming, so dancers can perform to the music. Computers add [special effects](special-effects), and [dubbing](dubbing-subtitles) artists may record new voices in Tamil or Telugu for release in other states.

Many studios and sets are at Film City in Goregaon, a large stretch of land on the edge of Sanjay Gandhi National Park, in north Mumbai. Leopards have occasionally wandered onto the sets.

## Remembering the founder

The Government of India's highest award for cinema, started in 1969, is named the Dadasaheb Phalke Award. Phalke himself made more than 90 films, but he died in 1944, poor and largely forgotten, just as talkies had made his silent style old-fashioned. Many of his films have been lost. The fragments that survive are looked after by the National Film Archive of India in Pune.
