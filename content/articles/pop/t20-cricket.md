## The short answer

T20 is a short form of cricket in which each side bats for just 20 overs. It began in England in 2003, to bring crowds back to county cricket by fitting a whole match into an evening.

It caught on fast. The first T20 World Cup was held in 2007, India won it, and a year later the [Indian Premier League](ipl) began. Today T20 is the most-watched form of cricket on Earth.

## A game for busy people

For more than a century, top cricket meant [Test matches](test-cricket), lasting up to five days, and later one-day matches of 50 overs each. Both took most of a working day or more. In England, crowds at county matches had shrunk.

So the England and Wales Cricket Board looked for a shorter game. A marketing manager, Stuart Robertson, asked fans what they wanted, and the answer was a match that took about three hours, after work or school. The first professional Twenty20 matches were played in June 2003, with music, fireworks and families in the stands.

## Special rules

A T20 match follows the same basic [rules](cricket-rules) as other cricket, with some twists. In the first six overs, called the powerplay, only two fielders may stand outside the inner circle, so batters attack. A no-ball gives the batter a free hit, off which they cannot be bowled or caught out. Each bowler may bowl only four overs.

Because there are so few balls, every run counts. [Strike rate](cricket-stats), the runs scored per 100 balls, matters as much as a batter's average. Strong, light [bats](cricket-bat) and big hitters have pushed scores higher and higher.

## From county fields to the world

The first men's T20 international was played in Auckland, New Zealand, in 2005. In 2007, India, led by [MS Dhoni](ms-dhoni), won the first T20 World Cup in South Africa. India's men won it again in 2024 and 2026.

The IPL, started in 2008, turned T20 into a giant show with players from many countries. T20 also helped cricket enter the Olympics: it returns at the 2028 Games in Los Angeles in this format. The short game's [momentum](?momentum) keeps growing, with new records set almost every season and a steady stream of young [tournament](?tournament) stars.
