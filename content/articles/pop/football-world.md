## The short answer

Football is the world's most popular sport because almost anyone can play it, almost anywhere. You need a ball, or something like one, and two things to mark a goal. The basic rule, get the ball in the net without using your hands, is something a four-year-old understands.

It also spread early. British sailors, soldiers, traders and teachers carried it around the world in the 19th century, and local people made it their own. Today FIFA, the world body, has 211 member associations, more than the [United Nations](united-nations) has member countries.

## Cheap to play, easy to start

Cricket needs bats, pads and a pitch. Hockey needs sticks. [Basketball](basketball) needs a hoop. Football needs only a ball, and children in many countries make their own from rags, plastic bags or rolled-up socks. Some of the world's greatest players learned on dusty streets, not grass.

A game can have three players a side or eleven. It works on a beach, in a school corridor or on a muddy field in the rain. Nobody has to buy anything before they can join in.

## Few goals, big feelings

A football match often ends 1–0, or even 0–0. That sounds dull, but it makes every goal precious. Fans can hold their breath for 90 minutes waiting for one moment. A weaker team can defend all match, score once and beat a famous one, which happens far more often than in sports with high scores.

That tension is a large part of the appeal. Each match is a story that nobody knows the ending of.

## From China to London

People have kicked balls for thousands of years. FIFA recognises cuju, played in [ancient China](ancient-china) more than 2,000 years ago, as the earliest known form. In medieval England whole villages played rough "mob football" through the streets.

In 1863, men from clubs and schools met at a tavern in London and wrote one [set of rules](football-rules). They called their organisation the Football Association. The game became "association football", shortened by students to "assoc", then "soccer". That is the name Americans still use, since their own [football](american-football) is a different game.

## India's barefoot heroes

Football reached India with the British, and Bengal took to it fiercely. In 1911 Mohun Bagan, a club from Calcutta, beat a British army team to win the IFA Shield. Most of the Indian players played barefoot. Coming at a time of anger against British rule, the win was celebrated as a national triumph.

The rivalry between Mohun Bagan and East Bengal still fills stadiums in [Kolkata](kolkata). India won the football gold at the Asian Games in 1951 and 1962 and finished fourth at the 1956 Olympics.

## A game the whole world watches

The men's World Cup is the most watched sporting event on Earth. FIFA says about 1.5 billion people watched the 2022 final in Qatar. In 2026 the tournament was shared by the USA, Canada and Mexico and grew to 48 teams for the first time.

In the United States, the women's national team has won four Women's World Cups and made football one of the most popular sports for American girls. Scientists study how players [bend a free kick around a wall](sport-science-football-curve), using the same physics that makes a [baseball curve](baseball-curve). Clubs now hire [sports scientists](sports-science) to track every sprint. Yet the game a child plays with a paper ball in a lane is, in every way that matters, the same game.
