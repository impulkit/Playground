## The short answer

The Youth Olympic Games are the Olympics for teenagers. Athletes aged about 15 to 18 from around the world compete in many of the same sports as the grown-up [Olympics](olympics).

They are held every four years, with summer and winter editions. The first Summer Youth Olympics took place in Singapore in 2010. The fourth, Dakar 2026 in Senegal, was the first Olympic event ever held in Africa.

## Why have a teenage Olympics?

The idea came from Jacques Rogge, who led the International Olympic Committee. He wanted young athletes to get a taste of the Olympics early, and to learn about health, fair play and other cultures along the way.

Many stars of the main Olympics first competed at the Youth Games. They get used to big crowds, new countries and huge pressure while they are still young. The Games also try out new events, such as mixed teams of boys and girls, and sports like breaking and 3x3 basketball before they reach the main Olympics.

## India at the Youth Games

India's young athletes have won medals in sports like shooting, weightlifting, wrestling and archery. In 2026, 16-year-old Dhinidhi Desinghu from Bengaluru won India's first Youth Olympics swimming medal, a bronze in the 100 m freestyle. At 14, she had been the youngest member of [India's team](olympics-india) at the Paris 2024 Olympics.

She joins a growing list of young [women athletes](sports-women-india) making history for India.

## The science of a fast swim

Freestyle is usually front crawl, the fastest swimming stroke. Swimmers stretch out long and flat to cut down [drag](?drag), the push of water against the body. Strong kicks and arm pulls need power and [stamina](?stamina).

Breathing matters too. Sprinters turn their heads only every few strokes, because each breath costs a tiny bit of speed. Learning to [hold your breath](body-breath-hold) calmly is part of [swimming](swimming) training.
