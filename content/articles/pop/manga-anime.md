## The short answer

Manga are Japanese comics, and anime is Japanese animation. In Japan, both are enjoyed by people of all ages, not just children. Manga cover every subject you can think of: sports, cooking, history, school life, [robots](robots), ghosts and even business.

Many anime series begin as popular manga. The characters often have large, expressive eyes and spiky or colourful hair, and the stories may run for hundreds of episodes. Today shows such as *Doraemon*, *[Pokémon](pokemon)* and *Shin-chan* are watched by children in India, the USA and around the world.

## Reading backwards

[Japanese writing](japanese-writing) is traditionally read from top to bottom and right to left. So manga open from what English readers think of as the back, and the panels on each page are read from right to left too.

When manga were first translated, publishers flipped the pictures so they read left to right. That made right-handed characters left-handed and turned signs into mirror writing. Today most translated manga keep the Japanese order, and the last page often has a note saying "Stop, you're reading the wrong way."

## The god of manga

The word "manga" is old. The artist Katsushika Hokusai, famous for his woodblock print of a [giant wave](waves-how), used it in 1814 for books of his lively sketches. Modern manga took off after the [Second World War](world-war-two).

The most important early artist was Osamu Tezuka, often called "the god of manga". He loved American cartoons and borrowed their large eyes, then added the fast pace and close-ups of films. His robot boy *Astro Boy* became a manga in 1952 and a weekly TV cartoon in 1963. To save money, his studio reused drawings and moved only mouths and eyes, a style still seen in anime today. You can compare it with [how cartoons are made](animation-how).

## Studio Ghibli

In 1985, the directors Hayao Miyazaki and Isao Takahata helped found [Studio Ghibli](studio-ghibli) in Tokyo. Its hand-drawn films, such as *My Neighbour Totoro* and [*Spirited Away*](myths-japanese), are known for detailed nature, quiet moments and brave young heroes.

*Spirited Away* won the Oscar for Best Animated Feature in 2003, the first film not in English to win that award. Ghibli artists still draw much of their work by hand, a style that goes back to the [earliest cartoons](cartoons-history).

## Anime in India

Indian children first met anime through dubbed TV shows. *[The Jungle Book](jungle-book)*, a Japanese series, was shown on Doordarshan in Hindi in the 1990s. Later came *Doraemon*, about a blue robot cat from the future, and *Shin-chan*, a naughty five-year-old.

Japan and India also made a film together. *Ramayana: The Legend of Prince Rama* was directed by Yugo Sako with Indian artists and writers, and was released in the early 1990s. It retells the [Ramayana](ramayana) in anime style and has gathered many fans since.

## A huge industry

Manga are sold in Japan in thick weekly magazines, often more than 300 pages, which readers flip through on trains and then leave behind. At its peak in the mid-1990s, *Weekly Shōnen Jump* sold over six million copies a week.

Anime now has fans across the world who learn Japanese words, draw their own manga and dress up as characters at conventions. Japan's government even calls anime and manga part of its "soft power", the way a country wins friends through culture rather than force.
