## The short answer

[The ancient Olympic Games](oddity-ancient-olympics-weird) began in Olympia, Greece, traditionally in 776 BCE. They were held every four years for over 1,000 years. Athletes ran races, wrestled, threw the discus and raced [chariots](wheel-to-chariot). Winners got a crown of olive leaves.

The modern Olympics started in Athens in 1896, led by a French educator named Pierre de Coubertin. Today the Summer Games bring together over 10,000 athletes from more than 200 teams, and the Winter Games, first held in 1924, add skiing, skating and ice hockey.

## The ancient Games

The Games were held at Olympia in honour of Zeus, king of the [Greek gods](greek-myths). For the first few Games, there may have been only one event: a sprint of one length of the stadium, about 192 metres, called the stadion. Over time, events were added: longer races, boxing, wrestling, a race in armour and chariot racing.

Only free Greek men and boys could compete, and most athletes competed naked. Married women were not allowed to watch. Yet a Spartan princess named Kyniska became an Olympic champion, because in chariot races the prize went to the owner of the horses, not the driver. Her team won in 396 BCE and again four years later.

The ancient Games faded away in the late 300s CE, when the Roman Empire turned Christian and pagan festivals were ended. For about 1,500 years, there were none.

## The modern Games

Coubertin wanted sport to build healthy young people and bring nations together. The first modern Games, in Athens in 1896, had about 240 athletes, all men, from 14 countries. Winners did not get gold medals. They got a silver medal and an olive branch. Women first competed in 1900, in Paris.

Many traditions are newer than people think. [The Olympic flame](pop-olympic-torch) was first lit at the 1928 Games in Amsterdam. The torch relay from Olympia began in 1936.

## The rings

The five rings were designed in 1913. Coubertin said every country's flag of the time had at least one of the six colours: blue, yellow, black, green, red and the white background. The rings first flew at the 1920 Games in Antwerp, Belgium.

## India and the USA at the Olympics

India first sent a proper team in 1920. Its [hockey teams](hockey-rules) won eight Olympic gold medals, starting in 1928, led by players such as [Dhyan Chand](dhyan-chand). Abhinav Bindra won India's first individual gold in shooting in 2008, and [Neeraj Chopra](neeraj-chopra) won gold in javelin at the Tokyo Games, then silver in Paris in 2024. At Paris, shooter Manu Bhaker won two bronze medals, the first Indian since independence to win two medals at one Games.

The USA has won more Olympic medals than any other country, and [Los Angeles](los-angeles) hosts the Summer Games in 2028. Those Games will include [cricket](cricket-history), for the first time since 1900, when Britain beat France in Paris in the only Olympic cricket match ever played.

## Could you beat a cheetah?

The fastest humans reach about 44 km/h. Some [animals](fastest-animal) are far, far faster. A cheetah can sprint at up to about 100 km/h for a short burst, more than twice [Usain Bolt](usain-bolt)'s top speed. During a sprint, a trained athlete's [heart](heart-beat) can beat close to 200 times a minute, pumping blood to [muscles](muscles) that burn energy fast.
