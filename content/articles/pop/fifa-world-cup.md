## The short answer

The FIFA World Cup began in 1930 in Uruguay. Football's world body, FIFA, wanted a tournament open to professional players from every country. Only amateurs were allowed to play at the [Olympics](olympics), and many of the best players were now paid. Its president, a Frenchman named Jules Rimet, pushed the idea for years.

Only 13 teams took part, and Uruguay won. Today the tournament is held every four years, and the 2026 edition had 48 teams, with qualifying matches involving over 200 countries.

## Why Uruguay?

Uruguay was a small country with a big football reputation. It had won the Olympic football gold in 1924 and 1928. It also offered to pay the travel and hotel costs of visiting teams, and 1930 was the hundredth anniversary of its first constitution.

The capital, Montevideo, built a new stadium for the event, named Estadio Centenario after the anniversary. It was not quite finished when the tournament began, so the first matches were played elsewhere in the city.

## The long voyage

There were no long-distance passenger flights in 1930. Teams from Europe had to sail across the Atlantic, a trip of about two weeks. Most European football associations said no.

Only four went: France, Belgium, Romania and Yugoslavia. Several of them travelled on the same [ship](floating-ships), training on deck as it crossed the ocean. The rest of the field came from the Americas, including the United States, which reached the semi-finals. Frenchman Lucien Laurent scored the first World Cup goal, against Mexico.

## A final with two balls

Uruguay met neighbour [Argentina](argentina) in the final. Thousands of Argentine fans crossed the River Plate by boat to watch. The teams argued over the ball, so they played one half with each.

Uruguay won 4–2. The country declared the next day a national holiday. In Buenos Aires, across the river, angry crowds threw stones at the Uruguayan consulate, and the two football associations stopped speaking for a while.

## A trophy with adventures

[The original gold trophy](pop-world-cup-trophy), later named after Jules Rimet, had an eventful life. During the [Second World War](world-war-two), an Italian football official is said to have hidden it in a shoebox under his bed to keep it from the occupying army. No World Cups were held in 1942 or 1946.

In 1966, months before England hosted the tournament, the trophy was stolen from an exhibition in London. A week later a black-and-white collie called Pickles sniffed it out under a garden hedge. Brazil won it for the third time in 1970 and kept it, but it was stolen again in 1983 and never recovered.

## Growing every time

The World Cup grew from 13 teams to 16, then 24, then 32 in 1998. In 2026 the USA, [Canada](canada) and [Mexico](mexico) hosted the first 48-team World Cup, with 104 matches. Mexico became the first country to host the men's tournament three times, after 1970 and 1986.

The Women's World Cup began in 1991 in China, and the United States won the first title. Brazil has the most men's titles, five, while Italy and [Germany](germany) have four each.

India has never played at a men's World Cup. It qualified for 1950 after other teams in its group withdrew, but did not travel to Brazil. Many Indian fans still wonder what might have happened if it had.
