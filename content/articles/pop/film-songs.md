## The short answer

Indian cinema grew out of traditions where stories were sung and danced, like folk theatre.

Songs show feelings, dreams and celebrations in a way words alone can't. A song can show a couple falling in love across a whole season, or a hero's sorrow, in four minutes. They also give a long film breaks, like the songs in a stage show.

Playback singers record the songs, and actors lip-sync on screen. Songs are often released before the film, and a hit soundtrack can fill cinema halls.

## Songs before cinema

[Indian storytelling](storytelling-india) has long mixed words, music and dance. Sanskrit drama, temple performances, the Ramlila and village folk theatre all used song. In the 1800s, Parsi theatre companies toured India with plays full of music, drama and spectacle. Early filmmakers grew up watching them.

So when films learned to talk, it seemed natural for them to sing. *Alam Ara*, India's first talking film, made by Ardeshir Irani in 1931, had seven songs. A year later, a film called *Indrasabha* had around 70. Audiences loved them.

## The voice behind the face

At first, actors had to sing for themselves, live on set, with musicians sometimes hidden behind scenery out of camera range. That meant actors were often hired for their voices, not their acting.

In 1935, filmmakers in Calcutta began recording songs separately and having actors mime to them. This was playback [singing](singing), and it changed everything. Now the best singers could sing for any actor. Singers such as [Lata Mangeshkar](lata-mangeshkar), Mohammed Rafi, Kishore Kumar and Asha Bhosle sang thousands of songs. In 2011, Guinness [World Records](world-records) named Asha Bhosle the most recorded artist in music history.

## Radio and the countdown

In the early 1950s, India's information minister decided film songs were too low-class for [All India Radio](radio-india), and they were largely taken off the air. Listeners simply retuned to Radio Ceylon, broadcasting from Colombo in [Sri Lanka](sri-lanka). Its weekly countdown, *Binaca Geetmala*, presented by Ameen Sayani, drew huge audiences from 1952.

All India Radio soon gave in and launched Vividh Bharati in 1957, packed with film music. Songs became the soundtrack of daily life: in tea stalls, buses and wedding processions.

## Making a song today

Today a film song is usually recorded months before it is filmed. The [composer](film-music-composers), lyricist and singer work in a studio, often recording each instrument and voice separately. Then the director and a choreographer plan the dance, and the actors learn to mime every word in time with the recording.

Songs are often shot in beautiful places far from the story's setting. Director Yash Chopra filmed so many songs in the Swiss Alps that Indian tourists began visiting the same valleys.

## Composers and their sound

Music directors became stars too. S.D. Burman and his son R.D. Burman mixed Indian folk with jazz and rock. Ilaiyaraaja shaped Tamil film music with orchestras. [A.R. Rahman](ar-rahman) used synthesisers and computers to create new sounds in the 1990s, and won two Oscars in 2009 for *Slumdog Millionaire*.

In 2023, "Naatu Naatu" from the Telugu film *RRR*, composed by M.M. Keeravani, became the first song from an Indian film to win the Oscar for Best Original Song.

## Musicals around the world

[Hollywood](hollywood) once made many musicals, like *The Wizard of Oz* in 1939 and *Singin' in the Rain* in 1952, but they became less common. Indian cinema kept singing. Today some films have fewer songs, or play them in the background instead of having stars dance. But songs still carry stories, and still give listeners [the shivers](body-music-chills) that scientists link to [music and emotion](music-goosebumps).
