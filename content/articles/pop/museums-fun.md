## The short answer

There is no single strangest museum, because people find different things strange. But some real museums are very hard to beat. New Delhi has a museum about toilets. Near Boston, a museum displays art that is too bad to ignore. In Zagreb, in Croatia, a museum shows objects people kept from friendships and romances that ended.

What joins these odd places is a simple idea: almost anything can tell a story about people. A museum is a place that collects objects, looks after them and explains them, and some curators choose to do that with things nobody else bothers to keep.

## A museum of toilets

The Sulabh International Museum of Toilets opened in New Delhi in 1992. It was started by Bindeshwar Pathak, a social reformer whose organisation built cheap, clean public toilets across India.

The museum traces toilets over about 4,500 years. It begins with the drains and toilets of the [Indus cities](indus-cities), among the earliest in the world, and moves through royal commodes, flushing toilets from Europe and designs for villages without sewers. The jokes come easily, but the purpose is serious. Clean toilets protect people from disease, and many Indian homes did not have one until recently.

## Proudly bad art

The Museum of Bad Art began in 1994 near Boston, Massachusetts, when an antiques dealer found a painting sticking out of a rubbish pile. It showed an old woman dancing in a field, and it was memorably awful. Friends began collecting more.

The museum only takes works made in good faith, by people who were really trying. A painting must be bad in an interesting way, not merely dull. Visitors laugh, but many also leave thinking about what makes art good in the first place, a question that even experts on the [Mona Lisa](mona-lisa) argue about.

## Memories of endings

The Museum of Broken Relationships started in 2006 as a travelling show by two artists in Croatia, after their own relationship ended. People from many countries sent in objects, such as a toaster, a wedding dress or a garden gnome, each with a short note about what it meant.

The objects themselves are ordinary. The notes make them powerful. It shows how a museum label can change the way you see a thing.

## Two giants

Some museums are famous for their size. The Indian Museum in [Kolkata](kolkata), founded in 1814, is the oldest museum in India. It holds ancient sculptures, [fossils](fossils-form), [meteorites](meteorites) and an Egyptian mummy.

[The Smithsonian](smithsonian) in [Washington, D.C.](washington-dc) began with a puzzle. In 1829 James Smithson, an English scientist, died and left his fortune to the United States, a country he had never visited. Gold coins worth over half a million dollars crossed the Atlantic by ship. Congress argued for years, then founded the Smithsonian in 1846. Today it runs about 20 museums and the National Zoo, and most are free to enter.

## Small and personal

Many towns have small, unusual museums too: of kites, spoons, typewriters, umbrellas or vintage cars. Some began as one person's hobby that slowly filled a house, then a garage, then a building of its own. Old [toys](toys-history), in particular, fill dozens of museums, because grown-ups love seeing what they once played with. Shankar's International Dolls Museum in Delhi holds thousands of dolls from many countries. A museum does not need a palace. It needs an object, someone who cares about it, and a label that makes you look twice.
