## The short answer

South Korea is a country on the southern half of the Korean Peninsula in East Asia, between China and Japan. It has about 5.2 crore people, and around half of them live in and around the capital, Seoul. The country is mountainous, with hot, wet summers and freezing winters.

Its story is one of the fastest turnarounds in history. After the Korean War ended in 1953, it was among the poorest countries in the world. Today it builds smartphones, cars and huge ships, has some of the fastest internet anywhere, and sends [K-pop](kpop) songs, films and dramas around the planet. Old palaces, Buddhist temples and kimchi pots sit right next to glass towers.

## A divided peninsula

Korea was a single kingdom for centuries. Japan ruled it from 1910 to 1945. After the Second World War, the peninsula was split between areas occupied by the Soviet Union in the north and the USA in the south. In 1950 the North invaded the South, and the Korean War drew in armies from many countries, part of the [Cold War](cold-war).

The fighting stopped in 1953 with an armistice, an agreement to stop fighting, but no peace treaty was ever signed. A strip of land about 4 kilometres wide, the Demilitarised Zone or DMZ, still divides the two Koreas. With few people inside it for 70 years, it has become a refuge for cranes and other wildlife. Seoul is only about 50 kilometres from that border.

## An alphabet made by a king

For centuries, Koreans wrote using [Chinese characters](chinese-characters), which took years to learn. In the 1440s King Sejong and his scholars created a new alphabet, Hangul, so that ordinary people could read and write. He ruled from Gyeongbokgung, the grand palace in Seoul first built in 1395. It was burned during an invasion in the 1590s and rebuilt in the 1860s, and today visitors who arrive dressed in hanbok, traditional Korean clothes, get in free. Sejong announced the new alphabet in 1446. Its letter shapes are based on the shape of the mouth and tongue making each sound.

South Korea celebrates Hangul Day every 9 October. Today almost everyone in the country can read, and Hangul is often praised by experts on the [history of the alphabet](alphabet-history) as one of the most logical scripts ever designed.

## Kimchi and the warm floor

Kimchi, spicy fermented cabbage with radish, garlic and chilli, appears at almost every meal. In late autumn, families and neighbours gather to make huge batches together, a tradition called kimjang that UNESCO lists as part of humanity's cultural heritage. The vegetables slowly [ferment](fermentation) through the winter, turning sour and tangy, a little like an Indian [achar](pickles-achar).

Korean homes have long been heated from below. In the old ondol system, smoke from a kitchen fire ran through channels under a stone floor. Modern homes use hot water pipes instead, which is why many Koreans sit, eat and even sleep on the warm floor.

## A river under a motorway

In the middle of Seoul there was once a stream called Cheonggyecheon. As the city grew, it was covered with concrete and an elevated motorway was built on top. In 2003 the city tore down the motorway and dug the stream back out. It reopened in 2005 as a green walkway about 11 kilometres long, cooler than the streets around it. [City planners](urban-planning) from many countries have visited to see how a road could be swapped for a river.
