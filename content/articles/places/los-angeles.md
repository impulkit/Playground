## The short answer

Los Angeles, often called LA, is the second-biggest city in the USA and the home of Hollywood, the American film industry. It is known for sunshine, beaches, long freeways and a skyline backed by mountains. Nearly 10 million people live in Los Angeles County (2024 estimate).

LA is also one of the most mixed cities in the world. People with roots in Mexico, [Korea](seoul), the Philippines, China, Armenia, Iran, India and many other places live there, and more than 200 languages are spoken. It has hosted the Olympics twice and is due to host them again in 2028.

## A small village by a river

Long before the city, the Tongva people lived in villages across the basin. In 1781, a group of 44 settlers came from New Spain, now Mexico, and founded a farming village near the Los Angeles River. They were of mixed Spanish, Indigenous and African roots. The village was named El Pueblo de la Reina de los Ángeles, the Town of the Queen of the Angels.

LA was part of Mexico until 1848, when it passed to the United States after a war. Railways, oranges and then oil helped it grow from a small town into a giant city in less than a century.

## How Hollywood happened

In the early 1900s, film companies started moving to Los Angeles from the east coast. The sun shone most of the year, which mattered when films needed natural light. Within a few hours' drive were beaches, deserts, mountains and forests, so one place could stand in for anywhere. Land was cheap, too.

By the 1920s, [Hollywood](hollywood) was the centre of American film. The Hollywood sign, with letters about 14 m tall, was put up in 1923 to sell houses. Today studios in LA make films, TV shows and [animation](animation-how) watched worldwide.

## Ice Age animals in the city

In a park in the middle of LA, sticky tar seeps up from underground at the [La Brea Tar Pits](la-brea). For tens of thousands of years, animals wandered in and got stuck. Their bones were preserved in the tar.

Scientists have found more than a million bones, including mammoths, dire wolves, giant ground sloths and over 2,000 [sabre-toothed cats](sabre-tooth). Visitors can watch scientists cleaning fossils in a glass-walled lab at the museum.

## Freeways and smog

LA spreads out over a huge area, and most people drive. Its freeways are famous for traffic jams. In the mid-1900s, exhaust from cars mixed with sunshine to form a brown haze called [smog](fog-smog). The mountains around the basin trap the air, making it worse. Strict rules on [car engines](cars-engine) have greatly cleaned the [air](air-pollution) since the 1970s, though LA still has bad days.

## Shaking ground and dry hills

Los Angeles sits on many faults near the San Andreas Fault, so [earthquakes](earthquakes) are common. The 1994 Northridge earthquake damaged freeways and buildings, and engineers learned lessons from it. Long dry seasons and hot winds called the Santa Anas also make the hills prone to [wildfires](people-firefighters-forest). In January 2025, large fires destroyed thousands of homes in and around the city.

## Stars in the sky, too

On a hill above the city stands Griffith Observatory, opened in 1935. Anyone can look through its big telescope for free. From its terrace you can see the Hollywood sign on one side and, on a clear night, the whole city sparkling below.
