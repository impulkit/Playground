## The short answer

Pune is a big city in Maharashtra, about 150 km by road south-east of [Mumbai](mumbai), on a plateau at the edge of the Western Ghats. It was once the seat of the Peshwas, who ran the Maratha Empire, and later became a centre of Indian reform and the freedom struggle.

Today it's known for its many colleges, which earned it the nickname "Oxford of the East", and for its car and motorcycle factories and software companies. It's also known for a pleasant climate, its public [Ganeshotsav](tilak), and street snacks like misal pav. Its old name, Poona, was officially changed to Pune in 1978.

## The city of the Peshwas

[Shivaji](shivaji), founder of the Maratha kingdom, spent much of his childhood in Pune in the 1630s and 1640s. A century later, the Peshwas, the chief ministers of the Maratha rulers, made Pune their capital. From here they controlled a large part of India.

In 1732, Peshwa Bajirao I completed a grand fortified palace called Shaniwar Wada. Most of it burned down in 1828, but its massive walls and gates still stand. One gate is studded with long iron spikes to stop elephants charging it. In 1818, the British defeated the Marathas, and Pune became an important British army base.

## Savitribai and Jyotirao Phule

In 1848, Jyotirao [Phule](jyotirao-phule) and his wife Savitribai opened a school for girls in Pune, at a time when very few Indian girls went to school. Savitribai became one of the first Indian women teachers. Many people were angry that girls, including girls from castes treated as low, were being taught to read.

Some threw mud at Savitribai as she walked to school, so she carried a spare sari. The Phules went on to open many more schools, and campaigned against caste discrimination and for widows' rights. Today, the University of Pune is named after Savitribai Phule, and Maharashtra celebrates her birthday, 3 January, in honour of girls' education.

## Festival and freedom

Ganesh Chaturthi was long celebrated mostly at home. In 1893, the freedom fighter Bal Gangadhar Tilak helped turn it into a huge public festival, with community pandals, music and speeches. British rules made political meetings difficult. A religious festival brought large crowds together, and ideas spread among them.

Pune's link to the freedom struggle continued. During the [Quit India](quit-india) movement in 1942, the British held Mahatma Gandhi and his wife Kasturba in the Aga Khan Palace in Pune. Kasturba died there in 1944, and her memorial stands in its gardens. Gandhi's secretary, Mahadev Desai, also died there during the same imprisonment, in 1942.

## Colleges, cameras and cars

Pune has dozens of colleges and research institutes. The Film and Television Institute of India trains actors, directors and camera operators, and many famous film people studied there. The National Defence Academy, where young officers train for the army, navy and air force, is just outside the city.

The city is also a major centre for making cars, motorcycles and machines, and later grew into a hub for software companies. Scientists at the Giant Metrewave Radio Telescope, about 80 km away, use 30 huge dish antennas, each 45 m across, to listen to radio waves from distant galaxies. On weekends, many people head out of the city to the forts and green hills of the [Western Ghats](western-ghats), climbing paths once used by Maratha soldiers.
