## The short answer

Puerto Rico is a tropical island in the Caribbean Sea, about 1,600 kilometres south-east of [Miami](miami). It has belonged to the United States since 1898, but it is not a state. It is a territory, a place governed by the USA without the full powers of a state.

Puerto Ricans have been US citizens since 1917. They can live and work anywhere in the country. But while they live on the island, they cannot vote for the president, and they send only one member to Congress, who cannot vote on final laws. About 33 lakh people live on the island, and even more people of Puerto Rican heritage live in the mainland USA. Most islanders speak [Spanish](spanish-language) at home, and many speak English too.

## Taíno, Spanish, American

The first people to live here were the Taíno, who called the island Borikén. [Columbus](columbus) arrived in 1493 on his second voyage, and Spain ruled for the next four centuries. Many Taíno died from war, forced labour and new diseases, and Spain later brought enslaved Africans to work on sugar farms. Puerto Rican culture today mixes Taíno, Spanish and African roots, from its food to its music.

To guard the harbour of San Juan, Spain built a massive stone fort, El Morro, starting in 1539. Its walls are several metres thick. In 1898, after the Spanish–American War, Spain handed Puerto Rico to the USA.

The island punches above its size in music and sport. Salsa and reggaeton grew partly here, and the 2017 hit song "Despacito" was by two Puerto Rican singers. The [baseball](baseball-world-series) star Roberto Clemente, from the town of Carolina, died in a plane crash in 1972 while carrying relief supplies to earthquake survivors in Nicaragua.

## State, country or neither

Puerto Ricans disagree about the island's future. Some want it to become the 51st [US state](states-usa). Some want to keep the current arrangement, and a smaller number want full independence. The island has held several votes on the question since the 1960s, with different results and different levels of turnout. Any change would also need the US Congress to agree, so the debate continues.

## Rainforest and glowing water

El Yunque, in the north-east, is the only tropical rainforest in the US national forest system. Its mountains catch clouds blowing in from the Atlantic, and some peaks receive several metres of rain a year. It is home to the coquí, a frog only about as long as your thumb, whose loud night-time call gave it its name.

Puerto Rico also has some of the brightest [bioluminescent](bioluminescence) bays in the world. In Mosquito Bay on Vieques, tiny single-celled creatures called dinoflagellates flash when the water moves. Fish leave glowing trails, and a hand trailed in the water looks as if it is covered in stars.

## Storms and a giant dish

The island sits in the path of Atlantic [hurricanes](hurricanes). In September 2017, Hurricane Maria struck directly. It knocked out electricity across the whole island, and some homes waited months to get it back. Thousands of people died in the months that followed, many because hospitals and medical equipment lost power.

For over fifty years, Puerto Rico was also home to the Arecibo Observatory, a radio [telescope](telescopes-how) with a dish 305 metres wide set into a bowl in the hills. It studied planets, asteroids and distant galaxies, and appeared in films. In 2020, after cables snapped, the platform above it crashed into the dish, and the telescope was not rebuilt.
