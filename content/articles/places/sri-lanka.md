## The short answer

Sri Lanka is an island nation shaped like a teardrop, just off the southern tip of India. It is famous for its tea, spices, beaches, elephants and ancient cities, and for its cricket.

The island packs a lot into a small space. It has misty hills covered in tea gardens, dry plains with huge old reservoirs, rainforest, and golden beaches all around. Most people are Sinhala, and most of them are Buddhist. There are also large Tamil, Muslim and Christian communities, and the country uses Sinhala and Tamil as official languages, with English widely spoken.

## Ceylon tea

Until 1972 the island was called Ceylon, and the name still lives on in its tea. [Coffee](coffee) was the main crop under British rule until a plant disease wiped it out in the 1870s. Planters switched to tea. A Scottish planter called James Taylor had already started the first commercial tea estate in 1867, at Loolecondera near Kandy.

Today Sri Lanka is one of the world's biggest tea exporters. Tea pluckers, many of them Tamil women whose families came from southern India in the 1800s, pick the top two leaves and a bud by hand. The cool hill country around Nuwara Eliya is sometimes called "Little England" for its cool weather and old colonial bungalows. You can trace the drink's long journey in [tea](tea).

## A palace on a rock

In the 5th century CE, a king called Kashyapa built a palace on top of Sigiriya, a rock that rises about 180 m out of the forest. Visitors climbed through the open jaws of a giant stone lion, whose huge paws still guard the stairway.

Halfway up, painted women still gaze out from a sheltered rock face. A polished wall nearby, called the Mirror Wall, is covered in poems scratched by visitors over 1,000 years ago, among the oldest [graffiti](oddity-ancient-graffiti) anywhere. Further north, the ancient cities of Anuradhapura and Polonnaruwa have giant stupas and reservoirs, called tanks, that still water rice fields.

## The sacred tree

In Anuradhapura grows the Jaya Sri Maha Bodhi, a sacred fig tree. According to tradition, Sanghamitta, a daughter of the emperor [Ashoka](ashoka), brought a cutting of the tree under which the [Buddha](buddha) found enlightenment at [Bodh Gaya](bihar-history). It was planted in 288 BCE.

Monks and pilgrims have tended it for over 2,000 years, propping up its heavy branches with golden supports. Many Hindus, too, link Sri Lanka with the island of Lanka in the [Ramayana](ramayana), and pilgrims visit sites connected with the story.

## Elephants, whales and spices

Sri Lanka has many wild Asian elephants for its size. Each year in the dry season, hundreds gather to graze around the shrinking Minneriya reservoir, an event known as "the Gathering". Off the southern coast, near Mirissa, boats take visitors to see [blue whales](blue-whale), the largest animals ever to live.

Spices drew traders here for centuries. Arab, Portuguese, Dutch and British ships came for cinnamon, which grows wild in the island's forests. In 1996, Sri Lanka won the men's Cricket World Cup, beating Australia in the final in Lahore.

## Hard times and recovery

From 1983 to 2009, Sri Lanka suffered a long civil war between the government and the Tamil Tigers, an armed group fighting for a separate Tamil state. Tens of thousands of people died, many of them civilians, and families on all sides still carry its memory. In 2004 the Indian Ocean tsunami struck its eastern and southern coasts.

In 2022, the country ran short of money for fuel, food and medicine, and protests forced the president to resign. Sri Lanka has been slowly rebuilding since.
