## The short answer

The USA is about three times the size of India, and its land changes a lot as you cross it. On the east coast there are low, worn-down mountains. In the middle there are flat grasslands so wide that the horizon looks like the sea. Then the land rises sharply into the [Rocky Mountains](rocky-mountains), falls into deserts and rises again before reaching the Pacific Ocean.

From New York to Los Angeles is about 4,000 km in a straight line, and more by road. Driving it, with stops to eat and sleep, takes about four days. Along the way you would cross four time zones and set your watch back three hours.

## Old mountains in the east

The Atlantic coast is low and flat, with long beaches, marshes and big harbours. That is why the early cities, such as Boston, New York and Philadelphia, grew there. Inland rise the [Appalachian Mountains](appalachians). They are some of the oldest mountains on Earth, formed hundreds of millions of years ago. Rain and wind have rounded them so much that their highest peak is just over 2,000 m.

Further north are the five [Great Lakes](great-lakes), shared with Canada. Together they hold about a fifth of all the fresh water lying on Earth's surface.

## The flat middle

West of the [Mississippi River](mississippi) come the Great Plains. For more than 1,000 km the land is almost flat and slowly rises towards the west. Huge herds of bison once roamed here. Today much of it is farmland growing wheat and maize, which is why it is sometimes called America's breadbasket.

The plains are also where warm, wet air from the Gulf of Mexico meets cold, dry air from Canada. When they clash in spring, they can spin up [tornadoes](tornadoes), so often that one area is nicknamed Tornado Alley.

## The young, sharp Rockies

Then the Rocky Mountains rise like a wall. They are much younger than the Appalachians, so their peaks are still sharp and many pass 4,000 m. Along their spine runs the Continental Divide. Rain falling on one side flows towards the Atlantic, and on the other side towards the Pacific.

Beyond the Rockies lies dry, high land with [salt flats](earth-salt-flats), red rock and canyons. The Colorado River cut the [Grand Canyon](grand-canyon) here. Much of this country is protected in [national parks](national-parks), so its strange shapes stay wild.

## Desert, then the Pacific

Close to the west coast stand more mountains, the [Sierra Nevada](yosemite) and the Cascades. They catch rain from the Pacific, so the land behind them is very dry. This is where you find [Death Valley](mystery-sailing-stones), the hottest and [lowest place](extreme-lowest-point) in North America. In summer it can pass 50 °C.

The Pacific coast itself is cooler, often foggy, and sits on top of active faults, which is why California has so many [earthquakes](earthquakes). Its Central Valley is one of the richest farming areas in the world. A large share of the almonds, grapes and tomatoes eaten in the USA grows there, watered by snow melting off the mountains.

## And two states far away

Two states are not joined to the rest. [Alaska](alaska), in the far north-west, holds Denali, the tallest mountain in North America. [Hawaii](hawaii-islands) is a chain of volcanic islands in the middle of the Pacific. Add them in and the USA stretches across six [time zones](time-zones). Compare that with [India](india-geography), which runs all its clocks on a single time.
