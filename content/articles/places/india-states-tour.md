## The short answer

India has 28 states and 8 union territories, and each has its own mix of landscape, language, food and festivals. A simple way to make sense of them is to think in regions. There's the mountainous north, the green northeast, the river plains of the east, the dry west, the forested centre and the southern peninsula.

Within each region, states often have their own main language. That's not an accident. In 1956, most state borders were redrawn around language areas. So when you cross from [Kerala](kerala) into Tamil Nadu, or from Gujarat into Maharashtra, the signboards usually change script.

## The north: mountains and plains

At the top of the map, the Himalaya rise above Jammu and Kashmir, [Ladakh](ladakh), Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand. Winters are snowy, houses have sloping roofs, and apples and walnuts grow on the hillsides.

Below them lie the wide, flat plains of Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, watered by rivers that start in the glaciers. These plains grow much of India's wheat and rice, and many of India's oldest cities, such as Varanasi and Patna, grew up beside their rivers. Uttar Pradesh alone had about 20 crore people at the 2011 census, more than most countries.

## The northeast: hills and rain

Eight states sit in the northeast, joined to the rest of India by a narrow strip of land. This is one of the greenest, rainiest parts of the country. [Assam grows tea](assam-tea) along the Brahmaputra river, [Meghalaya](meghalaya) has some of the wettest places on Earth, and Arunachal Pradesh reaches up into high mountains.

Dozens of communities live here, speaking many languages. Food often uses [bamboo shoots](northeast-food), smoked meats and fermented ingredients you won't find on a menu in Delhi. [Sikkim](sikkim), tucked between Nepal and Bhutan, became known for farming entirely without chemical fertilisers.

## The west and the centre

Rajasthan, India's largest state by area, is home to much of the [Thar Desert](thar-formed) and a famous ring of hilltop forts. Gujarat has a long coastline and the [salt flats](earth-salt-flats) of the [Rann of Kutch](gujarat-rann). Maharashtra holds Mumbai, the country's money capital, and the green hills of the Western Ghats.

In the middle, [Madhya Pradesh](madhya-pradesh) and Chhattisgarh are thick with forests, tigers and ancient rock paintings. [Goa, the smallest state](india-wonder-goa-history), sits on the western coast, with a long history of Portuguese rule that still shows in its churches and food.

## The east and the south

In the east, West Bengal and [Odisha](odisha) face the Bay of Bengal. Kolkata was once the capital of British India, and Odisha is known for the Konark Sun Temple and Rath Yatra.

The south is a large plateau, called the Deccan, edged by coasts. Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana each have their own Dravidian language with its own script, such as Kannada, Tamil, [Malayalam](malayalam) and Telugu. Rice, coconut and lentil batters turn up in many [southern dishes](regional-cuisines), and tall temple towers rise above the towns.

## One country, many calendars

Even the year starts differently across India. Many people in Punjab celebrate Baisakhi, in Assam [Bohag Bihu](art-bihu) and in Tamil Nadu Puthandu, all in mid-April. People in Gujarat often mark the new year the day after Diwali. Map out India's festivals and you get a map of its [states](states-india): each one familiar to its own people, and new to everyone else.
