## The short answer

Delhi is very old, but nobody can give one exact birthday. In the Mahabharata, the Pandavas built a city called Indraprastha on the Yamuna, and many people believe it stood where Delhi is now. Archaeologists digging at Purana Qila have found pottery and remains over 2,500 years old. An inscription of Emperor [Ashoka](ashoka), carved on a rock more than 2,200 years ago, was also found in south Delhi in 1966.

What we know for certain is that Delhi has been a capital again and again for about a thousand years. Each ruler built a new city beside the old one, so modern Delhi is really many cities stitched together.

## The seven cities

Historians often speak of "seven cities of Delhi", though they do not all agree on the list. One version starts with Lal Kot and Qila Rai Pithora, the fort of the Tomar and Chauhan kings. Then come Siri, Tughlaqabad, Jahanpanah and Firozabad, built by the rulers of the [Delhi Sultanate](delhi-sultanate). Sher Shah Suri and Humayun built around Purana Qila. Shah Jahan built the seventh, Shahjahanabad, which we now call Old Delhi.

Ruins of all of them still stand. You can walk from a 14th-century wall to a Metro station in ten minutes.

## Stone towers and an iron pillar

In about 1199, Qutb-ud-din Aibak began a tall tower of red sandstone. His successor Iltutmish added more storeys. Today the Qutb Minar is about 73 m tall, roughly as high as a 24-storey building, and covered in carved writing.

Next to it stands the [Iron Pillar](mystery-iron-pillar), about 7 m tall, made in the time of the Gupta kings. It has barely [rusted](rust) in about 1,600 years, which puzzled scientists for a long time. The answer turned out to be the special make-up of its iron.

## The Mughal city

In 1639, the [Mughal](mughals) emperor Shah Jahan, who also built the [Taj Mahal](taj-mahal), began a new capital. Its heart was the Red Fort, finished in 1648, with marble halls and canals of water running through them. Chandni Chowk, the main street in front, became one of India's great markets and still is. Every Independence Day, the Prime Minister raises the national flag at the Red Fort.

## Gardens for the dead

Some of Delhi's most beautiful buildings are tombs. Humayun's Tomb, finished in the 1570s, sits in a square garden divided by water channels into four parts. This kind of garden, called a charbagh, came to India from Persia and Central Asia. Many historians see the tomb as an early model for the Taj Mahal, built about 80 years later. Close by lies the shrine of the Sufi saint Nizamuddin Auliya, where singers still perform [qawwali](qawwali) music on Thursday evenings, as they have for centuries.

## The eighth city

In 1911, the British announced they would move the capital of India from Calcutta to Delhi. Architects Edwin Lutyens and Herbert Baker planned a new city with wide avenues, gardens and grand buildings in stone. New Delhi was opened in 1931. After independence in 1947, it became the capital of free India, and the [Parliament](parliament-india) meets there.

## A giant today

Delhi now spreads far beyond its old walls. Its wider urban area holds well over 3 crore people, one of the biggest in the world. The city has a large Metro, first opened in 2002, and also struggles with traffic and [air pollution](air-pollution), especially in winter. Yet a farmer from the Mughal age would still recognise the river, the Red Fort and the call of [street sellers](people-street-vendors) in Chandni Chowk.
