## The short answer

Dubai grew from a small trading town into a giant modern city in just a few decades. It began with a natural harbour, the Dubai Creek, and a ruling family that welcomed traders. Oil, found in 1966, paid for roads, ports and airports. But Dubai's oil was never huge, so its rulers used the money to turn the city into a hub for trade, flights, banking and tourism.

In 2025 Dubai passed 4 million people. Around nine in ten of them come from other countries, and the largest group is from India.

## Pearls and the creek

In 1833, members of the Bani Yas tribe, led by the Al Maktoum family, settled by the creek. The Al Maktoums still rule Dubai today. Fishing and [pearl diving](pearls) were the main work. Divers held their breath for a minute or more as they dropped to the sea floor to collect oysters, coming up again and again through long hot days.

The pearl trade collapsed in the 1930s, when Japan began farming cheaper cultured pearls. Dubai then made its money from trade, with dhows, wooden sailing boats, carrying goods to and from India, Iran and East Africa. Old houses in the Al Fahidi area still have wind towers, tall chimneys that catch breezes and send them down into the rooms to cool them.

## The rupee in the Gulf

For many years, Dubai and its neighbours used Indian money. From 1959 to 1966, the Reserve Bank of India even printed a special Gulf rupee for use in the region. Ties with India are still strong. Indian schools, temples and restaurants are found across the city, and flights connect Dubai to dozens of Indian cities.

## Oil, then a big plan

In 1966, [oil](oil-coal) was found off Dubai's coast, and exports began in 1969. In 1971, Dubai joined six other emirates to form the United Arab Emirates. Its rulers knew the oil would not last forever. They dredged the creek, built Jebel Ali port, one of the largest human-made harbours in the world, and set up zones where foreign companies could do business easily.

Today oil makes up only a tiny share of Dubai's economy. Dubai International Airport is [one of the world's busiest](extreme-busiest-places) for international passengers.

## Up into the sky

Dubai's skyline rose fast. The Burj Khalifa, opened in 2010, is about 828 m tall, more than twice the height of the Empire State Building. Its Y-shaped base and stepped shape help it stand up to desert winds, and its [elevators](elevators) are some of the fastest in the world. On a clear day you can see the top from about 95 km away.

## Islands made of sea sand

Dubai also built islands. Palm Jumeirah, shaped like a palm tree, was made by spraying millions of cubic metres of [sand](sand-where) dredged from the sea floor. You might wonder why they didn't use [desert sand](deserts-sand). Its grains have been rolled by wind until they are too smooth and round to pack firmly or make good concrete.

## Living with the heat

Summer temperatures in Dubai often pass 40 °C, and sometimes 45 °C. Almost everything is [air-conditioned](air-conditioning), from malls to bus stops. Rain is rare, so drinking water comes from desalination plants that remove salt from seawater. These use lots of energy, and the city is building large [solar farms](future-energy) in the desert. Out in the dunes, people still race [camels](camels), now often guided by small robot jockeys.
