## The short answer

Germany is a country in the middle of Europe, with the Alps in the south, the North and Baltic Seas in the north, and the Rhine flowing through hills dotted with castles. It has more people than any other country in the European Union.

It is known for many things. People think of its cars, engineers and fast motorways, its forests and [fairy-tale castles](neuschwanstein), its bread, sausages and pretzels. It is famous for composers like [Bach and Beethoven](classical-western) and for scientists like Einstein. Its history has some of the darkest chapters of the 20th century, and it is also known for how seriously it has faced them since.

## The machine that printed Europe

Around 1450, in the city of Mainz, a goldsmith named [Johannes Gutenberg](printing-revolution) built a printing press with movable metal letters. Each letter could be set, inked, pressed, cleaned and reused. His most famous book, a Bible, was printed by about 1455.

Before this, books in Europe were mostly copied by hand, which could take months. Within fifty years, printers across Europe had produced millions of books. Ideas, news and arguments spread faster than ever before.

## Fairy tales, trees and kindergartens

In 1812 two brothers, Jacob and Wilhelm Grimm, published a collection of German folk tales. It included Hansel and Gretel, Snow White, Cinderella and Rapunzel. Many of the original versions were much darker than the cartoons you may know. You can trace them in [fairy tales](fairy-tales).

Bringing a decorated evergreen tree indoors at [Christmas](christmas) is a German custom that spread across the world in the 1800s. And in 1840, a teacher named Friedrich Fröbel named his school for young children a "kindergarten", or children's garden, where they learned through play.

## Cars and motorways

In 1886 Karl Benz built a three-wheeled motor car driven by a petrol engine. It is widely called the first true car. His wife, Bertha Benz, drove it about 100 km to visit her mother in 1888, partly to show the world it worked.

Germany's motorways, the autobahns, are famous because some stretches have no fixed speed limit. Germans are also famous for recycling. Shops charge a small deposit on bottles and cans, which is paid back when you return them, so very few end up as litter.

## A painful history, faced openly

In the 1930s, the [Nazi party](holocaust) under Adolf Hitler took power. It started the [Second World War](world-war-two) in 1939 and murdered about six million Jewish people, along with many others, in the Holocaust. [Anne Frank](anne-frank), whose diary is read around the world, was born in Frankfurt.

After the war, Germany was split. In 1961 East Germany built the Berlin Wall to stop people fleeing to the West. It fell in 1989, as told in [the Berlin Wall](berlin-wall), and Germany reunited in 1990. Today German schools teach this history in detail, and memorials stand in the middle of Berlin.

## Germany and India

German scholars studied [Sanskrit](sanskrit) closely in the 1800s. Max Müller, born in Dessau, edited a full printed edition of the Rigveda, and the Goethe-Institut centres in India are called Max Mueller Bhavan in his honour.

Beethoven was born in Bonn in 1770. Albert Einstein was born in Ulm in 1879. Both towns have museums about them, and both men are still among the most famous people Germany has produced.
