## The short answer

From north to south, India is a land of giant steps. At the top are the Himalaya, the highest mountains on Earth. Below them lie the wide, flat plains of [the Ganga](rivers-himalaya) and Brahmaputra rivers, among the most crowded and fertile places in the world.

To the west is the [Thar Desert](desert-life). In the middle and south rises the Deccan Plateau, a huge raised table of old rock, edged by two ranges of hills called the Western and Eastern Ghats. Around it all runs a long coastline, ending at [Kanyakumari](kanyakumari), where three seas meet.

India covers about 32.9 lakh km², the seventh largest country. Two groups of islands, the Andaman and Nicobar Islands and [Lakshadweep](lakshadweep), add even more variety.

## The wall of mountains

The [Himalaya](himalaya-form) form India's northern wall, stretching about 2,400 km. They began rising about 5 crore years ago, when India collided with Asia. Kangchenjunga, India's highest peak, reaches 8,586 m.

The mountains do two big jobs. They block icy winds from Central Asia, keeping northern India warmer. And their glaciers and snow feed great rivers like the Ganga, Indus and Brahmaputra. Beyond them lies Ladakh, a high, cold desert where it hardly rains.

## The great plains

South of the mountains, rivers have spread mud and silt over millions of years, building some of the flattest land on Earth. The Indo-Gangetic plain runs from Punjab to West Bengal and Assam.

The soil here is deep and rich, so [farmers grow wheat](people-farmers-day), rice and sugarcane. Hundreds of millions of people live on these plains, in cities like Delhi, Lucknow, Patna and Kolkata. Where the Ganga and Brahmaputra reach the sea, they form the Sundarbans delta, the largest mangrove forest in the world.

## Desert and plateau

In the west, the [Thar Desert](thar-formed) covers much of Rajasthan, with sand dunes, thorny scrub and very little rain.

The [Deccan](deccan) Plateau fills most of southern India. Much of its western part is made of black basalt rock, from enormous lava floods about 6.6 crore years ago, around the time the dinosaurs died out. Its rivers, such as the Godavari and Krishna, flow east towards the Bay of Bengal, because the plateau tilts that way.

## Coasts and islands

The Western Ghats catch the [monsoon](monsoon) rains, making the west coast and Kerala lush and green. Low areas like Kuttanad even lie below sea level. Far to the north-east, [Mawsynram](meghalaya) in Meghalaya is one of the wettest places on Earth.

India's coastline was long measured at about 7,500 km. A new survey using more detailed maps, published in 2024, put it at more than 11,000 km, because it counted many more bends and inlets.

## Neighbours and borders

India shares land borders with Pakistan, [China](china-today), Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh and Myanmar. Sri Lanka lies just across a narrow stretch of sea called the Palk Strait, and the [Maldives](maldives) sits to the south-west.

Inside, India is divided into states and union territories, each with its own landscape. Rajasthan is the biggest state by area. Goa is the smallest.

## One clock, two sunrises

India stretches about 2,900 km from west to east. In Arunachal Pradesh, the Sun rises roughly two hours earlier than in western Gujarat. Yet the whole country runs on one clock, Indian Standard Time. Some people in the north-east have asked for a separate time zone, a question still being discussed.
