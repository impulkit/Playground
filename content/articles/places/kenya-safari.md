## The short answer

A [savanna](?savanna) is a wide grassland dotted with trees, often flat-topped acacias. Kenya's savannas are home to some of the largest groups of wild animals left on Earth: elephants, giraffes, zebras, buffalo, [wildebeest](animal-migration-land), rhinos, and the lions, leopards, cheetahs and [hyenas](wild-hyena) that hunt them.

The most famous patch is the Maasai Mara, in the south-west of Kenya. It joins the [Serengeti](serengeti) across the border in Tanzania. Together they make one huge ecosystem where animals wander freely between the two countries, without passports.

Kenya sits right on the equator, yet it is not one big hot plain. Mount Kenya, at about 5,200 m, has glaciers near its peak. The savanna lies in between, warm all year, with rainy seasons that turn the brown grass green almost overnight.

## The Great Migration

The grass decides everything. Wildebeest follow the rains, chasing fresh green shoots in a loop of hundreds of kilometres through Tanzania and Kenya. More than a million of them make the trip, along with hundreds of thousands of zebras and gazelles.

The most dramatic moment is the crossing of the Mara River, usually between July and October. Thousands of animals bunch up on the bank, then plunge in. [Crocodiles](croc-ancient) wait in the brown water. Lions and hyenas wait on the far side. Most wildebeest make it across, and the ones that don't feed the hunters for weeks.

## Who eats whom

Not all grazers eat the same thing, which is why so many can share one place. Zebras crop the tall, tough tops of grass. Wildebeest follow and eat the shorter, softer middle. Gazelles nibble what is left near the ground. Giraffes skip the grass entirely and browse acacia leaves from 5 m up, curling a long dark tongue around the thorns.

Then come the clean-up crew. Vultures can strip a carcass in under an hour. Dung beetles roll away animal droppings and bury them, which feeds the soil and helps next year's grass grow. Elephants, by pushing over trees, keep the savanna open instead of letting it turn into forest. Some scientists call them the gardeners of the grassland, and you can read how they [talk to each other](elephants-talk) over long distances.

## People of the plains

The Maasai have herded cattle on these grasslands for centuries, and the Maasai Mara is named after them. Cattle mean wealth and food, and many Maasai families still live in a ring of houses inside a thorn fence that keeps out lions at night.

Living close to big wild animals is not easy. Lions sometimes kill cattle, and elephants can flatten a field of maize in one night. Kenya now pays some communities to share their land with wildlife, so that tourists' money reaches the people who live next to the lions.

## The Big Five

You may hear guides talk about the "Big Five": lion, leopard, elephant, rhino and Cape buffalo. The name sounds like a list of the most beautiful animals, but it began with hunters. These were the five most dangerous animals to hunt on foot.

Today most visitors carry cameras instead. Kenya banned sport hunting in 1977, and its parks and reserves are now a big part of the country's income. India has its own big cats to protect, and the story of the Asiatic lions of [Gir](lions-gir) shows how a tiny group can be brought back. Kenya's own [Wangari Maathai](wangari-maathai) spent her life showing that trees, soil and people are all part of the same plain.
