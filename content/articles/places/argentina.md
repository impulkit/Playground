## The short answer

Argentina is a large country covering much of the southern part of South America. It is about 3,700 kilometres long from north to south, so it holds almost every kind of climate. The north has hot forests and giant waterfalls. The middle has flat grasslands called the pampas. The west is walled by the Andes, and the far south, Patagonia, is a land of wind, glaciers and [penguins](penguin-flight).

Most Argentines live in cities, especially in and around the capital, Buenos Aires. Argentina is known for football, tango, beef cooked over open fires, and a drink called mate. [Spanish](spanish-language) is the main language, spoken with an accent that many people say sounds a little Italian.

## Mountains, ice and falls

Aconcagua, in the Andes on the border with Chile, is 6,961 metres high. No mountain outside Asia is taller. In the far south, the Perito Moreno Glacier pushes into a lake in a wall of ice about 70 metres high. Every so often, huge chunks break off and crash into the water with a noise like thunder. Unlike most [glaciers](glaciers), it stayed roughly the same size for decades, though scientists reported in 2025 that it has started to shrink quickly.

In the north-east, on the border with Brazil, the Iguazú River tumbles over a curved cliff in about 275 separate waterfalls. The biggest is called the Devil's Throat. The spray is so thick that rainbows hang over the falls on sunny days.

## Gauchos, giants and the end of the world

On the pampas, cowboys called gauchos have herded cattle on horseback for centuries. The grasslands made Argentina one of the world's great beef producers, and the [asado](bbq), a barbecue of meat cooked slowly over wood embers, is a weekend ritual for many families.

Patagonia hides another kind of giant. Some of the largest dinosaurs ever found were dug up there, including [Argentinosaurus](argentinosaurus), a plant-eater that may have weighed as much as ten elephants.

At the very southern tip lies Ushuaia, often called the southernmost city in the world. Cruise ships and research ships leave from its harbour for Antarctica, about 1,000 kilometres away.

## A land of immigrants

Between about 1880 and 1930, millions of people sailed to Argentina from Europe, especially Italy and Spain, looking for work and land. Today a large share of Argentines have Italian ancestors. That is why pizza and pasta are everyday food in Buenos Aires, and why Spanish there has a sing-song, Italian-sounding rhythm.

In the poor dockside neighbourhoods of Buenos Aires and Montevideo in the late 1800s, immigrants, locals and people of African descent mixed their music. Out of it came the tango, a dramatic dance for two, set to accordion-like bandoneon music. UNESCO listed it as world heritage in 2009.

## Fingerprints and footballers

In 1892, Argentine police used a bloody thumbprint left on a door frame to solve a murder. The detective, Eduardo Álvarez, had been trained by Juan Vucetich, a police official who had built a system for sorting prints. It is usually described as the first murder anywhere solved by [fingerprints](fingerprints), and Vucetich's system was soon copied by police in many countries.

Football is close to a religion. Argentina has won the men's [World Cup](pop-world-cup-trophy) three times, in 1978, 1986 and 2022. Diego Maradona led the team in 1986, and [Lionel Messi](messi-ronaldo) lifted the trophy in 2022. Few rivalries are fiercer than [Maradona](pele-maradona) versus Pelé of Brazil, argued over in cafés across both countries.

Argentina has an Indian connection too. In 1924, the poet [Rabindranath Tagore](tagore) fell ill on a voyage and stayed for weeks near Buenos Aires as the guest of the writer Victoria Ocampo. They wrote to each other for the rest of his life.
