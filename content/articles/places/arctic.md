## The short answer

The Arctic is the region around the North Pole. At its heart is the Arctic Ocean, covered for much of the year by sea ice that freezes, cracks, drifts and melts with the seasons. Around it lie the northern edges of eight countries: Canada, the United States (through [Alaska](alaska)), Russia, [Norway](norway-fjords), Sweden, Finland, [Iceland](iceland) and Denmark (through [Greenland](world-greenland)).

Unlike Antarctica, the Arctic has people who have lived there for thousands of years. About 4 million people live in the Arctic today, in [fishing villages](people-fishing-villages), mining towns and a few cities. Polar bears, walruses, arctic foxes, narwhals and reindeer share it with them.

## Months of light, months of dark

North of the Arctic Circle, the Sun does strange things. In midsummer it never sets, circling low around the sky at midnight. In midwinter it never rises, and the days are a dim blue twilight or full dark.

This happens because the Earth is tilted. In June the north leans towards the Sun, and in December it leans away, as explained in [why we have seasons](seasons-why). At the pole itself, there is one sunrise and one sunset a year, six months apart.

## The people of the north

The Inuit live across Arctic Canada, Greenland and Alaska. The Sámi live in the north of Norway, Sweden, Finland and Russia, and many have herded reindeer for generations. Siberia has many more peoples, such as the Nenets, who move with huge herds across the [tundra](tundra).

Traditional Arctic life depended on knowing the ice. Hunters could read cracks, colours and the sound of ice underfoot to tell whether it would hold them. Inuit languages have many precise words for kinds of sea ice. Today most Arctic people use snowmobiles, phones and the internet, while keeping hunting, fishing and their languages alive.

## Warming fast

The Arctic is warming much faster than the rest of the planet, by some measures three to four times faster. One reason is simple. White ice reflects sunlight. When it melts, the dark ocean beneath absorbs more heat, which melts more ice.

Summer sea ice now covers far less of the Arctic Ocean than it did in the 1980s. This is hard on polar bears, which hunt seals from the ice. For people, melting ice and thawing frozen ground can crack roads and houses, and wash away coastlines.

## India at the top of the world

In 2008 India opened its Arctic research station, Himadri, in a tiny international science village called Ny-Ålesund on [Svalbard](world-svalbard). Indian scientists there study [glaciers](glaciers), the atmosphere and how Arctic changes might affect the Indian monsoon.

Svalbard has another famous building: the Global Seed Vault, cut deep into a frozen mountainside. It stores backup copies of seeds from crops all over the world, including India, in case disaster strikes a seed bank elsewhere.

## Lights and a star

Arctic nights have their own beauty. The [aurora](aurora), or [northern lights](solar-wind), ripples across the sky in green curtains when particles from the Sun strike the upper atmosphere.

And above the pole itself shines Polaris, the [North Star](north-star). From the North Pole it sits almost straight overhead, which is why sailors far to the south once used it to find north. From India it hangs low over the northern horizon, and the further south you go, the lower it sinks.
