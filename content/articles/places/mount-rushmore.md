## The short answer

Mount Rushmore was carved to bring visitors to South Dakota and to celebrate the story of the United States. In 1923, a historian named Doane Robinson suggested carving giant figures in the Black Hills to attract tourists. The sculptor Gutzon Borglum took on the job and chose [four presidents](usa-presidents-facts).

The National Park Service explains the choice like this. [George Washington](george-washington) stands for the birth of the nation and [Thomas Jefferson](thomas-jefferson) for its growth. [Theodore Roosevelt](pop-teddy-bear) stands for its development, and [Abraham Lincoln](abraham-lincoln) for holding it together through the [Civil War](us-civil-war). Work began in 1927 and stopped in 1941. Today more than two million people visit each year.

## Blasting a mountain

Borglum first made a plaster model. His workers used a simple measuring system to copy it onto the mountain, so that every 2.5 cm on the model became 30 cm on the rock. Then they blasted. Nearly 400 people worked on the project over 14 years.

Workers sat in harness seats, hanging from steel cables over the cliff, drilling holes and packing them with [dynamite](accident-dynamite). After blasting, they drilled rows of small holes close together, then knocked away the rock between them and smoothed the surface. Despite the danger, no one died during the carving. Many workers were local miners who already knew how to handle explosives. Borglum's team hired a skilled powder man who could blast away rock to within a few centimetres of the final surface.

## Faces 18 metres tall

Each face is about 18 m tall, roughly the height of a six-storey building. Washington's nose is about 6 m long, about as long as two autorickshaws parked nose to tail. The eyes have a block of rock left in the middle of each pupil, which catches sunlight and makes them seem to sparkle from far away.

The original plan was to carve the presidents down to their waists. Money ran short, and Borglum died in March 1941. His son Lincoln Borglum finished the faces, and work stopped in October that year. The [granite](rocks-types) is so hard that it wears away by only about 2.5 cm every 10,000 years.

## A room behind the heads

Borglum planned a great Hall of Records inside the mountain behind Lincoln's head, to store America's most important documents for future people to find. Workers cut a tunnel about 21 m deep before the project stopped. In 1998, a vault was placed in the floor of the tunnel, holding panels with the text of key documents and the story of the carving.

## Sacred land

The Black Hills, called Paha Sapa in Lakota, are sacred to the Lakota people and other Native nations. In the Treaty of Fort Laramie of 1868, the US government promised the Black Hills to the Lakota. After gold was found there in 1874, settlers and miners poured in, and the government took the land.

In 1980, the US Supreme Court ruled that the land had been taken illegally and ordered the government to pay. The Lakota refused the money, saying they want the land back instead. The money has grown in a fund to well over a billion dollars and has never been collected. For many Lakota, Mount Rushmore is a painful reminder of that history.

## A memorial for Crazy Horse

About 27 km away, another giant carving has been under way since 1948. The Crazy Horse Memorial honours a Lakota leader. Its face, about 27 m tall, was finished in 1998, but the whole sculpture, showing him on horseback, may take many more decades. Like India's [Statue of Unity](statue-of-unity), these giant [national symbols](national-symbols) show how nations use huge carvings to tell their stories, and how people can see the same story differently.
