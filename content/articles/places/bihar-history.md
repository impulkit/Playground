## The short answer

For more than a thousand years, the region that is now Bihar sat at the centre of Indian history. It was the heart of Magadha, the kingdom that grew into the [Mauryan Empire](mauryan-empire), India's first empire to cover most of the subcontinent. Its capital, Pataliputra, is today's Patna.

Bihar is also where two great religious teachers lived and taught. The Buddha attained enlightenment at [Bodh Gaya](bodh-gaya), and Mahavira, the great teacher of Jainism, was born and died in the region. Later came Nalanda, one of the world's great centres of learning. Even the state's name comes from vihara, the word for a Buddhist monastery.

## The Buddha under the tree

In the 5th century BCE, or perhaps a little earlier, a prince named Siddhartha Gautama left his comfortable life to search for an answer to human suffering. According to Buddhist tradition, he sat in [meditation](mindfulness) under a pipal tree at Bodh Gaya and reached enlightenment. From then on he was called the [Buddha](buddha), "the awakened one".

A descendant of that tree still grows beside the Mahabodhi Temple at Bodh Gaya, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2002. Buddhists from Sri Lanka, [Thailand](thailand), Japan, Tibet and many other places travel there to pray. Monasteries built by many of those countries now stand around the town, each in its own national style.

At around the same time, [Mahavira](mahavira) taught non-violence towards all living things. Jain tradition says he was born near Vaishali and reached his final liberation at Pavapuri, both in Bihar.

## An empire from Pataliputra

Around 321 BCE, Chandragupta Maurya, helped by his clever adviser [Chanakya](chanakya), seized power in Magadha. He and his successors built an empire that stretched from Afghanistan to Karnataka. A Greek visitor called Megasthenes described Pataliputra as a huge city surrounded by a wooden wall with hundreds of towers and a deep moat.

Chandragupta's grandson [Ashoka](ashoka) carved messages about kindness and fair rule on rocks and pillars across India. One of his pillar tops, the lion capital from Sarnath, is now India's national emblem.

## A university of the ancient world

From the 5th century CE, Nalanda grew into a vast [monastery-university](hist-first-university). Students came from China, Korea, Tibet and South-East Asia to study Buddhism, logic, grammar, medicine and [astronomy](astronomy-ancient-india). The Chinese traveller Xuanzang studied there in the 7th century and wrote about its towers, libraries and strict entrance tests.

Nalanda was attacked and badly damaged around the end of the 12th century, and slowly fell into ruin. Its red-brick remains were excavated much later and became a World Heritage Site in 2016. The great mathematician [Aryabhata](aryabhata), born in 476 CE, is also linked to the region, and is often said to have worked near Pataliputra.

## A republic, a Guru and a satyagraha

Vaishali was home to the Licchavis, who were governed by an assembly of leading families rather than a single king. That's why it is often called one of the world's earliest republics, although historians debate how far it was like a modern republic.

Bihar's story kept going. Guru Gobind Singh, the tenth Sikh Guru, was born in Patna in 1666, and a gurdwara marks the place. In 1917, [Gandhi](gandhi-south-africa) led his first major campaign in India at Champaran, in northern Bihar, supporting farmers forced to grow indigo for British planters. It became a model for the larger struggles that followed.
