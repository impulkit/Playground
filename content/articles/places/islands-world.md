## The short answer

The biggest island in the world is [Greenland](world-greenland), in the far North Atlantic. It covers about 2.2 million square kilometres, roughly two-thirds the size of India. Most of it is covered by a thick sheet of ice.

You might wonder about Australia, which is bigger still. Geographers usually count Australia as a continent rather than an island, so Greenland takes the top spot. After Greenland come New Guinea, Borneo and [Madagascar](madagascar). Together, the world's islands hold about one in ten of all the people on Earth.

## Greenland, the icy giant

Greenland is an island of rock, but you would hardly know it. About 80% is buried under an ice sheet up to about 3 km thick. [If all that ice melted](what-if-ice-melted), the world's seas would rise by about 7 m.

Only about 56,000 people live there, mostly Inuit, in towns along the coast. Greenland is a self-governing territory within the Kingdom of Denmark. Its capital, Nuuk, is home to about a third of the people. There are no roads linking the towns, so people travel by boat, plane, helicopter, snowmobile or dog sled.

## Maps that lie

On many common world maps, Greenland looks as big as Africa. This is because of a map style called the Mercator projection. It was designed in 1569 to help sailors steer, and it stretches places more and more the closer they are to the poles.

In reality, Africa is about 14 times bigger than Greenland. India is bigger than Greenland too. You can find out why flattening a round Earth always distorts something in [flat maps](maps-flat).

## How islands are made

Islands form in a few main ways. Some were once part of a continent and were cut off by [rising seas](sea-level-rise) or drifting [plates](?plate). [Sri Lanka](sri-lanka), Great Britain and Madagascar are like this.

Others are built by volcanoes rising from the sea floor, such as Hawaii and [Iceland](iceland), as you can read in [Hawaii's islands](hawaii-islands). A third kind is made by living things. Tiny [coral animals](coral-bleaching) build reefs that, over thousands of years, become low islands of sand, like India's [Lakshadweep](lakshadweep) and the [Maldives](maldives). Rivers make islands too. [Majuli](majuli), in the Brahmaputra in Assam, is one of the biggest river islands in the world.

## Borneo and New Guinea

New Guinea, the second-largest island, is split between two countries: Papua New Guinea in the east and Indonesia in the west. It has some of the world's most varied languages, more than 800 on one island, and birds of paradise with spectacular feathers.

Borneo, the third, is shared by three countries: Indonesia, Malaysia and Brunei. Its rainforests are home to orangutans, one of the great apes. Borneo also has the world's biggest flower, the rafflesia, which can grow almost a metre wide and smells like rotting meat to attract flies. Madagascar, the fourth-largest island, broke away from India about 88 million years ago.

## Life on islands

Islands often have animals and plants found nowhere else. Cut off from the mainland, living things change over many generations to suit their new homes. Madagascar has lemurs. The [Galápagos](galapagos) has giant tortoises. Mauritius had the [dodo](dodo), a flightless bird that vanished after people arrived in the 1600s.

India has many islands of its own. The Andaman and Nicobar Islands, in the Bay of Bengal, number over 800. They are home to ancient forests, [coral reefs](india-wonder-andaman-reefs) and some of the most isolated peoples on Earth.
