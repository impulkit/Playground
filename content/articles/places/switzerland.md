## The short answer

Switzerland is a small country in the middle of Europe, with no coast. About 60 percent of it is covered by the Alps, with peaks over 4,000 metres, glaciers, deep valleys and lakes. Mountains made Switzerland hard to invade, hard to farm and hard to cross, and the Swiss became experts at living with them: [building tunnels](extreme-longest-tunnels), railways and mountain villages.

Clocks and watches came from a different kind of hard work. In the long winters, farmers in the Jura mountains made watch parts at home. Over centuries, Swiss workshops became famous for tiny, precise machines that keep time. Switzerland is also known for chocolate, [cheese](food-why-cheese-holes), banks, peace talks and the red flag with a white cross.

## Snowy peaks and shrinking ice

The Matterhorn, a sharp pyramid of rock, rises to 4,478 metres above the village of Zermatt. It is the peak you may have seen on postcards and calendars. The highest point in the country is the Dufourspitze, at 4,634 metres. Glaciers fill the high valleys, but they are shrinking fast. Swiss scientists report that the country's [glaciers](glaciers) lost about a tenth of their remaining ice in just 2022 and 2023.

Indian audiences know these mountains well. From the 1970s onwards, film-makers such as Yash Chopra shot romantic [Bollywood](bollywood) songs in Swiss meadows and lakes. Indian tourists followed, and in 2016 a statue of Yash Chopra was put up in Interlaken.

## Watches from a jewellery ban

In the 1500s, the religious reformer John Calvin led the city of Geneva. Its strict rules banned showy jewellery. Goldsmiths needed new work, so many turned to making watches, which counted as useful rather than vain.

The skills spread into the Jura mountains, where towns like La Chaux-de-Fonds and Le Locle were even laid out around watchmaking workshops. In the 1970s, cheap [quartz](minerals-everyday) watches from Japan nearly wiped out the Swiss industry. It fought back in 1983 with the Swatch, a colourful plastic watch. Today Swiss watches are sold all over the world, and their tiny gears show how [clocks](clocks) turn a steady beat into hours and minutes. Cuckoo clocks, though, are not Swiss at all. They come from the Black Forest, just over the border in Germany.

Swiss chocolate has a similar story of clever tinkering. In 1875, Daniel Peter of Vevey mixed in condensed milk made by his neighbour Henri Nestlé and created one of the first successful milk chocolates. A few years later, Rodolphe Lindt invented a machine that stirred chocolate for days until it turned silky. That is how [chocolate making](chocolate-making) went from gritty to smooth.

## Tunnels through the Alps

Getting across the Alps has always been hard. Today trains do it underground. The Gotthard Base Tunnel, opened in 2016, runs for 57 kilometres under the mountains. It is the longest railway [tunnel](tunnels) in the world, and freight and passenger trains race through it at up to about 200 km/h.

Above ground, mountain trains climb with cogwheels that grip a toothed rail. The Jungfrau railway reaches a station at about 3,450 metres, the highest in Europe.

## Four languages and a lot of voting

Switzerland has four national languages: German in most of the north and centre, French in the west, Italian in the south and Romansh in a few mountain valleys. Many Swiss speak two or three.

Swiss citizens vote on national questions several times a year, from taxes to railways. This direct form of [democracy](democracy-what) lets citizens propose new laws themselves. Switzerland has also stayed neutral in wars since 1815, and Geneva hosts the [Red Cross](red-cross) and many United Nations offices. In 1989, at the [CERN laboratory](extreme-biggest-machines) near Geneva, [Tim Berners-Lee](hist-first-website) wrote the proposal that led to the [World Wide Web](web-invented).
