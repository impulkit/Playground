## The short answer

Mongolia is a large, landlocked country between Russia and China, mostly made of high grassland, mountains and the Gobi Desert. It is about half the size of India, but only about 35 lakh people live there, which makes it the most thinly populated country in the world.

Roughly a quarter of Mongolians are herders who live as nomads, or partly as nomads. They don't wander at random. Each family moves its animals between a few known camps through the year, following grass and water: a sheltered valley in winter, open pasture by a river in summer. They live in round felt tents called [gers](life-homes-world), which they take with them. In winter, temperatures can fall below minus 40°C.

## The ger

A ger has a folding wooden lattice wall, long roof poles and a round wooden crown at the top that lets out smoke and lets in light. Thick layers of sheep's-wool felt keep out the cold, and a white canvas cover goes over the top. A stove in the middle burns wood or dried dung for cooking and heat.

The door always faces south, away from cold north winds. Inside, there are customs about where to sit: guests go to the left, the place of honour is at the back, opposite the door. A family can put up a ger in an hour or two. Many families in Ulaanbaatar still live in gers on the edges of the city. In winter, smoke from thousands of their coal stoves makes the capital's air some of the dirtiest in the world.

## The five snouts

Mongolian herders talk about the "five snouts": [horses](horses), camels, cattle (including yaks), sheep and goats. In a recent count there were about 65 million animals, roughly 20 for every person.

Each animal has its job. Sheep give wool for felt, and meat. Goats give cashmere, the soft hair spun into expensive sweaters. Two-humped Bactrian [camels](camels) carry loads across the Gobi. Mares are milked in summer, and the milk is [fermented](fermentation) into a fizzy, slightly sour drink called airag.

The hardest times are a dzud, a winter so harsh, or following a summer so dry, that animals starve or freeze. In bad dzud years, millions of animals have died, and some families have had to give up herding and move to the city.

## Horses, wrestling and archery

Mongolians have a saying that a [Mongol](mongols) without a horse is like a bird without wings. Children learn to ride very young. In the summer festival of Naadam, the "three manly games" are wrestling, horse racing and [archery](archery). Women and girls take part in archery and racing too. The jockeys in the horse races are often children, riding across the open steppe for many kilometres.

Some eight hundred years ago, horsemen like these followed [Genghis Khan](genghis-khan), who united the Mongol tribes in 1206. His descendants built the largest connected land empire in history.

## Dinosaurs in the desert

The Gobi is one of the richest places in the world for [dinosaur fossils](dino-museums). In 1923, an American expedition led by Roy Chapman Andrews found nests of fossil [dinosaur eggs](dino-eggs). The place became known as the Flaming Cliffs, for the way its red rocks glow at sunset. The same expedition found the first known skull of [Velociraptor](velociraptor-real) there. Fossil hunters still find bones in the Gobi, sometimes simply lying on the surface after a windstorm.
