## The short answer

Texas is big because of how it joined the United States. It was once part of New Spain and then Mexico. In 1836, settlers there won a war and declared the Republic of Texas, an independent country. Nine years later, in 1845, the USA agreed to take it in as a single state, with all its land at once.

Today Texas covers about 696,000 km², more than a fifth of India's area. Only [Alaska](alaska) is bigger. About 31 million people live there, more than in any state except California. A drive across Texas from east to west can take more than 12 hours.

## Six flags

Texans like to say that six flags have flown over their land: Spain, France, Mexico, the Republic of Texas, the Confederate States during the [Civil War](us-civil-war), and the United States. Before any of them, many Native American nations lived there, including the Caddo, Comanche and Apache.

In 1836, a small group of Texan fighters was defeated at the Alamo, an old mission in San Antonio. Weeks later, Texan forces won the Battle of San Jacinto, and Mexico's leader signed away the land. People in Mexico and Texas still tell this history in different ways.

## One state, not five

When the USA annexed Texas in 1845, the agreement included an unusual promise. Texas could later divide itself into as many as five states. Texans never used it. In 1850, Texas sold its claims to land further west, now parts of New Mexico, Colorado and other states, for 10 million dollars. Even after that, it stayed huge.

## From cattle to oil to rockets

For years, Texas was cattle country. Cowboys drove herds of longhorn cattle north to railways in Kansas. Then, in 1901, a well at Spindletop, near Beaumont, struck [oil](oil-coal), which shot more than 40 m into the air. Texas became one of the biggest oil producers in the world. Today it also produces more [wind power](renewable-energy) than any other state.

In Houston is NASA's Johnson Space Center, home of Mission Control. When [Apollo 11](moon-landing-sites) landed on the Moon in 1969, Neil Armstrong radioed "Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed." Astronauts still train in Houston. They practise spacewalks in a giant swimming pool that holds a full-size copy of parts of the International [Space Station](space-station).

## Big land, wild weather

Texas has pine forests in the east, flat plains in the north, deserts and mountains in the west, and beaches on the Gulf of Mexico. Big Bend National Park lies on a great bend of the Rio Grande, the river that forms the border with [Mexico](mexico).

The weather can be extreme. Northern Texas gets many [tornadoes](tornadoes). In 1900, a [hurricane](hurricanes) struck the coastal city of Galveston and killed thousands of people. It is still the deadliest natural disaster in US history.

## Tex-Mex and Texas pride

Texas food mixes Mexican and American cooking. [Tex-Mex](tex-mex) dishes such as fajitas, nachos and chilli con carne are eaten across the USA. [Texas barbecue](bbq) is famous too, with meat smoked slowly for many hours. Large Indian-American communities in Houston and Dallas have added temples, cricket clubs and [Diwali](diwali-lights) celebrations.

Texans are known for being proud of their state. Their flag has a single star, which is why Texas is called the Lone Star State. People there also like to say "everything's bigger in Texas".
