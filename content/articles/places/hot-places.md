## The short answer

The highest air temperature ever officially recorded is 56.7 °C (134 °F), measured at Furnace Creek in Death Valley, California, on 10 July 1913. That is hot enough that a metal car door handle left in the sun could burn your hand.

But ["hottest place"](weather-records) can mean different things. It might mean the highest air temperature on one day, the hottest ground, or the place that is hottest all year round. Each has a different winner. And because old records were taken with simple instruments, scientists still argue about some of them.

## Death Valley

Death Valley is a long, deep desert valley in eastern California. Parts of it lie below sea level, the lowest at about 86 m below. Hot air gets trapped between the steep mountains on either side, and the dark, bare ground soaks up sunshine and heats the air further.

Summers there regularly pass 50 °C. In 2020 and 2021, it reached about 54.4 °C, among the highest temperatures reliably measured anywhere. Some weather historians think the 1913 record was a mistake, and that these newer readings may be the true highest. Others accept the old record. The question is still open.

## The record that was thrown out

For 90 years, the world record was 58 °C, measured at El Azizia in Libya in 1922. In 2012, a panel of experts from the World Meteorological Organization re-examined it.

They found several problems. The observer was new to the job, the instrument was a type that is easy to misread, and the reading did not match nearby places. The panel threw the record out, and Death Valley took over. A similar thing happened in India in 2024, when a weather station in Delhi reported 52.9 °C. The India Meteorological Department checked it and found the sensor was faulty.

## The hottest ground

Air temperature is measured in the shade. The ground in full sun gets much hotter. NASA satellites have measured land surface temperatures of around 70 °C in the Lut Desert of Iran, a region of dark rock and sand.

At Dallol, in Ethiopia, the average temperature over a whole year was measured at about 34 °C in the 1960s, one of the highest averages known for any inhabited place. Dallol also has bubbling [hot springs](geysers-hot-springs), acid pools and salt crusts in yellow, green and orange.

## India's hottest days

India's official record is 51 °C, measured at Phalodi in Rajasthan on 19 May 2016. Churu, also in Rajasthan, reached 50.8 °C in 2019. Before [the monsoon](monsoon) arrives, much of north and central India can swelter above 45 °C for days at a time.

Heat is harder on the body when the air is also humid. Your body cools itself by [sweating](sweat), and the [sweat](body-temperature) needs to evaporate. In very humid air it can't, which is why a 38 °C day in Chennai can feel worse than a 42 °C day in [Jaipur](jaipur). Drinking enough [water](water-drink) and staying in the shade really do matter.

## Hotter than Earth

As the climate warms, record-breaking [heatwaves](heatwaves) are becoming more common, as explained in [climate change](climate-change). But Earth's hottest places are still mild compared with other spots in the solar system.

The surface of Venus is about 465 °C, hot enough to melt lead, as you can read in [Venus](venus-hot). The surface of the Sun is about 5,500 °C. [Deep inside the Earth](what-if-hole-through-earth), the core is thought to be about as hot as the surface of the Sun.
