## The short answer

Mysuru Dasara is a ten-day festival in the city of Mysuru, in Karnataka, held during Navaratri in September or October. It celebrates the victory of good over evil, and especially the goddess Chamundeshwari, who, according to legend, defeated the buffalo-headed demon Mahishasura on the hill above the city. The name Mysuru is often linked to him.

Over the ten days, the city fills with music, dance, [wrestling](wrestling-india), food fairs and flower shows. Mysuru Palace glows each evening with nearly a lakh light bulbs. The festival ends on Vijayadashami with the Jamboo Savari, a grand procession led by an elephant carrying a golden howdah with an idol of the goddess.

## A royal festival 400 years old

Dasara in Mysuru goes back to the kings of the [Vijayanagara](vijayanagara) empire, who held an enormous autumn festival with processions, music and elephants. Visitors from Portugal and Persia wrote about it in wonder.

After Vijayanagara fell, the Wodeyar kings of Mysore carried the tradition on. Raja Wodeyar I is said to have begun celebrating it in 1610. The festival continued under later rulers, and the Wodeyar family still performs private royal rituals in the palace. Since India's independence, the state government has organised the public festival, and it is Karnataka's official state festival, the Nadahabba.

## The palace of light

The [Mysore Palace](mysore-palace) that stands today was built between 1897 and 1912, after an older wooden palace burned down. It was designed by a British architect, Henry Irwin, mixing Indian, Islamic and European styles, with domes, arches and towers.

During Dasara, the palace is outlined with nearly a lakh bulbs. When they switch on in the evening, the whole building seems to be drawn in gold [light](light-bulb). Musicians perform in the courtyard, and crowds gather on the lawns. For many families, seeing the lit palace is the highlight of the year.

## The elephant procession

The final day brings the Jamboo Savari. Weeks before, trained elephants walk in from forest camps in Karnataka with their [mahouts](people-mahouts), the people who look after and guide them. They practise walking through traffic, crowds and loud bands, so they stay calm on the day.

On Vijayadashami, the lead elephant carries a golden howdah, a seat weighing about 750 kg, with an idol of Chamundeshwari. Decorated elephants, horses, bands, dancers and colourful floats from districts across the state follow it about 5 km from the palace to the Bannimantap grounds. In the evening, a torchlight parade ends the festival.

Some animal welfare groups have raised concerns about the stress on elephants from crowds and noise. Organisers say the animals are carefully trained and checked by vets. The debate is part of a wider conversation in India about how temple and festival elephants should be treated.

## More of Mysuru

Mysuru is known for much more than Dasara. Its rulers in the 1700s included Hyder Ali and his son [Tipu Sultan](tipu-sultan), whose capital was at nearby Srirangapatna. Mysore pak, a rich sweet made from gram flour, ghee and sugar, is said to have been invented in the palace kitchen.

The city is also famous for Mysore [silk](silk) saris with gold borders, for sandalwood soap and incense, and for Mysore paintings with gold leaf. On Chamundi Hill above the city, a huge statue of Mahishasura greets visitors climbing up to the goddess's temple, as if he is still waiting for the next round.
