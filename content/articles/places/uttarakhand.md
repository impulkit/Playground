## The short answer

Uttarakhand is a Himalayan state in northern India, and many Hindus call it Dev Bhoomi, the land of the gods. Some of Hinduism's holiest rivers rise here, and its mountains hold ancient temples that pilgrims have visited for centuries. The four shrines of the Char Dham, at Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath and Badrinath, sit high in the valleys.

The name fits the land as well as the faith. Snowy peaks such as Nanda Devi, at about 7,800 m one of India's highest mountains, rise above deep river gorges and thick forests. The state was formed in 2000, when it was separated from Uttar Pradesh, and its capital is Dehradun.

## Where the rivers begin

The Yamuna starts near the Yamunotri shrine. The Bhagirathi, the main source stream of the Ganga, pours out of the Gangotri [glacier](glaciers) at a spot called Gaumukh, the "cow's mouth". Another river, the Alaknanda, flows down from near Badrinath.

At Devprayag, the Bhagirathi and the Alaknanda meet, one greenish and one greyer, and from there the river is called the Ganga. It reaches the plains at Haridwar, where millions gather for the Kumbh Mela, and flows on past cities like [Varanasi](varanasi) to the sea.

## Temples on the mountain

Kedarnath, a temple to Shiva, stands at about 3,580 m, near the source of the Mandakini river. Badrinath, a temple to Vishnu, sits beside the Alaknanda. Both close in winter, when snow blocks the paths, and reopen in spring. Many pilgrims still walk the last stretch on foot. The path to Kedarnath climbs about 16 km from the nearest road, and ponies, porters and helicopters help those who can't make the climb.

Rishikesh, on the Ganga near the foothills, is known as a centre for [yoga](ayurveda-yoga) and [meditation](mindfulness). In 1968, the [Beatles](beatles-music) stayed at an ashram there, and many songs on their "White Album" were written during that visit. Today, people from all over the world come to study yoga beside the river.

## The women who hugged trees

In the 1970s, villagers in the Garhwal hills grew angry as contractors [cut down forests](what-if-no-trees) the villagers depended on for firewood, fodder and water. In 1974, in the village of Reni, a group of women led by Gaura Devi stood between the loggers and the trees and refused to move.

Their protest became part of the Chipko movement, from a Hindi word meaning "to stick" or "to hug". It inspired environmental campaigns across India and beyond, and helped lead to limits on felling trees in the Himalaya. The idea of standing up for a forest by hugging its trees spread to villages in Karnataka and elsewhere.

## Tigers and flowers

In 1936, the British set up India's first national park in the foothills. It was later renamed after [Jim Corbett](kipling-india), a hunter who became a conservationist and wrote books about the region. Today, Jim Corbett National Park is one of the best places in India to see a tiger in the wild.

Higher up, the [Valley of Flowers](india-wonder-valley-flowers) bursts into bloom each monsoon, with hundreds of kinds of wildflowers. Together with nearby Nanda Devi National Park, it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The mountains can be dangerous, though. In 2013, sudden floods swept through the Kedarnath valley, causing great loss of life. Scientists continue to study how heavy rain, melting ice and building on steep slopes can combine to cause such disasters.
