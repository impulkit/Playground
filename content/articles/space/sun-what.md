## The short answer

The Sun is a [star](?star), a huge ball of hot gas made mostly of hydrogen and helium. It is not burning like a fire. Instead, deep in its core, at about 15 million °C, hydrogen [atoms](?atom) smash together so hard that they join to make helium. This is called [fusion](?fusion), and it releases enormous amounts of energy.

That energy slowly works its way out and leaves the surface as light and heat. The light then crosses about [150 million km of space](space-distances) to reach us in [about 8 minutes and 20 seconds](what-if-sun-vanished).

## Size and weight

The Sun is about 109 times as wide as Earth. About 1.3 million Earths could fit inside it. It holds more than 99% of all the mass in the solar system, which is why everything, from Mercury to the farthest comet, circles around it.

The Sun is a fairly ordinary, middle-sized star. It only looks special because it is so close. The next nearest star is about 2,70,000 times farther away.

## The furnace at the centre

Every second, the Sun fuses about 600 million tonnes of hydrogen into helium. In the process, about 4 million tonnes of matter vanish, turned into pure energy. Albert [Einstein](einstein)'s famous [equation](equations), E = mc², explains how a little mass becomes a lot of energy.

The energy does not escape quickly. The inside of the Sun is so packed that light keeps getting absorbed and given out again, bouncing around in random directions. It can take many thousands of years to reach the surface. So the sunlight warming your face started its journey long before the first cities were built.

## A restless surface

The Sun's surface is about 5,500 °C. It is covered in bubbling cells of hot gas, each roughly 1,000 km across, wider than Maharashtra. Dark patches called [sunspots](sunspots) come and go. They look dark only because they are slightly cooler than the gas around them. The number of sunspots rises and falls over a cycle of about 11 years.

Sometimes the Sun throws huge clouds of charged gas into space. When one hits Earth, it can make the sky glow with [auroras](aurora). In 1859, a [giant solar storm](space-weather) made auroras visible as far south as the Caribbean. Telegraph wires sparked, and some operators found they could send messages with their batteries unplugged.

## Watching the Sun

India's first solar mission, Aditya-L1, launched in 2023. It sits about 1.5 million km from Earth, at a point where the pulls of the Sun and Earth balance, so it can watch the Sun without ever being blocked. It studies the Sun's outer atmosphere and the storms it sends towards us.

NASA's Parker Solar Probe goes the other way, diving close to the Sun. In December 2024 it passed about 6 million km from the surface, moving faster than any spacecraft before it.

## The Sun's future

The Sun is about 4.6 billion years old, roughly halfway through its life. In about 5 billion years it will run low on hydrogen in its core and swell into a [red giant](star-life-cycle). Meanwhile, its light feeds almost every living thing, through the plants that capture it in [photosynthesis](photosynthesis), and more and more of our electricity comes from [solar panels](solar-panels).
