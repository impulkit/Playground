## The short answer

The Moon has almost no air, so [parachutes](parachutes) are useless there. Chandrayaan-3's lander, Vikram, slowed down the only way it could: by firing rocket engines downwards, against its own fall.

On 23 August 2023, just after 6 in the evening India time, Vikram touched down near the [Moon's south pole](moon-water). No spacecraft had landed that far south before. A few hours later, a small six-wheeled rover called Pragyan rolled down a ramp onto the grey dust. India became the fourth country to land softly on the Moon, after the Soviet Union, the USA and China.

## Learning from a crash

Four years earlier, India had tried the same thing. In September 2019, Chandrayaan-2's lander lost control in the last few kilometres and crashed. Its orbiter survived and kept circling the Moon for years.

ISRO's engineers studied every detail of what went wrong. For Chandrayaan-3 they gave Vikram stronger legs, more fuel, extra sensors and a much bigger target. Instead of aiming at a small spot, it could land anywhere in a patch about 4 km long and 2.4 km wide. The idea was simple: plan for things going wrong, and survive them anyway.

## Twenty minutes of braking

Vikram began its final drop from about 30 km up, moving at roughly 6,000 km/h, faster than a rifle bullet. For about 20 minutes its engines burned to slow it down. Radio signals take more than a second to reach the Moon, and there was no time to wait for instructions, so Vikram flew itself. Its cameras and sensors scanned the ground. About 150 m up, it hovered for a moment to choose a flat, safe place, and then came down gently on its four legs.

## What Pragyan found

Pragyan crept along at about 1 cm a second and travelled around 100 m in all. One of its instruments fired a laser at the soil and studied the light that flashed back. It confirmed sulphur near the south pole, along with aluminium, calcium, iron and other elements.

An instrument on Vikram pushed a probe about 10 cm into the ground. The surface was about 70 °C in the sunshine, yet a few centimetres down it was far colder. Moon dust turns out to be a very good blanket.

Near the end, ISRO fired Vikram's engines once more. It rose about 40 cm, shifted sideways and landed again. That little hop was practice for future missions that may bring Moon rocks back to Earth, as the [Apollo astronauts](apollo-moon) did.

## Why the south pole?

Near the Moon's poles, the Sun always stays low on the horizon. Some deep craters there have been in shadow for billions of years and are colder than −200 °C. Scientists think they may hold frozen water. Melted and cleaned, that ice could one day give astronauts drinking water, oxygen to breathe and fuel for [rockets](rockets-how).

India's first Moon mission, Chandrayaan-1, launched in 2008. It carried a NASA instrument that helped detect water molecules on the Moon's surface. The ice may tell us something about [where the Moon came from](moon-formed), too.

## A day to remember

A day on the Moon lasts about two Earth weeks, followed by two weeks of freezing night. Vikram and Pragyan were built to work for [one lunar day](what-if-live-on-moon). When night fell, ISRO switched them into sleep mode. They never woke up, but they had already finished their jobs.

India now celebrates 23 August as National Space Day, and the landing spot is called Shiv Shakti point. The lander's name honours [Vikram Sarabhai](vikram-sarabhai), the scientist who started India's space programme.
