## The short answer

The North Star, also called Polaris, sits almost exactly above Earth's North Pole. As Earth spins, the whole sky seems to turn around that point. Stars far from it sweep across the sky in big arcs. Polaris is so close to the centre that it hardly moves at all.

Think of a spinning merry-go-round. A child on the edge whizzes round in a big circle. A child sitting right at the middle just turns on the spot. Polaris is the child at the middle.

In India it is known as Dhruva tara. In a well-known story from the Puranas, the boy Dhruva prayed so steadily that he was given a place in the sky as the star that never moves.

## Earth is the one spinning

The stars don't really move across the sky each night. Earth turns once a day, which gives us [day and night](day-night), and that makes the sky look as if it is turning the other way.

Earth spins around an imaginary line called its [axis](?axis), which runs through the North and South Poles. Point that line out into space from the North Pole and it lands less than one degree from Polaris. So while everything else circles, Polaris stays put.

If you leave a camera pointed at the northern sky for a few hours, the stars draw bright circles in the photo. Polaris makes a tiny arc near the centre.

## A star for finding your way

Because Polaris always marks north, travellers have used it for centuries. Its height above the horizon tells you your [latitude](coordinates). Arab and Indian sailors crossing the Indian Ocean measured it with a kamal, a small wooden board on a knotted string. In Bengaluru, at about 13 degrees north, it sits low in the northern sky. In New York, at about 41 degrees, it is much higher. From Australia or South Africa you cannot see it at all, and there is no bright star marking the south pole of the sky.

Polaris shows true north, which is not quite where a compass points. A compass follows [Earth's magnetic field](earth-magnet), whose north pole is in a different place.

In the United States before the Civil War, many people escaping slavery travelled by night towards the North Star, heading for free states and Canada. [Harriet Tubman](harriet-tubman), who led dozens of people to freedom, is said to have used it to guide them.

## North Stars change

Earth's axis is not fixed forever. Like a slowing [spinning top](yo-yo-tops), it wobbles in a slow circle, taking about 26,000 years to go round once. As it does, it points at different stars.

When the [pyramids of Giza](pyramids) were built, about 4,500 years ago, the pole star was Thuban, a faint star in the constellation Draco. Polaris is getting closer to the true pole and will be nearest around the year 2100. In about 12,000 years, the brilliant star Vega will be close enough to serve as the North Star.

## More than one star

Through a big telescope, Polaris turns out to be at least three stars travelling together. The main one is a [yellow-white supergiant](stars-colours), far larger and brighter than our Sun. It looks faint only because it is a few hundred [light-years](light-years) away. Its brightness also pulses very slightly every few days, which makes it useful to astronomers measuring distances across space.
