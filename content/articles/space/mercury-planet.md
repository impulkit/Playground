## The short answer

Mercury is the smallest of the eight [planets](planets-size) and the closest to the Sun. It is a grey, rocky ball covered in craters, a little bigger than our Moon. It has almost no air at all.

Without air to hold in heat, Mercury swings between extremes. In sunlight, the ground reaches about 430 °C, hot enough to melt lead. At night it drops to about −180 °C. Standing on Mercury, the Sun would look about three times wider than it does from Earth, in a black sky.

## A year shorter than a day

Mercury races around the [Sun](sun-what) faster than any other planet. One lap takes just 88 Earth days. That's its year.

But it [spins very slowly](space-weird-day-longer-year), turning once every 59 Earth days. Put those two motions together, and the time from one sunrise to the next is about 176 Earth days. So a single Mercury day lasts two Mercury years. If you lived there, your birthday would come round twice between breakfast and the following breakfast.

Mercury's orbit is also stretched into an oval. From some spots on the surface, the Sun would appear to rise, stop, go backwards a little, then carry on across the sky.

## Hot planet, cold craters

Mercury's spin is barely tilted, so near its poles the Sun always sits low on the horizon. The floors of some deep craters there never see sunlight at all. They stay colder than −170 °C, all the time.

Radar from Earth first spotted bright patches in these craters in 1991. Then NASA's MESSENGER spacecraft, which orbited Mercury from 2011 to 2015, confirmed they hold [water ice](moon-water), possibly delivered by [comets](comets) long ago. It's a strange thought: ice on the planet nearest the Sun.

## A shrinking planet

Mercury has a huge iron core, filling most of its inside. As that core slowly cooled over billions of years, the planet shrank, like an apple drying out and wrinkling. Its rocky crust cracked and buckled into long cliffs, some of them hundreds of kilometres long and over a kilometre high.

Scientists think Mercury's radius has shrunk by several kilometres. Its giant core also gives it a weak magnetic field, something [Venus](venus-hot) and Mars lack.

## Visiting the nearest planet

Getting to Mercury is surprisingly hard. A spacecraft falling towards the Sun speeds up, so it has to brake a lot to slip into orbit. Only a few missions have tried.

NASA's Mariner 10 flew past three times in 1974 and 1975. MESSENGER mapped the whole planet, then ran out of fuel and crashed into it in 2015. BepiColombo, a joint mission by Europe and Japan launched in 2018, has been looping past Earth, Venus and Mercury itself to slow down. It was built to put two spacecraft into orbit around Mercury.

## Spotting it from Earth

Mercury never strays far from the Sun in our sky, so you can only see it low on the horizon just after sunset or just before sunrise. In [Hindi](hindi-language), Mercury is Budh, which gives Wednesday its name, Budhvaar. Many old [calendars](calendars) around the world named the days of the week after the Sun, the Moon and the five planets people could see. In French, Wednesday is mercredi, named after the same planet. Every few years, Mercury crosses in front of the Sun as a tiny black dot. The next time will be in 2032.
