## The short answer

Meteorites are rocks from space that survive the fall through Earth's air and land on the ground. Most are made of stone, similar to rocks on Earth but often containing tiny round grains. Some are made almost entirely of [iron and nickel metal](asteroid-mining). A few are a mix of the two, with green crystals set in shiny metal.

Nearly all meteorites are broken pieces of asteroids, most from the belt between Mars and Jupiter. A small number came from Mars or the Moon, knocked off by big impacts. Many are older than any rock on Earth, so they tell scientists what the solar system was made of when it was brand new.

## Meteoroid, meteor, meteorite

These three words are easy to mix up. While a space rock is still in space, it is a meteoroid. When it hits Earth's air and glows as a streak of light, that streak is a meteor, or [shooting star](shooting-stars). If any of it reaches the ground, that piece is a meteorite.

Most meteoroids are no bigger than a grain of sand and burn up completely. Only rocks at least the size of a pebble or a fist, moving fairly slowly, survive to land.

## Stones, irons and stony-irons

More than nine out of ten meteorites seen falling are stony. Most of these are chondrites, packed with chondrules, little round beads of rock that melted and cooled in space before the planets formed. Some chondrites also contain specks of white mineral that are the oldest solid material known, about 4.57 billion years old. Like Earth's [rocks](rocks-types), they are made of minerals, but they have barely changed since they formed.

Iron meteorites are chunks of the metal cores of small worlds that melted, separated into layers and later broke apart. When an iron meteorite is cut, polished and treated, it shows a striking criss-cross pattern of metal crystals that can only grow while metal cools extremely slowly, over millions of years. They are heavy, and a [magnet](magnets-how) sticks to them strongly.

Stony-iron meteorites are the rarest. Some, called pallasites, are among the most beautiful rocks known, with golden-green olivine crystals in metal.

## Giants and famous falls

The largest meteorite ever found is the Hoba meteorite in Namibia, a slab of iron weighing about 60 tonnes. It was found by a farmer in 1920 and is too heavy to move, so it still lies where it landed.

The Willamette meteorite, about 15.5 tonnes, is the largest found in the United States and is on display at the American Museum of Natural History in New York. The Grand Ronde people of Oregon hold it sacred, and they and the museum reached an agreement in 2000 that allows them to visit it for ceremonies.

In 1954, in Sylacauga, Alabama, a meteorite crashed through a house roof, bounced off a radio and hit a woman named Ann Hodges as she napped on her sofa. She was left with a large bruise. It is the best-documented case of a person being hit.

India has its own falls. In June 2017, a rare carbon-rich meteorite fell at Mukundpura, near [Jaipur](jaipur), and scientists studied it for clues to the chemistry of the early solar system.

## Hunting on the ice

[Antarctica](antarctica) is the best place on Earth to find meteorites. Dark rocks stand out on white ice, and slowly moving [glaciers](glaciers) carry them to certain areas where wind strips the ice away, leaving them on the surface. Teams have recovered tens of thousands of meteorites there.

Some contain amino acids, the building blocks of proteins, made in space. The Murchison meteorite, which fell in Australia in 1969, contains tiny grains of stardust older than the Sun. Holding a meteorite is the closest most of us will come to touching another world.
