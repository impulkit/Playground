## The short answer

Gaganyaan, meaning "sky vehicle" in [Sanskrit](sanskrit), is India's human spaceflight programme. Its aim is to launch two or three astronauts on an Indian rocket, from [Sriharikota](isro-launch) in Andhra Pradesh, into orbit about 400 km above Earth. They would circle the planet for up to three days, then return in a capsule that splashes down in the sea.

If it succeeds, India will become the fourth country to send people into space on its own, after the Soviet Union in 1961, the United States in 1961 and China in 2003. [ISRO](isro-missions) is testing each part carefully first: the rocket, the capsule, the [parachutes](parachutes) and an escape system to save the crew if something goes wrong.

## The astronauts

In February 2024, India named the four astronaut-designates for Gaganyaan. All four are test pilots from the Indian Air Force: Prasanth Balakrishnan Nair, Ajit Krishnan, Angad Pratap and Shubhanshu Shukla. They [trained in Russia and in Bengaluru](astronaut-training), learning everything from spacecraft systems to how to survive if the capsule lands somewhere unexpected.

In June 2025, Shukla flew to the [International Space Station](space-station) on a private mission called Axiom-4, launched from the United States. He became the first Indian to visit the station, and the experience he brought home is feeding into Gaganyaan.

## Safety first

The most important part of any crewed rocket is the part you hope never to use: the crew escape system. If the rocket fails, small powerful motors pull the capsule away and parachutes bring it down.

On 21 October 2023, ISRO tested this with a special test rocket from Sriharikota. At about 12 km up, the escape system fired, pulled away a test capsule, and brought it down under parachutes into the Bay of Bengal, where the Navy recovered it. ISRO has also dropped capsules from helicopters to test the parachutes.

The [rocket](rockets-how) chosen to carry the crew is a strengthened version of LVM3, India's heaviest launcher, the same type that sent [Chandrayaan-3](chandrayaan) to the Moon.

## A robot goes first

Before any astronaut flies, ISRO plans uncrewed test flights. One is meant to carry Vyommitra, a half-humanoid [robot](robots) with a head, arms and a body but no legs. Vyommitra can speak, watch the capsule's control panels and report back on temperature, air and other conditions, just as a crew member would.

## From Rakesh Sharma to Gaganyaan

[India's first astronaut](indian-astronauts), Rakesh Sharma, flew to the Soviet Salyut 7 space station in April 1984, on a Soviet Soyuz rocket. Prime Minister Indira Gandhi spoke to him by video link and asked [how India looked from space](earth-from-space). He replied, "Saare jahan se achha", better than the whole world, a line from a famous patriotic song. He spent nearly eight days in orbit and did yoga exercises to study how the body copes without weight.

India's space programme was started in the 1960s by [Vikram Sarabhai](vikram-sarabhai), who believed space technology should help ordinary people through weather forecasts, communication and education. Human spaceflight was a later dream.

ISRO has said it aims to fly the first Gaganyaan crew in 2027. By November 2026, though, the first uncrewed test flight, with Vyommitra on board, had not yet launched. India's longer-term plans include its own space station, the Bharatiya Antariksh Station, by 2035, and an Indian astronaut on the Moon by 2040.
