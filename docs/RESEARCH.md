# What we learned from Encarta and Microsoft Student

## Encarta (1993–2009)
- It launched in 1993 as a CD-ROM. The 1993 edition had nine subject categories, "hot links" between articles, a *Topic Tracker* history, a timeline from 15 million BC to the present, and an atlas with clickable hot spots. Media icons showed which articles had sound, video or animation. ([COMPUTE!, 1993](https://www.atarimagazines.com/compute/issue156/74_Encarta.php))
- Later editions added the **MindMaze** quiz game, a 3D atlas (built on MapPoint, with about 1.8 million locations), a Visual Browser, a Research Organizer and interactivities. *Encarta Kids* arrived in 2005. Encarta Premium 2008 had about 62,000 articles, 25,000 pictures and 300 videos. ([Wikipedia: Encarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encarta))
- Its search tool, "Pinpointer", used compact bit-array indexes so it would run fast from a CD. Usability testing taught the team to put search first and keep navigation shallow. ([Interview with an Encarta developer lead](https://medium.com/@mistymadonna/q-a-my-interview-with-microsoft-encarta-developer-lead-56d1ff054ec6))
- **Microsoft Student** (2006–2009) bundled Encarta Premium with homework tools: a step-by-step equation solver, a graphing calculator, *Learning Essentials* templates, foreign-language help, a dictionary and thesaurus, and the atlas. ([Microsoft, 2006](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2006/07/06/microsoft-releases-student-with-encarta-premium-2007-teachers-parents-and-students-give-software-an-a/))
- Microsoft closed Encarta in 2009. People widely put this down to free, constantly updated Wikipedia.

## What Kyun keeps (as ideas, not content)
| Encarta / Student | Kyun |
|---|---|
| Hot links, Topic Tracker | Inline links, "See also", History, Your journey |
| Pinpointer | Search grouped into articles, words, places and moments |
| Atlas with hot spots | World and India atlas; every pin opens an article |
| Timeline | Timeline with era filters; each moment opens an article |
| MindMaze | Quest: a 10-floor quiz tower generated from the content |
| Interactivities and media | 22 interactive models, plus openly licensed photos |
| Dictionary | A tap-to-define dictionary, inside articles |

Encarta's text, images and the names "Encarta" and "MindMaze" belong to Microsoft. Kyun doesn't use any of them.

## What this means for Kyun
Encarta lost to free and constantly updated content. Kyun's answer is free stories in both markets, a weekly publishing rhythm (the Today feed) and depth that Wikipedia can't offer children: plain language, certainty labels and India- and US-relevant examples. Kyun Plus charges only for extras, never for knowledge.
