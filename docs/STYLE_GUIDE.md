# Kyun style guide

## Who we write for
A curious 9–14 year old in India who reads well in English but isn't a native expert. Talk to them the way a good teacher does: respectfully, clearly, and with real facts. Don't talk down to them.

## The shape of an article
1. **Title = a real question.** "Why is the sky blue?" rather than "Rayleigh scattering".
2. **Hook:** one line that makes you want to know more.
3. **The short answer:** 2–3 short paragraphs. Someone who stops here should still have the real answer.
4. **Deeper sections:** each answers one follow-up question ("How do we know?", "Where in India?", "What's still unknown?").
5. **Surprising but true:** one fact that makes people say "wait, what?". It must still be true and sourced.
6. **Links:** at least two, each with a *why* written as a question that pulls the reader on.

## Sentences
- Aim for 8–20 words. The validator warns above 32.
- One idea per sentence. Use the active voice.
- Explain a hard word the first time, or link it to the dictionary with `[word](?id)`.
- Use comparisons a child can picture, especially Indian ones: "as long as two autos", "taller than Qutub Minar".
- Use metric units, with Indian number names where natural (lakh, crore), plus the international figure.

## Honesty
- Say what scientists are **sure** of, what's **mostly agreed**, and what's **still debated**.
- When numbers are estimates, say "about".
- Never invent a quote, a statistic or a source.

## Fairness
- Show India's contributions with pride and accuracy, without exaggerating them.
- For civics and history, describe what happened and what people disagree about. Don't take political sides.
- People: describe what they did. Don't use photos of real people unless they're openly licensed and clearly relevant.

## Never
- Copy text from copyrighted sources (Encarta, Britannica, textbooks, news sites).
- Include song lyrics, copyrighted characters, film stills or brand logos.
- Add external links in the body.

## Punctuation
- **No em dashes (—), ever.** Use a full stop, a comma, a colon or brackets instead. The validator rejects them.
- En dashes are fine only in number ranges (8–9 m, 1632–1653).
